Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Eight

The Same Four-Leaf Clover, Different Hearts

The first week closes · the roadshow ends

The warmth of that summer beer and barbecue not yet gone

Seoul · and a departure for Los Angeles

The warmth left by that summer cold-beer-and-barbecue meal seemed not yet to have faded when Bae Joo-hyun was already standing back in the practice room.

The September comeback date for ‘Dumb Dumb’ was approaching; the choreography’s blocking grew denser, and the space left for individual schedules narrowed day by day.

Baek Si-on, meanwhile, shuttled between theaters, elevators, and the van.

The meal on which the two had briefly left their work orbits was soon covered over again by their separate schedules.

‘Sado’s first-week roadshow finally came to a close that day.

Before the film opened, the Korean film world had not been uniformly optimistic about Baek Si-on.

His most representative performance to date came from ‘Green Fly’.

That was an independent film relying on close-up shots, realistic texture, and the actor’s own state of being.

Baek Si-on had won the Venice Best Actor with it, proving the weight of his acting, and proving that an independent film could generate enormous volume.

But awards and commercial drawing power belonged to two separate evaluation systems.

Jeon Do-yeon was the Cannes Best Actress, Korea’s number-one actress.

That didn’t mean every commercial film she appeared in automatically achieved box-office success.

So, to many old-school film people, Baek Si-on’s die-hard fans could carry the first day.

The Best Actor halo could carry the first three days.

Once fan enthusiasm faded, a heavy court tragedy whose ending everyone already knew would quickly hand the real box office back to ordinary viewers.

Some media had even prepared their pieces in advance.

“How long can Baek Si-on’s fan box office last?”

“An independent-film actor may not suit a commercial blockbuster.”

“Can the Best Actor halo mask the acting gap between Baek Si-on and Song Kang-ho?”

Just waiting for the day the fans receded and the box office dived, to press send.

Seven days passed.

That day never came.

The pieces didn’t even get the chance to be published.

Because ‘Sado’s box office not only failed to fall — it was still rising.

❧ ❧ ❧

Eleven in the morning.

The Korean Film Council’s computer network updated ‘Sado’s first-week results.

KOFIC network · ‘Sado’ first week

Seven-day cumulative admissions — 3.87 million

Cumulative box office — about ₩29.8 billion

First-week market share — 55.4%

Sunday single-day admissions — 780,000

Week-two presale share — 46.9%, still ranked first

Any one of these numbers, taken alone, was astonishing enough.

But what truly sent distributors and theaters into collective loss of control were the last two.

the two that broke the curve

First-week occupancy drop — under 5%

Word-of-mouth drop — close to zero

Korean commercial films’ box office usually followed a very clear decay curve.

The normal decay curve

Day one explodes.

Day two, the box office begins to fall.

Opening weekend is the first peak.

Week two’s weekdays dive at once.

A film that could keep occupancy basically undropped and word-of-mouth uncollapsed across a seven-day first week was rare enough to count on one’s fingers in Korean film history.

‘Sado’ did it.

Less than an hour after the first-week data was published, the media headlines surged in like a tide.

first-week headlines · across the wires

“‘Sado’ draws 3.87 million in its first week! The strongest opening for a Korean historical drama — unshakeable!”

“Baek Si-on’s first film after Venice delivers perfectly — his commercial box-office power is now beyond doubt!”

“Song Kang-ho and Baek Si-on’s ‘father-son war’ sweeps the summer season — nearly ₩30 billion in week one!”

“From Billboard number one to summer box-office king — Baek Si-on’s all-round domination has no end.”

“Song Kang-ho’s thirty years meet the rookie monster Baek Si-on — Korean film has finally awaited this ‘national-treasure showdown’.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Those media that had been waiting to watch Baek Si-on be “publicly executed” by Song Kang-ho had quietly revised their headlines and content.

the quietly revised headlines

Before

“Can the Best Actor halo mask the acting gap between Baek Si-on and Song Kang-ho?”

After

“Two Best Actors elevate each other — ‘Sado’ presents the cruelest father-son relationship in years.”

Before

“How long can Baek Si-on’s fan box office last?”

After

“From fan economy to family viewing — how ‘Sado’ completed its audience expansion.”

Before

“An independent-film actor may not suit a commercial blockbuster.”

After

“Baek Si-on proves commercial scale and acting depth are not contradictory.”

To sum up, Song Kang-ho really had publicly executed Baek Si-on — only the method of execution was forcing him to deliver the best performance of his career.

❧ ❧ ❧

The industry shock of the first-week data quickly spread from the media pages to another conference room.

Lotte Entertainment headquarters.

On the distribution department’s projection screen hung the poster of a film about to open — ‘Memories of the Sword’.

A period martial-arts commercial film.

The lead cast included Lee Byung-hun, Jeon Do-yeon, and Kim Go-eun; the production scale, star configuration, and visual selling points were all strong.

By Lotte’s original judgment, ‘Sado’ opened August 3, and by the time ‘Memories of the Sword’ entered, it would already be in its second week.

A over-long, heavy historical tragedy whose ending everyone knew — however high its opening, it should have begun to fall back naturally.

Lotte’s plan

Week one, avoid ‘Sado’s strongest fan and media heat; week two, enter when the other’s word-of-mouth falls back and theaters release screens.

Rely on martial-arts action, the star cast, and stronger entertainment value to catch the summer audience.

Internally, the distribution department called this operation: off-peak scavenging.

Validated countless times in the Korean film market, it was practically the standard play for a mid-sized film to avoid a blockbuster’s crush.

But it had one premise: the blockbuster had to decay along the normal curve.

The theater-liaison head projected the latest scheduling forecast onto the screen.

“CGV isn’t planning to cut ‘Sado’s showtimes on a large scale.”

“Lotte Cinemas’ own purchasing department is also demanding the prime slots be kept.”

Someone at the conference table frowned.

“That’s our own theater chain.”

The head answered helplessly:

“So they know better which film makes money.”

The promotion head, holding on to a last shred of hope:

“It’s bound to drop in week two, right?”

The data analyst shook his head.

“Week-two presales are still near forty-seven percent.”

“Worse, the share of viewers over forty is still rising.”

The conference room fell quiet.

The marketing manager broke the silence first, staring at the scheduling forecast:

“If we open as originally planned, the scheduling we can get is roughly 12% to 14%.”

12%.

For a cast of Lee Byung-hun, Jeon Do-yeon, and Kim Go-eun, that number was close to giving up treatment — flat-out lying down.

Worse, they couldn’t pin the blame on the theater chain.

Lotte Cinemas could of course look after a film distributed by Lotte Entertainment.

But it couldn’t, for intra-group face, throw a ‘Sado’ with over ninety-percent occupancy out of the prime halls and leave the empty seats to a new film not yet proven by the market.

The theater head’s logic was very plain.

Whoever’s film fills the seats owns the screen for now.

Even the group’s own son was no exception.

The creative representative finally couldn’t hold back:

“Didn’t we agree on off-peak release?”

“Why isn’t Lee Joon-ik’s film decaying normally?”

The marketing manager smiled bitterly.

“Because it isn’t an ordinary commercial film.”

The distribution head flipped through the media coverage on his phone.

“Someone wrote an analysis.”

“Saying ‘Sado’ has become a kind of social topic.”

“‘Have you seen ‘Sado’?’ has already become a standard topic in offices, family dinners, and social settings.”

This meant the box office was no longer driven by the film’s own appeal alone.

It had entered the “social currency” stage.

People bought tickets not only to watch Baek Si-on and Song Kang-ho act, but because everyone was watching, and not watching yourself meant one less thing to talk about.

The creative representative asked:

“Then what do we do?”

The distribution head pushed up his glasses:

“Either postpone two weeks.”

“Or force it out.”

The marketing manager was already doing the math.

“If we force it, the odds of the promotion budget going down the drain are at least seventy percent.”

There was no third option.

Because the third option was called accepting the reality of being crushed.

The distribution head looked at that ever-rising presale curve and finally closed his laptop.

“Postpone. Wait until ‘Sado’s scheduling drops below thirty percent.”

“And if it never drops?”

The distribution head swept his gaze across the creative representative from the other side of the table.

“Then pray Baek Si-on flies to America to shoot his film soon, and stops doing roadshows in Korea.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The Lotte Entertainment distribution head who had prayed for “Baek Si-on to fly to America soon” finally got his good news.

August 11.

Baek Si-on appeared at Incheon International Airport.

Per the long-set schedule, he would depart for Los Angeles to prepare the formal shoot of the Hollywood film ‘La La Land’.

The distributors of same-window competitors breathed a temporary sigh of relief.

For the Korean film market, the face with the most box-office pull on the ‘Sado’ roadshow had officially left the field.

He would no longer appear at a different theater every day, hauling audiences into the halls busload by busload.

A little after seven in the morning.

Incheon International Airport’s departure hall had already gathered a large crowd of fans and media.

Baek Si-on’s van stopped outside the lane.

The instant the door was pushed open, neat and loud cheers came from outside.

The home-site masters’ long lenses lit up their shutters first.

Baek Si-on, in a black cap and sunglasses, got down from the van.

Baek Eun-ah followed at his side, one hand pushing a suitcase, the other holding his passport and boarding materials.

The bodyguards quickly established a security lane on both sides.

The reporters in the media area closed in fast.

The interview area had been cordoned off in advance; microphones and audio equipment reached over from several directions.

“‘Sado’ drew nearly four million in its first week and is in the most critical expansion phase of its box office. Yet you’re going to America at this moment — don’t you find it a pity?”

Baek Si-on stopped and faced the microphones.

“The film has already been handed to the audience.”

“What comes next is decided by the audience and the distribution team together.”

“Me standing alone in a theater can’t change anything.”

Another reporter pressed right after:

“‘Green Fly’ and ‘Sado’ have consecutively exceeded expectations; the industry has started calling you a ‘box-office elixir.’ Do you accept that title?”

Baek Si-on thought a moment.

“After a film succeeds, everyone remembers only one actor’s name — it isn’t fair to the other creators.”

He paused.

“But if the production company next time is willing to pay more because of the title, I wouldn’t refuse.”

A ripple of laughter ran through the press.

“So you’re open to a higher salary?”

“I’ve always been open to plans that reasonably respect the value of an actor’s labor.”

The reporters quickly turned the question to the American project.

“You’re about to shoot a Hollywood film in America — can you reveal which director’s work it is?”

“The specifics are still under wraps,” Baek Si-on answered.

“Can’t you even say the type of role?” the reporter pressed.

“I can only say it’s completely different from my previous roles.”

“I hope that once the film is officially announced, everyone can give me a little time to adjust to the new challenge.”

A fashion-section reporter suddenly noticed something peeking at Baek Si-on’s neck.

He raised his hand at once:

“Si-on-ssi, are you wearing a Van Cleef & Arpels four-leaf clover necklace today?”

The flashes quickly converged on his neck.

“Is this a personal choice, or an upcoming brand collaboration?”

Baek Si-on looked down and glanced at the necklace.

❧ ❧ ❧

Seoul’s summer night was very quiet; insects chirped now and then outside the window.

Choi Jin-ri lay draped over Baek Si-on, her chin against his chest, her long hair spilled at his shoulder.

Blin was balled on her back.

Both front paws tucked at its chest, tail curled at its side — as steady as if that spot had always been a cat bed.

Baek Si-on raised a hand and rubbed Blin’s head.

Blin narrowed its eyes and didn’t move.

To a cat, a human’s reluctance to part had no participation value.

After who knew how long, Choi Jin-ri straightened up from him.

Blin, which had been settled comfortably on her back, found its support suddenly change angle and could only let out a displeased cry and jump to the edge of the bed.

Choi Jin-ri ignored the protest, reached both hands behind her neck, and undid the clasp.

The four-leaf clover necklace slid off her neck and fell into her palm.

It was the one Baek Si-on had given her.

Since receiving it, she had worn it almost every day.

Even for public events, she would hide it in her collar per the styling, or put it back on after coming home.

Choi Jin-ri bent down and looped the necklace around Baek Si-on’s neck.

The clasp closed behind his neck.

The four-leaf clover, still carrying her body heat, fell against his chest.

“It’ll go to America in my place, with you.”

Her fingertip lightly touched the metal leaf.

“Bless you with everything going smoothly.”

Baek Si-on touched the necklace with his fingertip too.

“One necklace can stand in for you?”

Choi Jin-ri gave a little hmph.

“Temporarily.”

“When you’re back, you repay it with principal and interest.”

“How is the interest calculated?”

She considered it seriously and said:

“The interest is that you have to think of me every day.”

Baek Si-on reached out and gathered her loose hair behind her ear.

“Then I lose out.”

“How do you lose out?”

“The interest is too low.”

Choi Jin-ri blinked, and only then caught the meaning of the line.

Baek Si-on was saying he would think of her every day anyway.

So it didn’t count as interest — it counted as zero interest.

She buried her face in the hollow of his shoulder and said, muffled:

“Can’t you say something nice normally, just once?”

“Wasn’t that nice just now?”

“It took a whole circle to get nice.”

“At least it got nice.”

She stopped talking, only tightening the arm draped across his front.

The insects outside the window kept chirping.

Blin yawned at the foot of the bed, rolled over, and went on sleeping.

❧ ❧ ❧

Incheon International Airport’s glaring flashes returned before him.

Baek Si-on answered the reporter:

“Personal preference.”

“No endorsement collaboration at the moment.”

The fashion reporter obviously wasn’t prepared to let the information go.

“Did you buy it yourself?”

“Why suddenly wear a women’s necklace?”

“Does it carry a special meaning?”

Baek Eun-ah stepped half a step forward and took over the topic.

“Mr. Baek Si-on’s boarding time is nearly here; the interview ends here.”

“Thank you all for coming so early in the morning.”

Baek Si-on bowed to the media area.

“Thank you for your trouble.”

Someone in the interview area still pressed about the necklace’s origin.

Baek Si-on didn’t keep answering; he put his sunglasses back on and turned toward the fan barricade.

❧ ❧ ❧

Behind the barricade there were many people; the wave of sound rose again at Baek Si-on’s arrival.

Posters, autograph books, and phones were handed out from every direction.

Baek Si-on took a signing pen from a staff member and began signing one by one.

After a few posters, someone handed over a LAVUE shopping bag printed with Bae Joo-hyun’s face.

Baek Si-on glanced at it and looked up to ask her:

“Sign where?”

“On Irene’s face.”

“And the product?”

“Couldn’t get one.”

“……”

Couldn’t get the product, yet had collected the shopping bag in advance.

Consumers had developed an advanced model of managing stock and fan merchandise separately.

Baek Si-on finished signing and handed it back, then noticed the next poster handed over belonged to a visibly very young girl.

Not tall.

Still baby fat on her face.

By age, probably a middle-schooler.

Baek Si-on pointed at her.

“How old are you?”

The girl, confirming he was asking her, stiffened all over.

The home-site master beside her prompted her to answer.

“Fif— fifteen!”

Her voice trembled with excitement.

“Oppa, I really, really like you!”

“Came by yourself?”

“No, I came with the fan club’s unnies.”

Several fans beside her raised their hands at once to vouch.

“We’ll see her home!”

“Her parents know!”

“We’ve already contacted home!”

Baek Si-on, confirming she had company, handed the signed poster back.

“Thank you for liking me.”

The girl nodded hard.

“But your most important task right now isn’t chasing my schedule.”

“It’s walking your own path well first, and making yourself better.”

The area near the barricade fell quiet.

Baek Si-on’s tone was gentle.

He knew a fifteen-year-old could hardly accept a “don’t come anymore,” which amounted to telling her that her liking was a burden on others.

“Don’t worry about missing anything.”

“When you’re seriously moving forward, I’m always here.”

The girl froze.

The fans near the barricade also went quiet for an instant.

The next moment, the emotion burst completely.

“Oppa is so gentle!”

“I’ll seriously move forward too!”

“When I get into college I’ll tell oppa first thing!”

“You have to always be here!”

A few home-site masters in the front row had confirmed the whole exchange was recorded completely.

The girl’s eyes reddened; she nodded hard.

“I’ll work hard!”

Baek Si-on nodded at her.

Then went on signing.

❧ ❧ ❧

Boarding time drew near.

Baek Si-on handed the signing pen back to the staff and waved to the fan area.

“Thank you all for seeing me off.”

“Get home safely.”

“Ne!”

He walked toward the terminal interior, accompanied by Baek Eun-ah and the security staff.

The shouts behind him were still clearly audible through the lane.

After entering the VIP security area, the media lenses and fans were finally all shut outside.

Baek Si-on took off his sunglasses and said to Baek Eun-ah:

“That fifteen-year-old girl just now.”

Baek Eun-ah looked up.

“Have a home-site master or the fan club head confirm her details.”

Baek Eun-ah caught on at once.

“You want to mail her a gift?”

“Mm.”

She was already running through the options in her head.

“A signed album? The Under Armour collab?”

Baek Si-on shook his head.

“Neither.”

“Then what?”

“Workbooks.”

Baek Eun-ah stopped walking.

“What?”

“Full-subject workbooks — social studies and science can’t be left out.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

“Do you know that an idol gifting signed workbooks might leave a minor with a lifetime of psychological shadow?”

“At least it can leave knowledge.”

“She was nearly moved to tears just now.”

“She might also cry after receiving the workbooks.”

“Is that the same?”

“The effect is close.”

Baek Eun-ah took a deep breath.

She finally understood that Baek Si-on’s line, “When you’re seriously moving forward, I’m always here,” had an unpublished second half.

He wasn’t only there.

He would also check whether you were seriously moving forward.

Eun-ah pre-plays the girl unboxing the parcel

Book one.

An idol’s signature — moved.

Book two.

Still signed — even more moved.

Book three.

Why is it still a workbook?

Book four.

Does Baek Si-on have some misunderstanding about her grades?

Book five.

The price of liking an idol finally lands on the knowledge points.

“I’ll throw in a signed album set too.”

Baek Eun-ah decided to leave a little normal fan-club component in the gift.

Baek Si-on didn’t object.

“Fine.”

Only then did Baek Eun-ah keep walking.

❧ ❧ ❧

Outside the terminal.

The fifteen-year-old girl still stood near the barricade.

Hugging the signed poster, she repeated herself over and over to the unnies beside her.

“Oppa said, when I’m seriously moving forward, he’s always here.”

“He really said it!”

“He really said that!”

A few older unnies laughed beside her.

What she didn’t know was that a private gift from her idol had already entered the procurement process.

Nor that, besides the signed album, the gift also held a full set of every-subject workbooks.

Knowledge was on its way.

And what Baek Si-on likewise didn’t know was that a photo of his outfit as he left the airport was spreading rapidly across Korean social platforms.

❧ ❧ ❧

Evening.

The Red Velvet dorm.

After practice ended, the sound of showers and the hum of hair dryers came now and then down the corridor.

Bae Joo-hyun lay on her own bed, scrolling through the photos of Baek Si-on’s Incheon Airport departure today.

The home-site masters’ high-definition photos were already out.

Black cap.

Sunglasses.

A simple white tee with jeans — the whole outfit so plain it bordered on perfunctory.

But that Baek Si-on looked good in anything — she had long accepted it.

No use objecting.

After saving a few, she laid the phone on her chest and stared at the ceiling.

She had actually been very happy lately.

The group had won a number-one, the LAVUE ad was a huge success, the magazine cover had reacted well, and the counter-clerk cameo had gone smoothly.

She had also privately danced with Baek Si-on, eaten barbecue, and dropped the honorifics.

You could say she was in a phase where both career and feelings were steadily rising.

But only today, confirming Baek Si-on had gone to America, did she finally realize one thing.

That day’s dance, however sweet and heart-fluttering, was essentially Baek Si-on using her to practice dancing with another woman.

The film’s female lead was someone else.

She was only a convenient, correctly sized partner who happened to need to hand in homework.

Bae Joo-hyun rolled over.

The pillow was pressed into a crease.

The sourness came very suddenly.

She lit the phone again.

Opened the search box.

Typed:

the search · late at night

Emma Stone

American actress

Filmography: multiple Hollywood commercial films and independent films

The search results popped up a mass of information.

Bae Joo-hyun began browsing from the photos.

Blond hair, big eyes, a bright smile.

The preset image of standing side by side with Baek Si-on on a red carpet formed automatically in her head.

Shoulder to shoulder, hand in hand, photographed by the world’s media.

Bae Joo-hyun’s thumb paused on the screen a while.

She didn’t like this image.

But she knew clearly she had no right to dislike it.

They were only senior and junior.

Or rather, had just gone from senior-and-junior to a relationship where they could call each other by name.

That was all.

After flipping through more photos, Bae Joo-hyun’s mood gradually changed.

She would never deliberately compare her looks with someone else’s.

Such comparison was both meaningless and undignified.

But if you had to compare.

She chose to trust Baek Si-on’s taste.

After all, he had spent three years and ₩600 million investing a brand in this face of hers; if he really thought her unattractive, he wouldn’t have invested so generously.

With that standard, at least she wouldn’t lose.

❧ ❧ ❧

A knock sounded suddenly.

Bae Joo-hyun instinctively pressed the phone screen dark.

It was still on Emma Stone’s photo page.

If her younger members found her searching Baek Si-on’s Hollywood female partner in the middle of the night, tomorrow the whole dorm would know the leader was “running a background check.”

Park So-young was especially dangerous.

“Who is it?”

A deliberately lowered voice came from outside the door.

“Unnie, can I come in?”

Son Seung-wan.

“Come in.”

Bae Joo-hyun stuffed the phone under the pillow.

The door was pushed open, then quickly closed.

“What is it, in the middle of the night?”

Bae Joo-hyun sat up.

Son Seung-wan, hands behind her back, stood at the door and gave two heh-heh laughs.

That way of laughing sounded like an underground merchant pushing contraband.

“What are you laughing at?”

“Nothing.”

Son Seung-wan walked to the bedside, drew a jewelry box from her pocket, and held it out in front of her.

“What is it?”

“Unnie, open and look?”

“Tell me first.”

“If I tell you, there’s no surprise.”

Bae Joo-hyun took the box and flipped it open.

In the center of the dark lining lay a gold necklace, a four-leaf clover pendant resting in the middle.

Bae Joo-hyun blinked a few times.

Somehow familiar.

She lifted the pendant, and her memory quickly matched it with the airport photos she had just saved.

The necklace Baek Si-on had worn at his neck at Incheon Airport today.

Bae Joo-hyun turned her face:

“What does this mean?”

Son Seung-wan, looking smug:

“The same one Baek-sunbae has!”

“I went hunting right after I saw the airport photos today — raised the price twice to grab it.”

“A gift for unnie!”

If you asked who in Red Velvet “admired” Bae Joo-hyun most, Son Seung-wan ranked as the number-one fan.

She wasn’t good at expressing gratitude with long speeches and sentimental words, so she chose the most direct way.

— Spending money.

Koreans have many ways to express sincerity; cash expenditure is usually the hardest to fake.

Bae Joo-hyun felt gratified inside.

This younger member had truly grown up, and truly knew how to repay.

But gratification aside.

Though Son Seung-wan had landed the ‘Everytime’ vocal, the OST wouldn’t go live until ‘Descendants of the Sun’ aired next year, and the royalty split was half a year further off.

Van Cleef & Arpels was not cheap.

Bae Joo-hyun gently closed the lid and pushed the box back.

“I’ve received the thought — take the necklace back.”

Son Seung-wan froze.

“Why?”

Bae Joo-hyun didn’t say outright “you still owe the company debt” — that would wound her pride too much.

She smiled:

“Keep it for yourself.”

Son Seung-wan, holding the box, frowned.

“But I bought it specially for unnie.”

“Return it.”

“The daigobuyer won’t allow returns.”

“Then you wear it.”

“It’s the same one Baek-sunbae has — what would I wear it for?”

Bae Joo-hyun: “……”

Son Seung-wan had precisely found the leader’s weak point.

She took the necklace out of the box and waved it once in front of Bae Joo-hyun.

“Unnie.”

“It’s the same one Baek-sunbae has, you know.”

Son Seung-wan waved it a second time.

“It’s sold out across all of Korea.”

“I raised the price twice to grab it.”

A third time.

“Can unnie really bear to not want it?”

“……”

Bae Joo-hyun’s reason was still telling her she should refuse.

But her heart had already begun to waver.

Sold out across Korea, no restock short-term.

If she didn’t take it now, who knew how long she’d wait to buy it again.

If she took it, she’d own an object whose meaning only she knew.

Of course, it was only the same model.

It couldn’t count as a couple’s necklace.

At most it could count as… a couple’s edition.

Stepping back.

It was also a four-leaf clover.

She had once firmly believed that being near Baek Si-on made her luck better; the link between a four-leaf clover and good luck fitted her metaphysical theory to a T.

Her thoughts multiplied quickly; the hesitation was already written on her face.

Son Seung-wan, seeing this, immediately struck while the iron was hot.

“If unnie really can’t rest easy about it.”

She deliberately paused, making a very aggrieved face.

“Then I’ll just go ask Baek-sunbae to reimburse me.”

Bae Joo-hyun’s face changed at once.

“Ya!”

She flipped the covers off and looked down for her slippers.

Son Seung-wan knew this signal too well.

Bae Joo-hyun looking down for her slippers in the Red Velvet dorm only ever meant one thing.

Preparing to give chase.

Son Seung-wan turned and ran.

The door was yanked open.

“I said reimburse!”

“Not confess!”

“Unnie, stop chasing me!”

The slap-slap-slap of slippers on the floor came down the corridor.

It lasted a dozen-odd steps.

Then went quiet.

Bae Joo-hyun, probably remembering other members were already asleep, gave up the chase.

❧ ❧ ❧

The room grew quiet again.

Bae Joo-hyun sat back on the bed.

The jewelry box still lay on the covers.

She lifted the necklace before her eyes.

Her other hand found the phone.

Unlocked it.

Opened the airport photos she had saved today.

She zoomed the image in to Baek Si-on’s collar.

The four-leaf clover hung clearly at his neck.

Bae Joo-hyun looked down to confirm the model in her hand, then raised the phone to compare.

She checked back and forth a long time.

Finally she set the phone on the bedside, reached both hands behind her neck, and put the necklace on.

The metal carried a little coolness as it touched her skin.

She looked down at it, then opened the front camera.

In the lens, she wore pajamas, her hair loose, a small four-leaf clover added at her neck.
