Chapter One Hundred Seventy One

The Play Count That Goes to War for Its Country

December 22 · one Monday, two mornings A counter that refuses to sit still · one 7:46 a.m. push · six headlines and a checkout TV · a 99+ red morning · a three-word thank-you that waits seventeen hours Seoul · a portal newsroom, the morning commute, one convenience store, one bedroom → Los Angeles · a black Escalade on the 405 An editor bolts a qualifier onto history, the nation’s commute gets drafted, a woken singer finds the whole world waiting in her comments — and a thank-you crosses the Pacific just in time to spend the night unread.

December 22. A little after seven in the morning.

Seoul. The office of the entertainment-desk chief at a Korean web portal.

Baek Si-on’s full-length ‘Love Yourself’ music video.

Fourteen hours since release.

Play count: seventeen million.

the video page · the counter, top right

17,000,000

17,080,000

17,210,000

Refresh. Refresh again. The number climbed like it had somewhere urgent to be.

Kim Jae-wook stared at it for three full seconds, then swore.

Kim Jae-wook

Entertainment desk chief · a Korean web portal

Iced americano · the qualifier doctrine · “Ship it.”

The editor who refuses to print a bare crown. He bolted Nicki Minaj and Taylor Swift onto a play count, called it serving the nation, and accidentally authored the arithmetic of a country’s mornings.

“This is insane. These numbers are genuinely insane.”

The young reporter beside him was already typing. He didn’t look up.

“Chief, how do we write the big headline?”

“Can we write ‘first in Korean history’?”

Kim Jae-wook gave it some thought.

Strictly speaking, at the current pace — seventeen million in fourteen hours — twenty million inside the first twenty-four was a done deal.

But Psy’s 2013 ‘Gentleman’ still owned the first-day record: thirty-eight million.

And that had been a globally viral song, riding the traffic inertia and curiosity ‘Gangnam Style’ had banked — a different racetrack entirely from the way normal pop music actually travels.

Write “first in Korean history” bare, and the outlet next door would be hammering them with it within the hour.

Kim Jae-wook took a sip of iced americano and gave himself two seconds to cool down.

“Put a qualifier in front.”

The reporter’s fingers stopped on the keyboard.

“What qualifier?”

Kim Jae-wook leaned forward:

“Past Nicki Minaj. Head-to-head with Taylor Swift. Charging the non-viral, twenty-four-hour, world pop record.”

“……”

He typed it out, then stared at the headline field in silence.

“Chief, isn’t this a little too unhinged?”

“Unhinged?”

Kim Jae-wook looked up at him.

“This country loves exactly one kind of story — the kind where we march out to war on the nation’s behalf and beat the Western stars senseless.”

“Ship it.”

The reporter was persuaded.

Or, to be precise, the play count persuaded him.

His fingers came back down on the keyboard and clattered.

“Which picture do we run?”

Kim Jae-wook glanced at the asset library and pointed at the screen.

“That one.”

The reporter looked at the frame — the two of them, mid-kiss, a still from the music video — and hesitated.

“Won’t this read like a dating story?”

Kim Jae-wook’s face was stone.

“You think people are clicking to inspect a play-count table?”

The reporter understood at once.

“Shipping it now.”

7:46 a.m.

The first push went live.

The headline was long.

Longer than a phone’s notification bar — which did nothing to stop it from climbing to number one on the entertainment board within five minutes.

Naver · real-time search · entertainment · 7:52 a.m.

1Baek Si-on Love Yourself

2MV world record

3IU acting

Entered the board at 7:47. Took the top spot before the subway reached the next station.

A little after eight.

Morning rush — the hour when Korea is at its most defenseless.

On the subways.

On the buses.

At the office elevator banks.

In front of convenience-store registers.

Phones lit up, one after another after another.

The pushes went out like dispatches from a front.

IU’s MV acting wins broad praise — the “Nation’s Little Sister” transition signal now explicit

The list went on.

Inside the convenience store, the little TV behind the checkout was running KBS’s morning news.

KBS morning news · the checkout TV · live

The anchor smiled the way morning anchors smile — calibrated to wake absolutely no one.

“Since its YouTube release, the music video for Baek Si-on’s new song ‘Love Yourself’ has continued to climb. As of eight this morning, it has passed eighteen million views…”

The uncle at the register, there for a triangle gimbap, glanced up at the TV.

“Him again?”

The cashier smiled.

“Seems like it’s been him for a while now.”

The uncle shook his head, picked up his gimbap, and left.

At the door, he turned around and asked one more thing.

“Is the video any good?”

“It’s pretty good.”

“Any fight scenes?”

“…No.”

“Then never mind.”

The uncle left.

The cashier looked down and went back to secretly refreshing the play count on her phone.

❧ ❧ ❧

Lee Ji-eun was woken by a serial phone barrage from Jeong Han-teuk.

The phone on the nightstand rang the first time.

She didn’t pick up.

The second time.

She pulled the blanket over her head.

The third time.

She reached out, groped around the nightstand for a long moment, found the phone, and answered with her eyes shut.

“Yah.”

The voice was low.

The fire was not.

On the surface: wake-up grumpiness.

In fact: the resentment of a beautiful dream being strangled in its crib.

As for what the dream was.

Ahem.

There had been a guitar in it.

There had been a skyline through a window.

And there had been a person who did not talk much.

The rest would not be elaborated.

Elaborating was not appropriate in a call report to one’s manager.

Jeong Han-teuk, on the other end, had long since gone immune to this state of hers.

“Open Instagram. Right now.”

“Won’t.”

“There’s a surprise!”

“Baek Si-on’s girlfriend-fans are yelling at me?”

“No — it’s foreigners. The whole world’s foreigners!”

“What foreigners?”

“Look for yourself.”

Bleary, Lee Ji-eun took the phone away from her ear, tapped on speaker, and opened Instagram.

Before her home feed even finished loading, the notification bar got there first.

Instagram · the notification bar · 8:11 a.m.

Instagram

Unread messages — 99+

New followers — 99+

New comments — 99+

every counter red · the morning’s arithmetic still running

She opened her profile, checked the follower count, and found it more than five hundred thousand higher.

Lee Ji-eun rubbed her eyes.

Confirmed she was not still dreaming.

She opened the comments.

The comments used to be, overwhelmingly, Korean.

the comments · as she remembered them

@uaena_01

Aigoo, so cute.

@our_jieun

Our Ji-eun worked hard today too.

@honey_voice

Your voice is too sweet.

Now it had all changed.

the comments · this morning

@la_midnight

THE MV BROKE ME. The kiss. THE KISS.

@madrid_nights

Spanish, all of it — the only word she reliably caught was “hermosa.”

@desert_rose

Arabic — a long line that ran right to left and ended, unmistakably, in hearts.

@paris.soyez

French — she caught “magnifique” and decided to be pleased.

@unidentified.script

A whole ribbon of some alphabet she could not identify at all.

Before Lee Ji-eun had finished digesting any of this, Jeong Han-teuk’s voice came through again:

“Seen it? We’re meeting someone this afternoon.”

Lee Ji-eun was still staring at the comments, brain not fully engaged, and asked on pure reflex:

“Isn’t Baek Si-on in America?”

“Hm?”

“I mean — who are we meeting?”

Jeong Han-teuk went selectively deaf and did not pursue the earlier question.

“Gucci Korea’s PR director.”

“Gucci?”

“Right. The creative team at the Milan headquarters saw the MV.”

Jeong Han-teuk went on.

“Their new creative director is very taken with that neurotic, vintage feel of yours in it.”

“Neurotic?”

“The point is the vintage feel.”

“What I heard was ‘neurotic.’”

“It’s an artistic assessment.”

“……”

Great.

She had evolved from “the Nation’s Little Sister” into “the neurotic vintage woman.”

It did not sound like evolution.

It sounded like the review you receive after an art-film director tricks you into standing in the rain for three days.

But she had to admit it.

She sort of liked it.

Jeong Han-teuk continued:

“So they want to fly you to Milan in February for the fall-winter fashion week.”

Lee Ji-eun sat straight up in bed.

“Milan?”

“Mm.”

“Fashion week?”

“Yes.”

“To watch the shows?”

“Not to sweep floors.”

“……”

Fashion week. Milan. Gucci.

Taken separately, Lee Ji-eun recognized each of the three words.

Combined — and with “an invitation for Lee Ji-eun” attached as the subject — the package stopped feeling real.

Her private wardrobe style was, to put it kindly, “relaxed.” To put it honestly: a plaid-flannel girl.

Her closet held six of the same shirt, the only difference the color of the plaid.

What’s more, in 2014 the Korean fashion scene was not, internationally, a market anyone took seriously.

When European luxury looked at Asia, it looked at Japan and the mainland.

Japan had mature buyer-shop infrastructure and decades of accumulated fashion-consumption culture.

The mainland had a market of 1.4 billion people.

Korea?

Korea, in their eyes, was a place where actresses still wore black-and-white gowns to award ceremonies.

Luxury brands opened stores in Seoul mostly to catch East Asian tourist spending — not because they believed a Korean star could tell their story.

And now.

Gucci’s Milan headquarters had sent the invitation itself.

To someone whose fashion resources were as thin as the plastic film on a convenience-store triangle gimbap, this was not an ordinary opportunity.

Which was exactly why she kept confirming it.

“Also — they’ve already spoken to ELLE Korea. The February cover is yours.”

“The shoot theme will be tied to the fashion-week invitation. It doubles as the warm-up.”

Lee Ji-eun looked at herself in the mirror, hair a total disaster.

In the MV she had played a woman nobody could decide whether to like.

And that woman had now shoved her, out of the box marked “Korea’s sweet girl,” into the sightlines of an international fashion house.

Jeong Han-teuk asked:

“Are you listening?”

“I’m here.”

“I’ll pick you up in half an hour.”

“Okay. Oh — and.”

“What?”

“Tell Baek Si-on I said thank you.”

Jeong Han-teuk paused.

“You won’t tell him yourself?”

Lee Ji-eun thought about it.

“Never mind. I’ll say it myself.”

She hung up.

Sat on the bed, looking out at Seoul’s morning.

Sunlight squeezed in through the gap in the curtains and landed on the blanket.

Half a million new followers.

A Gucci invitation.

An ELLE cover.

Milan fashion week.

Added together, enough to make any Korean actress’s team pop champagne on the spot.

But the thing Lee Ji-eun wanted to do right now was not celebrate.

It was to send one message to a man currently running radio stations in Los Angeles.

She picked up her phone.

Opened the chat window.

Thought for a long time.

In the end, all she typed was three words.

KakaoTalk · to Baek Si-on · composed at length, dispatched in one tap

Lee Ji-eun

Thank you, really.

sent · read receipt pending — on the far side of the ocean, the recipient is dead to the world

After sending it, she decided it was too short.

Not enough to carry what she actually meant.

But she had no idea how to pack all of that into one message.

Whatever.

He would understand it.

She pressed send.

Eight in the morning, Los Angeles time.

The Los Angeles sun was as generous as ever, entirely unaware that Seoul had been frozen into swear-at-the-weather territory by December.

The black Escalade moved along the highway.

Scooter Braun sat in the front passenger seat, tablet in hand, shaking his head and clucking at it.

“Seriously — have your Korean compatriots evolved past the biological function of sleep?”

“Thirty hours since the MV went up. Thirty million plays.”

“My God.”

“The more I think about it, the more I admire your foresight, putting on that concert when you did.”

Baek Si-on was in the back seat, working through a hamburger, and looked up at that.

“Which is why I hired you.”

Scooter froze for a beat and turned around.

“You know that sentence should be recorded and played for the Universal people?”

“Don’t.”

“Why not?”

“They’ll assume you paid me to say it.”

“……”

Just then, Baek Si-on’s phone rang.

Caller ID: Kobe.

He picked up.

Baek Si-on ← Kobe · the 405, Los Angeles · incoming

Baek. You’ve been back in Los Angeles and you don’t call me?

Working.

Not a reason. Lunch, my house, noon.

The tone did not sound like an invitation.

It sounded like a notice.

Baek Si-on covered the mic and looked over at Scooter. “Kobe wants me at his place for lunch.”

Scooter was already digging through the schedule.

Noon had been booked for Pepsi North America’s marketing lead — the details visit for the endorsement conversation.

Not a small thing.

Pepsi’s binding to North American music and sports ran deep — the Super Bowl, tour sponsorships, TV spots, campus channels, every strand of it wired directly into Baek Si-on’s American plan.

Moving it was possible. But the Pepsi man had flown in from New York specifically.

And Kobe was, after all, one of Baek Si-on’s benefactors in North America.

‘Legend’ becoming the NBA season anthem had Kobe’s help behind it.

Some lunches are not lunches.

They are maintenance.

Scooter ran the math in seconds.

“You can. But you get forty minutes.”

Baek Si-on put the mic back to his ear.

Baek Si-on ← Kobe · the same call, continued

I have forty minutes of private time. That’s the whole budget.

A laugh came down the line.

Forty minutes? You come to my house for lunch and I get a time limit?

The schedule is a monster.

Fine. I’ll tell Vanessa to put the steaks on early.

A beat — then, as if remembering something:

And don’t bring three hamburgers into my house.

Baek Si-on looked down at the hamburger in his own hand.

Your intel is fast.

Los Angeles has no secrets.

“……”
