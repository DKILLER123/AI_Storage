Chapter Four

The Half-Basement Girl and the Bully

The following day Anyang · a half-basement flat The last collection

The next day, as expected, Choi’s desk was empty too.

The boss chewed a toothpick and tossed a file folder in front of Si-on.

“Si-on, you’ve tagged along with three old hands and learned plenty. This account is a dead loss — go handle it yourself. Collect it, and your commission is settled on the spot.”

Si-on opened the folder and read through it. The debtor’s surname was Kim, and he had borrowed fifty million won and vanished off the face of the earth. But in the “emergency contact” box on the loan contract, two names were filled in: a former wife, and a daughter.

A former wife, divorced for years.

Si-on slid the paperwork back in the folder and stood up, taking it with him. This was his last day on the job.

That afternoon Half-basement, Anyang Summer · the hottest hour

The address was in Anyang. An old estate, a half-basement flat. When Si-on traced the address there, it was the most suffocating hour of the afternoon. A few moss-caked steps led down to a rusted iron door; the exhaust fan above the frame turned listlessly, pushing out the smell of years of damp mixed with old half-eaten meals.

He knocked.

“Who is it?”

The woman who answered was middle-aged, in an apron, a half-shelled bean in her hand. Her face was tired but she was tidy. Si-on spread out the photocopied contract and explained his business.

The moment she saw the name on the paper, her face went white.

Tears brimmed in her eyes, but she bit her lip hard and made no sound.

Si-on watched her for two seconds.

Then he squeezed past her into the cramped living room and dropped onto the old sofa, the springs gone soft under him, and swung his long legs up onto the coffee table.

“Sister-in-law, don’t blame me. Kim’s run off and the money has to be collected. From today, I live here. Money arrives, I leave.”

The woman stood in the doorway, and two beans slipped from her hand and rolled near her feet. She didn’t bend to pick them up.

❧ ❧ ❧
Evening · dusk The half-basement flat A girl comes home

That night, just as the sky dimmed, Kim Se-jeong pushed open the door, sweating, her t-shirt soaked through the back. Four hours of waiting tables at a barbecue joint, and her legs were nearly gone.

Kim Se-jeong

The debtor's daughter, dreaming of the stage

She works the grill · idolises IU

She counts the hours and hides a letter to her favourite singer under her mattress. The worst thing in the world and the best thing in the world can be the same person.

“Mother—”

The word died in her throat. There was someone on the sofa. A floral shirt, a crew cut, two long legs propped on the table, laughing without a care at Running Man on the television.

Se-jeong froze in the doorway. Then the door to the inner room opened, and her mother hurried out and yanked her into the bedroom, slamming the door shut.

“Who is that?” Se-jeong kept her voice down.

“A debt collector.”

“What?”

“The money your father borrowed. He ran off, and they’ve come to us.”

Se-jeong’s head spun.

“Why should we— he’s a debt collector, why is he staying in our house? Where’s the—”

“Keep your voice down!”

Her mother pressed a hand over her mouth.

“Don’t cross him, do you hear me? We can’t afford to.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Five minutes passed. The bedroom door reopened.

Kim Se-jeong came out with a face like stone. She treated Si-on as if he were air, walking without a glance to the rattling old fridge and pulling open the door to find a red-bean popsicle.

It was the one comfort she had for a whole day of work.

“Hey.”

A lazy voice from behind her.

Se-jeong’s hand on the popsicle paused. She ignored him, kept tearing at the wrapper.

“I’m talking to you, little thing.”

Si-on straightened, swung his legs off the table, and let his gaze travel past her shoulder to land on the half-open bedroom door. On the wall in there hung a poster — the cover art from Lee Ji-eun’s Good Day album.

“You a fan?”

Se-jeong whipped around, eyes blazing.

“So what? Is that a crime?”

“No.”

Si-on shrugged.

“Just find it a little funny.”

“You—”

“What use is chasing a star you can’t even touch? Or do you think that Lee Ji-eun’s going to step off that poster and settle your bill?”

“Mind your own business!”

Se-jeong sank her teeth into the hard popsicle, biting as if she could chew up the jerk in front of her, turned, and slammed the bedroom door shut.

❧ ❧ ❧

The moment the door closed, Se-jeong collapsed onto her bed. Through the thin door she could still hear that jerk laughing.

She rolled over, frustrated, and dug out her earphones, stuffing them in, jabbing at her phone. She scrolled through the playlist for a long time, and finally picked out something obscure.

A song produced by Park Jin-young, sung by Lee Ji-eun — the insert song from Dream High: “Someday.”

It had once been caught in a plagiarism dispute, but that had nothing to do with her. What she knew was that right now, in this moment, every line was landing dead in the centre of her chest.

Someday · Lee Ji-eun (IU) · Dream High OST

“Someday, I hope these tears finally stop…”

“Someday the darkness will lift…”

“And the warm sunlight will dry them away.”

“It’ll be alright.” The line that had never felt more like a lie, or more like a prayer.

Through the earphones, that clean and clear voice cut through the lightless half-basement, tapping gently against the nerve she had stretched to its limit. The tears that had been circling for so long finally broke loose, falling in a stream onto her pillow.

She cried for a few minutes. Then she wiped her face roughly and sat up, pulling a sheet of paper from her satchel.

She laid it on her knee and began to write.

The Letter · unaddressed, unsealed

To unnie Ji-eun. I am so tired.

A debt collector has come to live in our house. He wears a floral shirt and he will not leave. My mother cries every day.

I want to sing too. I want to stand on a stage. But…

Maybe I cannot hold on much longer.

If it were you, unnie, how could you keep going in a half-basement where even the sunlight does not reach?

— a girl who wants to sing

By the last line, the tears had smeared the ink into a blur. She folded the paper, tucked it carefully into an envelope, and wrote the address of Loen Entertainment on it.

She knew the odds were it would be treated as just another letter between fan mail and thrown into a corner. But in this suffocating place, it was the only thing she had left to hold on to.

❧ ❧ ❧
The next afternoon Outside the half-basement A figure in the alley

Si-on went out the next afternoon. Three meals on the trot of kimchi with rice and he couldn’t take it. He’d died in a rented room in his last life; he wasn’t about to die on kimchi in this one. He went to the market and bought two pounds of pork belly and a few leaves of lettuce.

Carrying the plastic bags, he turned the corner into the mossy alleyway and slowed.

Someone was crouching outside the half-basement window. A man, black baseball cap, black mask, all of it hiding him. He was leaning in, peering through the glass, one hand gripping the sill and the other holding a phone, taking a photograph of the door number.

Si-on watched from the mouth of the alley for three seconds.

He set the pork and the lettuce on the ground.

“Hey.”

The man jolted and spun around. Si-on was already right in front of him — crew cut, floral shirt, over six feet, and filling up the narrow alley. The man took a step back and his spine hit the window bars.

“W-who are you?”

Si-on didn’t answer. He closed in in one move, hooked an arm around the man’s neck and hauled him along toward the stairs.

“Brother! Brother, let’s talk—”

“Less talk. Get inside.”

The iron door flew open. Se-jeong’s mother was rinsing vegetables in the kitchen and looked up at the noise, and her grip on the knife near went loose when she saw Si-on dragging a stranger in.

Si-on flung the man onto the sofa and stood over him, arms crossed.

“Name.”

“J… Jeong Han-teuk.”

“What do you do?”

“I’m from — I’m from LOEN Entertainment…”

“LOEN?”

Si-on’s eyebrow twitched. Jeong Han-teuk gasped for breath, his mask knocked askew, half his face a mask of fear.

“I came to verify something! Our … our IU received a fan letter. It mentioned a debt collector harassing people at this address… so I was sent to check whether it was true…”

The words tumbled out faster and faster, like he thought a slower answer would get him hit.

“I’m not a bad person! Really! I’m just an assistant!”

Si-on looked at him. About five seconds.

“You got a badge?”

Jeong Han-teuk hurriedly dug a laminated work card out of his jacket and held it up with both hands. Si-on took it and turned it over.

LOEN Entertainment. Jeong Han-teuk. Artist Management. The photo matched the man now close to tears in front of him.

Jeong Han-teuk

Artist Management · LOEN Entertainment

Agency assistant · the one who came to verify

The man dispatched to confirm a fan letter's plea, and the first of LOEN's people to meet the debt collector face to face. Mild-mannered, easily rattled, and terrified of being folded into a chokehold twice in one day.

Si-on tossed the card back.

“Alright.”

He walked back to the mouth of the alley and picked up the pork and the lettuce he’d set down, then returned.

“Stay put. Now, from the beginning — tell me the whole thing.”
