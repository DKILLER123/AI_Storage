Chapter Thirty-Eight

A Strange Kind of Possession, and the Longest Quiet

July Night Hapjeong-dong, the laptop A song a stranger wanted to keep for himself

The first real thing that happened on SoundCloud was, in its own small way, the most human accident of the summer.

Si-on posted the track. Private, at first — he wanted to see it hold together before he let it out into a room full of strangers he could not see. He went to sleep. He woke up to a comment.

miklo·berlin

This needs to be heard everywhere. The way the beat slows just before the voice comes in — I've listened to this four times, I'm not moving until it's on a platform I can actually own.

baek_sion_offline

It is on a platform. That's where you're reading this. — Baek

miklo·berlin

No, I mean a real release. Streaming, with an album page, with cover art, so I can put a name on it and find it again. Right now it's a private drop in the middle of nowhere. Keep it, covet it, don't give it to a law firm to bury. Put it out.

anon_loudspeaker

I found it through the sheep channel. Came here to leave a heart and a half. Whoever you are, stop holding this one hostage.

The comment pinned itself to the back of Si-on's mind. Not because it was right, and not because it was wrong — but because of the word the stranger kept circling: private. He'd made the track private for safety. The stranger had read it as something else entirely.

Jae-joon, when Si-on showed him the thread, laughed short and flat.

“You've got a German telling you to give up your thing because he wants to be able to find it again. That's the whole industry in one comment. He doesn't want to share it — he wants to own it enough to know where it lives.”

“He called it hostage.”

“Every listener feels that way about the one song they can't put down. It's not a bad sign. It's the only real sign there is.” Jae-joon turned back to his console. “Put it out. Wide. The whole thing. Let him find it.”

✿ ✿ ✿

July Morning Across the city — SM Entertainment The announcement that ended a season

The announcement came on a Friday, mid-morning, in the flat professional language these things always arrive in. It wasn't a scandal, wasn't an accusation, wasn't any of the things the comments had been practising for. It was the dullest and heaviest sentence in the business.

SM Entertainment · official

Regarding f(x) member Sulli's activities

We wish to inform you that, on the advice of her medical team and after discussion with the company, member Sulli (Choi Jin-ri) will suspend all scheduled activities, effective immediately, in order to concentrate fully on recovery from the fatigue she has accumulated over several years.

We apologise to the fans who have waited, and we ask for your warm and understanding support so that Choi Jin-ri can focus on her treatment and rest.

— SM Entertainment

Si-on read the statement twice, standing at the kitchen counter with the morning sound of Eun-ah's kettle behind him. The words were careful, professional, each one polite the way a door is polite right before it closes.

He knew what the comments would do with it before he saw them. He opened the phone anyway.

Online · comment wall · 24 hours after the statement

“Fatigue. Right. She just doesn't want to work anymore.”

“Three years of riding the group's name and she can't even stand on a stage. Embarrassing.”

“They should save the spot for someone who actually shows up.”

“As if she was ever going to debut solo. This is the exit.”

A request from the company, a statement from the doctors, a handshake from a whole fanbase — none of it quieted a single syllable. Si-on read the replies to the top comment for exactly forty seconds, then set the phone down.

✿ ✿ ✿

July Midday Yeonnam-dong Eun-ah, working herself up to a bad idea

Eun-ah came through the door the way she did everything, half a sentence ahead of herself.

“Oppa. We should go see her.”

Si-on looked up from where the laptop sat on the sofa arm.

“Her name's Jin-ri.”

“Jin-ri, then. We should go see Jin-ri. Today. She just — every comment — oppa, she's been in that studio with us. She was in the film with us.”

Eun-ah's hands were up, one palm flat against the air like she was already explaining herself to a guard she hadn't met yet.

“I know she's not allowed to have visitors. I know. But she can't be reading those things alone.”

“She's not reading them alone. She's resting. That's the whole point of the statement.”

“Oppa.”

Si-on set the laptop down. He got up, walked to the window, and stood there with his hand on the sill for a moment, the way he did when he was deciding whether to say the honest thing.

“Tomorrow it'll be celebrity news. The day after, a photographer will figure out which building her mother goes into. You walk up to her door today, you'll be the story, and then the story will be why is the strange man visiting the resting idol — and she'll spend the next month reading about herself being visited.” He turned. “If you want her to get better, you stay away until she's asked for you.”

“Then when?”

“When she wants someone who isn't reading the comments.”

Eun-ah stood there, arms crossed, jaw set, the way she did when she was losing to a point she knew she had already conceded. She hated it. He hated it too. But neither of them said the obvious thing, which was that they were both angry at the sentence they were confirming as reasonable.

She sat down heavily on the sofa.

“Fine. Then we do what you're probably already doing.”

“Which is?”

“Waiting for the song to do something, so that when she does ask for someone, there's a reason for you to be the one who goes.”

Si-on didn't answer her. He stood at the window and looked out at the street, where the afternoon had gone white and still, the kind of afternoon that does nothing so the day can stay out of your way.

He'd put the song out. The wind still hadn't come.
