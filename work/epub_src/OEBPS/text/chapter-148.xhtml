“Baek!”

Both siblings looked at him. By everything the two of knew about this man, even if he wasn’t planning a victory party, he should have been scheming to keep Baek Si-on in America a few extra days. The phrasing was criminally compressed. And yet, horribly accurate. Sado did have its heaviest scenes stacked at the end — he’d already pushed the palace-break-in and the rice chest back a week to make this LA trip. The sooner home, the better. The only problem was that none of it sounded like Scooter.

Baek Si-on let it go.

“All right.”

“Good. Let’s move.”

People came up the whole walk through the corridor — congratulations, great stage, let’s work sometime — and Scooter ran interference in front of him, expertly and hard. The man who normally worked a room like a harvest — one more sentence out of every greeting, one more number exchanged per wave — today acted as if Baek Si-on might expire if he stopped moving for half a minute.

The black Equus convoy was already waiting at the exit. Before getting in, Baek Si-on looked back once at the blazing theater. Too much had happened in one night: two trophies, a throne, Taylor, apples, and one photo of a wipe pressed to a cheek. Piled into the same evening, it made the American entertainment industry look better at screenwriting melodrama than any Korean film set.

At LAX, the luggage had already been delivered to the VIP channel; staff handed over passports and boarding documents. Everything moved like it had been arranged in advance. Taking the papers, Baek Eun-ah stole one more look at Scooter. This was not seeing someone off. This was pre-packed shipping, expedited.

Baek Si-on had noticed too. He didn’t ask. A man like Scooter, if he wanted to say something, would have said it in the car. If he didn’t want to, asking would only get you another bite of apple.

At the gate, Scooter came up one last time with open arms. Again, Baek Si-on met it. A back-thump.

“America’s mine. Shoot your movie in peace.”

“Mm. Thanks for everything.”

“Spare me the thanks.”

Boarding was called. Baek Si-on turned into the jet bridge, Baek Eun-ah at his side. Ten-odd steps in, she couldn’t hold it.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“Did he seem… weird to you?”

He didn’t turn around.

“Yes.”

“Why is he suddenly so desperate to ship you home?”

His steps didn’t slow.

“Probably worried something would happen if I stayed in America.”

“Like what?”

He thought about it.

“The apple situation.”

“……”

Behind them, at the mouth of the VIP channel, Scooter watched the two of them vanish around the corner. The smile came off his face slowly, like a light on a dimmer.

He hadn’t packed Baek Si-on home because of any rice chest. The rice chest was the excuse. Taylor was the problem.

Years ago, his relations with Taylor’s camp could charitably be called workable — nothing that made either side wince at the sight of the other’s name. But ever since Justin and Selena had broken up and reunited in full public view, the whole thing had curdled. Taylor was Selena’s best friend. He was Justin’s manager. Sides aren’t always chosen; sometimes they’re walked to your feet by the people near you, the things you’ve done, the interests you’ve had to protect.

Worse: Taylor was an extremely good talker. If she and Baek Si-on actually became friends, she would absolutely bend his ear in private. Make him wary of his own management. Tell him not to trust the American machinery too far. Suggest — sweetly — that maybe he should start clawing contract leverage back into his own hands.

Could that be allowed? He had planted this tree himself — watered it, fed it, pruned it, chased off the birds — and just as it finally flowered and fruited, a hand comes over the fence, smiling, saying what a lovely tree, let me help you pick.

Scooter could stand that? If he could stand that, he wouldn’t be Scooter.

But he couldn’t tell Baek Si-on not to play with Taylor, either. That sentence was too stupid. Taylor had done nothing wrong tonight — accepted a pocket square, given a cheek kiss, extended a party invite, offered a phone number; every move stood up straight from etiquette to social grace. Bad-mouthing her now would only look like guilt.

So Scooter did it another way. Ship the man home first. Put him back in the rice chest, where things were safe.

It could only hold for so long. Tonight’s Taylor-and-Baek footage had been spoon-fed to the entire planet by the director. If nobody intervened, the moment Human went live, the Western platforms would hard-cut the two of them into a love story. And Scooter knew exactly how dangerous those edits were.

What he actually needed was to move now — while the Taylor material was still ambiguous, not yet cemented as destiny — and bury it under footage of a different woman.

Who? Faces flipped past behind his eyes and stopped on one. The Love Yourself video. Baek Si-on kissing a girl in it. The girl’s name took him ten seconds to dig up — IU. Fresh-faced. Asian. The video happened to contain one publicly shot kiss. And that frame happened to form a perfect visual counterweight to tonight’s publicly shot cheek kiss.

He dialled the team lead — the one running Baek Si-on’s account, working late — without further ceremony.

Love Yourself was her video — she’d shot it — but inside the album’s concept it was a mid-record emotional thread, and it had no business being shoved to the front right after Baek Si-on came home from a double AMA and a throne. Jung Han-teul watched her switch, in one second, from glacial top idol to bewildered twenty-one-year-old, offered no commentary, and handed over his phone.

An apartment. A sofa. A kitchen. A carpet. Soft light — none of the usual idol-drama filter; more like a photographer who had wandered by accident into an ordinary couple’s weekend. Her curled on the sofa with her legs dropped across his knee. Him reading, head down. Her toes used as artillery. His eyes coming up. Her laughing.

It had all been directed, staged, per the shot list — and yet cut together like this, even she thought it looked too true. Especially the Polaroid: him standing in front, her face peeking over his shoulder, eyes curved, arm straining to hold the camera high. An imperfect photo. The kind a real couple actually sticks to a refrigerator.

The video kept going. The kitchen: she tasted soup at the counter, called him back over, raised the spoon; he bent and took one mouthful; she watched his face; he nodded; she turned back, satisfied.
