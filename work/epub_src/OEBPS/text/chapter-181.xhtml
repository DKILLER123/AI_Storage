Chapter One Hundred Eighty One

Give the Ankle a Vacation

January 1, past 9 p.m. → January 2, past 9 a.m. One knock that asks nothing and means everything · one headline that arrives before breakfast · an outline cut from thirty to ten · one parcel the address book never predicted The Four Seasons, Beverly Hills → the top floor, two doors apart → Seoul, the Red Velvet dorm A man checks on a day he did not see; a manager robs the Federal Reserve with a burlap sack; a veteran journalist meets the calmest double-crown king alive; and in a dormitory in Seoul, a girl who never orders anything online receives the one box with her name on it.

Past nine at night, Baek Si-on finished the day’s schedule and got back to the Four Seasons in Beverly Hills.

The front desk recognized him and smiled.

“Good evening, Mr. Baek.”

Baek Si-on nodded. “Evening.”

As the clerk handed over the room card, something seemed to occur to him, and he added with a smile:

“Oh — pardon me for mentioning it, but the young lady traveling with you seems to have spent rather a long time in the back garden today.”

Baek Si-on stopped.

“Doing what?”

The clerk’s expression turned delicate.

“According to the gardener — looking for four-leaf clovers, apparently.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

He thanked the man and took the elevator up.

Rising, his face floated in the metal of the doors — an utterly unremarkable face. Behind it, though, the image would not leave: Choi Jin-ri crouched in a lawn, flipping blades of grass one at a time. She really was a natural at giving Los Angeles gardeners professional confusion.

The doors opened. The top-floor corridor was deep-carpeted and soundless.

At his own door, he pressed the card to the sensor. It beeped.

He didn’t push the door open. He turned his head to the door diagonally across the hall.

A few seconds passed.

Baek Si-on slid the card back into his pocket, walked to Choi Jin-ri’s door, and pressed the bell.

Ding-dong.

A wait. Then the faint sound of slippers on carpet. A soft click, and the door opened a crack. Her head poked out — hair fluffy, hotel white bathrobe, freshly showered written all over her. The instant she saw who it was, her eyes lit up. Then she remembered something, and hurriedly pulled the door wider.

“You’re back?”

“Mm.”

He glanced at her.

“How was your day?”

She blinked. Of all things he might knock for near midnight, a rote question had clearly not been on her list. Then the lawn trophy crossed her mind, and the corners of her mouth climbed on their own.

“Very good.”

She turned it back:

“Yours?”

“Also very good.”

And there they went quiet — a silence neither of them was a stranger to.

Her fingers found the edge of the doorframe and picked at it. Her heartbeat began, gradually, to pick up speed. She looked at him. She looked at the empty corridor behind him. Her head went hot, and out it came:

“Do you want to come in?”

The words were barely out before she froze first.

Late night. A man, a woman, a hotel room. As invitations went, it sounded exactly like the high-risk line from a third-rate romance movie.

Baek Si-on took in the expression of obvious, immediate regret, and shook his head.

“No.”

Just as she was hunting for a crack in the floor to crawl into, he added:

“Knowing your day went all right — that’s enough.”

Perfectly ordinary words. But landing on her ears, they made the tip of her heart tremble in a way she could not govern.

“Oh…”

A dry little sound. Her fingers pressed harder into the doorframe.

The air went quiet again. She judged that another minute face-to-face and her face might actually catch fire, so she scrambled for a new topic.

“What time do we leave tomorrow?”

“Afternoon. When the interview’s done, we go.”

“Then…”

She hesitated — and in the end did not ask whether the aurora would actually show. She was afraid of asking and receiving another probability lecture at Professor Yoon level.

Romance, occasionally, needs to decline higher education.

“Good night.”

“Good night.”

Then, as if remembering:

“Dress warm tomorrow. And don’t fight me for clothes again the second we get there.”

Her brain instantly flashed the two down jackets she had forcibly “requisitioned” from this man — both times, with impeccable self-righteousness. Called out on it in person, her half-cooled cheeks reignited on the spot.

“Th-that’s because you can’t wear that many layers…”

The voice got smaller as it went.

Baek Si-on looked at her and said nothing — but the corner of his mouth appeared to move.

She dared not hold the eye contact. To keep this man from unearthing any further unbearable old accounts, she dropped a second “good night” at double speed, retreated, and pulled the door to a crack. One last look at him through the gap.

Then the door shut all the way.

❧ ❧ ❧

January 2. Past nine in the morning.

When Scooter pounded on the door, Baek Si-on was holding a coffee cup, gazing blankly at the palm trees outside the window.

“Baek!”

The voice got in before the man did. A top manager who had seen every kind of weather was currently radiating the exact energy of someone who had just emptied the Federal Reserve’s vault into a burlap sack.

“Did you hear? Bruno Mars just got knocked off his throne — by you!”

“‘Love Yourself’ is number one!”

Scooter pointed both hands at the ceiling.

“God above! You did it! One album, two crowns! First in Asian history!!”

Billboard Hot 100 · the chart update, January 2

Love Yourself

Baek Si-on

No. 1

‘Uptown Funk’ — the monster that was supposed to be unwritable out of history’s way — displaced. Second chart-topper from the same album after ‘Legend’. One album, two crowns: no Asian act had ever held it.

In behind Scooter came Richard, senior writer for TIME, and a cameraman already hauling his rig. The camera had started recording before the door was even open.

Richard was a reporter with years on him. He had interviewed presidents, Nobel laureates, men who pulled off the enormous at history’s junctures. Never once had he watched a person who had just set the highest record in half a century of Asian music history respond to the news by taking a long, unhurried sip of coffee.

Richard mentally rewrote his opening paragraph. Then he traded a brief glance with the equally choked Scooter. Between the two of them passed a single silent message: is something wrong with this person?

Richard spoke first.

“Baek…”

He worked to keep his tone professional rather than baffled.

“You just set the highest record any Asian artist has ever set in American pop history. One album, two number-one singles.”

“Isn’t your reaction… just a little too calm?”

Baek Si-on set the cup down and gave him a look.

“You’d prefer I jump up on the table and scream?”

Richard was an honest man.

“You could, honestly. Our readership rather enjoys that kind of unguarded emotional tension.”

Scooter, from the side:

“I’d also enjoy that.”

Baek Si-on let that pass unanswered.

“How many questions did you prepare?”

Richard glanced down at his outline.

“Thirty-odd.”

“Cut twenty first.”

Richard: “……”

He suspected he had misheard.

“Why?”

“Because you won’t get through them. You have all the time in the world today.”

Baek Si-on looked at his watch.

“I don’t.”

Scooter coughed. It was, admittedly, true. Baek Si-on had a plane to Canada to catch — and every time he thought about that, something sat slightly stuck in his chest.

Richard studied the freshly minted double-crown king of the Billboard Hot 100. A moment of silence. Then he looked back down at the outline he had stayed up all night writing, and crossed out the life-trivia questions and the pre-set sentimentality ones. A man who had dragged Bruno Mars off the top of this chart was not, plainly, someone you reached with tears.

TIME · the interview outline · as amended on the spot

“Where do you buy your sneakers” — struck.

“What do you miss most about home” — struck.

“How did it feel, the moment you heard” — struck, see subject, currently drinking coffee.

Surviving questions: the chart, the music, the next move. Tears were never going to be the angle. The man dethroned ‘Uptown Funk’; he does not cry on cue.

Richard finished cutting, raised his head.

“All right. Let’s begin.”

“Mm.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Seoul. The Red Velvet dorm.

The rare New Year lull had finally given the four girls license to lie around the living room in total collapse, limbs everywhere, dignity nowhere. Snack packets lay strewn across the coffee table; the TV murmured some entertainment news.

The picture flashed, and cut to a special report. The anchor’s voice, shrill on pure excitement, ran straight over the crunch of potato chips:

Seoul · entertainment news · special report, interrupting everything

“On today’s updated Billboard Hot 100, our singer Baek Si-on’s ‘Love Yourself’ has officially reached the summit!”

“His second chart-topping single, after ‘Legend’!”

“The highest miracle in the history of Korean popular music refreshes itself once again…”

Four girls stop chewing in the same instant. The chips lose.

Baek Si-on’s Love Yourself. Park Soo-young murmured:

“Isn’t this already Michael Jackson-level mythology?”

Son Seung-wan nodded.

“Should be… and we were guests at his concert just a few days ago.”

Kang Seul-gi stared at the TV, blank.

“So does that mean we… met historical figures early?”

Park Soo-young looked at her.

“Unnie, that phrasing makes us sound like we’re seventy.”

Son Seung-wan corrected her, earnest:

“Not historical figures. People currently making history.”

“That sounds even scarier.”

Joo-hyun alone had not joined the discussion. She simply watched Baek Si-on on the screen, quiet. The broadcast had hung a whole row of enormous words on him — each one so big it sat strangely against the small things in her memory. And yet it was exactly those small things propping up her whole day’s good mood.

Ding-dong. The doorbell.

Park Soo-young shuffled over in her slippers and opened it: their manager, arms full of parcels.

“Packages for all four of you today.”

He dealt them out.

“Seulgi’s… Wendy’s… Soo-young’s…”

He checked the last label.

“Irene-yah — you’ve got one too.”

“Me?”

Joo-hyun had zero memory of ordering anything online. Could it be her mother in Daegu, worried the Seoul winter was getting to her, sending a box of warm clothes?

Bewildered, she took it — and glanced, out of habit, at the sender line on the label.

S.W Studio.

Joo-hyun stood very still.

The other three had been opening their own parcels; a few seconds later, noticing hers had gone motionless, they all looked over.

“Unnie?”

Park Soo-young leaned in first.

“What’s wrong?”

“Nothing.”

Joo-hyun picked up a pair of scissors and opened the tape with care. The box came open.

Inside lay a pair of low-top trainers. A clean gray-white body, one crisp line down the side, and on the tongue, small and understated: the personal collab mark of Baek Si-on.

Pressed at the very bottom of the box was a card. She took it out. One line, nothing else.

Inside the shoebox · the card

Give the ankle a vacation.

Joo-hyun went still all over again. She had not imagined that Baek Si-on sunbaenim remembered her ankle.

Park Soo-young had now noticed Joo-hyun staring blankly into a shoebox, and leaned over, curious, to look—

“AAAAAAAAAH!”

The soprano ambush made Joo-hyun jolt a full centimeter off the sofa.

“Ya!”

“Unnie, do you KNOW what this is?!”

Park Soo-young sailed straight past her captain’s death glare:

“This is Baek Si-on sunbaenim’s global collab limited edition! The Gangnam pop-up sold out in ten minutes yesterday! Resellers are at five times the price on the secondhand sites now — there’s no stock anywhere on the planet!”

Park Soo-young’s attention to hype sneakers was the highest in the dorm. When the job description includes balancing the team’s average height, beautiful flat shoes are a structural necessity.

Kang Seul-gi leaned in too, and read the card in a small voice:

“Give the ankle a vacation.”

She looked up at Joo-hyun.

“Unnie — Baek Si-on sunbaenim remembers your ankle?”

“…Maybe Eun-ah mentioned it to him.”

Park Soo-young was already orbiting the room with excitement.

“Unnie, are you going to wear them?”

Son Seung-wan said at once:

“Wouldn’t wearing them now be a waste? It’s a limited edition.”

Park Soo-young deeply agreed.

“Right! This belongs in a shrine!”

Kang Seul-gi considered the question.

“But aren’t shoes for wearing?”

Joo-hyun looked left. She looked right.

Then she clicked the lid firmly shut, hugged the box to her chest, and walked straight to her bedroom. Park Soo-young called after her:

“Unnie, you’re really going to wear them?!”

The sound of the closing door answered her — crisp as a final verdict that accepts no appeal.

❧ ❧ ❧

The bedroom was quiet.

Joo-hyun sat on the edge of the bed, shoebox on her knees, and opened it again.

The gray-white body lay in the box, calm; the mark on the tongue was small, asking no attention. She took the shoes out, turned them to study the soles, ran a thumb over the weave of the upper.

Then she toed off her house slippers and slid her feet in.

The fit was exact.

She had no idea how he knew her size. Maybe Baek Eun-ah had told him. Maybe it was in the medical team’s records from the day her ankle got hurt at the concert. Maybe he simply knew.

She preferred the last one.

Wearing them, she rose and shuffled, unhurried, to the full-length mirror by the wardrobe. The girl in the glass wore the most ordinary loungewear in the world — and on her feet, a pair of sneakers the resellers had priced into the stratosphere.

She looked at the version of herself whose ankle had been properly taken care of.

And discovered, a little to her surprise, that she wanted — somehow — to stand on her toes more than she used to.

From beyond the door, Park Soo-young’s voice, muffled:

“Unnie, do they fit?”

“Come out and show us!”

“No.”

“Why not?”

Joo-hyun looked once more at the gray-white trainers in the mirror.

❝

Because this pair was never meant for other people’s eyes.

— a dorm bedroom in Seoul, behind a door that accepts no appeal
