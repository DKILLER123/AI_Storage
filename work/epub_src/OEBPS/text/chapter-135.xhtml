Chapter One Hundred Thirty Five

Occasionally, Even Humans Need a Kind Misunderstanding

November 2 Late morning · between takes Jeonju studio lot · the space between two support trucks A word just said twice. Two ways of hearing it. And a Crown Prince about to learn exactly how much light two top female stars give off at the edge of a lens.

The word had been spoken twice, once by each of them, and now it sat inside Choi Jin-ri’s head, where she picked it up, turned it over, and read it three times.

Coincidence.

Two support trucks.

The same morning.

The same film set.

The same man.

If that counted as coincidence, then Seoul Metro Line 2 in the morning rush could reasonably be described as spacious.

She knew, of course, that she should not think this way. Lee Ji-eun was an eonni she was close to. But people became small about certain things. Particularly when the certain thing had Baek Si-on’s face on it.

She had only just begun, in her head, to split “what a coincidence” apart into “what a” and “coincidence” and put each half on trial separately, when Lee Ji-eun stepped closer and, very naturally, took her arm.

“Peach.”

The little theatre inside Choi Jin-ri’s head stalled mid-scene.

“Mm?”

Lee Ji-eun was looking at her, eyes curved into crescents.

“I went to see your movie last night.”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

“The Green Bottle Fly?”

“Mm~”

Still smiling, Lee Ji-eun freed her other hand, reached into the canvas tote hanging off her shoulder, and brought something out.

A stack of paper.

Bound with a rubber band.

She waved it at Choi Jin-ri.

“Look.”

Choi Jin-ri looked properly.

Whoa.

All movie ticket stubs.

Neatly stacked, one on another, a rubber band wound three times around the middle.

She took it, ran a thumb along the edge to gauge the thickness.

At a glance — fifty, sixty?

No.

More.

Seventy, eighty.

That was the scale of a private booking for a small screening hall.

Holding the stack, Choi Jin-ri felt the little store of sourness, of guard, of “why does eonni have to be here too” that she had been carrying — get knocked flat by it. Knocked so flat she could not lift her head for a second.

Lee Ji-eun had gone to see her movie last night.

Not words of support.

Actual tickets. Actual seats. The film watched to the end — and the stubs brought here, into her hand.

And her own first reaction had been hostility.

Wrong.

Very wrong.

They had been close once. Really close. It was Baek Si-on who had made her start minding positions she had never minded before — minding whether a casual “coincidence” carried a second meaning underneath, minding whether Lee Ji-eun also had some thread, some unnameable connection, to him.

She had not used to be like this.

Wait.

Wait.

She had decided, only last night, to come to Jeonju at all — after the video call with Goo Hara ended. Even her own manager had only learned this morning that she was coming to deliver a support truck.

Which meant Lee Ji-eun could not possibly have known she would be here today.

So if eonni had not known she would be coming, why would she “coincidentally” have this whole stack of Green Bottle Fly stubs on her person?

Ticket stubs were not the kind of accessory a person carried around daily.

They were not a charm.

“Ji-eun eonni.”

“Mm?”

“How come you bought so many?”

“Aigoo—”

Lee Ji-eun sighed, in the tone of a person relating something exasperating but unavoidable.

“I owe someone a favour. I was going to treat him to a meal, but he said no need — just go and support the movie. So I went and supported it.”

She pointed at the stack in Choi Jin-ri’s hand.

“Of course, the movie itself — that was watched for you. The stubs are for someone to look at.”

By the end of that speech, Choi Jin-ri’s brain was a server under heavy load, processing no fewer than three strands of information at once.

First.

The acquaintance between Baek Si-on and Lee Ji-eun might not be simple.

Second.

Baek Si-on had turned down a dinner invitation from Lee Ji-eun.

Third — and the strand that tied her brain into a knot.

If their acquaintance was not simple, why would Baek Si-on turn down a meal that could only have brought them closer?

And if it was simple — then what favour was worth Lee Ji-eun personally driving to the Jeonju set, sending a support truck, carrying the stubs, and presenting them for “someone” to see?

So.

Simple, or not simple?

Choi Jin-ri felt her brain run out of headroom.

When she acted, the most complicated emotional arc had always come pre-written, black on white in the script.

But the scene in front of her had no script.

No storyboard.

No line prompts.

And no director calling “Cut.”

And yet everyone in it was acting.

“And you?”

“What?”

“Why did you send a support truck?”

Choi Jin-ri glanced back toward her own white support truck.

Inside the window, Baek Eun-ah was still handing out sandwiches — but the rate at which her eyes flicked over to the two of them was roughly once every three seconds.

Choi Jin-ri looked away, thought about it.

“Someone came back from a business trip to Dubai and brought me souvenirs. I thought I’d return the courtesy.”

Lee Ji-eun’s brow lifted a fraction.

“What souvenirs?”

Choi Jin-ri counted on her fingers.

“Camel-milk chocolate, dates, and saffron.”

Lee Ji-eun blinked.

“What kind of flower is saffron?”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

She could not tell whether Lee Ji-eun genuinely did not know, or was asking on purpose.

But looking at those clear, clean eyes — probably the former.

This eonni was the queen of digital charts on a stage and the nation’s little sister in front of a camera, but in ordinary life her common sense occasionally came up one puzzle piece short. It was not the first time.

“……A very pretty flower.”

The rest — “it’s for cooking and steeping in water,” “it sounds more like a health product,” “Baek Si-on nodded very seriously and said she needed building up” — all of it she pressed down to the bottom of her throat.

Let the romance fly first.

Lee Ji-eun nodded with great gravity.

“Oh——”

She drew the vowel out on purpose.

Then her eyes turned loaded with meaning.

“So he’s pursuing you?”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

The question landed too suddenly.

Her logical chain snapped first.

Then slowly reconnected.

When a man sends a woman flowers, apart from visiting-the-sick, apologising, and pursuing — it is genuinely hard to name a fourth reason the world will understand at once.

Admittedly, saffron and roses were separated by an entire Compendium of Materia Medica.

But the name contained the character for flower.

Rounded up, it was a flower.

And all of a sudden Choi Jin-ri felt that this misunderstanding — absurd as it was — was absurd in a way that worked in her favour.

So she went along with the logic, half yielding, lifting a hand to tuck a strand of hair behind her ear, and lowered her head with a studied coyness:

“I wouldn’t know~”

The voice was soft enough to raise goosebumps on her own arm.

The truth of the situation, of course, was closer to Choi Jin-ri inching, little by little, toward Baek Si-on on her own.

But if Lee Ji-eun chose to misunderstand — let her misunderstand.

Occasionally, even humans need a kind misunderstanding.

Lee Ji-eun watched her.

Was quiet for about two seconds.

Then she copied the gesture exactly — lifted a hand, tucked back her own hair — and produced, in a voice three times sweeter, five times tackier, and eight times more deserving of a slap:

“I——wouldn’t——know——laa~~”

Choi Jin-ri’s face went red on the spot.

Not the red of shyness.

The red of having her own affectation copied and pasted straight back at her — the social-death red of “was that really how I sounded just now?”

Lee Ji-eun did not stop. The psychic assault continued:

“Aigoo, and I also don’t know whether he’s pursuing me~”

Choi Jin-ri, mortified and incensed in equal measure, reached out to cover Lee Ji-eun’s mouth.

Lee Ji-eun dodged, and let out a laugh so hearty it was entirely unbecoming of the nation’s little sister:

“Hahahahaha——”

The film set.

The side hall, interior.

Sado · the side hall · the Crown Prince and his father-in-law · scene thirteen · take one

The seed of the break — the private reckoning, an eavesdropper at the door

Action

The Crown Prince Sado sits at the low writing table, his father-in-law across from him. The crimson gonryongpo is still on him, collar pressed flat and neat — and yet the whole air around him is nothing like the man who stood before the court that morning.

SADO “Father-in-law.”

SADO “What does Father King truly want?”

He sets the brush back on its rest. When he speaks again, the sentences come flat, worn smooth by a whole morning of being answered.

SADO “I propose reforming the grain-transport system, and he says I am wasting the state treasury.”

SADO “I raise the matter of the coinage laws, and he says I am recklessly tampering with ancestral statutes.”

SADO “If I say nothing, he calls me incompetent.”

SADO “If I speak, he calls me reckless.”

Hong Bong-han keeps his head lowered, and counsels with care.

HONG BONG-HAN “Your Highness. His Majesty, too — the deeper the love, the sharper the rebuke.”

The Crown Prince gives a short laugh.

SADO “Love?”

SADO “The deficit Father King left behind before he took the throne, the bad accounts left by the old factional strife, the military-cloth levies, grain and pay, land tax that every office has let fall into arrears — which of these is not a debt piled up over years?”

SADO “Now they want me to sit in court and govern. But the moment I open my mouth, every one of those debts becomes ‘the Eastern Palace, too young to know better.’”

SADO “So does he want a Crown Prince — or a puppet that only kowtows and confesses its faults?”

Hong Bong-han cuts him off, urgently. The older actor’s craft is steady: that single “Your Highness” carries alarm, and protection, and the particular sobriety a man of the royal in-law clan must keep at all costs.

HONG BONG-HAN “Do not speak recklessly!”

Having said it, Hong Bong-han’s eyes sweep toward the door, where a young eunuch stands. Per the written scene, the eunuch will pale, drop to his knees, and swear he has heard nothing; then the Crown Prince turns and fixes him with a stare that is nearly pathological. That stare is the seed of everything the man will later become — the seed of his breaking.

The Crown Prince follows his father-in-law’s glance. He turns his head sharply, eyes sharp as blades. The take is exactly on the rails — until, in that instant, his eyes cross the edge of the frame.

Outside the lens: Lee Ji-eun and Choi Jin-ri stand at the edge of the set like two fireworks burning side by side. Impossible to miss. So impossible it looks like a hidden stage the director installed beyond the camera on purpose, to test an actor’s concentration.

The Crown Prince’s stare goes, in one frame, from “I will kill this eavesdropping eunuch” to “why are the two of them standing together.”

The lapse lasted barely a blink.

But a lens magnifies everything.

“Cut!”

Lee Joon-ik’s voice came, as expected, from behind the monitor.

The set stopped.

The young eunuch actor, halfway into his kneel, hung there with his knees suspended and an utterly lost look on his face.

Baek Si-on pulled his gaze back.

Lee Joon-ik looked at the monitor, then at the direction Baek Si-on had turned his head toward.

He was not angry.

He smiled instead.

“Is the Crown Prince hungry?”

A soft ripple of laughter crossed the set.

The tease was gentle.

Its damage, however, was not light.

Baek Si-on lowered his head.

“Sorry.”

Lee Joon-ik waved a hand.

“No harm. One more take.”

Baek Si-on glanced toward the edge of the set again.

His gaze rested on Lee Ji-eun and Choi Jin-ri for exactly one second.

Both women stood there, utterly innocent.

Each one better-behaved than the other.

He turned back to his place, straightened the hem of his court robe, and prepared to do it again.

At the edge of the set, Choi Jin-ri guiltily shrank back half a step and whispered:

“Eonni… are we standing here affecting his shoot?”

Lee Ji-eun glanced at her.

“That’s his problem, not ours.”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

“……Huh?”

Lee Ji-eun lifted a hand and tucked her hair behind her ear.

The gesture was very elegant.

The certainty in her posture was absolute.

“Two dazzling beauties like us standing here — his slip-up is completely understandable~”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

She wanted to argue.

But she could not.

Because while that self-assessment was cocky to the level of high art — it was, technically, not wrong.

Choi Jin-ri lowered her head and snuck a look at her own outfit for today.

She had not dressed up.

But she was not ugly, either.

And Lee Ji-eun today had gone for a fresh, clean look as well.

Two of the hottest female entertainers in the Korean industry, standing side by side. If Baek Si-on were a normal twenty-two-year-old man — it would have been strange if he had not lost his focus.

In her heart, Choi Jin-ri quietly accepted Lee Ji-eun’s cocky logic.

Then she began a much more precise calculation.

Eonni had said “the two of us.”

But that glance just now — the one that had slipped — he had definitely lost his focus because of her.

Of that she was certain.

One hundred percent.

Absolutely.

Beside her, Lee Ji-eun’s gaze drifted, seemingly at random, up to the lamp rig above the set.

But inside, she was running almost exactly the same programme.

Peach was there too, of course.

But that glance just now — he had definitely lost his focus because of her.

She was quite sure of it.

After all, on the Love Yourself music-video shoot, she and Baek Si-on had held each other’s gaze for no short time.

The gold content of that kind of eye contact — Peach had probably never experienced it.

Two women stood at the edge of the set.

One with a gentle expression.

One with a faint smile.

And in their hearts, each had completed a reasoning process of flawless logic, ample evidence, and absolutely no possibility of losing.

✿ ✿ ✿

Shooting resumed.

Sado · the side hall · scene thirteen · take two

No distractions this time — the eye-break has to land on the eunuch

Action

The same speech from the top; this time nothing outside the frame exists. The list stays flat, and the bitterness lives in the pauses between items — from “the deficit Father King left behind” through “a puppet that only kowtows and confesses its faults,” the emotion piles up in layers.

Hong Bong-han’s “Do not speak recklessly!” lands, and his eyes sweep to the door. The Crown Prince snaps his head around — cold, ferocious. This time nothing outside the frame disturbs the take. The young eunuch’s legs give way and he drops straight to his knees.

EUNUCH “Your Highness! This servant heard nothing!”

The Crown Prince stares at him. The camera pushes in. In his eyes, the humiliation of the morning has already curdled into something else: vigilance, suspicion, even a trace of killing intent that will not be governed. He looks at the eunuch — and through him, at every eye in the palace that might carry word back to Father King.

SADO “You had better have heard nothing.”

“Cut.”

This time Lee Joon-ik called it quickly.

He sat watching the replay for three full minutes.

“Good.”

The assistant director asked:

“Keep a safety take?”

Lee Joon-ik wound the footage back to the frame of Baek Si-on turning his head.

The break in the eyes was unmistakable.

In the first half of the frame: the prince crushed by his father.

In the second half: something mad already beginning to grow.

He nodded.

“No need. This take is fine.”

Then he raised his head and called out:

“Lunch!”

The set let out its breath all at once.

Extras flexed their knees as they moved, eyes already drifting toward the support trucks. Crew members were visibly delighted.

Two support trucks. Coffee in unlimited supply. Sandwiches in unlimited supply.

The quality of this lunch break rivalled the success of a full Joseon fiscal reform.

Baek Si-on rose from the low table as well, and walked over toward Choi Jin-ri and Lee Ji-eun.

“Did you two arrange to come together?”

Choi Jin-ri was just about to shake her head when Lee Ji-eun spoke first.

“That’s right.”

Choi Jin-ri’s neck, halfway through the shake, stopped at a deeply awkward angle.

She looked at Lee Ji-eun.

Lee Ji-eun smiled at her.

Slowly, Choi Jin-ri turned her neck back to its proper position.

She did not shake her head.

Baek Si-on nodded. He did not dig deeper.

“And you came empty-handed?”

Choi Jin-ri reacted fast, holding out the coffee in her hand.

“No. This is for you.”

Lee Ji-eun refused to be outdone. From her canvas tote she drew the stack of ticket stubs, rubber band still holding it neat and square, and held it out.

“This is also for you.”

Baek Si-on’s gaze moved from the coffee cup in Choi Jin-ri’s hand, to the thick stack in Lee Ji-eun’s hand.

Back to the coffee cup.

Back to the ticket stubs.

A look to the left.

A look to the right.

Then, slowly, he turned his head toward Lee Joon-ik and the assistant director standing by the monitor.

Lee Joon-ik was staring at the monitor.

His expression was extremely professional.

The assistant director was also staring at the monitor.

His expression was even more professional.

And even less able to hold it in.

“Ahem. Director. Let’s go eat.”

Lee Joon-ik stood up at once.

“Good.”

The two of them walked out, steps steady, with all the dignified bearing a film-crew leadership should have.

Once they had put a little distance between themselves and the set, the faint sound of the assistant director lowering his voice could be heard:

“Director.”

“Mm?”

“I don’t think this film of ours should be called Sado.”

“Oh?”

Lee Joon-ik’s tone was indifferent.

“What should it be called?”

The assistant director, perfectly serious:

“The King’s Women.”

Lee Joon-ik: “……”

“This afternoon, add thirty minutes of kowtow practice for you.”

“Director, I was joking.”

Lee Joon-ik kept walking.

“I was joking too.”

The assistant director had just let his breath out in relief when the next second Lee Joon-ik added:

“Forty minutes.”

“……”
