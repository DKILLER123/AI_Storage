Chapter Sixty-Five

The Tribute Is Underway, the Wallet Is Dead

August 1 Afternoon KBS · waiting room A visiting-tribute routine, and the end of the folding cash

Once they'd lined up, Irene presented the physical single to Baek Si-on with both hands.

Baek Si-on took it.

It didn't weigh much, but the interpersonal credit it carried was enormous.

This was the most classic K-pop institution there was, the "waiting-room social."

By the rules, a junior came to your door and handed over their album, and the senior accepted it, then immediately turned around and pulled a signed physical album — inscribed with a handwritten line like "congratulations on your debut, may you flourish" — out of their own bag to return the favour.

A favour-machine that had run for years.

But it didn't apply to Baek Si-on right now.

He released digital singles. He didn't have a single paper CD booklet on hand that he could sign.

If he only took and never gave, then by tomorrow the word would get around that Baek Si-on threw his weight around, was arrogant and rude.

Baek Si-on swept a glance over the snack basket on the coffee table.

He couldn't very well hand back a pack of shrimp crackers.

He glanced at the makeup counter.

Nor a bottle of styling spray.

He thought about it.

Then he turned to look at Baek Eun-ah.

“Bag.”

“Huh?”

“Your bag. Give it to me.”

Without thinking, Baek Eun-ah handed over the bag sitting on the makeup counter.

Baek Si-on pulled the zip open, sorted around inside, and produced his wallet.

He'd put the wallet in Baek Eun-ah's bag because a bulge in the suit pocket would ruin the drape.

He opened the wallet.

He'd drawn a little cash for emergencies before leaving yesterday.

His fingers shuffled through the sleeve, and he counted out two fifty-thousand-won notes, handing them over.

Irene's eyes met the portrait of the classical woman artist on the banknote.

“Su… sunbae?”

“Take it.”

Baek Si-on's expression was entirely serious.

Someone else brought a gift, so he returned a gift. Perfectly reasonable.

As for why cash — because cash carried the most ceremony.

Irene was still hesitating.

Not that there was no precedent for a senior handing out pocket money.

But that only ever happened on particular holidays.

And today was the first of August. Not New Year's. Not Chuseok. Not a holiday of any sort.

Baek Eun-ah couldn't watch any longer.

She knew far too well how broke her unnis at SM were.

“Eonni, take it.”

Baek Eun-ah grabbed Irene's hand, pulled it up, and pressed the hundred thousand won into her palm.

Irene glanced at her, and only then did she turn and bow to Baek Si-on once more.

“Th… thank you, sunbae…”

With the leader leading the way, the rest was simple.

Like an elder distributing gifts on New Year's Eve, Baek Si-on walked one by one to Kang Seul-gi, Son Seung-wan, and Park Soo-young, two fifty-thousand-won notes each.

Son Seung-wan

Red Velvet

Real-name counterpart · Son Seung-wan

The kind of face that makes a room decide to be friendly; the member whose warmth reads even in a crowd of strangers, and the one who makes it okay to be nervous out loud.

Park Soo-young

Red Velvet · maknae

Real-name counterpart · Park Soo-young

The maknae, the one who leans slightly toward Irene in the dark of a broadcast hall to ask whether Baek sunbae can really take number one. Her optimism comes in its youngest, least armoured form.

“Take this and buy yourselves something good to eat.”

When he'd handed out the last one, Baek Si-on folded the wallet shut and tossed it back into Baek Eun-ah's crossbody bag.

Baek Eun-ah looked at her own rifled-through bag, then at the four ex-trainee contemporaries each clutching cash.

Other seniors gave signed albums, wrote warm little notes, took a photo, and posted it online.

Her cousin just handed out folding cash.

Like a foreman paying out a day's wages to the crew.

✿ ✿ ✿

As the leader, Irene was the first to shake off the shock of the money.

“Sunbae, thank you so much! We'll work hard!”

“Mm. Good luck.”

“Then… sunbae, we won't trouble you any longer. Rest well.”

The four girls bowed in perfect unison, and like four little penguins in a row, backed their way out the door.

The sound of footsteps scattered down the corridor.

Baek Eun-ah looked at Baek Si-on. He waved a hand toward the doorway.

She turned and trotted after them.

✿ ✿ ✿

In the corridor.

The four girls with the dyed hair ends walked ahead, and Baek Eun-ah hurried two steps up and slipped into the row without a moment's pause, as if they were heading off to the convenience store together again.

“Eun-ah, when did you become a manager?”

Kang Seul-gi turned her head, curious.

“A couple of months ago.”

“So you're the manager for that Baek Si-on sunbae on the door?”

“Mm. He's my cousin.”

“Cousin?!”

“Yeah?”

“Oh my god! Eun-ah, your cousin's so handsome!”

Park Soo-young leaned in.

“When I bowed just now I sneaked a look. That jawline, that shoulder width, and that buzz cut… aish.”

Baek Eun-ah's face crumpled.

That face was old news to her by now.

To everyone else, a cold, restrained actor's face. To her, a tyrant who spent the whole day demanding she work and could choke a person with a single sentence.

“Get a grip. You're blinded by a hundred thousand won.”

“But he really does have presence.”

“What presence. Yesterday he spent half an hour in front of the mirror squeezing out blackheads.”

Park Soo-young covered her mouth and laughed.

Kang Seul-gi laughed too.

Walking at the very front, Irene didn't turn her head, but her shoulders plainly shook twice.

And just then, footsteps sounded from the end of the corridor.

Four of them.

Heeled shoes, an even stride, walking in with their own background music.

Irene's laugh vanished. Her back straightened. One hand settled on Kang Seul-gi's arm and gave a light squeeze.

Kang Seul-gi caught on a beat later, following her gaze down the corridor.

SISTAR.

The red, yellow, blue, and green heads snapped into a line out of reflex and all four of them bowed.

“Sunbae!”

“Debuting today?”

Hyolyn, at the front, stopped. Her gaze swept over the four tense girls and lingered a second on Irene's face.

“Yes! We're Red Velvet!”

“Mm. Good luck with your first stage.”

“Thank you, sunbae.”

It was the standard corridor-greeting template.

Impossible to fault, and yet carrying a class boundary you couldn't step across.

Baek Eun-ah watched them reach the door, saw Hyolyn raise a hand and knock three times.

The door opened from inside.

Baek Eun-ah gave it a thought, then waved toward the girls.

“Eonnis, I'm heading back first.”

She trotted the other way.

✿ ✿ ✿

When Baek Eun-ah pushed the waiting-room door open, SISTAR's four members were already standing at the makeup counter.

The exact same positions Red Velvet had taken.

But the mood was different.

This was probably the most awkward visit the building would see all day.

Number one on the chart and number two on the chart, meeting face to face.

What's more, their fandom STAR1 had spent the whole week hounding this airborne independent singer online.

Hyolyn and Bora wore awkward smiles, most likely afraid Baek Si-on bore a grudge.

But Baek Si-on accepted the album completely calmly.

He knew exactly what they were embarrassed about, and he understood perfectly well it wasn't SISTAR's fault.

Then he opened the wallet.

And took out another eight.

Two apiece, a hundred thousand won each.

“Take it.”

Hyolyn: “?”

Stood outside the door, she'd presumably prepared several sets of responses. The one option she hadn't prepared was "sunbae hands out folding cash."

“Sunbae, this is too—”

“I gave Red Velvet exactly the same amount just now. Not a won more, not a won less.”

“…”

Four veterans who'd clawed through the industry for four years stood gripping the cash like brand-new trainees, thanked him in a daze, and backed out.

✿ ✿ ✿

But the trouble didn't end there.

Music Bank brought together the most active singers in the country. SISTAR had barely cleared the door before a fresh knock sounded down the hall.

Bang Min-ah led the group in, Hye-ri trailing at the back peeking over heads.

“Sunbae! We're Girl's Day!”

Baek Si-on accepted the album.

Opened the wallet.

Eight.

A hundred thousand won each.

“Thank you, sunbae!”

Gone.

Another knock.

B1A4.

Five of them.

Ten notes.

Two each.

“Good luck.”

“Thank you, sunbae!”

Gone.

Another knock.

Block B.

Then BESTie.

HALO.

B.I.G.

Boys Republic.

Group after group.

Korea's lush, teeming idol assembly line, on this one day, had materialised in the shape of the paper money steadily draining out of Baek Si-on's wallet.

Until at last the corridor broadcast sounded, calling for the afternoon rehearsal prep.

The door stopped knocking.

Baek Si-on sat on the sofa.

On the coffee table sat a small mountain of physical signed albums, all colours, a dozen or so of them.

He pulled out his wallet.

Opened it.

Both sleeves were empty.

All that was left in the card slots was one bank card and one ID card.

The cash he'd drawn yesterday. Entirely dead.
