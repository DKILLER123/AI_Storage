Chapter Eighty-Eight

Baek Eun-ah’s ₩500M Salary Dream Shatters

The evening of the return Taxi, then Yeonnam-dong Mapo Bridge · then the balcony Five brands. A ninety-two. Samsung and Lotte, cut.

The taxi was stuck on Mapo Bridge.

Buildings on both banks of the Hangang had come on. Traffic on the deck crawled and stopped. Red brake lights made a long snake.

Baek Eun-ah sat in the back, a box of strawberry milk in both hands, the straw in her mouth, flipping her cousin’s schedule on her phone.

The phone rang.

She looked at the display.

Unknown number.

Starting 02. A Seoul landline.

Eun-ah took the straw out of her mouth and picked up.

KB Kookmin Bank · Team Lead Jeong → Baek Eun-ah · Mapo Bridge

“Hello, am I speaking with Mr. Baek Si-on’s manager?”

“You are. And you are?”

“Hello. This is the brand marketing department at KB Kookmin Bank. My name is Jeong. I’m the team lead on the annual image-endorser project.”

Eun-ah’s back left the seat. KB Kookmin Bank. One of Korea’s four commercial banks. A monster with more than three hundred trillion won in total assets.

“Team Lead Jeong, please go ahead.”

“Here’s the situation. Our brand-endorser plan is in motion. When the upper floor discussed the candidate list, Mr. Baek Si-on was the name nominated most often. I’m calling today to do a first conversation with the manager side, and to understand Mr. Baek’s current endorsement calendar and fee range.”

Eun-ah took the phone five centimetres off her ear. Looked at the screen. Confirmed it was not a scam call. Put it back.

“All right, Team Lead Jeong, thank you very much for reaching out. Mr. Baek Si-on has just come home; the calendar is still being adjusted. Would it be convenient to send a concrete proposal to my email? I’ll speak with the artist and get back to you as soon as I can.”

“No problem. I’ll send it shortly.”

“All right. Thank you.”

The call had just hung up.

The phone rang again.

“Hello, is this Mr. Baek Si-on’s manager?”

“It is. Please go ahead.”

“We’re the brand communications office at Hyundai Motor Group. Regarding Mr. Baek Si-on serving as our next-season global brand ambassador, we’d like a first conversation. The contract period we’re tentatively planning is two years…”

Eun-ah answered with the exact same script as a moment ago.

Hung up.

The phone rang again.

“Hello, Mr. Baek Si-on’s manager. This is the corporate PR department at SK Telecom…”

“…”

Five calls in all.

Eun-ah ran the memo from top to bottom.

KB Kookmin Bank.

Hyundai Motor.

SK Telecom.

Samsung Electronics.

Lotte Chilsung Beverage.

In Eun-ah’s old map of the world, these five brands were the kind that only ever appeared next to names like Lee Byung-hun, Jeon Ji-hyun, Kim Yuna.

When Eun-ah was a trainee, she had stood in a subway station, looked up at those giant boards, and thought: that is another world’s business. That was the threshold.

Now.

Five of them had come at once.

And before this, Eun-ah had turned over every memory she had of Korean entertainment endorsement history.

Not one artist had ever received endorsement offers from these five brands at the same time.

Two was already the top.

Three was legend.

Five was what kind of concept?

Eun-ah did not have a word for the concept.

The taxi finally crawled off Mapo Bridge and turned onto the service road toward Yeonnam-dong.

Eun-ah watched the streetlights moving past the window, thought, and pulled the first number from the call log just now.

The KB one.

Called back.

Two rings. Picked up.

Baek Eun-ah → KB Kookmin Bank · Team Lead Jeong · callback

“Manager Baek? Is there a problem?”

“Sorry to bother you. I wanted to ask one more thing. What was the occasion for considering Mr. Baek Si-on this time? Was it because of the Venice prize?”

“The Volpi Cup’s gold content inside the film world is beyond question, but honestly, a winner at one of the three festivals doesn’t necessarily convert, in Korean national recognition, into brand favourability. A prize alone is usually not enough for our upper floor to pass a new face’s endorsement proposal in a budget meeting.”

That was the fact. After Jeon Do-yeon took Cannes Best Actress, she had not walked into the kind of “ads piling into the hands” situation. The advertising circle’s verdict: the image is too real, too heavy. It does not let people invent something beautiful.

“What actually made us decide to move was the report Insight put out.”

“The report?”

“Mm. The title was something like the first woman he went to see after coming home. After it ran, we did a round of internal sentiment testing. Mr. Baek Si-on’s brand-trust index in the twenty-five-to-fifty-four group went straight to ninety-two.”

Ninety-two. Eun-ah did not really know the scoring standard for a brand-trust index, but she knew what the number ninety-two meant. A hundred was full marks.

“Our marketing department’s summary went like this—”

“Venice Best Actor gave him the qualification to endorse a high-end brand. But the donation-covenant report gave him the trust base to endorse a mass brand. The first decided whether he could appear on our candidate list. The second decided whether he could pass final approval.”

“The two together are a complete proposal.”

Eun-ah wrote the speech into the memo, word by word.

“Understood. Thank you.”

“Not at all. Looking forward to working together.”

Eun-ah hung up.

Stared at the paragraph she had just written in the memo for two seconds.

Then left the memo, opened Naver, typed “Insight Baek Si-on” into the search bar.

Results in a second.

First on the list.

The instant she saw the title, both eyes went dark.

Insight · exclusive

[Exclusive] SHOCKING! The first woman the new Venice Best Actor went to see after coming home — is actually her?!

On the afternoon of 8 September, this magazine’s reporter, at the door of the Korea Women’s Hot Line (KWHL), witnessed a scene that moved the heart. Baek Si-on, Best Actor, just back from Venice with the honour, did not go home to rest, did not throw a celebration. He went alone to KWHL headquarters to make good the promise he had given in his first-place speech on KBS Music Bank on 1 August.

According to the official donation receipt provided by KWHL staff, Mr. Baek Si-on donated the entirety of this week’s digital-revenue from his single Way Back Home — seventy million won in all — to the foundation. The sum will be used to help women and children who have suffered domestic violence, and to provide legal aid and psychological support for single mothers.

It is worth noting that Mr. Baek Si-on did not notify any media for on-site coverage. If Insight had not followed the lead and reported it, this donation of seventy million won would likely have been drowned in the wall of news about his Venice win, and no one would have known.

The Green Fly is about domestic violence, bottom-rung debt, the generational pass of patriarchal violence. With today’s seventy million won, Baek Si-on told us one thing: art is not for dressing a façade. Art is for changing the world.

Donor: Baek Si-on · Receiving institution: KWHL · Amount: 70,000,000 KRW · photo: Park Jae-won

No wonder.

That title — no living person could be expected not to click.

Eun-ah had clicked.

Further down: the photograph Park Jae-won had taken of the KWHL donation receipt.

KWHL · official receipt

DonorBaek Si-on
InstitutionKWHL

₩70,000,000

Way Back Home digital, this week, in full.

Finished.

Eun-ah scored Son Nam-won in her head.

Nine.

The one point docked was because the word “SHOCKING” was too cheap.

✿ ✿ ✿

The taxi finally turned off the service road into a Yeonnam-dong alley.

Speed dropped. Residential buildings on both sides, streetlights on.

“Here is fine. Thank you.”

Eun-ah passed the fare to the driver, got out with the box of strawberry milk already drunk to the bottom.

Closed the door.

The taxi’s tail lights turned at the end of the alley and were gone.

Eun-ah stood under the streetlight, about to walk toward the unit door.

A voice from overhead.

“Hey.”

Eun-ah lifted her head.

The streetlight hit her eyes. She squinted, hunted two seconds, locked the figure on the balcony.

Baek Si-on leaning on the balcony rail, both arms on it, looking down at her.

Black T-shirt, big shorts, slippers.

Standard home look.

“Oppa! You’re on the balcony feeding mosquitoes again?”

“…”

Eun-ah gave a heh-heh, then thud-thud-thud toward the stairwell.

Twenty seconds later, Eun-ah pulled the door Si-on had already opened from inside.

“Auntie!”

Yoon Hye-ja lifted her head from the living-room sofa, an economics journal half-turned in her hand.

“Eun-ah. Have you eaten?”

“I have!”

“That’s good.”

Yoon Hye-ja nodded once and went back to the journal.

Only then did Eun-ah turn to Si-on.

“Oppa, just now several brands called me!”

“Who?”

“KB Bank, Hyundai Motor, SK Telecom, Lotte Beverage, and Samsung phones!”

Eun-ah one finger per brand. All five fingers used.

“They said they decided to move after seeing that Insight report.”

“Let me see.”

Eun-ah tapped the phone, found the Naver article, held it out.

Si-on took the phone.

Looked down.

A jump at the temple.

The KWHL staff who had processed his donation had, in fact, been a woman.

Not exactly false.

Technically.

Si-on skimmed the body fast.

Scrolled to the comments.

Insight · comments

Si-on scrolled two screens of comments, then gave the phone back to Eun-ah.

The one hundred fifty million won he had put into Son Nam-won was, at this moment, completing its return cycle in an extremely efficient way.

“How are the actual quotes?”

Eun-ah had been waiting for that question.

“All first-round intent, but I asked around the range.”

First-round endorsement intent

She put up one finger.

“KB Bank, annual endorsement. Their budget range is about eight to twelve hundred million.”

Second finger.

“Hyundai Motor a bit higher, but they want to bind a new model’s global launch. Contract period might start at two years.”

Third.

“SK Telecom and Samsung Electronics are both short contracts by the quarter. About two to three hundred million a quarter.”

Fourth.

“Lotte Chilsung Beverage hasn’t named a number yet. Said they’re waiting for internal approval to finish.”

Eun-ah reported the whole menu in one breath, looking at Si-on opposite with both hands in the pockets of the big shorts.

“Oppa, if we sign all five…”

She swallowed.

“Conservative estimate, annual endorsement income at least four billion plus.”

Four billion won.

In her head the split ran itself.

The split Si-on had set for her was the top-tier fifteen percent.

Six hundred million won.

Tax off, in hand, close to five hundred million.

Eighteen years old.

Annual salary five hundred million won.

More than the four hundred fifty million her father had taken home after shooting a whole film, flying to Venice, and winning the Luigi De Laurentiis.

“Don’t rush.”

Si-on’s voice cut the financial fantasy.

“Huh?”

“Samsung and Lotte — don’t take those.”

“Why?!”

Eun-ah’s voice jumped half an octave. One look from Si-on pressed it back down.

He could not explain it to this brat.

Two years from now, Samsung Electronics’ annual flagship, the Galaxy Note 7, would stage a worldwide chain of battery explosions that stunned the world.

Still less could he explain the extremely soap-operatic family civil war Lotte Group was about to walk into.

The founder’s two sons tearing their faces off over succession, reporting each other, suing each other.

The chaebol family’s dirty laundry thrown, piece by piece, in front of the whole country.

And that was only the starter.

The real main was later: Lotte agreeing to host a certain transnational plan on its own golf course.

That move would make the Lotte brand’s reputation in the whole East Asian market go up in smoke.

These two names, in the next few years, were powder kegs strapped with timers.

Touch them, die.

“Don’t poke into society’s business.”

“…”

She ran a silent account in her head.

Samsung four quarters plus Lotte for a year of endorsement fees, conservative, at least one and a half billion.

Two hundred twenty-five million, gone.

Her two hundred twenty-five million!

“Fine. Then KB Bank, Hyundai Motor, and SK Telecom — those three we can talk?”

“We can. Go ask your auntie if she knows a lawyer.”

Eun-ah stalled a beat.

“Oppa, don’t you know LOEN’s Attorney Choi?”

Si-on looked at her once.

“You also know he’s LOEN’s.”

One sentence woke the person in the dream.

Now that a plate of nearly four billion was on the table, they were destined to found a fully independent personal management label.

Then all their core commercial cards, the running books, the breach clauses, could absolutely not be exposed again to any outside person with another capital background.

That was called a bottom line.

Also called a commercial firewall.

“…Oh.”

Eun-ah had not opened her mouth to ask.

Yoon Hye-ja’s voice had already come from the sofa.

“A colleague of mine has a favourite student at Kim & Chang.”

Eun-ah and Si-on turned at the same time.

The economics journal was still open in Yoon Hye-ja’s hands. Her gaze had not even lifted from the page.

She was not deaf.

Every word the two of them had said three metres away, she had heard clearly.

“Seo Eun-ju. Graduated first in Ewha Law. Top ten on the Korean bar that year.”

“I’ll send you her contact.”

Finished, picked up the phone, tapped a few times, and Eun-ah’s phone rang.

The whole process did not exceed ten seconds.

Eun-ah looked down at the contact card that had popped up.

Seo Eun-ju.

Kim & Chang.

Global top hundred, Korea’s first, more than 1,200 lawyers.

Almost every chaebol chairman, after something happened, was handled by Kim & Chang.

The chaebol of the legal world, as it were.

Eun-ah lifted her head, looked at the figure on the sofa still turning the journal.

Walked over fast, sat down hard beside Yoon Hye-ja, both arms around the professor’s arm.

“Auntie you’re amazing! How do you know everyone?!”

The hand on the arm Eun-ah was holding moved a little. The turning of the journal paused.

“Teach at a university this many years, you always have a few students who make something of themselves.”

“Auntie, later if there’s something I don’t understand, can I ask you too?”

“Ask your cousin.”

“Oppa always says don’t poke into society’s business!”

“Look for the reason in yourself.”

“Auntie~~!”

Eun-ah’s spoiling offensive lasted about thirty seconds.

Yoon Hye-ja’s mouth bent, behind the journal, once.

Si-on stood to the side watching a while, then turned toward his room.

At the door, he stopped.

“Eun-ah.”

Eun-ah lifted her head off Yoon Hye-ja’s arm.

“Mm?”

“You did well.”

“Hmph. I’m a manager on a five-hundred-million salary. Of course I did well!”

“Three hundred million. Samsung and Lotte were cut.”

“…You had to bring that up, huh!”
