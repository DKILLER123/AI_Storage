Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Six

‘Sado’ Opens to a Smash

The same night, and the morning after

A sofa too narrow to be innocent

Nonhyeon-dong · the studio lounge

The sofa was too narrow.

Once Lee Ji-eun fell asleep she took over most of it, her body still crowding toward Baek Si-on.

The studio had no windows; there was no telling the time of day by the sky.

He stayed awake the whole time.

The reason could hardly be reduced to “being used as a pillow.”

At some point one of Lee Ji-eun’s legs had settled onto his, her knee pressing into a spot extremely unfavorable — for a man — to falling asleep.

Baek Si-on had tried adjusting his posture.

She moved with him, ending up pressed even closer.

The sofa left him no real retreat.

He simply gave up.

The soundproofing panels on the ceiling were arranged very neatly.

Baek Si-on stared at them and, over a long while, admitted one thing.

What he felt for Lee Ji-eun was more than admiration.

Compatibility, creative fit, passively accepting her closeness — none of those words could hold everything that had happened tonight.

❧ ❧ ❧

Six o’clock sharp.

The alarm went off.

Baek Si-on reached out at once and pressed the sound off.

The person in his arms moved anyway.

Lee Ji-eun frowned slightly and slowly opened her eyes.

Her vision cleared from blur to focus, and last night’s memory connected back piece by piece.

When she finally met Baek Si-on’s face, she realized he had been quietly studying her.

“You’ve been up the whole time?”

“Lying down doesn’t count as sleeping.”

“What do you mean?”

“Someone used me as a pillow and kept moving around.”

Lee Ji-eun looked down.

Only then did she find her leg sprawled shamelessly over Baek Si-on’s, her knee pressing into a spot that could only be classified as a physical attack.

Not only did Lee Ji-eun not hurry to pull it back; she deliberately gave it a small nudge.

Then she lifted her face again.

“Is that the only reason?”

Baek Si-on’s body tensed slightly.

“You’d better not ask.”

Lee Ji-eun understood.

Baek Si-on’s sleepless night had little to do with the sofa being too narrow.

He had been holding back.

That realization made the gloom of being woken by an alarm vanish at once.

The corner of her mouth lifted, then was pressed back down.

She couldn’t let him see how satisfied she was.

“Then should I apologize?”

“Take your leg off first. That’d be more sincere.”

“My leg’s numb.”

“It could move a moment ago.”

“Now it’s suddenly numb.”

Lee Ji-eun simply pressed her face back against his shoulder.

“I’ve been leaning on you all night anyway. A little longer won’t matter.”

“My arm has an opinion.”

“Let it speak for itself.”

Baek Si-on tried to move the arm she had pillowed all night.

As his shoulder rose, Lee Ji-eun swayed with it.

“You did that on purpose.”

“Checking whether it still has feeling.”

“And?”

“The arm doesn’t.”

A pause.

“Elsewhere does.”

That sentence edged up to the boundary of what couldn’t be discussed openly.

Lee Ji-eun still caught it steadily.

“Then that shows you’re in good shape.”

“Thanks to the Jinro endorser for the product test.”

“It’s not like I poured it down you last night.”

Baek Si-on gestured at the tea table.

Five empty bottles lay strewn across it.

The number of pieces of evidence hardly supported her defense.

Baek Si-on started to sit up.

His pinned arm had no strength; the moment his shoulder moved, Lee Ji-eun leaned further into him.

“Up.”

“One more minute.”

“I’m in a hurry.”

“That’s why only one minute.”

Baek Si-on picked up his phone.

Six-oh-three.

He didn’t forcibly push her away.

“Fine.”

Given permission, Lee Ji-eun closed her eyes again.

Her arm still lay across his front; her leg, at least, shifted a little, no longer lingering in the danger zone.

Baek Si-on was clearly in a hurry.

She asked for only one minute, and he was willing to stay a while longer.

More than last night’s kiss, Lee Ji-eun liked this quiet.

Perhaps this was what truly made her want to linger and not get up.

Harder to obtain than a kiss.

And more worth remembering.

“Time’s up.”

Baek Si-on spoke right on time.

Lee Ji-eun opened her eyes.

“You actually timed it?”

“Otherwise why call it a minute?”

“……”

“Let’s go.”

Baek Si-on stood and held out his hand to Lee Ji-eun:

“Give me my jacket.”

She glanced at the suit jacket still covering her, then pulled it up to her chin.

“It already has my scent on it.”

Baek Si-on reached over and pulled the jacket away.

“That’s called soju smell.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

She decided not to keep arguing about body odor at six in the morning with a man she had just tormented all night and whose arm had not yet fully recovered.

❧ ❧ ❧

The glass doors of the building housing Jung Jae-joon’s studio slid open on both sides.

Baek Si-on walked out of the lobby and got into the van parked at the curb.

The door closed.

He sank into the captain’s chair, closed his eyes, and quickly entered a state of refusing to communicate with the world.

Baek Eun-ah sat on the other side, sorting the day’s schedule.

Roadshow, media interviews, theater stage greetings — the day was packed tight.

When the distributor drew up the schedule, they clearly regarded actors as special equipment: five minutes of charging, eighteen hours of service.

Having checked the arrival time for the first theater, Baek Eun-ah looked up.

“Cousin, how many hours did you sleep last night?”

Baek Si-on answered with his eyes closed:

“Lay down for a few hours.”

“I asked how long you slept.”

“That question has no statistical value.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

Which meant no sleep at all.

She went back to the schedule, and happened to catch a faint scent drifting from Baek Si-on’s shirt.

She leaned in to confirm.

“You drank too?”

“Disinfecting.”

Baek Eun-ah studied her cousin seriously.

Refused to give sleep statistics, explained pre-dawn drinking as disinfecting, and his clothes carried a perfume that plainly wasn’t his.

Digging further, the truth would very likely endanger the cousin’s mental health.

Baek Eun-ah decisively retreated to her own seat.

“Fine.”

She looked back down at the tablet.

“If you say disinfecting, then disinfecting it is.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The van drove onto Gangnam-daero.

Early-morning Seoul was gradually waking.

Buses passed along the dedicated lane, roadside convenience stores began restocking, and cars stopped one after another before the intersection.

Still a dozen-odd minutes to the salon.

While his eyes were closed, Baek Eun-ah opened the film’s rating page.

“The word of mouth is in.”

Baek Si-on didn’t open his eyes.

“Read.”

opening-day scores · Eun-ah reads them out

Naver audience score — opened 9.17, now climbing to 9.32

Professional media composite — 7.9

CGV Golden Egg index — 96%

Baek Si-on’s eyebrow twitched.

“The audience score is still climbing?”

Baek Eun-ah enlarged the rating curve.

Most of the new reviews after dawn came from ordinary viewers who had completed ticket-purchase verification.

Right after the premiere, Baek Si-on’s fans made up a large share of the rating users.

By reason, as ordinary viewers came in, the high scores given by star fans would be gradually diluted, and the final rating would slowly fall.

‘Sado’s curve ran the other way.

After midnight more verified viewers came, and the share of nine- and ten-point ratings kept rising.

Baek Si-on’s vast fan ecosystem could indeed help the film win a handsome opening, but the force now pushing the score to 9.32 had begun to shift from fans to ordinary viewers.

A touch of smugness entered Baek Eun-ah’s voice.

“At least so far, it isn’t being treated as a film that only sells a star’s face.”

“It’s only the first day.”

“Can’t you be a little happy?”

“If I’m happy now, it’ll be awkward once the score drops.”

“So you plan to wait until the film leaves theaters to be happy?”

“I could.”

Baek Eun-ah reeled in half her smugness.

She kept the other half; if the score kept climbing tomorrow, she planned to file this line away.

❧ ❧ ❧

“Want to hear some audience comments?”

“Pick the representative ones.”

Baek Eun-ah began scrolling the top-liked.

Naver Movie · ‘Sado’ · top-liked comments

Before watching I thought Song Kang-ho would steamroll everything. Afterward I realized Baek Si-on gave him no chance to.

I came for Baek Si-on; on the way out the first thing I wanted to do was call my dad.

Song Kang-ho and Baek Si-on didn’t overpower each other — the two of them drove each other into a corner.

I used to think Baek Si-on won Venice because the ‘Green Fly’ role was close to his own life. After ‘Sado’ I know: he really can act.

Baek Si-on showed no reaction.

Baek Eun-ah continued:

Having finished, she asked:

“How’s that one?”

Baek Si-on answered with his eyes closed:

“Mediocre wording, correct conclusion.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

He even absorbed flattery with this much self-righteousness.

She flipped to the media-review section.

She couldn’t only read the good.

“Want to look at the professional reviews too?”

“Mm.”

Baek Eun-ah picked out a few negative ones.

the professional minority · opening-day reviews

“The film weighs Crown Prince Sado’s victimhood too heavily and skims over his historical killing of many palace attendants — it risks beautifying the figure.”

“Lee Joon-ik is too fixated on the father-son tragedy, reducing Lady Yeong and Lady Hyegyeong to functional figures who push two men toward ruin.”

“Song Kang-ho and Baek Si-on’s star halos are too strong; in some scenes the audience sees not Yeongjo and Sado but two Best Actors in a performance contest.”

Baek Eun-ah set the tablet down and asked:

“Do these negative reviews need handling?”

“A film isn’t a product ad; you can’t allow only five-star reviews.”

Baek Si-on finally opened his eyes.

“Have Son Nam-won compile the points of contention, but don’t take the field to rebut.”

An actor personally arguing with critics over whether the character was beautified — winning wouldn’t add box office, and losing would easily hand the film an uglier headline.

the headline he refused to feed them

“Venice Best Actor cracks, publicly lectures audiences on how to correctly understand ‘Sado’.”

A media editorial desk might love that headline, but Baek Si-on wouldn’t give them the chance.

❧ ❧ ❧

Styling done.

Baek Si-on changed into a suit, and the van set off again for Yongsan CGV.

The first stage greeting was scheduled for the nine-a.m. show.

After the screening, Baek Si-on walked in through the theater’s side door with the main crew.

The instant the lights came up, the audience rose as one.

Applause and cheers surged together.

The host, holding the microphone, asked:

STAGE GREETING · Yongsan CGV · the nine-a.m. show

Host: “Was the film good?”

Audience: “Yes!”

Someone in the back row shouted at the top of his lungs.

“Si-on-oppa, hurry and come out of the rice chest!”

The whole theater burst out laughing.

Baek Si-on took the microphone.

“I already have.”

Before the laughter stopped, he added:

“Otherwise what showed up for today’s stage greeting would be my memorial portrait.”

Song Kang-ho drawled from beside him:

“I can be in charge of bringing it.”

The whole hall laughed louder.

Lee Joon-ik pressed his forehead.

In the film, one was in charge of locking the son in, the other of dying inside.

Now the two stood on stage discussing who’d bring the memorial portrait; Korean historical film’s after-sales service was somewhat macabre.

❧ ❧ ❧

The first show ended.

The van immediately turned toward Konkuk University station.

Then Yeongdeungpo.

After each screening, the full-house standing ovation, flashes, cheers and questions cycled again.

Baek Si-on ran back and forth between theaters.

Lunch could only be handled on the road.

Baek Eun-ah held a burger in one hand and kept refreshing her phone with the other.

“The data’s out.”

Baek Si-on leaned into the seat eating his third burger.

“How?”

Baek Eun-ah turned the screen around.

The Korean Film Council’s box-office computer network had published ‘Sado’s first-day data.

KOFIC box-office network · ‘Sado’ day one

Day-one admissions — 684,000

Day-one box office — about ₩5.2 billion

Average occupancy — 83%

Share of daily box office — 45%

Baek Eun-ah pushed the screen a little closer.

“684,000!”

“I see it.”

“You’re not surprised?”

“Surprised.”

‘Green Fly’, as an independent film, had worked a miracle with 120,000 on day one; ‘Sado’, on a single opening day, left that number far behind.

The continuous news alerts made Baek Eun-ah take the tablet back.

The media were faster than the distributor, and more excited than the actor himself.

opening-night headlines · across the wires

“‘Sado’ draws 680,000 on day one, a new opening record for a Korean historical drama.”

“Baek Si-on’s first film after Venice delivers — the summer season meets expectations.”

“Song Kang-ho and Baek Si-on’s ‘father-son war’ sweeps theaters nationwide.”

“Not a fan film: ‘Sado’s over-35 audience share overtakes viewers in their twenties.”

“Day-one box office ₩5.2 billion — ‘Sado’ locks in the summer’s top seed early.”

“Beautifying a madman or rewriting a victim? ‘Sado’s controversy and heat rise together.”

“The Baek Si-on effect pays off again: film, music, and commercial influence close the loop.”

“Showbox bets right on the summer hit — the payback period may shorten sharply.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The true industry impact of the first-day data quickly showed in theater scheduling.

Before release, though the distributor was confident in Baek Si-on and Song Kang-ho’s drawing power, they had still left enough room for other summer films.

After all, theaters aren’t charities.

The chains wouldn’t bet the whole summer season on one historical tragedy before the film actually delivered data.

After the 680,000 figure was published, the three major chains held emergency scheduling-adjustment meetings one after another.

On the table there was no artistic appreciation — only each branch’s real-time occupancy, next-day presale curves, and prime-slot sellout ratios.

The adjustments landed quickly.

Exhibition adjustment · the day after opening

Screens lifted to 1,180 or more; some slots approaching 1,250.

Daily showtimes increased to 6,200–6,700; scheduling share raised from the original 37% to between 46% and 49%.

Late-night slots added in the core districts of Seoul, Gyeonggi, and Busan.

Some same-window films with occupancy under 20 percent had their showtimes compressed.

Pre-show ads and trailers were trimmed as far as possible, to free up scheduling space for the added shows.

But a theater’s total showtimes are finite.

Every added ‘Sado’ show meant another film lost one.

In the industry groups, protests from other distributors kept coming.

Some accused the three chains of monopolizing scheduling.

Others hinted the chains were bowing to Baek Si-on’s commercial influence.

Baek Si-on’s star halo did help ‘Sado’ get high-spec first-day scheduling, but the core reason the chains added showtimes was still that audiences were buying tickets.

The film market reserves no prime slots for anyone on account of artistic ideals.

Whoever fills the seats owns the screen for now.

Of course, that line was hardly a comfort to the distributors whose showtimes got squeezed.

❧ ❧ ❧

The first-day data reached Showbox headquarters.

680,000 admissions.

₩5.2 billion.

Set these two numbers on the conference table and every previously hesitant topic was flattened.

Director Lee Joon-ik had long harbored ambitions of an Oscar Best Foreign Language Film run.

Baek Si-on’s Venice Best Actor status, plus North American media resources and a PR team, did provide workable room for this route.

But at the premiere’s post-screening Q&A, the cultural-barrier question raised by that young European had made the investors hesitate over the PR budget.

Money could get Academy jurors into a screening room, but couldn’t guarantee they’d understand, in one viewing, why Lady Hyegyeong and Lady Yeong would “betray” Sado.

But the first-day box office could already cover a large part of the production and distribution costs.

As scheduling kept increasing, the film could almost lock in profit early on the strength of its first week.

Taking the earned profit to pour into Hollywood PR, even without ultimately winning, could raise ‘Sado’ and Showbox’s international brand considerably.

Win, and it could be written into Oscar history.

Lose, and it was only a bet placed with profit.

The Orion Group’s executives decided on the spot.

The Oscar PR budget — approved.

The ceiling doubled.

After the meeting, the overseas distribution team received instructions.

Showbox directive · the Oscar run, three steps

Step one — re-examine the English subtitles and the film’s accompanying promotional materials.

Step two — confirm with the North American PR firm the start timetable for the Academy campaign.

Step three — formally fold Baek Si-on and Song Kang-ho’s industry standing and international influence into the Oscar narrative framework.

At the end of the day, the iron must be hard in itself. However strong the campaign, the jurors casting the final votes still have to sit through two hours in the theater.

❝

Box office can buy an entry ticket; only the work itself can win the resonance.

— Showbox, on committing ‘Sado’ to the Oscar race

❧ ❧ ❧

On the fourth day after ‘Sado’ opened, Baek Si-on completed his twentieth theater stage greeting.

The original contract said twelve.

The extra eight were spread across several theaters in Seoul, Gyeonggi, and Incheon that had temporarily added showtimes.

After the film’s first-day results exploded, invitations from theaters around the country kept arriving at the distributor.

Some stressed local audiences’ enthusiasm, some produced sold-out shows, and some said the theater had already printed welcome banners for the crew.

With the banners printed, if the actor didn’t come, it was as if that cloth would be left wounded alone in the wind.

Baek Si-on found it hard to refuse.

He had poured far too much into ‘Sado’.

Now that the film had finally won audience recognition, he was willing to visit more theaters, and willing to personally thank the people who bought tickets.

Besides, Director Lee Joon-ik and senior Song Kang-ho were no longer young; as the young lead, running a few more shows was reasonable.

The distributor quickly tasted the sweetness.

The twenty-first was squeezed into the new schedule.

Then the twenty-second.

While the other side was still discussing whether to add a last-minute regional route for the weekend, Baek Eun-ah simply closed the file.

“No.”

The conference room fell quiet.

The promotion head tried to ease the atmosphere.

“Director Eun-ah, the calls from regional theaters really are loud. If Actor Baek could put in one more hard day, it would help the film’s follow-up box office a great deal.”

“He’s already put in eight extra shows.”

Baek Eun-ah turned the contract toward them.

“Twelve shows was the promotional obligation both sides confirmed. The following eight carried no extra fee, and no demand for the crew to adjust other resources.”

The distribution head said:

“Everyone hopes the film does better.”

“So do we.”

Baek Eun-ah’s tone stayed polite.

“But he’s about to go to America, and the next film has already entered formal preparation. Dance, piano, and character training all have clear progress requirements.”

“Continuing to add to the schedule will affect training. If it delays his departure for America as a result, who bears the liability for breach?”

No one across the table answered.

Baek Eun-ah pulled up ‘La La Land’s project schedule and related clauses, and pushed the tablet to the center of the table.

Additional investment, actor scheduling, overseas team, training plan — each followed by a specific date.

The promotion head looked down and scanned the dollar breach amount.

The regional audiences’ enthusiasm, so moving moments ago, suddenly lost its voice.

In the end, the added shows were held to a limited range, the long regional run was cancelled, and Baek Eun-ah won Baek Si-on some precious rest.

❧ ❧ ❧

The van stopped in the underground parking lot.

Baek Eun-ah pulled the door open, got in, set the contract and tablet aside, and turned to Baek Si-on, who was resting with his eyes closed.

“Wasn’t I especially cool just now?”

Baek Si-on didn’t open his eyes.

“You only turned the contract to page three.”

“But I turned to page three with good reason.”

Baek Eun-ah grew smugger as she spoke:

“I’m no longer the Baek Eun-ah who used to only relay messages, run errands, and order food for you.”

“I can face the distributor’s moral blackmail on my own, and shut them down with the American project’s breach penalty.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“You really have grown up.”

The smile on Baek Eun-ah’s face was just about to widen.

“It’s just that when you bare your teeth, you look very much like a Yorkshire terrier.”

The smile froze.

Baek Eun-ah turned her head away and, scraping together her meager vocabulary of malice, earnestly cursed this ungrateful, rotten capitalist once.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on got home, showered, and collapsed into bed.

His body finally got a stretch of time undisturbed by work.

The last time he’d slept a full eight hours already belonged to the ancient era of memory.

In the afternoon, his phone rang for a long time.

Baek Si-on reached out from the covers, found the phone, and pressed answer.

The caller ID read Los Angeles.

Damien Chazelle.

He first congratulated him on ‘Sado’s results in Korea.

The film’s first-day box office was strong, every roadshow packed, and the Korean media were casting Baek Si-on as the figurehead of “the Venice Best Actor’s return to mainstream commercial film.”

This information had already reached Los Angeles through the distributor and North American film media.

Damien was quite satisfied.

The higher the lead’s international commercial influence, the easier it’d be for ‘La La Land’s follow-up promotion to open the Asian market.

But he was equally aware that Baek Si-on’s recent schedule had almost no gaps.

He worried that 53.7 GB of training material had only served as hard-drive ballast.

Damien Chazelle → Baek Si-on · voice · the afternoon after opening day

“The basic training I sent you — how much have you completed?”

“I’ve watched all of it.”

“Watching and practicing are two different verbs.”

“I can record it for you.”

“Good. I only need to see the tap dance and the partner dance.”

“Piano — I’ll confirm in person once you’re in America.”

The call ended.

Baek Si-on stared at the darkening phone screen.

This director had successfully ruined his day off with one sentence.

Pride usually isn’t a reliable alarm clock, but at least it needs no batteries.

After a quick wash, he took out his laptop and opened the training folder.

Then he changed into workout clothes, set up his phone in the open space of the living room, and ran through the tutorial videos in full.

The rhythm was no problem.

Core strength was sufficient.

But when he got ready to record the basics of the partner dance, Baek Si-on stopped.

On screen, two dancers were performing a set of crossover steps and turns in coordination.

The lead had to guide the partner’s shift of weight.

The follower had to surrender their body to the other’s guidance without jumping the beat early.

One person obviously couldn’t do it.

Baek Si-on stared at the partner demonstration on screen and suddenly recalled something long buried under a busy schedule.

He opened the chat box and sent two characters.

KakaoTalk · Bae Joo-hyun

Baek Si-on

Homework.

❧ ❧ ❧

When the message reached Bae Joo-hyun’s phone, she was sitting in her van, admiring her own endorsement ad flashing past the window.

She looked down at her phone; Baek Si-on’s message popped up on the screen.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Bae Joo-hyun’s body went stiff.

That file.

That enormous folder named ‘La La Land Dance Basics’.

Baek Si-on had sent it to her, told her to practice the basic steps and the partner-dance intro first, and said he would check.

Of course she had practiced.

But the practice time was sliced very fine.

A stretch after comeback rehearsals, a few moves memorized while waiting in the wings of a music show.

When she had no schedule, she’d occasionally open the tutorial video — then be talked out of it by the joint efforts of bed and pillow.

Bae Joo-hyun had originally assumed Baek Si-on, busy filming, making music, and running promotion, had long forgotten the assignment.

As it turned out, the Venice Best Actor’s memory was exceptionally suited to settling old scores.

Bae Joo-hyun replied at once:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Bae Joo-hyun

I haven’t forgotten!

Baek Si-on

Send a practice video within an hour.

Bae Joo-hyun: “……”

One hour.

She looked down to check how far the car was from the company, then opened her phone calendar to see whether the practice room was free.

Finally she pinned her hope on the person beside her.

“Joy.”

“Unnie, that tone — I can tell it’s not good news.”

“Come be my dance partner.”

“What dance partner?”

Bae Joo-hyun opened the file and showed her.

The familiar footwork appeared on the screen.

Park So-young recognized it.

Bae Joo-hyun had once dragged her into practicing it in the dorm living room, and had grandly claimed it was so she could show off a talent on ‘We Got Married’ one day.

Now the truth was finally exposed.

That training had nothing to do with Yook Sung-jae; the real client was Baek Si-on.

“So you dragged me into dancing all that time — for yourself, unnie?”

“……”

“Unnie.”

“Mm.”

“Beg me.”

Bae Joo-hyun, expressionless, raised a fist.

Park So-young immediately changed her tune:

“…… Ne, unnie, I understand.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The two rushed to the SM practice room.

The phone was propped in front of the mirror.

The angle was adjusted several times.

“Unnie, does this recording need facial-expression management?” Park So-young asked.

“Just follow along. No need to smile.”

“Then can I cry?”

“…… Are you looking to die?”

The music started.

First run, Park So-young forgot her direction on the second turn.

Second run, Bae Joo-hyun jumped the beat herself.

Third run, both their feet landed on each other at the same time.

Not until the fifth run did the two stumble through one full set.

The flaws were many.

But at least every move got made.

Fifteen minutes after the video went out, the phone buzzed.

Baek Si-on’s reply unfolded like a court judgment, item by item:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · the grading

Baek Si-on

Camera angle too high, footwork unclear: minus ten.

Half a beat late on the start: minus ten.

Jumped the beat on the first turn: minus ten.

Eyes kept hunting the camera: minus ten.

Partner broke character laughing: minus ten.

Used smiling to cover a mistake: minus ten.

final line

Total score: forty. Failing.

Bae Joo-hyun held the phone, unable to believe it.

A moment ago, collapsed on the floor, she had still thought she’d done well — though the standard for “done well” rested mainly on “didn’t fall over midway.”

Park So-young leaned in, read the deduction list, and raised a hand in excitement.

“I contributed ten points!”

Bae Joo-hyun turned to her in despair.

“Those were points deducted.”

“Deductions are still a contribution.”

“There is no such contribution.”

Park So-young pressed on:

“Then how many did you contribute, unnie?”

Bae Joo-hyun re-read the list.

“At least I wasn’t the one who laughed.”

“I laughed because you turned wrong first.”

Bae Joo-hyun raised a fist again.

Park So-young immediately shut up.

The phone buzzed once more.

A new message:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · one more

Baek Si-on

Come retake it in person.

An address followed below.
