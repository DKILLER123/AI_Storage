The Opinion War Closes Over Soft Tofu

Son Nam-won hit publish at 12:17.

The piece had been sitting in draft for eleven minutes. Headline explosive, pictures clean, wording bold. Two people walking a mall underground at 11:39 p.m. A man in a black T-shirt. A woman in a cap. Distance: half a step. The caption did not say dating. The caption did not have to.

Insight · exclusive

Seoul · 13 Sep 2014 · 00:17 KST · entertainment

Midnight at Yongsan CGV — Baek Si-on, Choi Jin-ri, One Underground

Venice’s Best Actor and f(x)’s Choi Sulli, photographed leaving a late screening. Insight confirms the pair were together. Insight does not confirm what that together means.

One. At 11:39 p.m. on 12 September, Insight photographed Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri in the B2 car park of Yongsan CGV. They arrived in the same vehicle. They left in the same vehicle.

Two. No agency statement had been issued at time of publication. SM Entertainment did not return a call. Baek Si-on’s side did not return a call, because the call was not made.

Three. The film on the marquee was Lucy. Choi Min-sik is in it. That is, Insight notes, a fact, and not an alibi.

Insight reports what the shutter held. The rest is the country’s hobby.

— Insight editorial office

The first forward hit at 12:18.

The tenth at 12:19.

By 12:24 the piece was on every entertainment desk in the city, and on the lock-screens of people who did not have desks.

Son Nam-won sat with both hands on the keyboard and did not type the next sentence, because the next sentence was not his.

He waited.

At 12:31, Baek Si-on’s socials put up a post. No photograph. No emoji. Four lines, as if a man had written them with the phone on a steering wheel and had not needed a fifth.

The comments under the Insight piece, which had been climbing in a single direction, split.

Split is a kind word.

He opened a blank apology.

Typed it in the voice of a shop that had been told to kneel and had decided kneeling was, this once, a kind of standing.

The other outlets, which had been holding Park Jae-won’s tail in their teeth and waiting to see whether Insight’s piece would live, watched it die in thirty-five minutes.

Sports Chosun killed the folder.

The Chevrolet killed the folder.

Dispatch, which had not been in B2 at all, killed a draft that had never had pictures, because even a draft can become a rumour if you leave it on a desk.

By 1:10 a.m. the only photographs of that underground still circulating were screenshots of a page that no longer existed.

A rumour-storm that might have run for a week had been lit, steered, and put out inside an hour.

Insight kept the first-run traffic.

Baek Si-on kept the sentence that said he did not fold.

Choi Jin-ri kept the idol image, minus a few thousand comments that would have existed anyway, about anything, because the country is like that.

Everyone else’s cards became scrap.

Son Nam-won closed the laptop.

Park Jae-won, who had been sitting on the other desk with the badge still faintly pink on his forehead from the adhesive, said, very quietly:

“Sajangnim. Did we just get yelled at by the man who pays us?”

“We got used by him. There is a difference. Go home. Don’t sit GD tomorrow. GD can date a Japanese model without us.”

Six of the top ten. Two are the apology. One is a film. The film is not the point, and is the only point that will still be true at noon.

The opinion war did not end because anyone won it.

It ended because the object of the war had been removed from the table, and a war without an object is just people shouting at a deleted URL.

By eight, the search terms had started to slip. Not gone. Slipping. The way a fever slips when you have stopped feeding it.

Baek Si-on had slept four hours in Hapjeong. He woke to Baek Eun-ah in the doorway with two phones and the expression she wore when a number had become a person.

“Seo Eun-ju-ssi can do eleven. Seochon. The tofu house behind the hanok café, the one with the iron kettle in the window. She said she doesn’t do offices for a first meeting. I said you don’t do offices for a first anything.”

“Hyundai?”

“Hyundai, KB, SK. In that order. Samsung is still no. Lotte is still no. I have stopped highlighting Lotte. It was getting pathetic.”

He washed his face. The black T-shirt from last night was in the basket. Park Ji-hun had left a grey shirt on the chair, because Park Ji-hun had started leaving clothes on chairs as if chairs were a wardrobe and the apartment were a set.

He put the grey shirt on.

“You’re coming.”

“I am the 15 percent. Of course I am coming. Also I called her. If I don’t come she’ll think I am a child who can only send KakaoTalk.”

They took the Carnival. Park Ji-hun drove. Baek Eun-ah in the passenger seat with a folder that had three tabs and a yellow highlighter that had, in the last twenty-four hours, been put back on its lid like a corpse.

Seochon at 10:40 a.m. was the Seochon that tourists had not yet fully eaten: alleys, hanok roofs, a café with too many plants, and, behind it, a tofu house that had been there longer than any of the plants.

The sign was wood. The kettle in the window was iron. Steam on the glass.

Baek Si-on went in first.

The woman at the four-top by the window had already arrived.

Navy blazer. Ivory blouse, the collar open one button, a thin gold chain. Shoulder-length black hair, a clean centre part, gold studs small enough that a courtroom would not notice them and a camera would. She had not taken the blazer off. The tofu house was not cold. The blazer was not about temperature.

Seo Eun-ju

Counsel · Kim & Chang · entertainment contracts

OC · she doesn’t do song splits · she does the other kind of money

Mid-thirties, a navy blazer that had come from an office with a glass wall, an ivory blouse, a chain so fine it only caught when she turned her head. Jeong Han-teuk had sent the card. She does not do first meetings in offices. She does them over soft tofu, where a man has to put his phone down to use a spoon.

She stood. Not all the way into a bow. Enough to say she had been taught, and had decided how much of the teaching to keep.

“Baek Si-on-ssi. Baek Eun-ah-ssi. Seo Eun-ju. Kim and Chang. You can call me Seo-byeonhosa, or Seo Eun-ju-ssi, or, if the tofu is good, just Seo. I don’t answer to unnie. I am not that kind of lawyer.”

“Seo Eun-ju-ssi.”

Baek Si-on sat. Baek Eun-ah sat with the folder in her lap, then put the folder on the table because a lap is not a desk and she refused to pretend.

The ajumma came. No name. A face that had seen every kind of lunch. Three bowls of sundubu-jjigae, extra-soft, and a side of pajeon because Baek Eun-ah had looked at the menu the way she looked at a contract, which was: all of it, then subtract.

She doesn’t do song splits. She does cars, banks, and the sentences that keep a rumour from becoming a clause.

The ajumma did not ask who they were. The kettle already knew.

Seo Eun-ju poured the tea herself. The chain moved once and went still.

“I read the Insight piece. I read the deletion. I read your four lines. That was clean work. A morality clause in a car contract is not as clean. Hyundai’s draft arrived at 7:12 this morning. I have it. You should eat first. Then we will take the clause apart.”

Baek Si-on took a spoon of tofu. It was extra-soft. It went down without an argument. He liked food that did not argue.

“How bad?”

“Not bad. Standard, which is sometimes worse. They want a face that does not date, does not drink in public, does not crash a car, does not insult the group, and does not, and I am quoting, ‘become the subject of a scandal that materially diminishes the brand.’ Last night is already a test of that sentence. You passed it by deleting the object. If you had let the piece live, Hyundai would have used the clause to shave the number. They may still try. I will not let them.”

Baek Eun-ah opened the folder to the Hyundai tab.

The number at the bottom of the page was the number that had been on her laptop two nights ago, in a different colour, under a different house.

Not ₩525,000,000. Not the ₩500,000,000 dream. The dream had shattered on a kitchen floor and been swept into a highlighter cap. This was the number that was left, if the three that still wanted him still wanted him after a cinema and a deletion.

Seo Eun-ju looked at the Hyundai line the way a surgeon looks at a cut that is already closing.

“Hyundai is the one we close first. They have a car. The car has a name. Aslan. They want you walking it out of a hall in two days as if the car had been waiting for a Best Actor and not the other way around. ₩1.2 billion, two years, six shoot days, no competitor automobiles, and a one-off Aslan with the plate 0001 that they will call a gift in the press release and a prop in the contract. I will make them call it a gift in both. A prop can be reclaimed. A gift cannot, unless you are a very stupid kind of grateful.”

Baek Si-on ate. The pajeon had gone cold at the edges. He did not mind.

“Choi Jin-ri.”

Seo Eun-ju’s spoon stopped.

“Is not in any of these contracts. Good. Keep her out of them. Last night you did the correct thing for an idol and the correct thing for a car company, which is a rare overlap. If you date her, I do not need to know. If the country dates her for you, I need to know in the first hour, not the fifth. I don’t do song splits. I do the sentences that keep a person’s name from becoming a discount.”

Baek Eun-ah had been turning the yellow highlighter between her fingers. She set it down.

“Can you start Monday?”

“I started at 7:12 this morning. The retainer is on the second page of the folder you haven’t opened. It is not a song split. It is a number you can live with, and a number Kim and Chang can live with, which is the only overlap I care about at eleven in a tofu house.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

The blazer. The chain. The tofu, extra-soft, which she had not yet finished, because she talked in complete sentences and the spoon had to wait for the full stop.

“Welcome aboard.”

Seo Eun-ju’s mouth did a small thing that was not quite a smile and was not quite not.

“Eat. Hyundai’s hall is the day after tomorrow. You will need a lawyer who has seen the floor, and a cousin who has seen the number, and a face that can walk a car out of a building without looking like the car is walking you. I can do the first. She can do the second. The third is you. Don’t be late, and don’t post four lines about a car. Four lines are for rumours. Cars want photographs.”

The ajumma came back with more tea. She looked at the three of them, at the folder, at the blazer, at the grey shirt, and did not ask.

The kettle in the window kept steaming.

Outside, Seochon’s Saturday had started to fill with people who had not been in a B2 car park at 11:39, and who would, by Monday, have forgotten the search terms, or sworn they had never looked them up.

The opinion war was over.

The other kind of money had sat down, and picked up a spoon.
