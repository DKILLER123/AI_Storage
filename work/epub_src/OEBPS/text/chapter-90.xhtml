Chapter Ninety

New Song Legend Is Born

The same afternoon After the lecture Hapjeong-dong · Studio 401 Two million plays. Demo_v3_0908 becomes Legend_v1.

Hapjeong-dong.

Studio 401.

Baek Si-on pressed the bell.

Three seconds later the door was pulled open from inside.

The two of them bumped fists.

Jung Jae-joon’s gaze left Si-on and landed half a step behind him.

Choi Jin-ri came in from behind Si-on, inclined a little, and greeted him.

“Hello. I’m Choi Jin-ri. Sorry to disturb.”

Jae-joon looked at her once, looked at Si-on once, then the corner of his mouth picked up a teasing smile.

“Hello. Muse of the Venice Best Actor.”

Something extremely fast went across Choi Jin-ri’s face, then she turned her head very naturally and used the tail of her eye to glance at Si-on’s expression.

Si-on looked at Jae-joon.

His whole face written in question marks.

Jae-joon turned and walked into the studio, pointed at the monitor with his chin.

“Look yourself.”

Si-on walked over.

YouTube was open on the computer screen.

In the frame, a man in a suit standing on a long dining table covered in food, a microphone in his hand, and beside him another man holding a saxophone.

The video was playing.

Oh pretty baby—

Jae-joon dragged the bar back a little, froze it on the frame where that index finger went out.

Then turned his head, looked at the screen, looked at Choi Jin-ri.

“The one he’s pointing at in the video. That’s her, right?”

Si-on took his gaze back from the screen.

“You’re that free?”

Jae-joon shrugged, a face of innocence.

“I didn’t go looking. A Korean netizen in the comments @’d my account, said ‘Producer Jung Jae-joon please make a studio version of Baek Si-on singing Can’t Take My Eyes off You.’ I clicked in, and it was this video.”

He paused.

“Then I watched it.”

Paused again.

“Seven times.”

“…”

Si-on didn’t pick it up. Walked to the computer, pulled the mouse, scrolled the page down to the comments.

Comments sorted by heat.

Si-on looked at the play count in the upper right.

2,147,832.

Uploaded less than four days.

Choi Jin-ri had at some point already walked behind him, peeking at the screen over his shoulder.

When she saw the floor asking which woman—

She retreated half a step without a sound.

Pretended she had been standing at that distance the whole time.

Si-on turned his head and looked at her once.

Choi Jin-ri was studying the ceiling light with an extremely concentrated expression.

Jae-joon had seen all of it.

He very much wanted to keep asking.

But knowing the moment was a craft, and his hand at the craft was not bad.

Jae-joon reached and snatched the mouse out of Si-on’s hand, slapped YouTube shut.

The screen cut back to the DAW.

“Come listen to the work.”

He double-clicked a folder open.

The filename marked Demo_v3_0908.

He moved the mouse to the start of the timeline.

“Ready?”

“Mm.”

Play.

An electronic riff run through heavy distortion split down out of the spectrum, the tone between the grit of an electric guitar and the metal of a synth.

On the third beat, handclaps cut in.

Right after, the kick came in too.

Stomp — clap — stomp — clap —

After four bars of drum had laid the ground colour, the synth came in.

But it only walked two bars and stopped, giving the space, the kick and the claps taking the lead again.

The chorus section.

Every element stacked in at once.

Kick, claps, the metal shatter of the hi-hat, the synth riff, a bass line buried at the very bottom.

The spectrum from low to high stuffed full.

One minute twenty of arrangement demo walked to the end.

The after-sound gone.

Jae-joon turned his head.

“How is it?”

Si-on stared at those flower-coloured track waveforms on the screen a few seconds.

“Play it again.”

Jae-joon didn’t ask why. Dragged the bar back to the start, hit play once more.

The second time.

Si-on closed his eyes.

Not looking at the waveforms. Only listening.

The second time ended.

“Again.”

The third time.

Si-on’s lips started to move.

No sound at all.

The mouth-shape was opening and closing along some internal rhythm.

He was filling lyrics.

If you were going to talk “writing history” and “lasting a thousand years.”

Then the protagonist, made concrete, had to hook to the utmost competitive honour.

It could not be me.

It had to be someone else.

The first person he thought of was the man across the ocean who had carved obsession into bone.

Last season the Achilles had snapped. The whole world had thought he was finished.

ESPN’s annual player ranking had thrown him from first in the league at his peak down to fortieth.

Forty.

One kick off the altar into the mud.

But he had not stepped back.

A gym at four in the morning, no audience, no flashes, no commentator’s voice, only the dull of a basketball hitting the floor and the continuous pain coming from the Achilles.

That was the highest-grade industrial seasoning.

“One more time.”

The fourth time.

Si-on felt over a water-based pen on the table, pulled a blank A4 sheet.

The tip pressed hard on the paper.

Following the extremely oppressive mid-low kick, he wrote down, line by line.

we, not I · first sheet

Go, go

It is time to go

Injury cannot stop me

Hardship cannot stop me

Tread the darkest mockery into a stair

Boil broken regret into a miracle

Fevered dreamers hunger for success

Fevered explorers chase the name

Never stop

Until we become legend

The private goods slotted in without a seam.

Listeners over the whole world would feel this was a blood anthem written for athletes, for people chasing a dream, for every bastard who refused to lose.

Only he himself knew: the first version of these lyrics, the subject was “I.”

A love letter a twenty-two-year-old man had written to himself under a first-class reading light.

Si-on passed the finished lyrics to Jae-joon.

He took them, gaze scanning from the first line to the last, then played the demo once more, matching the beat.

“It works.”

Jae-joon set the A4 beside the keyboard, looked up at Si-on.

“Try a pass?”

Si-on nodded once and walked into the booth.

Jae-joon went back to the board, put on the monitors, pulled a new track out on the DAW.

“Eight empty for you. Come in on the claps.”

Si-on signed an OK.

The demo intro came through the cans.

Kick first.

Then the claps.

Stomp — clap — stomp — clap —

Eight empty done.

Si-on opened his mouth.

Sound from in front of the mic down the line to the board, then out of Jae-joon’s monitors.

The first pass was only finding the feel of the language, not adding much detail.

Walked to the end.

Jae-joon stared at the new-recorded waveform on the screen a few seconds, pressed talkback.

“Try swapping the whole lyric into an English version.”

“Why?”

“The ground colour of this kick and this arrangement is stadium-rock DNA, Europe and America. When Korean goes in, there’s a gap between the syllable count and the bite on the beat. Swap to English, the spread of vowels and consonants will sit on the beat better.”

Si-on nodded once.

Felt a pen and a blank A4 off the headphone hook, leaned on the booth wall, and started to write again.

Converting the English lyric was not a straight translation.

The rhythm was different, the breath-places different, the weight of each syllable falling on the beat different too.

Wrote about seven or eight minutes.

Stood in front of the mic again.

The second pass.

English version.

Walked to the end.

Jae-joon took the monitors off his neck, set them on the board, was silent a few seconds.

Shook his head.

“A little short on feel.”

“Where?”

“Technically not bad… but too clean. What you sang is like a sober person describing madness, and he himself is not mad.”

Jae-joon leaned back, both hands behind his head, thought.

“Try drinking a little? That feel you had singing Can’t Take My Eyes off You was pretty good.”

Si-on looked at him through the acrylic.

“I drove here.”

“Let your little errand-runner haul you back.”

Jae-joon looked around.

“Speaking of that, where’s Eun-ah? Usually isn’t she the one running in front and behind you? How come I haven’t seen her today?”

“She’s talking projects in the billions.”

“…What?”

Jae-joon’s hands came down off the back of his head.

“Endorsement contracts. KB Bank, Hyundai Motor, SK Telecom.”

Jae-joon was silent a long time.

Decided to kill this topic that was extremely injurious to his self-respect.

“Fine. Come back tomorrow.”

“All right.”

Si-on came out of the booth, hung the cans back on the rack.

Jae-joon leaning in the chair, both hands folded behind his head, staring at the ceiling a few seconds.

“What do we call it?”

Si-on thought.

What was the core of the lyric?

Immortal.

“Call it Legend.”

“Legend?”

“Right.”

“Fine.”

Jae-joon tapped a few times on the DAW project name, changed Demo_v3_0908 to Legend_v1.

Then turned the chair around, leaned on the back, stretched.

“One more problem you have to think about.”

“What?”

Si-on looked at him.

“The MV?”

“Right.”

Jae-joon folded both hands behind his head again.

“Flip Melon’s Top 100 from head to tail and you won’t turn up a single proper stadium rock, so this song’s positioning from the start is not for Korean listeners. It’s for overseas.”

“But to go out — whether you hit Billboard or Spotify’s global chart — YouTube you cannot go around.”

“Especially a song in this style. Visual and audio have to explode at the same time. Just a good song sitting there, without an MV with enough visual impact holding it up, you cannot open a situation on YouTube at all.”

Si-on leaned on the booth doorframe, didn’t speak.

The 2014 Billboard Hot 100 scoring rule was not: you sell a lot, you chart.

It was a multi-weight compound algorithm.

Put plainly.

The reason PSY’s Gangnam Style two years ago could punch into the Billboard Hot 100 and peak at number two was that Billboard had, in 2012, just formally taken YouTube plays into the Hot 100 scoring system.

Gangnam Style

PSY

YouTube plays counted from 2012. First video to break a billion.

The Gangnam Style MV’s plays on YouTube that year were the first video in human history to break a billion.

That astronomical streaming score had shoved it, hard, from a “Korean comedy god-song” into Billboard’s core chart.

In other words, what PSY had stepped on was not a music wind-mouth. It was an algorithm wind-mouth.

The situation Si-on faced now had one key similarity with PSY two years ago:

No US domestic fan base, no radio connections, no major-label promo resource.

The only card that could hit was YouTube.

And to manufacture explosive plays on YouTube, a good song alone was not enough. You had to have an MV visually explosive enough.

The kind that made a person want to share it after one watch, and after sharing want to watch it again.

“You’re right.”

Si-on took his hand off the doorframe.

“I’ll think about the MV when I go back.”

“Mm.”

Jae-joon nodded, then as if he’d remembered something.

“Right.”

“Mm?”

“That Can’t Take My Eyes Off You cover. Do we do it?”

Jae-joon both hands on his knees, body tipping forward.

“If we’re doing a cover, I suggest not dropping one song alone. Just gather a few songs and make a mini album. Legend at the head as the title. The cover at the tail. In the middle stuff two or three other styles, pad the volume out.”

He counted on his fingers.

“Five to six songs, one mini album. New songs, a cover, tries at different styles. Presence way stronger than dropping one or two. When it goes out, every overseas digital platform — Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music — lay it all at once.”

Si-on’s gaze left Jae-joon and landed on the A4 with the lyrics on the worktable.

Thought.

“Finish this one first.”

Jae-joon looked at him.

“The album, wait until this song’s MV plan is set, then consider. One step at a time.”

“Fine.”

Jae-joon didn’t say more.

He had worked with Si-on a few months and had already felt out this person’s working logic.

Si-on straightened off the doorframe and walked toward the studio door.

Halfway.

Looked back at Choi Jin-ri.

On her knees a copy of Sound & Recording she had turned up from somewhere, opened to some middle page, head down, reading seriously.

“We’re going.”

“…Oh.”

Choi Jin-ri closed the magazine, stood from the folding chair, put it back on the top of the original stack.

“Thank you for letting me sit in.”

She inclined a little toward Jae-joon.

Jae-joon didn’t turn his head, lifted one hand and waved twice in the air.

“Next time you come, bring something to eat. All I have here is fried chicken. Wronged you.”

Choi Jin-ri smiled once and followed Si-on out.

The door closed.

Jae-joon leaned in the chair, picked up the lyric A4 Si-on had left on the table, folded it twice, clipped it into the pen cup beside the keyboard.

Then opened YouTube, and watched that two-million-play Venice dinner video one more time.
