Chapter One Hundred

A North American Partner? No. A North American Split

Los Angeles After LAX Beverly Hills · two floors of glass A diamond Gangnam Style. Eighteen. I want you. Two years, not three. Oppa is a bastard.

The meeting was in an office building near Beverly Hills. Two floors, glass to the ground, a few black SUVs outside, a plant at reception trimmed extremely regular. Typical L.A. entertainment office. Looking loose. Every square metre actually burning money.

A row of gold frames of record certs in the corridor. Baek Si-on scanned left to right. Justin Bieber, Ariana Grande, Carly Rae Jepsen, Psy. PSY’s was a diamond for Gangnam Style. Baek Eun-ah saw that Psy frame too, but she had no time to feel it. Because the door at the end of the corridor was already open.

Scooter Braun standing in it. A little more built than video and press stills. No jacket, white shirt sleeves to the forearm, the watch on the wrist not looking cheap. He came in first with a solid handshake.

Scooter Braun

Manager · SB Projects · Justin, Ariana, a map that is not a song-split

Real · Beverly Hills, a table, twenty percent for two years

He does not want one song’s twenty. He wants the person. Asia stays with the cousin. North America stays with him. The cake, he says, gets larger.

“Finally meeting you.”

“Same.”

“Twenty-two, right? Venice Best Actor? And you write songs? Are all you Koreans this complete?”

“No. Most Koreans only do one thing. I’m more special.”

Scooter laughed. Si-on turned, introduced Eun-ah and Seo Eun-ju one by one. Scooter shook each, said to Eun-ah, “You look way too young for the job.” Eun-ah, in her most standard business English, “Thank you. I’m eighteen.” Scooter’s brows went straight to the hairline.

“Eighteen.”

“Yes.”

Scooter looked at Si-on once. Si-on pretended not to read that look. They sat. Coffee, ice water, sparkling, up quickly. Scooter did not go around, cut in.

“You came specially to ask about What Do You Mean? dropping?”

“No.”

Si-on lifted the coffee, drank.

“But since you brought it, I’m a little curious why it dragged this long.”

Scooter did not hide it, explained simply. Bieber’s this return had been defined inside as “the most critical restart of the career.” They had not only asked Si-on for a song. Also in contact: Skrillex, Diplo, Ed Sheeran, and a few hot independent producers in North America. The problem was “too many candidates.” What Do You Mean? was high quality; Scooter personally liked it a lot. But a lead-single choice was not only the song. Also return rhythm, market window, image-rebuild direction, and the narrative order of the whole album after. That process could not be fast.

Done explaining, Scooter pulled the subject back.

“Then what are you in L.A. for?”

“Shooting an MV.”

Scooter clearly interested.

“You have a new song?”

“Mm.”

“Come on. Let me hear it.”

Si-on did not make a show of it, put the phone straight on a Bluetooth speaker. Played Legend. Intro out, Scooter’s face changed a little. Not surprise at “good or not.” Surprise that this and Way Back Home were completely not one system. Way Back Home was good to hear, pop, easy in the ear. Legend was clearly harsher. Finish higher, ambition larger. Arrangement logic aimed at a large frame, large spread, large weather-resonance — one ear could hear it. After listening Scooter leaned on the sofa back, fingers tapping the arm twice.

“This doesn’t sound like your last one.”

“People have to move.”

Scooter laughed.

“Only this one?”

“No.”

Si-on said it as it was.

“I’m thinking of a mini, but the songs aren’t fully picked. There’s an ad song that just got a demo. You can hear that too.”

Done, he put on Way Down We Go, the Hyundai ad demo. This one finished, the looseness on Scooter was gone. Because in his head it was no longer a simple “song’s not bad.” He was re-evaluating Baek Si-on. Way Back Home was warm, pop, easy-ear electronic. Legend was hard, burning, stadium rock. Way Down We Go was dark, heavy, blues full of pressure. Three songs, three completely different music DNAs. In Scooter’s experience, an artist taking one style to the extreme was already remarkable. Crossing two, rare as phoenix feathers. Switching freely among three utterly different styles, and none of them looking like a “hard pose” — living, probably not more than two hands. Then, that face. Si-on’s feature-hardness enough to hold the Western system’s basic ask on “male weather,” the overall proportion and line still keeping that East Asian face’s particular fine and held. In a Hollywood casting director’s words, a universal face. A face that would not produce wrongness in any cultural context. Stack a Venice Best Actor title. Twenty-two. Scooter had run Psy. Put plainly — Psy was a “hit.” Si-on had a chance to become an “IP.” And what Scooter liked most had never been a hit, but a long asset that could snowball. Thinking to here, he already had an answer inside. Take him. Best start now. On the face, Scooter did not move a hair. Only lifted coffee, drank. Put that loose look back on, looking at Si-on.

“So. What do you need me to do?”

“North American drop and push for the new song.”

“Terms.”

“I give you twenty percent of this new song, for your distribution and push.”

Scooter heard Si-on’s number, did not nod, did not shake. He went back on the chair, two fingertips to fingertips on his chest.

“I don’t want your split.”

Si-on’s brow moved a little. First thought — too little. Twenty percent, for a company at SB Projects’ level, was indeed not much. Any artist in their hand had a North American split higher. Si-on was running numbers fast. Raise to twenty-five. Or thirty. Thirty, and Jung Jae-joon’s producer split would have to be pressed down…

“I want you.”

Si-on’s counting stopped. He looked at Scooter Braun. Scooter looked at him. Face serious. Not joking. Si-on: “…” He opened his mouth, face empty.

“Sorry, Mr. Braun. I don’t like men.”

Scooter froze first. Then a burst of laughter. Eun-ah beside them originally still tense, then the mouth starting to shake, finally could not hold it, laughed out. Seo Eun-ju’s professional overlay let her hold two more seconds, but in the end she was also behind a hand, shoulders shaking twice. Scooter laughed enough, put a hand up in surrender.

“Alright, alright. You’re more interesting than you look.”

He put the laugh away. Sat forward. Tone changed.

“I mean, Mr. Baek. Twenty percent of one song, to me, has no meaning.”

He opened both hands.

“But a long, strategic relationship covering music and film — that has meaning.”

“You need someone on the North American ground with resource, channel, experience, to help you.”

Scooter pointed at himself.

“And that person should be me.”

Si-on turned and looked at Eun-ah once. This kid already had the unhappiness written on her face. Normal. Anyone having their rice bowl taken, being happy would be the strange thing. Scooter had clearly also noticed. He turned to Eun-ah, blinked on purpose.

“Hey, sweetheart. Don’t glare at me.”

“You take Korea and Asia. I take North America. A split of work. Not a replacement.”

Eun-ah’s look did not leave his face. The glare’s force dropped about thirty percent. Si-on looked at Scooter.

“If it’s long, then it’s not song by song.”

Scooter’s look back on Si-on.

“That’s it.”

He sat a little more forward, fingertips together into a triangle.

“Now we can actually talk.”

Si-on did not go around.

“How much split do you mean to take?”

Scooter lifted his right hand, two fingers.

“Twenty percent.”

Eun-ah’s brow went tight at once. Twenty percent. Five percent higher than hers. Scooter saw that face, smiled, added.

“Up front I put in more. Channel, push, press, platform, North American business doors — these are not free. Taking a little more is reasonable.”

“Of course this rate will not stay forever. As your name and reach in North America get higher, my use will drop, and the rate will drop with it.”

“Fifteen.”

“Ten.”

“In the end maybe lower.”

He said it naturally. Not like cheating anyone. Just stating a growth model he thought fully fit industry logic.

“Years.” Si-on asked.

Scooter did not even think.

“First three years, twenty.”

Si-on shook his head.

“Two.”

Scooter in his head ran a P&L at speed. If Si-on could actually stand in North America inside two years. Then from year three, even down to fifteen, the absolute would instead be higher than the first two years’ twenty. Because the cake got larger.

“Deal.”

He put a hand out, decisive. Si-on took it. After letting go Scooter turned at once to Seo Eun-ju.

“Counsel. Work time.”

He tipped his head toward a small meeting room beside.

“With me.”

Seo Eun-ju nodded, stood, followed Scooter into the next small room, and began the first round of pull on the framework’s detail clauses. Si-on stayed. Turned and looked at Eun-ah once. This kid’s face very mixed. Unhappiness at having territory taken, shock at Scooter’s way of running, and a little “I really seem to need to learn more” clarity. Si-on put a hand out, rubbed her head. In Korean:

“You have two years to learn the rules of the Western entertainment circle.”

Eun-ah under his palm turned her head. Blinked.

“Oppa. You mean…”

“After two years Scooter’s rate drops. How far it drops depends on whether our own North American ops can take over then.”

“If you can independently handle North American drop, push, and brand — even only a part — our dependence on Scooter drops.”

Two years. A two-year window. From an eighteen-year-old Korean girl who could not even read an English contract that smoothly, to a manager who could independently deal with American music-industry people in an L.A. office. Was this ask reasonable. No. A normal person would not learn this in two years. But Eun-ah had never been asked by Si-on as a “normal person.”

“Fighting.”

Si-on said it, took the hand off her head. Then looked down at his own palm. Quietly wiped it twice on the shoulder of her clothes.

“…”

Eun-ah looked down at the two wipe-marks on her shoulder. Then up at that hand going back.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“Are you saying my head is oily?”

“Mm.”

“I washed this morning! It’s L.A. sun!”

“Mm.”

Eun-ah glared at him once. After glaring, head down, a new line in Notes.

Notes · Baek Eun-ah

Study plan: Western entertainment rules. Term: two years.

Oppa is a bastard!
