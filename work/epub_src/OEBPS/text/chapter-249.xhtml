Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Nine

Welcome to La La Land

Los Angeles International Airport

California sun through the terminal glass

The whole world several shades brighter than Incheon

Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah pushed their luggage out of the arrivals lane.

California sunlight poured in through the terminal’s floor-to-ceiling windows; the whole world seemed several shades brighter than Incheon.

Scooter was already waiting outside the exit.

The moment he saw Baek Si-on, he strode over, arms open, and gave a standard full American bear hug.

“Welcome back!”

Baek Si-on, squeezed, stepped back half a step.

“Every time you see me it’s like three years apart.”

“Distance makes the heart grow fonder.”

“We last met in June.”

“For you it may be June; for me it’s already the last century.”

Scooter released him, then turned to Baek Eun-ah beside him:

“Good work, sweetheart — was the flight smooth?”

Baek Eun-ah was already used to being called sweetheart.

When they first met last year she would still blush; now she only nodded calmly.

“Thank you, everything went smoothly.”

The group got into the car.

The destination was a large rehearsal warehouse the ‘La La Land’ crew had rented in Los Angeles.

❧ ❧ ❧

Once the car was on the freeway, Scooter leaned into the seat and opened in a deliberately mysterious tone:

“By the way, Justin asked me to pass a gift along to you.”

“What gift?”

“You’ll know when we reach the crew.”

Scooter spread his hands, refusing to reveal it early.

Baek Si-on didn’t press.

His mind, though, had already started running on its own.

The last time Bieber gave him something was at a dinner, when he casually handed over ‘Love Yourself’, written by Ed Sheeran, saying “it’s just gathering dust at my place.”

That song later helped Baek Si-on and Lee Ji-eun shoot an MV that Choi Jin-ri still couldn’t let go of.

Combining the known data.

Baek Si-on quickly ran through several possibilities in his head.

The possibilities, ranked in his head

An English love letter even more absurd than the last social-media essay?

A gratitude single recorded by Bieber’s own hand?

A custom big gold chain?

Baek Si-on touched the four-leaf clover Choi Jin-ri had hung around his neck.

Whatever it was, as long as it wasn’t a second necklace.

There was no longer room on his neck.

Baek Eun-ah noticed her cousin’s pensive expression and asked:

“What are you thinking about?”

“Praying.”

“Praying for what?”

“Praying Bieber’s taste has improved.”

Scooter let out a laugh.

“Relax, this gift is very practical.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

“Bieber” and “practical” appearing in the same sentence.

His unease only deepened.

❧ ❧ ❧

After the laughter, Scooter quickly withdrew the expression and entered work mode.

“While you’re shooting the film, the new album’s direction has to be set in advance.”

“Mm.”

“The original plan was this —”

Scooter opened the schedule on his tablet.

“After ‘La La Land’ wraps, attend the LOL S5 Worlds final, then launch the second world tour of the second half of the year.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“The back half of the first tour was basically filled to two hours with covers, audience interaction, and guests.”

Baek Si-on admitted:

“That time really was a bit rushed.”

“So this time won’t do.”

Scooter’s voice turned serious.

“The second tour has to have enough new songs to support it.”

“The audience paid. What they want to hear is something only the live show can give — it can’t rely on covers every time.”

The line made a lot of sense.

The first tour had already proven Baek Si-on had enough stage pull.

But the larger the tour scale, the higher the audience’s expectation of original content.

A run of over thirty shows, if it still relied on old songs, covers, and improvised interaction, could easily turn its back half into an expensive nostalgia event.

Scooter first helped him sort through the inventory he already had but hadn’t formally released.

The unreleased inventory · Scooter runs through it

“Starboy.”

The limited-track Jung Jae-joon wrote for the Doha show.

The melody was highly finished and the live reaction excellent, but so far there was only a performance version — it had never entered a studio.

“One Call Away.”

A song about a long-distance relationship, written by Charlie Puth.

Sung at the LA New Year’s show and in several cities — likewise only a live version.

“If these two are formally recorded and released, plus two or three brand-new songs, a mini-album is no problem.”

“With tour guests, the set list can hold the whole show.”

Baek Si-on listened to Scooter’s analysis, fingers tapping lightly on his knee.

Suddenly he stopped.

He took out his phone and scrolled a while.

Opened an audio file.

Pressed play.

♪

‘Perfect’ · the demo

Ed Sheeran · guitar, in the car

A guitar melody rang through the car.

Warm.

Simple.

Carrying a highly recognizable British songwriting style.

the demo · the opening lines

“I found a love, for me…”

“Darling, just dive right in and follow my lead…”

‘Perfect’.

Ed Sheeran’s demo.

Baek Si-on had shamelessly asked Ed for the song back then, and on Choi Jin-ri’s birthday had personally played and sung it for her.

That night, the relationship between the two had also crossed the last line.

Later many things were covered over by work and crowds, but the melody of this song had always stayed in Baek Si-on’s private memory.

Hearing it again now, Baek Si-on still felt it was a complete enough love song.

Baek Si-on played about half a minute, then signaled to Scooter.

“How’s this one?”

Scooter tilted his head and listened to a few lines.

Then turned back, his expression a little surprised.

“Ed’s song?”

Baek Si-on was mildly surprised.

“How did you tell?”

Scooter put on a “you’re insulting me” expression.

“I’ve heard more demos than you’ve had meals.”

“That guitar-chord progression of Ed’s, and that Anglo-Saxon streak of romance.”

“I can identify it within three lines.”

Baek Si-on nodded, giving him face.

“Impressive.”

Scooter got even more fired up.

“Do you know how many demos I’d already heard before I signed Bieber?”

“How many?”

“Over fifty thousand.”

Baek Si-on answered:

“So forty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine of them were no good?”

Scooter’s expression stiffened for a moment.

“That’s not the point.”

Laughter scattered through the car.

The palm trees on both sides of the California freeway rushed backward.

After Scooter’s laughter fell, he suddenly went quiet.

He tilted his head, sizing Baek Si-on up again.

I found a love, for me.

I found a love that belongs to me.

Scooter’s mind raced.

This song wasn’t written by Baek Si-on himself.

But Baek Si-on choosing it meant he resonated with the lyrics.

What kind of person digs out a love song on his own and asks his manager “how’s this one”?

Only love or life-and-death can truly change a person.

Baek Si-on clearly wasn’t near death.

Then only love was left.

Several faces flashed through Scooter’s mind.

The Korean idol he’d spent millions founding a brand for, and personally chosen as its endorser?

Or the Korean singer who kissed him senseless in the ‘Love Yourself’ MV?

Or the Korean actor who’d flown with him to Canada to see the aurora?

Every one was a suspect.

Every one was outside America.

Scooter weighed the handling plan for about three seconds.

Since it wasn’t someone on the American side, it didn’t fall under a North American manager’s business jurisdiction.

Korean matters — let the Korean manager worry about them.

Scooter withdrew his scrutinizing gaze and picked up the tablet again.

“If you’re sure about ‘Perfect’, plus ‘One Call Away’.”

“Then this mini-album’s style is basically set.”

“Ballad-leaning.”

“Both songs are about love and companionship.”

“Then ‘Starboy’ doesn’t quite fit.”

“The electronic feel and club atmosphere don’t match a ballad album.”

Baek Si-on agreed too.

“I plan to rework ‘Starboy’ and use it later as a Genesis ad song.”

Scooter looked up.

“Which brand?”

“The new luxury brand Hyundai is preparing to spin off.”

“Oh.”

Scooter made a note on the tablet and kept pushing forward.

“Once the ballad style is set, we still need three or four more songs.”

“You can write them yourself, or find them outside.”

“But I have a suggestion.”

“Say it.”

“Do a duet.”

Scooter said it very directly.

“Ariana or Bieber — SB Projects’ internal synergy maxed out at once.”

Baek Si-on thought it over.

“Sure.”

With the business settled, Baek Si-on put his sunglasses back on and leaned into the seat to rest with his eyes closed.

Scooter looked at his profile and wanted to ask:

When exactly did you start dating?

Does the other person know your relationship isn’t so simple?

But in the end he asked nothing.

Because in a manager’s career, an artist’s private life only needs intervention when it affects commercial judgment.

If it made the artist’s state better, then don’t touch it.

Baek Si-on’s state now really was better than last year.

More relaxed.

More human.

Even after a thirteen-hour intercontinental flight, his complexion looked better than before.

❧ ❧ ❧

The van stopped outside a large converted warehouse in Los Angeles.

The building’s exterior was painted grey, the iron roller door half open; from inside came faint music and the sound of soles scuffing the floor.

Damien Chazelle stood at the entrance.

T-shirt, jeans, sneakers.

If not for the two crew members holding shooting schedules beside him, he looked more like a graduate student here to apply for an internship than a young director who had just landed a Hollywood A-list musical project.

Baek Si-on got out of the car.

Damien came over and reached out to shake hands.

“Welcome back to Los Angeles.”

“And welcome to officially becoming Sebastian.”

The warmth was genuine.

Baek Si-on had not only added ten million dollars in investment, but agreed to act for the Screen Actors Guild minimum salary.

These two conditions were the key push that let the film smoothly enter preparation.

To Damien, Baek Si-on’s arrival meant the project’s most core puzzle piece had finally fallen into place.

Baek Si-on squeezed back once:

“Sounds like you’ve been waiting impatiently.”

Damien admitted it frankly:

“Emma has trained for over three months.”

“She dances, practices singing, and blocks scenes every day now.”

“I’ve nearly trained her into a professional dancer.”

The smile left Baek Si-on’s face.

Over three months.

His practice in Korea had focused mainly on tap basics and the waltz frame, most of his time eaten by ‘The Pig, the Snake and the Pigeon’ filming, the ‘Sado’ roadshow, and the Worlds theme song.

That partner-dance homework with Bae Joo-hyun had let him find the feel of a partner, but compared with a professional actor who had trained three months straight, his dance reserve was clearly not enough.

Damien was still going over the upcoming rehearsal schedule; Baek Si-on had already begun silently calculating how many days he had left to cram in intensive practice.

As he talked, Damien’s attention slowly drifted from Baek Si-on’s face to the top of his head.

Then he forcibly looked away.

After a moment, he still couldn’t help glancing again.

Baek Si-on’s current hairstyle was in its hardest stage to handle.

It had been nearly three months since he shaved his head for ‘The Pig, the Snake and the Pigeon’; his hair was now too short for complex styling, yet too long to keep a buzz cut’s crispness.

Fashion magazines would call this state “natural growth texture.”

Stylists would call this state “a human disaster.”

The character Sebastian needed slightly long, somewhat down-on-his-luck hair — the romance of a jazz pianist, plus the dishevelment of someone long unable to pay his bills.

Baek Si-on’s current head looked more like an ex-con just out of prison who had been trying to reintegrate into society for two months.

Damien made a mental note: later, meet with the art director and hair team to discuss whether to extend his hair, use a wig piece, or just cut it shorter and start from another styling direction.

Whatever they chose, this head could not appear on screen in its current state.

❧ ❧ ❧

The group walked into the warehouse interior.

Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah immediately felt an unusually warm atmosphere.

Crew members kept greeting Baek Si-on on their own initiative.

“Welcome to the crew.”

“Finally meeting you.”

“Thank you for the gift.”

The first two were normal.

The third made Baek Si-on slow his steps.

Going further in, similar thanks grew more frequent.

Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah exchanged a glance; neither remembered arranging this.

Baek Eun-ah lowered her voice:

“Cousin, when did you send gifts?”

“I thought it was you.”

Scooter finally revealed the answer:

“Justin sent a gift to every crew member.”

“The gifts are theirs; the thanks goes on your account.”

Baek Si-on turned around.

“Out of his own pocket?”

Scooter nodded.

“‘What Do You Mean?’ was his first number-one in life.”

“You rejected his public confession; he decided to learn to do something practical.”

A gift couldn’t resolve professional doubts, but it could first remove some of the resistance.

Baek Si-on had made the crew adjust the start date, and entered the project as both investor and lead; even if the crew didn’t say it aloud, privately they couldn’t help muttering.

Now everyone had received a gift suited to their role’s needs.

The amount wasn’t enough to constitute bribery, but the sentiment was clear enough.

The social rule that a gift obliges the receiver is internationally universal.

Baek Si-on glanced back at the crew members smiling at him down the corridor, still finding it suspicious:

“This isn’t like something Justin would do.”

Scooter admitted:

“I suspected that too.”

“Later I found someone recently taught him not to put all his gratitude on social media.”

Baek Si-on asked:

“Did he get back together with Selena?”

“No, it’s Hailey.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

That made sense.

To make Bieber upgrade from long gratitude posts to paying out of pocket to gift others, there had to be someone more mature than him standing behind.

Selena most likely wouldn’t suggest he do this.

Hailey, though, might.

❧ ❧ ❧

From the end of the corridor came the crisp sound of tap shoes striking the floor.

The rhythm was tight.

Tap-tap-tap-tap.

Before Baek Si-on’s group reached the rehearsal room door, the sound inside stopped.

The door was pushed open.

Emma Stone walked out.

She wore dance practice clothes, the loose hair at her forehead damp with sweat, her skin flushed from exertion, her breathing not yet fully settled.

“Baek!”

The first time Emma noticed Baek Si-on was at the Venice Film Festival closing ceremony.

An Asian young man had taken the Volpi Cup for Best Actor, and that night, at the closing party, had been egged on into standing on a table to sing.

The image was very abstract.

And very memorable.

Later, this person began appearing on the Billboard charts.

His debut album went platinum.

A North American tour.

TIME’s 100 most influential people.

Now, the historical film he headlined had drawn nearly four million viewers in its first week, sweeping Korea’s summer box office.

An actor who could take both art-house and commercial film — in the whole industry, you could count them on your fingers.

And her male lead happened to be exactly that tier.

Emma walked toward Baek Si-on with genuine excitement.

Arms open.

The standard American hug, winding up.

Baek Si-on shifted his body slightly to one side.

Before Emma’s arms could close, he extended his right hand and took the palm she still held suspended in the air.

By the time Emma registered it, the hug had been cut off on the spot into a handshake.

“Very glad to meet you again.”

Emma: “…… Hi.”

The handshake ended in awkwardness.

Emma withdrew her other hand, still hanging in the air, and set it on her own waist, her expression swinging between awkward and confused.

Damien quickly stepped forward and patted Baek Si-on’s shoulder.

“Welcome ceremony over.”

He gestured toward the inner side of the rehearsal warehouse.

“Sebastian, your piano teacher has been waiting forty minutes.”

Baek Si-on turned that way.

“I just got off the plane.”

Damien answered with perfect self-justification:

“That’s why I arranged work you can do sitting down.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Investing ten million dollars hadn’t won him the privilege of adjusting to the time zone.

It only gave the director a fuller budget with which to arrange more teachers to torment him.

A crew member came to guide Baek Si-on to the piano area.

Damien, meanwhile, herded Emma back into the rehearsal room.

❧ ❧ ❧

After confirming Baek Si-on had entered the piano lesson, Scooter began settling the entourage’s lodging.

Before leaving, he said one thing to Baek Eun-ah:

“If he cuts off the teacher’s hug again in the piano lesson, tell me.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

The speed at which this joke spread through the crew would most likely beat any new song.

All afternoon, Baek Si-on was in the warehouse piano room, grinding basics with the teacher.

Sebastian had to present, on screen, the feel of “having played piano for many years.”

Jazz piano demanded not only accurate chords but improvised ornament, rhythmic variation, and a relaxation in the fingertips.

Baek Si-on had basic playing ability.

But the infectiousness of live performance and fingers that hold up to a close-up were two separate sets of demands.

He followed the piano teacher starting from the most basic jazz chord substitutions.

Practiced over and over.

Repeated and repeated.

His fingers ached the first round; rest five minutes, continue.

Ached the second round; switch to a new chord progression, continue.

Ached the third round; the teacher suggested wrapping up.

Baek Si-on glanced at the time.

He had practiced from afternoon straight into evening.

His fingers trembled faintly on his knees.

Then off work.

❧ ❧ ❧

The hotel room.

Baek Si-on showered, changed into a clean T-shirt, and leaned against the headboard.

The phone screen lit up.

A video-call request.

He answered.

Choi Jin-ri’s face appeared on the screen, her hair loosely tied, as if she’d just come back from the practice room.

Video call · Choi Jin-ri

Los Angeles ↔ Seoul · his late night, her evening

“Arrived?”

“Arrived in the morning.”

“How was the first day?”

“Learning piano; my hands are still shaking.”

Baek Si-on held his right hand up to the camera.

Spread open, the fingers did have a slight tremor.

Choi Jin-ri leaned close to the screen, studying it seriously.

“Hold it out.”

“Already holding it out.”

“A little closer.”

Baek Si-on brought his right hand a little nearer the camera.

Choi Jin-ri blew lightly at the lens twice.

“Fuu—”

“Fuu—”

Having blown, she asked, satisfied:

“Better?”

Baek Si-on flexed his fingers.

“Trans-Pacific remote treatment — limited effect.”

“I blew on it for you.”

“The main problem is probably network latency.”

Choi Jin-ri glared at him, displeased.

“Can’t you just say it’s a little better?”

“A little better.”

“Too perfunctory.”

Choi Jin-ri pouted, then quickly returned to normal.

She asked what he’d eaten — he said the crew’s boxed meal; how the hotel was — he said it was fine as long as he could sleep; whether he’d met any new colleagues — he said he’d gotten to know the piano teacher.

What Choi Jin-ri really wanted to ask about was Emma.

Had he met her?

How were they getting along?

But the question had reached her lips, and she wanted to wait for Baek Si-on to bring it up himself.

But Baek Si-on didn’t bring it up either.

The two, separated by the Pacific, in this “if you don’t ask, I won’t tell” way, silently skipped over the handshake event of the day.

While the video call was still going, the door knocked.

Baek Si-on called toward the door:

“Who is it?”

A woman’s voice came from outside.

“Emma.”

Choi Jin-ri heard it clearly through the phone speaker.

“This late?”

Baek Si-on, phone in hand, walked to the door and pulled it open a crack.

Emma had already changed into clothes easy to train in, the crew-issued dance score in her hand.

“Hi, the rehearsal hall downstairs is still open — I wanted to ask if you want to come practice the partner dance.”

Baek Si-on asked:

“You’re sure it’s only dance practice?”

Emma started, then, remembering the hug cut off that day, knitted her brows and waved the dance score twice in front of him.

“Of course it’s dance practice! What did you think?”

“I also thought it was dance practice.”

“See you in ten minutes.”

With that, she turned and left.

Her footsteps receded quickly down the corridor.

Baek Si-on closed the door and raised the phone again.

Choi Jin-ri’s face was still on the screen.

Her expression normal.

At least on the surface.

“I’ll change and go practice a while.”

“Okay, go.”

The answer was brisk.

Baek Si-on confirmed once more.

“You’re really letting me go?”

“You’re shooting a film — aren’t you going to practice dance?”

“Fair.”

Choi Jin-ri kissed the screen.

“The necklace will keep watch over you.”

Baek Si-on looked down at the four-leaf clover.

“Got it.”

The video hung up.

The screen went dark.

Choi Jin-ri stared at her own reflection and silently added a line in her heart.

Best if it’s only dance practice.

She trusted Baek Si-on.

But for an American actress who knocked on the male lead’s hotel door late at night and invited him to practice a partner dance, she would for now offer only basic politeness.

❧ ❧ ❧

The hotel’s multipurpose hall wasn’t large; the floor was even, the lights bright enough.

Emma was already warming up inside.

Seeing Baek Si-on come in, she didn’t say much — she simply played a waltz rehearsal track on her phone.

♪

Waltz · rehearsal track

the hotel hall · first run-through

The two began the first run.

Baek Si-on led.

Emma followed.

The melody advanced about three minutes.

The music ended.

The two let go at the same time.

Each had only one first reaction — that’s it?

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on’s feeling was very concrete.

By Damien’s description that day, Emma, after three months of intensive training, was already close to a professional dancer in level.

Actually dancing it, Baek Si-on even felt the two runs he’d rehearsed with Bae Joo-hyun had been smoother.

Emma had her own disappointment too.

Damien had told her Baek Si-on had completed high-intensity training in Korea, and his partner dance was already at shooting standard.

Actually dancing it, some of Baek Si-on’s movements carried a “please execute per chapter two, section three of the tutorial video” kind of rigor.

The dance in the film needed natural ease, and he currently looked more like he was completing a workplace-safety procedure.

❧ ❧ ❧

The two were each silent a while.

The atmosphere in the rehearsal hall returned to the subtle awkwardness of their meeting that day.

Emma spoke first:

“What did Damien tell you about me?”

Baek Si-on answered honestly:

“He said you’ve practiced over three months, and your level is already close to a professional dancer’s.”

The corner of Emma’s mouth twitched.

“He told me that in Korea you practiced dance every day after filming.”

“And that the first time you touched a waltz, you made the practice partner doubt his own reason for existing.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

The two looked at each other again.

Baek Si-on asked:

“Do I look like someone that gifted to you?”

Emma countered:

“Then do I look like a professional dancer to you?”

The answer didn’t need to be spoken.

Damien Chazelle — the young director who had made ‘Whiplash’ and knew well how to use pressure to push performers to their limit — had fed each of the two actors an equally generous bowl of high-concentration pep talk.

What he told both had a factual basis.

Emma really had practiced three months.

Baek Si-on really had completed waltz rehearsal in Korea.

But both statements had been directionally amplified.

The purpose was single: to make the two actors, before formal shooting, use anxiety and competitiveness to push themselves to their best.

Emma was the first to laugh out loud.

“He really knows how to manage people.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“That devil teacher in ‘Whiplash’ is probably his self-portrait.”

Emma laughed more openly.

“You’d better not tell him to his face.”

“Why?”

“He’d take it as a compliment.”

With both sides’ understanding finally aligned, the awkwardness between the two actually dissipated a good deal.

Their situations were completely identical.

Both had been fooled by the director.

Neither was as impressive as imagined.

Both were still far from the state the film needed.

Once the false strong-person filter was stripped away, cooperation actually became easier.

Emma reopened her phone and cued the music.

“Since neither of us is as impressive as the director said, shall we go again?”

Baek Si-on raised his left hand again.

“Please.”

The second run.

The two, working in at their true level, actually went considerably smoother than the first.

Baek Si-on no longer expected Emma’s following to be as precise as a dancer’s, and began consciously slowing his guiding rhythm.

Emma no longer relied on his guidance to judge direction for her, and began actively confirming each beat’s footwork.

After Damien’s lie expired, the effect seemed even better.

❝

Two actors, each told the other was nearly a professional, met and found neither was — and once the director’s flattering lie collapsed, the waltz finally had room to breathe.
