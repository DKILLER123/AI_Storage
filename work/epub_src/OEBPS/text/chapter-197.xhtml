Chapter One Hundred Ninety Seven

Happy Birthday, Peach

March 29 · the Train to Busan action training base, then home · her twenty-first birthday Seven falls and a word called ukemi · a cake with a chocolate plaque · a wish so small it would earn a scolding · an inbox with no Baek Si-on in it · seaweed soup acquired by card · a doorbell in the middle of the third hope · a gift that couldn’t clear customs, so he memorized it Seoul outskirts → Jin-ri’s apartment

March 29. The Train to Busan action training base, on the outskirts of Seoul.

Jin-ri hit the mat for the seventh time.

Strictly speaking, it didn’t count as a fall.

The action director called it ukemi.

But when a body meets the ground that intimately, professional vocabulary does not reduce the pain.

Her back slammed into the soft mat, half the air knocked out of her chest; she lay there for two seconds before slowly raising one hand.

“I’m still alive.”

Several of the actors nearby laughed out loud.

The action director squatted down beside her and gave her a thumbs-up:

“Much better than last week. Today you at least look like a person.”

Jin-ri lay on the mat, considered this seriously, and decided it probably counted as praise.

In early April, Train to Busan would officially begin shooting.

So this week was the final intensive training period. Running, falling, taking impacts, being chased, squeezing through the narrow aisle of a train carriage — even the angle of a terrified look over the shoulder had been broken down into trainable movements.

Jin-ri used to think idol stages were the hardest thing there was.

Now she had discovered that film crews had crueller ways of torturing people.

And yet, somehow, she didn’t hate it.

After one more set of sprints, Jin-ri stood bent over her knees, gasping, hair stuck to the side of her face, forehead shining with sweat.

A staff member handed her a towel. She whispered her thanks, and had just pressed it to her face when someone on the director’s side clapped their hands.

“Jin-ri-ya.”

Her first reaction was that she had done something wrong again.

Instead, the action director stepped aside, and several staff members came walking over carrying a cake.

The cake, as carried in

Not big. White cream. One small chocolate plaque standing upright on top.

— TO ACTRESS CHOI JIN-RI: HAPPY BIRTHDAY.

She froze.

The next second, a thin, scattered rendition of the birthday song rose around the training hall.

Every face wore sincerity. Yeon Sang-ho had even ordered the behind-the-scenes camera switched on, the lens pointed at her.

Jin-ri instinctively pulled the towel away from her face, then hurried to smooth her hair down with one hand.

Too undignified. No styling today, training clothes wrinkled, and her face probably still had mat dust on it.

But everyone was looking at her.

So she smiled.

“Thank you, everyone.”

She pressed her palms together and bowed to the crew.

Yeon Sang-ho, smiling, told her to make a wish.

Jin-ri stood in front of the cake and watched the single thin candle being lit.

The training hall was bright; that little point of light was nearly invisible. She bowed her head and closed her eyes anyway.

People around her heckled her into wishing for something big.

She heard someone say: may the movie sell huge.

Someone else: may nobody get hurt once real shooting starts.

Behind her closed lids, Jin-ri’s lashes trembled once, softly.

She wished a very small wish.

So small that, said out loud, it would probably earn her a scolding from Goo Hara about having no ambition.

She hoped that today, someone would think of her.

Even just one “happy birthday” text would have been enough.

Jin-ri opened her eyes and blew out the candle.

Everyone applauded.

The behind-the-scenes camera was still rolling, so she instantly restored herself into a proper junior actress — smiling, handing out cake to Yeon Sang-ho, to the action director, to the staff.

The slices were cut thin.

Nobody on a film crew truly dares to eat much sugar — least of all her.

But she still took one small piece and ate it, seriously.

The cream was a little cloying.

But today could be forgiven.

❧ ❧ ❧

By the time they wrapped, the sky had gone dark.

Jin-ri changed back into her own clothes and came out of the locker room, and the first thing she did was check her phone.

The screen lit up.

There were a lot of messages.

The inbox, as found at the end of the day

Fan sites — birthday art, one after another.

SM — the routine, corporate blessing.

The f(x) members — “happy birthday.”

Even Lee Ji-eun — a birthday greeting.

She read them one by one, all the way down.

No Baek Si-on.

Her finger hovered above the screen for two seconds — then swiped on, casually, as if nothing were there.

It was fine. He was in America. Being busy was only to be expected.

She told herself this very, very sensibly. Then she locked the screen and put the phone in her pocket.

At the training base gate, Goo Hara’s car was already waiting.

Sunglasses, driver’s seat, window half down — the moment Jin-ri appeared, Hara lifted her chin at her.

“Our actress Choi looks like she got chased three kilometers by zombies today.”

Jin-ri pulled open the passenger door, sat down, and countered under her breath while fastening the seatbelt:

“Not officially chased yet.”

Hara looked her up and down once — and suddenly frowned.

Jin-ri instantly tensed.

“What’s wrong? Is there something on my face?”

“There is.”

“What?”

Hara nodded, utterly serious:

“A bit of pretty.”

Jin-ri stared blankly for a beat.

Then she laughed.

❧ ❧ ❧

The car pulled out of the training base. Seoul’s night spread out bit by bit, streetlights sliding past the windows.

Hara didn’t drive fast, and didn’t rush to talk. Only at a red light did she reach back, grab a thermos out of the back seat, and push it into Jin-ri’s hands.

“Seaweed soup. Two sips now to hold you — the rest when we’re home.”

Jin-ri held the thermos and twisted the lid open. Steam rose.

She lowered her head and took a small sip, and the heat stung the tip of her nose into soreness.

“You made it yourself, unnie?”

Hara drove, eyes fixed straight ahead:

“Of course not. If I’d made it, you’d be spending this birthday in an emergency room.”

The small amount of being-moved that had just risen in Jin-ri was instantly scattered; she laughed so hard she nearly choked.

“Then who made it?”

“I bought it.”

Hara declared, righteous:

“But I swiped the card myself. That is a form of participation in the production.”

Jin-ri hugged the thermos and laughed.

And while she was laughing, her phone buzzed in her pocket.

She had it out instantly.

It was an advertisement. Some brand wishing her a happy birthday — coupon attached.

She stared at the screen for one second, pretended completely that nothing had happened, and put the phone back.

The car sat quiet for a while.

Then Hara said, suddenly:

“It should be the small hours in America right now?”

Jin-ri looked out the window. “Mm.”

“He might be shooting an MV. Might be on a plane. Might be pinned to a chair in a meeting by the Universal people.”

Jin-ri pressed the corners of her mouth flat:

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You didn’t.”

Hara kept one hand on the wheel and tapped her own cheek with the other:

“But it’s written here.”

Jin-ri’s hand went to her face on instinct.

“Written what?”

“It says — why hasn’t Baek Si-on texted me yet, did he forget about me, does he not love me anymore, should I pretend not to care first, but I really, really care.”

“I did not!”

“You did.”

“Did not.”

“Did.”

Jin-ri screwed the thermos lid back tight and elected to end a debate she had no chance of winning.

Hara looked at that state of affairs and meant to tease on — but at the next intersection, she abruptly changed her tune.

“Although. If he really did forget — then it’s his fault.”

Jin-ri turned her head to look at her.

Hara, face utterly grave:

“Busy with work, understandable. On a plane, understandable. Drowning in promotion, understandable. But a girl’s birthday with no message is simply wrong.”

Jin-ri looked down at the thermos.

“It’s not that serious.”

Hara gave a cold laugh:

“And who, just now, nearly had her heart stop over a coupon text?”

“……”

“When he gets back, I’ll punch him for you.”

Jin-ri hesitated.

“Punch him lightly.”

Hara: “……”

“Yah, Jin-ri. Am I avenging you, or giving him a massage?”

Jin-ri finally lost to laughter.

❧ ❧ ❧

By the time she got home, Blin was already waiting at the door.

Strictly speaking, a Sphynx’s face makes it very hard to determine whether it is “waiting” at all.

He wore a pale-yellow little sweater, and on his head, installed in advance by Goo Hara, a mini birthday hat tilted to one side; the whole cat sat on the doormat like a middle-aged employee conscripted into a mandatory company team-building event.

Jin-ri crouched the moment she was through the door and scooped him up.

“Is our Blin celebrating mama’s birthday too?”

Blin regarded her with cold indifference.

Hara was taking off her shoes behind her.

“He tried to rub that hat off three times this afternoon. I suppressed him three times. But today, his spirit of professionalism deserves commendation.”

Jin-ri carried Blin inside.

The living room had been simply arranged. No over-the-top balloons, no walls of glittering decoration.

On the dining table: one small cake, one pot of seaweed soup, sweet-and-sour pork, fried chicken, steak — and one bottle of champagne, unopened.

“Unnie…”

Hara tossed her bag onto the sofa and waved a hand, elaborately casual.

“Don’t be moved. Most of it is delivery. I only arranged it to look like I’m blessed in the domestic arts.”

Jin-ri carried Blin over and gently nuzzled his birthday hat with her forehead.

“But I love it.”

“Then love it faster — wash your hands and eat. Today you’re the birthday girl. You are entitled to two extra bites of carbohydrates.”

“Really?”

“No. But you’re allowed to pretend I never said it.”

“……”

When Jin-ri came back out with clean hands, Hara killed the big living-room light. On the cake sat a candle shaped like the number “21.”

“You’re a year older, and I still hold your mental age at seventeen.”

“Unnie is the seventeen-year-old.”

Jin-ri muttered it, small-voiced, folded her hands at her chest, and sat down in front of the cake.

The flame came up.

Looking at those candles, she suddenly felt that today had, in fact, already been very good. The crew remembered her birthday. Hara unnie was eating with her. Blin was here too. The room was warm, the seaweed soup was good, and the cake looked edible.

She should be content.

❝

A person’s wishes are greedy things: you can collect blessings all day and still find yourself waiting for one particular line from one particular person.

— the arithmetic of a twenty-first birthday, done honestly in the dark

Hara stood beside her and urged her softly:

“Make a wish.”

Jin-ri closed her eyes.

This time, she wished more directly than she had at the training base.

Hope he hasn’t forgotten me.

Hope he thinks of me today.

Hope—

Before the third hope could finish itself, the doorbell rang.

Jin-ri opened her eyes.

Hara looked toward the door too.

The candle flame swayed, gently.

The living room went quiet.

Jin-ri’s heartbeat, entirely without her permission, went unreasonably fast.

A few days earlier, Baek Eun-ah had texted to ask whether she had any plans on the evening of March 29.

Jin-ri was no fool.

She had, of course, guessed.

But guessing was one thing. Daring to believe it was another.

So she had kept telling herself: don’t expect.

❝

But people are built that way: the more firmly you forbid yourself to expect, the more certainly you hand your heart over, intact, the instant the doorbell rings.

— the doorbell, doing what doorbells do

Jin-ri stood up slowly.

Blin sat on a chair, watching her walk toward the entryway with total cold indifference.

She was walking fast — and still felt she was walking too slow.

Jin-ri reached the door and laid her fingers on the handle.

She opened it.

Baek Si-on stood outside.

Hair a little messy, a light stubble along his jaw, tiredness sitting at the bottom of his eyes. He looked like a man who had been forcibly squeezed back through the seams between an airport and a schedule.

He raised his wrist and checked the time.

“Good thing I’m not late.”

Something in Jin-ri’s throat jammed shut.

There were so many sentences lined up behind her teeth —

Why aren’t you in America?

When did you get back?

Why didn’t you message?

Did you plan this?

— and what actually squeezed out was a string of fragments that formed no sentence at all.

“How did… you’re not in… when did… the plane… you…”

Baek Si-on lowered his wrist and waited for her to finish.

She never finished — because she discovered she didn’t need to.

He was here. That was the whole answer.

The next second, Jin-ri stepped forward and crashed into his chest.

The impact knocked Baek Si-on back half a step.

Her strength exceeded what he’d imagined. The Train to Busan training, it appeared, had not gone to waste.

Her face buried in his chest, her hands gripping the fabric of his coat at the back.

Baek Si-on looked down at her, and let his voice go a little softer.

“Happy birthday.”

Jin-ri didn’t answer.

She just lifted her head.

Baek Si-on looked at her.

The distance between them collapsed all at once — too close now for anyone to need to ask permission.

Jin-ri rose onto her toes, a little.

Baek Si-on lowered his head.

And the kiss simply came down.

She was bolder than the Jin-ri of ordinary days — the one who blushed, who took detours, who hid affection inside sticker choices and small schemes.

Baek Si-on’s hands found her waist and drew her a little further into him.

The door stood open; the corridor’s cold air poured in; neither of them managed to care.

In the living room, Goo Hara watched the two figures in the entryway, and silently reached down to cover Blin’s eyes.

“Kittens who are minors may not watch this.”

The sound from the living room reached her, and Jin-ri’s body abruptly went rigid.

She remembered, finally, that the household did not consist solely of her and Baek Si-on.

There was also Hara.

And there was a cat wearing a birthday hat.

In a fluster she raised a hand and swatted him twice on the shoulder — two swats that landed not with the force of intervention but with the force of some coquettish, officially registered complaint.

Baek Si-on understood them in exactly that spirit.

So not only did he not let go — his arms tightened, and the kiss went deeper.

What remained of the rationality marked “Hara unnie is right there” shattered on the floor.

Her fingertips gripped his clothes; her lashes trembled; and then she simply, comprehensively, gave up the struggle.

Let it be embarrassing, then.

Hara unnie knew everything anyway.

She closed her eyes and answered him.

Goo Hara stood in the living room with the expression of a person watching a film whose ticket she had purchased long, long ago.

❧ ❧ ❧

Nobody knew how long. Thirty seconds, maybe a minute.

At last the two of them parted.

Jin-ri opened her eyes. From this close, she could see her own reflection inside his pupils.

There was none, in Baek Si-on’s gaze, of the calm he usually used to dismantle romance; no omen of a well-aimed remark loading.

They stayed like that — forehead to forehead, nose to nose, breaths interleaving.

Jin-ri felt she ought to say something. Ask when he had landed. Ask whether he was exhausted. Scold him for a full day of silence.

But what came out, mouth opening, was:

“Is this a dream?”

“Dreams don’t nearly arrive late.”

She had been about to cry; the sentence made her laugh instead, her breath spilling against his lips.

Baek Si-on dipped close again, and Jin-ri tilted up to meet him —

“Ahem.”

Goo Hara’s forbearance officially expired, audibly.

“You two. The cake is about to outlive its own birthday.”

Her voice wasn’t loud. But dropped into a room reduced to the sound of breathing, it was indistinguishable from a struck gong.

Jin-ri startled out of her whole skin. Heat climbed her face, starting from her ears.

She pulled free of his arms, spun, and hid behind him, burying her face between his shoulder blades.

Suddenly vacated, Baek Si-on looked at Goo Hara.

“…Long time no see.”

“Long time no see.”

Hara nodded:

“That performance just now was excellent.”

“…Thank you.”

“The runtime was also well judged.”

Baek Si-on elected not to catch that line.

“Come in first.”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on changed into the slippers and came in. Jin-ri trailed behind him, not quite daring to look at Hara.

Hara didn’t tease her any further.

Her gaze made one full pass over Baek Si-on — and then snagged on a very real problem.

His hands were empty.

No flowers.

No gift box.

Not even a bag shaped like a birthday present.

Jin-ri, in truth, didn’t care. The moment he appeared, today’s wish had already been granted.

But Goo Hara clearly did not intend to let him off that easily.

“So. Sunbae Baek Si-on.”

She began, unhurried:

“Where is the birthday present?”

Jin-ri’s head came up at once; she said, small:

“She doesn’t need—”

Hara pressed her back down with a look.

Baek Si-on, for his part, was entirely at ease.

“Brought it. But it couldn’t clear customs. So I memorized it.”

Hara blinked.

Jin-ri’s face went full question-mark.

“What kind of present can’t clear customs?”

Baek Si-on looked at the Bluetooth speaker on the coffee table.

“Can that connect?”

Hara froze for a beat — and then her expression traveled, visibly, from confusion to a very particular species of comprehension.

“It can. It can right now!”

Jin-ri stood to one side and watched him produce his phone.

The successful-connection chime sounded; Baek Si-on looked down and opened an audio file.

She was still wondering what exactly he intended to do —

Then the first chord came up, and her heart went suddenly, completely quiet. Baek Si-on was singing a song she had never heard.

The gift that couldn’t clear customs · her living room · the last minutes of her twenty-first birthday

“I found a love.”

“For me.”

“Darling just dive right in and follow my lead.”

“Well I found a girl beautiful and sweet.”

Halfway through, Blin seemed to recognize Baek Si-on’s voice, and prepared to leap out of Hara’s arms to rub against him.

Hara, faster, caught the whole cat in one arm.

“This is not your moment to steal the scene.”

Blin struggled briefly.

Hara went on, in a whisper:

“Your father is working.”

Jin-ri heard it.

Baek Si-on heard it too.

But he was singing.

“……”

The song wasn’t long. At least, the version he sang for her tonight wasn’t long.

As the last guitar chord dissolved slowly into the speaker, the living room went quiet.

On the cake, the candle had burned down to a stub; wax had dripped into the cream.

Baek Si-on looked at the expression on Jin-ri’s face — the one on the verge of tears — and asked:

“Bad?”

Jin-ri shook her head.

“Beautiful.”

“Then why the face?”

“…You flew thirteen hours. To sing me one song?”

“The way you once flew thirteen hours to hear me sing one.”

Jin-ri suddenly remembered her own flight to Los Angeles.

What Baek Si-on was doing now was the same thing. From the other direction, across the same length of air, wedged into time that had no gap in it at all.

“Hey hey hey — pause the being moved!”

Hara rapped her knuckles on the table:

“Blow it out now, or this becomes a charcoal-grilled birthday cake.”

Jin-ri wiped the corner of her eye and sat back down in front of the cake.

The flame was much smaller than before. But it was still alive.

Jin-ri looked at that little point of light, drew one deep breath — and blew out the candle.

“What did you wish?” Baek Si-on asked.

“Not telling.”

A pause — and then Jin-ri added, eyes curving:

“It already came true, anyway~”

Hara cut cake beside them, face completely blank, her knife-work turned aggressive by emotional overload.

She told herself to stay calm. It was her little sister’s birthday. One may not drive the cake knife into the table merely because romance is occurring at close range.

Baek Si-on examined the lopsidedly-cut piece and offered his assessment:

“The cake is innocent.”

Hara pushed the plate over.

“It can take the punishment in your place.”

“Although—”

Hara couldn’t help herself after all:

“Was that song written specifically for her?”

Baek Si-on glanced at Jin-ri, who was mid-sneak-peek at him.

Then he turned to Hara.

“Yes.”

Strictly speaking, the first seed of that melody had come from a red-haired Englishman. Baek Si-on planted it in the night of March 29, watered it with her name, pruned it into a shape that fit no room but this one — and then sang it to her. So when he said yes, it was yes.

❝

But a seed is not a flower.

— the first seed: a red-haired Englishman; the flower: March 29, in her living room

And all Jin-ri knew was this: on the night of March 29, in the last few minutes of her twenty-first birthday, Baek Si-on stood in her living room and told Goo Hara —

The song was written for her. Specifically.

That was enough.
