Chapter Two Hundred Thirteen

A Venice-Certified Actor

April 19

Mid-afternoon

A coffee house, Beverly Hills · the corner with the upright piano

A coffee house somewhere in Beverly Hills.

The doorway was flanked by neatly clipped shrubbery, the air-conditioning ran strong, and even the sound of cups against saucers kept its voice down. In the corner stood an upright piano, where a young house pianist in a pale-grey shirt was playing background music that nobody was expected to actually listen to.

When Baek Si-on walked in behind Scooter, Damien Chazelle and Emma Stone were already there.

Damien Chazelle rose to his feet.

He was younger than Baek Si-on had pictured. Thinner, too.

Damien Chazelle

Film director · the man holding ‘La La Land’

‘Whiplash’ · Sundance breakout · a script that sings when dialogue runs out

Asked for three months of training, was offered one and ten million dollars instead. Listened to an uninvited café recital with a cup frozen halfway to his mouth, and decided he did not want to meet anyone else for the role.

“Baek. Good to meet you.”

Damien put out his hand.

“Same.”

Baek Si-on shook it.

Beside him, Emma Stone stood up as well.

She greeted them with a smile, opened her mouth — and her voice gave her away on the spot.

“Hi — sorry.”

She pointed, a little embarrassed, at her own throat.

“I’ve been on Broadway two weeks straight. I keep leaving my voice on the stage.”

“It’s fine.”

Baek Si-on shook her hand too:

“At least it proves you sing it live.”

Emma’s smile turned a shade sweeter.

“That one I’ll accept.”

Emma Stone

Actor · America’s sweetheart, currently hoarse

Two weeks of Broadway · a voice left on stage · one bench duet

Came to the meeting apologizing for her throat and stayed to be sung to. Walked to the piano on apartment-creaky floorboards, sat down at the far end of a narrow bench, and read one well-written line over and over in a man’s face.

The four of them sat. A waiter came over with coffee, and Damien skipped the pleasantries almost entirely.

“Did you read the script?”

“On the plane.”

“What did you think?”

Baek Si-on took a sip of coffee, thought for two seconds, set the cup down, and said:

“Everything I’ve done until now has been conventional film.”

Damien watched him.

“In conventional film, what a character feels inside usually gets delivered by indirection. Camera language, rhythm, silence, micro-expressions — worst case, a voice-over.”

“Roundabout, all of it.”

“But in this material, when a feeling outgrows what the dialogue can hold, it comes straight out as song.”

“That way of saying the heart directly — I genuinely haven’t touched anything like it before.”

Baek Si-on said it plainly, making no pretense of understanding everything.

“But it does feel fresh.”

“And interesting.”

Damien gave a single nod.

Then asked:

“And the story itself?”

Baek Si-on gave him a strange look.

“If I thought this script was bad, I wouldn’t be sitting here today.”

Scooter, standing conveniently nearby, drove the knife in one more turn.

“The fact that he personally nodded and got on a plane already tells you everything worth knowing. Our second-half world tour has the calendar shaded down to slivers of blank. If he hadn’t insisted on coming, this cup of coffee wouldn’t have existed.”

Emma Stone turned and looked at Damien.

Damien turned and looked at Emma Stone.

Emma shrugged.

The gesture was small, and its meaning was unmistakable:

He’s asking for a punch, but he’s not wrong.

❧ ❧ ❧

Damien turned his attention back to Baek Si-on.

“In that case.”

“While you’re still sitting here — could we hear something?”

He tipped his head toward the piano in the corner.

“Play something. Or sing whatever you like.”

“Sure.”

Baek Si-on rose and headed for the corner, where the house pianist was just finishing a piece.

He waited for the last note.

Baek Si-on bent down slightly.

“May I borrow it for a bit?”

The pianist looked up.

And his eyes went wide, instantly.

He swallowed the Holy shit, but his lips moved twice anyway — it had clearly already been said, somewhere in there.

“Of— of course.”

He sprang off the stool.

The moment he’d cleared the seat, his hand had already gone into his pocket, completely without authorization, for his phone.

Baek Si-on sat down.

The stool height was passable.

He didn’t adjust it.

His fingers came to rest on the keys, and stayed there for two seconds.

Then they dropped.

The intro of ‘Love Yourself’ spilled out — and the café’s scattered attention began drifting toward the piano.

The café, as it stopped pretending not to listen

Behind the counter, a barista stopped mid-wipe, cloth stalled against the glass.

Two tables that had been bent low in conversation lifted their heads at the same time.

And the man at the keys was not looking at the keys at all. His gaze carried over the raised lid and came down on a seat a dozen meters away.

Emma Stone clearly understood exactly why she was here.

As if the melody itself had hooked a finger in her direction, she stood and walked over.

The coffee house’s wooden floorboards creak now and then — but her steps landed precisely inside the piano’s cover.

She stopped at the piano’s side.

Her hand came to rest on the edge of the lid.

‘Love Yourself’ · the café audition nobody scheduled

“Oh baby you should go and love yourself…”

Baek Si-on let the tail of the line fade, and raised his head.

The three-o’clock sun came in through the window behind her, and drew a very thin line of light along the outline of her shoulders.

Against the light, her hair had turned a translucent caramel; the details of her face were partly eaten by the glow.

He looked at her.

His fingers settled back onto the keys.

The melody never broke.

But the line that came out next had its tail lifted, just slightly, at the end.

The corner of Emma Stone’s mouth moved.

Out in the café, a waitress carrying a latte came around the counter, hesitated at the route between the two of them, and went the long way.

And so the two of them stood looking at each other across the width of an upright piano.

No lines.

No blocking notes.

Even the background music was something he was playing himself.

Fifteen seconds went by.

Or twenty.

Time gets unreliable in moments like these.

Emma Stone’s fingers slid forward along the lid’s edge, all the way to the far end of the bench — and she sat down.

The bench was narrow.

A fist’s width of daylight between them, roughly.

The melody arrived at the last transition before the chorus.

The left hand’s bass sank; the right hand’s treble brightened.

He tilted his head, a little.

She tilted hers, a little less.

Their lines of sight crossed about thirty centimeters above the piano.

Then Baek Si-on sang the chorus out.

Her gaze traveled with his voice — from his eyes to his mouth, to the fingers on the keys, and back up to his eyes.

❝

Like reading a beautifully written sentence — finishing it, and starting over from the top.

— the audition, as actually conducted

Once in a while, in the gap of a breath, Baek Si-on would look up at her.

Every single time, she was looking at him.

When the last note landed, his fingers left the keys, hung in the air for one second, and came softly down to rest on his knees.

The resonance thinned out, little by little, beneath the coffee house’s wooden ceiling.

Emma Stone spoke first.

“That is so much worse than the radio version.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“I thought you were going to sing.”

Emma Stone looked down, laughed, and then pointed at her own throat.

“If I open my mouth right now, all I’ll do is ruin those three minutes for you.”

A dozen meters away.

Damien was holding his coffee cup with its rim stopped at his lips. Not one sip had gone in.

That was when the rest of the café woke up, as if out of something.

Someone started clapping.

First one person, then two, then a whole wash of it.

Someone whistled.

Someone held up a phone, already recording.

Baek Si-on stood and put out a hand, naturally.

She looked at the hand for a beat, then set hers on it and rose.

The two of them walked back one behind the other.

The café’s atmosphere settled back into its three-thirty self, and the background noise returned to the nameless little tunes of the house pianist.

Damien watched the two of them sit down, and asked something that doesn’t usually come up in a director’s business meeting.

“Baek. Have you ever been in love?”

The hand moving Baek Si-on’s coffee cup stopped for a beat.

“What?”

“The way you looked at her just now.”

Damien tipped his head toward Emma Stone.

“Very deep in it.”

Emma Stone didn’t cut in — but her ears were unmistakably standing at attention.

Baek Si-on knew exactly what Damien was talking about.

Just now, at the piano, Emma Stone had been standing there in the backlight, looking at him.

Her outline. Her angle. That exact curve at the corner of her mouth —

And his brain, in that instant, had done something entirely natural.

It had swapped her face for someone else’s.

❝

Actors do it sometimes — steal a little of what is real from their own lives, and hide it inside someone else’s story.

— the Venice-certified defense

“I’m an actor.”

He said.

“The Venice-certified kind.”

Damien held his look for two seconds, then nodded.

Consider the answer accepted.

As for whether he believed it — that was a different matter altogether.

❧ ❧ ❧

“Then let me ask something proper.”

Damien folded his hands on the table.

“If you take this role — can you give it three months of concentrated training?”

“Piano technique, dance, physical rhythm. You’ve never carried a type like this. The fundamentals would start from zero.”

Baek Si-on shook his head.

“There’s a film in Korea waiting to start shooting. The dates are locked; they don’t move.”

“One month, I can find a way. Three months, no.”

Damien’s brow creased, faintly.

It was the answer he had expected and had not wanted to hear.

“However —”

Baek Si-on steered the conversation around a corner.

“More than the training window, one thing genuinely interests me.”

“How does the director plan to convince investors and audiences to accept an Asian man and America’s sweetheart, falling in love across Los Angeles?”

Damien went quiet for two seconds. Then he answered with complete frankness:

“We’ve sat with that question, seriously.”

“If this film were releasing in North America only, there would have been no meeting today.”

“Even with you the best available choice in every other respect.”

Baek Si-on understood.

Round and round, and underneath it was still that one piece of arithmetic.

The upside of the Asian market was enough to cover North America’s conservative risk.

❝

To capital, Baek Si-on was not “an actor worth the risk.” He was “a commodity that ships with its own insurance.” Not romantic. But very honest.

— the arithmetic under the coffee

“So my face and my box office now come as a bundle deal.”

Damien didn’t deny it.

“In Hollywood, almost everyone does.”

“You’re simply worth more.”

Baek Si-on let that one go unanswered.

He looked down at the script on the table, then raised his head again.

“Then back to the earlier question.”

Damien nodded, and laid it out:

“Principal photography won’t run long. A musical of this scale shoots about a month.”

“But the prep matters more — especially —”

He glanced at Emma Stone.

“The two of you need to have chemistry built before the first day of shooting.”

Emma Stone murmured, in that husky voice of hers:

“We should at least confirm in advance that you won’t step on my feet.”

“That depends on how well you lead.”

Emma Stone blinked.

Then she coughed behind the back of her hand — eyes bent into crescents the whole time.

Damien took the thread back.

“So the three months wouldn’t all be training, but prep needs two months at minimum. Add the shoot, and the project needs you to clear a window of three to four months.”

“That’s the floor.”

Baek Si-on thought for a while. Then he said:

“What if I put in an additional ten million dollars as investment.”

The café’s background piano kept playing.

“Could the start of filming move back one month?”

“And — my fee can be calculated at the union minimum.”

Damien, frankly, got a little dizzy.

The budget, as rewritten in real time

What does it mean when the male lead refuses a fee? It means the entire line item budgeted for the lead migrates, wholesale, into the production itself.

On top of which: another ten million dollars, injected.

The extra month of training? Covered.

Rehearsal space and the choreographer? Covered.

A second round of marketing spend? Now it could be considered.

The whole architecture of the film’s money, redrawn between one sentence and the next.

Damien looked down at the script on the table.

Sebastian.

❝

A jazz obsessive playing Los Angeles. A man keeping watch over something that is dying, and refusing to say the word out loud. And the Asian man across the table — he couldn’t just play him. He could make the whole world pick up the tab.

— the director, reading his own script through a stranger

Damien raised his head.

“I’ll need to re-run the numbers with the producers and Lionsgate.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Of course.”

“But…”

Damien said.

“Personally speaking — for this role, I have no interest in meeting anyone else.”
