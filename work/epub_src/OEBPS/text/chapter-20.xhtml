Chapter Twenty

The Money-Delivery Messenger of the SM Building

June SM Entertainment Not pressing the down button

Coming out of the legal department, Si-on didn't press the down button. He pressed up.

Eun-ah blinked in confusion.

“Cousin, where are we going?”

“Seventh floor.”

“Seventh floor?”

The artist practice wing.

Ding. The doors opened. The corridor was twice as wide as the one downstairs, the floor a pale wood-grain linoleum that swallowed footsteps. The walls were hung with enormous posters of SM's artists, lit bright, every face so polished it looked cut out of a magazine.

Eun-ah stood at the elevator mouth, looking at the corridor, not moving. She'd been here before. Only once. Two years ago, the company had bussed the trainees through the artist practice wing, calling it “inspiration.” In truth it was to show you how far away you were from them.

Now she was here again. But her standing had changed.

Si-on was already walking ahead. Eun-ah took a breath and followed.

The corridor was lined with practice rooms, each door labelled. Every door had the word “dedicated.”

Artist Practice Wing · 7F

SHINee — Dedicated Practice Room

Girls’ Generation — Dedicated Practice Room

EXO — Dedicated Practice Room

Red Velvet — Dedicated Practice Room

Posted rule · Authorised personnel only · 10:00–22:00

Eun-ah’s pace slowed at the “Red Velvet” plate. It was supposed to have been hers. Now it wasn’t.

Si-on stopped at the door of f(x)’s practice room. An A4 sheet was taped to it, a few lines printed:

f(x) · Practice Room · in use

f(x) On duty

Authorised personnel only · Practice hours 10:00–22:00

The door was shut, but the sound of music came through. The subwoofer vibrated the door panel, like something was striking it from inside. Si-on didn't move. He turned, leaned back against the corridor wall, one leg bent, foot flat against the baseboard, hands in his pockets.

“Wait for the music to stop.”

Eun-ah nodded. The two of them just stood there. The music was loud. Through the door panel you could hear someone counting out the beat, muffled, the words unclear, but the rhythm tight.

About three minutes later, the music stopped. From inside came the sound of breathing, footsteps, a water bottle being unscrewed, all mixed together. Someone said something, unclear, but the tone was tired.

Si-on nodded toward Eun-ah. She walked over and knocked on the door.

Knock, knock, knock.

The noise inside stopped for a beat. Then the door opened. The girl who opened it had her hair in a ponytail — Eun-ah recognised her, an assistant to f(x)'s manager.

“Who are you looking for?”

“Sulli unnie.”

The assistant turned and called back inside. “Sulli, someone's here!”

Footsteps from inside.

“Eun-ah?”

Choi Jin-ri stepped over the threshold, about to ask “how have you been,” when her head lifted and she saw the person leaning against the corridor wall. Buzz cut, flower-print shirt, hands in pockets, looking at her.

The words curved off mid-sentence.

“Why are you here?”

Si-on pushed off the wall and walked over. Pulled a wad of cash from his pocket and held it out.

“Bringing you some luck.”

Jin-ri looked down at the wad of money, blinking.

“That five thousand won you sent me turned into 1.8 million. But since my stake was big enough, it dragged your order into the big-winnings bracket — so an extra 8% tax. In the end you got 1.2 million.”

Ah.

Jin-ri remembered. She'd transferred five thousand won to him and asked him to buy a ticket for her. That day she'd just wanted to join in the madness, to be part of it, so she wouldn't feel so alone. These past few days of comeback, she'd been rehearsing ten in the morning till ten at night, so exhausted she fell asleep the moment she got back to the dorm, barely opening her phone. Then she'd forgotten about it.

And yet here this man was, standing at the practice-room door holding 1.2 million in cash, saying “bringing you some luck.” And explaining why it was six hundred thousand less.

Jin-ri took the money and clutched it, suddenly wanting to laugh, and then feeling like maybe she shouldn't.

“You… came all this way just to bring this?”

“Mm.”

“You could've transferred it.”

Si-on stepped back and leaned against the wall again.

“Cash has ceremony.”

Jin-ri didn't know what to say. Eun-ah, standing at the side, glanced back and forth, then, her eyes rolling, spoke up.

“Unnie, my cousin's come into money. Want help leeching a meal off him?”

Jin-ri looked at her.

“I… can I?”

“Why not?”

Eun-ah turned to Si-on.

“Cousin, you don't mind one more mouth, right?”

“Of course.”

Jin-ri's head went hot and she wanted to say “okay.” But the words changed midway — she glanced back at the practice room. The door was open a crack, the people inside still resting. Some lay on the floor, some leaned against the wall, and the ponytailed assistant was stealing a look this way.

Her brow knit.

“I might be a little later… they're still waiting…”

Si-on glanced at the door.

“No problem.”

“If going out is inconvenient, the cafeteria works too.”

Jin-ri had thought he'd say “then another day.” She hadn't expected him to say the cafeteria was fine too. She bit her lip and tightened her grip on the money.

“Then… can you wait for my lunch break?”

“Sure.”

Si-on didn't waste words, turned and walked toward the elevator. Eun-ah waved at Jin-ri.

“Unnie, keep going. See you in a bit!”

Jin-ri nodded, watched the two disappear into the end of the corridor, then turned and pushed open the practice-room door. Stepping back into that suffocating space, she suddenly felt the deafening subwoofer wasn't quite so harsh on the ears.

June SM Entertainment, elevator Eun-ah mimics a man who owes money

The elevator went down. Eun-ah leaned against the wall and suddenly laughed.

“Cousin, the way you did that… it was exactly like a delivery guy.”

“Delivery guy?”

“Yeah, the type who stands at your door holding a bag — ‘Hello, your order has arrived.’”

She mimicked his posture from a moment ago, hands thrust forward.

“Bringing you some luck.”

Si-on: “…”.

The first floor. SM's coffee shop was to the right of the entrance, not large — seven or eight tables, normally held hostage by squatting fans. The two of them got lucky; a corner table had just come free.

Si-on ordered an iced Americano. Eun-ah asked for a strawberry smoothie. The iced coffee came, and Si-on took a sip. Eun-ah poked at her smoothie, stirring the strawberry syrup into the milk until it whirled into a pink haze. After a while she looked up.

“Cousin, what do we do next?”

What she meant by “do next” wasn't the immediate moment. The immediate matter was waiting for Jin-ri's lunch break and eating at the cafeteria together. She was asking about the future.

The sunlight through the window was blinding. Si-on turned to look outside. Traffic roaring, crowds surging, Gangnam's midday as lively as ever. He was thinking about the same question.

The plan had been clear. Green Bottle Fly was finished; now he waited for the release. After release, whether it was Busan or Venice, just to show its face at an international film festival would be his entrance ticket into Korean cinema. Then next March, the audition for Reply 1988. He was confident he'd land Kim Jung-hwan or Choi Taek. Land either one, and—

“Cousin?”

Eun-ah waved a hand in front of his face. Si-on came back.

“Mm?”

“I asked what you're doing next.”

Si-on looked at her. She was watching him, a little lost, a little hopeful. Fresh out of a four-year trainee career and stuck in “what am I supposed to do now.” She was asking for real, and also waiting for him to give her a direction.

Si-on suddenly realised something. He could wait. He could go quiet. He could spend nine months slowly working out Dog-hwan and Taek, and wait to audition next March.

But Eun-ah couldn't. She needed something she could start now. Not nine months later. Now.

“Eun-ah.”

“Mm?”

“You're going to be my manager now, right?”

Eun-ah nodded. “Right.”

“Then what does a manager do?”

“Uh…”

Eun-ah thought.

“Answer phones? Arrange schedules? Negotiate contracts?”

“Right. Then do you have any calls to take? Any schedules to arrange? Any contracts to negotiate?”

Eun-ah went still and shook her head honestly.

“No.”

“Then as a manager, you're a sinecure.”

Eun-ah pouted.

“What can I do? You're not even famous…”

“So we find something to fill the time ourselves.”

Si-on pushed the iced coffee aside, folded his hands on the table, and looked at her.

“I had an idea earlier. I think it's time to start it.”

“What idea?”

“Writing songs.”

Eun-ah blinked.

“Writing… songs?”

“Mm.”

“You?”

“Me.”

“Songs about what?”

“Not decided yet.”

“But there must be a direction? What kind — ballad? Dance?”

“The kind that grabs you the moment the melody starts.”

“…Does that kind of song exist?”

“It does.”

“Where?”

“In my head.”

Eun-ah stared at him for three seconds, then reached out to feel his forehead.
