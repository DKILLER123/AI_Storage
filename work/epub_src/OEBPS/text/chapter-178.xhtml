Chapter One Hundred Seventy Eight

Trying to Please People Is Exhausting

December 31 · New Year’s Eve, gate to stage One arrival forty minutes early · two cups of warm water · one sentence that costs nothing and pays for thirteen hours · a stage jump in the middle of the fourth song · a hotel downgraded to the wrong chain Incheon International Airport → first class over the Pacific → Los Angeles · the Temple Theatre, afternoon light A performer of composure arrives early, changes seats twice, and loses every rehearsed script to a woman who orders warm water; a logic loop defeats a perfect son; and the most expensive gift on the plane turns out to be permission to say nothing at all.

December 31. Incheon International Airport. Departures hall.

Choi Jin-ri had arrived forty minutes before boarding.

She sat at the café’s window seat for five minutes, decided the window was too exposed, and moved to the corner. Three minutes there, and she found the view entirely blocked by an enormous potted plant — so she moved back to the window.

Truthfully, the anxiety had started days ago, at the moment she handed Blin over to Hara’s custody.

She checked her watch. Fifteen minutes to boarding.

When she looked up again, Yoon Hye-ja was walking into view with a dark-gray carry-on.

“…Auntie!”

She hurried over. Yoon Hye-ja turned toward the voice.

“Been waiting long?”

“No, no — I just got here too.”

Yoon Hye-ja did not demolition-check the girl’s thin layer of dignity.

“Then let’s go.”

“Yes.”

Choi Jin-ri walked half a step behind her, outwardly steady, inwardly sprinting the eight-hundred-meter dash of her life.

❧ ❧ ❧

Several days earlier, when the invitation call had come, Yoon Hye-ja had been, privately, a little surprised.

Choi Jin-ri → Yoon Hye-ja · the invitation · several days earlier · as it was

Auntie — it’s Jin-ri. Um. I hear the New Year’s fireworks in California are supposed to be really something…

The excuses had arrived in a stammer, one flimsier than the next — and the discounted plane tickets, two of them, bought by accident, wasted if unused…

Every excuse had the craftsmanship of a sick note composed the morning of the exam.

On New Year’s Eve — you don’t have a home to go back to?

The other end of the line went silent all at once.

Yoon Hye-ja waited a few seconds. And understood.

The girl had no home to return to. Or — no home she was willing to return to.

I happen to be going to Los Angeles anyway. Come with me.

She had asked nothing further. That was the whole invitation.

❧ ❧ ❧

“Would you like something to drink?”

The flight attendant’s gentle question pulled Yoon Hye-ja back from her thoughts; the two of them were already settled in first class, Los Angeles-bound.

Yoon Hye-ja glanced at Choi Jin-ri, who was holding the drinks menu and studying her out of the corner of one eye — apparently hoping to order whatever she ordered.

The corner of Yoon Hye-ja’s mouth curved, slightly.

“A glass of warm water, thank you.”

Choi Jin-ri shut her menu at once and nodded to the attendant.

“Same for me — warm water, thank you.”

The menus were collected, and two glasses of warm water arrived in short order. Choi Jin-ri thanked the attendant twice over — and then sank into a dilemma.

What do you talk about, for thirteen hours beside a university professor?

Economics? She didn’t know any. Sociology? Even less. Entertainment gossip? Too shallow to offer a professor. She stalled for a long time and finally lobbed out the safest-looking topic she could find.

“Do you… watch many films?”

Yoon Hye-ja took a sip of water.

“Not really.”

“Oh…”

The silence stood out sharply against the low hum of the engines. For Yoon Hye-ja, it was a stretch of blank in which her thoughts could rest among the clouds. For Choi Jin-ri, it ran long and unsupported, like a black-and-white silent film with no score.

Sunlight through the window lit the warm water translucent in the glass — and could not get through the careful nervousness sitting behind her eyes.

Thirty seconds later, she tried to break the ice again:

“Is your teaching load heavy at school?”

“It’s all right.”

“Then winter break should give you a good rest…”

“Mm.”

Choi Jin-ri realized, with a small private sorrow, that she was warming up a university professor the way guests warm up a Happy Together couch — and doing it far worse than those guests. At least when they crashed, Yoo Jae-suk was beside them, laughing loud enough to rescue the segment and tossing out a gag to glue the mood back together.

She had no Yoo Jae-suk.

She had one glass of warm water and an increasingly expert silence.

“Jin-ri-ya.”

“Yes!”

She sat bolt upright on reflex.

Yoon Hye-ja turned her head, looking at the girl’s rigid shoulders, at the glass held in both hands like something that might escape.

“Trying to please people is an exhausting way to live.”

“Huh?”

“You’re an actress. On camera, at the company, in every room where you have to be sensible and read the air — you compromise enough already.”

“But with me, none of that is needed.”

“Sleep if you want to sleep. Watch a film if you want to watch one. And if you don’t feel like talking — then for these thirteen hours, we can be perfectly quiet together.”

“Understood?”

The hands around the glass loosened, a little.

She looked down at the faint ripple on the water, and felt her eyes go hot without warning.

That permission — you are allowed to stop trying — sounded exactly like something Baek Si-on would say.

Like mother, like son. The magnetic field that made people feel safe had, apparently, been installed in this family twice.

“Understood…”

“Good. Rest.”

Choi Jin-ri nodded, pulled the blanket over herself, and closed her eyes.

❝

Not every kind of company needs to fill the silence. Sometimes, letting another person be quiet is the most expensive gift there is.

— somewhere over the Pacific, seat 1A and seat 1B

❧ ❧ ❧

A little after three in the afternoon, Los Angeles, the Temple Theatre.

Guitar strings plucked somewhere in the building’s vast echo. Baek Si-on stood center stage, running the last full rehearsal before the show.

Down in the house, Baek Eun-ah held a signed receiving sheet and walked a few local staff through tonight’s fan gift bags.

These were people coming to hear Baek Si-on sing. What Korea had gotten — cushions, hand warmers, hot drinks, buses, the full inventory — could not silently become nothing at all just because the venue was in California.

This time the bag held two things: the BodyArmor sports drink Kobe had invested in, and frozen dumplings from Bibigo, under the CJ umbrella.

Commercial placements, yes — placed with commercial hearts.

But beside the usual concert sponsor-ware of flyers and QR codes and “scan for coupons,” his advertising at least could fill a stomach.

She counted the last two crates, handed over the sheet, and stretched — and her gaze drifted, casually, to the theater entrance.

Someone stood in the halo of light at the entrance.

Baek Eun-ah rubbed her eyes. Back-to-back shifts, under five hours of sleep a night — this was almost certainly a hallucination.

When she opened her eyes again, the figure had not vanished. It was walking toward her.

It really was her aunt.

“Auntie!”

She buried herself into Yoon Hye-ja’s arms, hugging hard enough to knock her back half a step. Yoon Hye-ja laughed and patted her back.

“Why didn’t you tell me you were coming? I’d have picked you up at the airport!”

“Didn’t want to interrupt your work.”

Yoon Hye-ja reached up and tidied the loose strands at her forehead.

“You’ve lost weight.”

Baek Eun-ah grinned, winding up to wheedle — and her gaze slid sideways, to the person walking half a step behind.

“Jin-ri eonni!”

“Eun-ah-ya, long time no see.”

Choi Jin-ri waved, eyes curved into crescents.

“Eonni, how come you didn’t tell me either!”

“It was a last-minute thing…”

As she said it, Choi Jin-ri’s eyes drifted — entirely on their own — down the corridor toward the stage.

Baek Eun-ah followed the look. The corridor ran straight to the stage door.

Understood.

“Come on — I’ll take you in.”

One arm through her aunt’s, the other naturally through Jin-ri’s, she led them into the theater.

❧ ❧ ❧

Four songs into the rehearsal, Baek Si-on glanced toward the house.

Habit, mostly — a sweep of the room’s sightlines and lighting angles.

But this sweep caught.

The vocal lagged half a beat. The guitarist looked up at him. The drummer followed, half a beat behind that.

Baek Si-on signed to the sound engineer and the band, lifted off his in-ears and dropped them on the high stool — and jumped straight down off the stage edge.

“Mom. What are you doing here?”

“Am I not allowed to be?”

“…I mean, you should have told me you were coming.”

“I came.”

“……”

The loop closed perfectly.

So perfectly that no angle of follow-up question survived it. He abandoned the topic and turned to the woman standing to one side.

A camel-colored coat. Her hair looked a little longer than the last time he’d seen it, tucked loosely behind her ears. The expression management was working overtime; the delight in her eyes was not staying bought.

“Which hotel?”

“……”

She had prepared for “why are you here.” She had prepared for “you came alone?” She had even prepared for “where’s Blin.”

Every scenario had a scripted answer. The confident one — Blin missed you, so it sent me to check on you — she had rehearsed silently on the plane no fewer than twenty times.

He asked about the hotel.

“…The Hilton.”

Her voice had gone slightly flat.

“Eun-ah — handle it. Move her to the Four Seasons.”

Baek Eun-ah answered crisply and took Yoon Hye-ja’s arm.

“Auntie, let’s get the luggage settled first — and I’ll show you the streetscape. The palm trees here are really something.”

“Sure.”

The elders left.

And Choi Jin-ri’s polished, demure posture evaporated on the spot.

“Tired?”

“Mm.”

She had a follow-up planned — the natural next move. Thirteen hours in the air, so tired, a hug would recharge her, something in that family. She had rehearsed this script on the plane too. Not as well as the Blin script, but a solid five run-throughs.

She opened her mouth to deliver the line.

Baek Si-on got there first.

“Go rest at the hotel for a bit, then.”

Her mouth was still open.

“We’ll talk after the show tonight. All right?”

“…Okay.”

She nodded, a little deflated. The small scheme had failed to pass.

Her steps out were slower than her steps in.

At the door she looked back once. He was already climbing back onto the stage, saying something to the band. The in-ears went back on. The guitar started up again.

Choi Jin-ri watched the figure, and murmured it, barely aloud:

“I flew thirteen hours.”

No one heard it.

She turned and walked out of the rehearsal hall.

The Los Angeles afternoon sun was generous.

Generous enough that when she squinted into it, she could pretend the squinting was about the sun.
