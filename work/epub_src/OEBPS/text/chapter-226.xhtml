Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Six

The Fake Husband Among Fake Husbands

May 16 · morning

Pre-recording wrapped — a waiting room, no longer quiet

SBS · the music-show backstage

The backstage waiting room of an SBS music show.

BTS’s seven had just finished their pre-recording and lay scattered across the sofa and the carpet.

Their waiting rooms used to be quiet.

They were not quiet anymore.

Since ‘The Most Beautiful Moment in Life pt.1’ dropped, every number had been climbing.

Album sales were visibly up, ‘I NEED U’ had entered Melon’s main chart top ten, and the growth of their online buzz and fandom was visible to the naked eye.

More important: May fifth. ‘The Show’.

The moment the first-place result was read out, the seven of them stood on the stage.

Blank.

Crying too hard to finish a syllable.

Then holding on to each other.

Nearly two years since debut.

Tiny stages, empty seats, canceled schedules, flyers for free performances handed out on American street corners, nights of not knowing whether there would be a next month — and at last they had received one unambiguous answer from the market:

This road had not been the wrong one.

❧ ❧ ❧

Three knocks sounded on the waiting-room door.

Kim Nam-joon went to open it.

Baek Eun-ah stood outside.

The bag on her shoulder was still last year’s gift — the one Baek Si-on had bought her from Kim Jae-wook.

“Manager Baek-nim, hello!”

The other six in the room rose from their spots almost simultaneously.

And bowed in perfect unison.

Baek Eun-ah was driven half a step back by the momentum of seven grown young men.

“No need to be this nervous.”

She waved a hand, smiling.

“First of all — congratulations on your first-ever first place.”

“It’s been a hard stretch. Well done.”

Kim Nam-joon thanked her earnestly on the team’s behalf.

The others added their lines in turn:

That they’d never expected to get one so soon; that they were grateful to the company and to every staff member; that they owed Baek Si-on sunbae for the help he’d given them before; that from here on they would throw their lives into the work.

Every word delivered with extra solemnity.

Because Baek Eun-ah, standing before them, represented Baek Si-on in a certain sense.

The greetings done,

Baek Eun-ah unzipped the handbag in the crook of her arm and drew out a stack of envelopes.

Seven in all.

She handed them out one by one.

“These are first-place bonuses from Baek Si-on sunbae.”

When the last was handed over, she paused a deliberate beat and stressed one line.

“They will not be counted into the group’s future settlement debt.”

“Purely personal rewards.”

Money that Korean entertainment companies shower on rookie groups is, without exception, entered into the ledger of pre-production and promotion costs.

A so-called executive bonus is, much of the time, merely the money an artist will owe anyway, issued early under a nicer name — the wool, in the end, coming off the same sheep.

The money Baek Si-on gave did not enter the group’s costs.

It would not be offset against future schedule income, nor would it touch the settlements to come.

Given was given.

The bonuses, as delivered

Recipients: seven — one envelope each.

Contents: ten million won apiece, crisp, high-denomination notes.

Settlement debt: untouched — excluded by explicit statement.

Future schedule income: no offset. Coming settlements: no effect.

Terms of use: free and personal. Gambling alone, withheld.

Total: seventy million won. Given was given.

Baek Eun-ah went on:

“He’s been swamped lately and couldn’t come himself, so he asked me to deliver these for him.”

Jeon Jung-kook couldn’t help himself; he picked the seal of his envelope open.

A corner of crisp, high-denomination notes showed itself.

His eyes went round by degrees.

“O-one... ten million?”

The other six bowed their heads over their own envelopes in a rush.

Ten million won, every single one.

To the Baek Si-on of today, it was a trivial rounding error on the books.

But to Bangtan — who had never yet completed one real settlement and still budgeted their daily spending line by line — ten million won was a vast, system-shocking pile of cash.

“We can really spend it however we want?” someone asked.

Baek Eun-ah nodded.

“As long as it doesn’t go to gambling.”

Seven heads shook in unison.

“Absolutely not!”

The room was quiet for a while.

Kim Nam-joon bowed to Baek Eun-ah once more.

“Please thank him for us.”

“We’ll keep working. We won’t let him down.”

The others bowed after him.

How packed Baek Si-on’s schedule was, the whole industry could see.

The man was in the air, running between film sets and recording studios — and he still remembered they’d taken first place, and had given the most concrete affirmation there was.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Eun-ah re-shouldered her bag and delivered Baek Si-on’s last message.

“He says the first trophy is worth commemorating.”

“But once you have it, don’t keep hugging it and refusing to let go.”

“There will be more.”

The room went quiet again.

If an agent said this, it was called promising pies in the sky.

But from the mouth of a man with three Billboard champion singles, a Venice Best Actor trophy, and a TIME 100 entry to his name, it sounded more like a verdict of fate — one with real weight.

❧ ❧ ❧

Same day · midday

Meanwhile — clippers, and a ring of black hair

Gangnam · a private beauty clinic

Meanwhile.

A certain private beauty clinic in Gangnam.

The very man who had just sent out seventy million won in first-place bonuses was sitting in front of a floor-length mirror.

A white barber’s cape around his shoulders.

A ring of black hair already on the floor.

Days earlier he had stood on the Metropolitan Museum of Art’s red carpet in old-Shanghai vintage tailoring, pocket-watch chain at the waist, hammered by the world’s flashbulbs for an entire night.

Now the electric clippers skimmed up along the edge of his scalp — and New York’s young golden lord died on the spot.

Cause of death: the director asked for a buzz cut.

A public image the styling team had spent months constructing — a film crew needed one sentence to sweep it into the trash with the clippings on the floor.

The image, as executed

Input: the Met carpet — vintage, pocket-watch chain, one full night of flashbulbs. Procedure: clippers, scalp line, upward. Interval since: days.

Reconstruction timeline: none published. The crew’s whole contribution: one sentence.

Park Ji-hoon stood at Baek Si-on’s right rear, his expression losing a little more life with every lock that fell.

“Could the top keep half an inch more?” he couldn’t help asking the barber.

The barber switched off the clippers and looked, in the mirror, to Baek Si-on.

Where the decision lived was very clear.

“Per the role’s requirements,” Baek Si-on said.

The buzz resumed, merciless.

Park Ji-hoon’s voice carried a mourning drone.

“Do you know how many styling products I tested for that one strand of forehead hair at the Met Gala?”

“No.”

“Seventeen!”

Baek Si-on watched the stubble in the mirror grow shorter.

“Saves time now.”

Park Ji-hoon: “……”

Of course he knew a buzz cut saved styling time.

The problem was that the last thing a stylist needs is an artist over-saving styling time.

The clippers kept climbing.

The forehead strand that seventeen styling products had attended to fell too.

Park Ji-hoon’s professional dignity fell to the floor with it.

“The Paris paperwork is done,” Baek Si-on said abruptly.

“What Paris?”

Park Ji-hoon was still at the funeral of the hair.

“The Make Up For Ever Academy in Paris.”

When Park Ji-hoon had signed on, the contract had stipulated, in black and white, the benefit of regular professional training.

Baek Si-on was about to be locked inside a closed film set for a long stretch, and a buzz cut genuinely required no daily research into the arc of a hairstyle — the perfect window for shipping a man abroad to study.

The deeper calculation: the LAVUE cosmetics brand had already launched.

Baek Si-on would badly need a visual director who understood the international makeup system; this training would push the man from personal stylist toward a wider position.

Normally, this was a superb career resource.

Paris. An international makeup academy. The company paying in full. Full salary throughout.

Any stylist on earth would rejoice.

But Park Ji-hoon, contemplating the near-finished braised egg in the mirror, could not taste one grain of reward’s joy no matter how he tried.

“Are you sending me away because you don’t need me anymore?”

“The training that’s written in the contract.”

“Then why now, of all times?”

Baek Si-on’s gaze met his through the mirror.

“What would you stay and do?”

Park Ji-hoon looked down at the ring of clippings on the floor, which no longer held one grain of styling value.

The sentence was too reasonable.

The fuller the reason, the deeper the cut.

The barber finished the final trim, swept the clippings away with the brush, and unfastened the cape.

Baek Si-on stood.

The man in the mirror had changed his entire air.

Everything the long hair had supplied — vintage, splendor, polish — was gone, replaced by a sense of danger that would solve problems by the most direct route available, at any moment.

The terminally-ill fugitive finally showed his outline from inside the script.

Park Ji-hoon walked a circle around him.

Professional judgment finally outweighed the grief for the lost hair.

“It does fit the role.”

Then added:

“But not red carpets.”

“I’m not walking red carpets lately.”

“So THAT’s why I’m being exiled to Paris?”

“It’s training.”

“Is there a difference?”

“Salary continues.”

Park Ji-hoon swallowed the rest of his words.

“Then there is.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Park Ji-hoon left the clinic first.

The Paris paperwork was settled; he had to go home and pack, and confirm his enrollment date with the program coordinator.

Before leaving, he looked back once at the clinic’s second floor.

Baek Si-on was in the middle of pre-production skin care.

A terminally-ill fugitive is allowed to be rough.

The actor himself could not actually enter frame wearing closed comedones, blackheads, and allergic red patches.

Film cameras have no sympathy.

Their whole job is to enlarge your skin problems onto the screen for the audience to study together.

Park Ji-hoon put on his sunglasses, nodded to the bodyguard, and moved to call a car home to pack.

He did not notice the van parked across the street.

A man sat inside it.

An executive PD of ‘We Got Married’.

He was in the neighborhood doing advance location scouting for Park Soo-young and Yook Sung-jae’s first meeting.

When Park Ji-hoon drifted into his field of view, the face first seemed vaguely familiar.

He watched two beats longer.

Placed him.

The stylist beside Baek Si-on — the man who decided, daily, exactly how handsome Baek Si-on was going to be today.

Combine that with Park Ji-hoon walking out of this particular high-end clinic, and a van with a bodyguard parked at the door, and the deduction took him less than five seconds.

Baek Si-on was probably inside.

He dialed the field PD at once.

❧ ❧ ❧

The message climbed, floor by floor.

From the executive PD to the field PD, and from the field PD to the chief PD.

Once Baek Si-on’s proximity was confirmed, the production’s professional instinct engaged at extreme efficiency.

Today happened to be the day Park Soo-young and Yook Sung-jae recorded their ‘We Got Married’ first meeting.

If Baek Si-on could be got to appear, even for three minutes, the topic volume would feed the show for several episodes.

The signal chain, as escalated

Executive PD → field PD → chief PD. Latency: minimal. Instinct: fully professional.

Topic math: three minutes of Baek Si-on buys several episodes.

“Ask first.”

“Ask HIM directly?”

“Relay it through the clinic. Nobody barges in — whoever barges opens the door to the lawyers personally.”

❧ ❧ ❧

In the clinic’s treatment room, Baek Si-on lay on the treatment bed.

A layer of hydrating treatment on his face.

Eyes closed.

The manager on duty pushed the door open and bent down, carefully.

“Mr. Baek — the ‘We Got Married’ crew heard you were here and would like to ask you to make a brief cameo.”

“Not interested.”

The refusal was crisp enough that she couldn’t get a second sentence assembled.

She was about to turn back and relay when another addendum came through the earpiece.

She bent down again.

“They say it wouldn’t be a formal appearance and wouldn’t take long. They only want your help pranking today’s female guest.”

Baek Si-on’s eyes stayed closed.

“Who?”

“Red Velvet’s Joy.”

Baek Si-on opened his eyes.

“How?”

The manager stood where she was, phone in hand, silent.

The previous line had been “not interested.”

Upon hearing Park Soo-young’s name, the world-class star had skipped the “whether to participate” stage entirely and entered the “method-of-mischief” stage.

The speed of the pivot led her to some unnecessary speculations about the relationship between Baek Si-on and this particular female idol.

❧ ❧ ❧

The PD and the field writer hurried to the clinic.

Baek Si-on had by then finished his facial and sat on the lounge sofa.

Entering, the two of them were stopped cold first by the new haircut.

The PD hesitated.

“That’s... a new look?”

“Movie.”

The PD retracted the curiosity back into work scope.

The prank plan was not complicated.

Today Park Soo-young would follow the mission card to the designated open-air café to meet her virtual husband.

Baek Si-on would go in first and sit waiting.

When she came in, she would naturally take the man before her to be the “husband” the production had arranged.

Baek Si-on needed only to play along with the misunderstanding for a few minutes.

When the atmosphere had developed sufficiently, the real virtual husband, Yook Sung-jae, would appear bearing flowers.

The prank, as designed

One: Park Soo-young follows the mission card to the terrace café, expecting her virtual husband.

Two: Baek Si-on is seated first — back to the door, a magazine up.

Three: she concludes; he plays the misunderstanding for a few minutes.

Four: at peak atmosphere, the real husband enters, flowers in hand.

Guardrails, per Baek Si-on: his footage cleared with his side before the final cut; light tone; no malicious steering — or the lawyers call.

When he finished listening, Baek Si-on set his terms:

“Before the final cut — anything involving me gets confirmed with my side first.”

“Of course.”

The PD nodded rapidly.

Baek Si-on went on:

“If the edit paints Soo-young as a gold-digger, or uses her disappointment to steer hate comments, my lawyers will be in touch.”

The PD’s back tightened.

“Absolutely not. We guarantee a light tone throughout — no malicious steering of any kind.”

Baek Si-on stood.

“Let’s go.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Same day · afternoon

Red lights on — every small motion on the record

An open-air terrace café · booked out for one prank

The production’s cars rolled toward the meeting point.

Park Soo-young sat in the van; fixed cameras were mounted behind the front passenger seat and beside the door.

The red lights were on; every small motion would be recorded in full.

Today’s outfit had plainly been prepared with care.

A pale top, a pleated skirt, her hair fluffed soft and smooth.

Her lips in a pink-leaning water-gloss finish; even the nail color had been matched to the outfit specially.

The PD’s voice came from the front row.

“What kind of person is Joy thinking today’s virtual husband will be?”

Park Soo-young held the mission card to her chest and smiled shyly.

“I hope he’s gentle. The kind who takes care of people.”

A pause; then, honestly:

“Preferably on the taller side.”

“So I can feel safe.”

And then she couldn’t stand her own romance-speak and covered her face with a hand.

“Aigoo, I’m SO nervous...”

The PD continued:

“Anyone you’re especially hoping for?”

The dorm-room words from a few days ago dove straight into her head.

“If it were Baek Si-on sunbae!”

Said in front of the members, that line was harmless enough.

Shouted at a rolling official camera, it tended to upgrade from variety wish to internet testament.

“Nope — anyone I’m comfortable with is fine.”

The camera quietly recorded it.

The post-production caption was already pre-bookable.

The caption, as pre-booked

“Really — no one at all?”

Subtitle unit: scheduled before the recording even ended.

❧ ❧ ❧

The terrace café had been bought out by the production.

Wind crossed the canopy; the white parasol over the table swayed faintly.

Park Soo-young came in, mission card in hand.

The sunlight was good.

The café’s outdoor section had exactly one table set.

At the table sat a man, his back to the entrance.

Broad shoulders.

One arm over the chair back.

Legs crossed.

Head down over a magazine.

Park Soo-young’s steps slowed, noticeably.

The silhouette was familiar.

Familiar to an excessive degree.

She stole a glance at the cameraman beside her.

The cameraman’s face was blank; the lens followed her steadily, offering no hint.

The words on the mission card suddenly became useless.

She could only keep approaching.

Her heels knocked on the wood floor.

The magazine closed.

The man looked up.

Park Soo-young froze on the spot, her palm clapped over her mouth in an instant.

The buzz cut had rearranged Baek Si-on’s whole aura; it had not rearranged that over-recognizable face.

“B-Baek Si-on sunbae?”

Her voice rose by more than one register.

Professional instinct took over the body soon after.

She bowed at the waist.

“Sunbae, hello!”

And at the same moment, another thought surfaced — a deeply offensive one: why did this buzz cut look so much like he’d just walked out of prison?

Baek Si-on gestured at the chair opposite.

“Sit.”

Park Soo-young straightened up, still in a state of vertigo.

“Sunbae, why are you here?”

Baek Si-on countered:

“Why are YOU here?”

“I’m recording a show...”

“So am I.”

Park Soo-young looked down at the mission card.

Then glanced at the camera.

An extremely tempting answer — one that did not quite fit the show’s budget — floated up.

“Don’t tell me it’s... ‘We Got Married’?”

Baek Si-on nodded.

Park Soo-young took it like a giant candy to the forehead.

“Daebak...”

She had made the wish in the dorm only days ago.

Today the production had delivered the man to her table.

A dream cashing out this fast made logic redundant.

Park Soo-young sat down across from him, both hands on her knees.

A posture well-behaved enough for an outstanding-students award ceremony.

“Sunbae...”

She suddenly felt the address was wrong.

The show was called ‘We Got Married’.

Keep saying sunbae and the marital atmosphere would never get built.

Park Soo-young gathered her courage and tried again.

“Oppa.”

Baek Si-on raised a brow.

She very nearly covered her face again.

But the word was already out; she had to bull forward.

“Oppa’s schedule must be hard to work around, right?”

“It is indeed very busy.”

“That’s fine, I can work around oppa’s hours.”

“Three in the morning too?”

A crack ran through Park Soo-young’s expression.

“...Yes.”

“Overseas?”

“If the company allows...”

“Film sets?”

“I can visit set, too.”

The more she answered, the less this felt like a date.

More like a job interview — one demanding constant overtime, overseas travel, and on-site field visits.

But if the boss was THIS man...

She would sign the labor contract first and supplement the marriage contract later.

Park Soo-young reassembled herself and tried to reclaim the right to ask questions.

“Then what kind of wife does oppa like?”

“One who doesn’t do strange dances in the KBS corridor.”

Park Soo-young’s face went fully blank.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

A criterion that specific made one suspect there was a victim behind it.

But she quickly decided not to press — in front of “my virtual husband is Baek Si-on,” no question was worth the time.

Just then, Yook Sung-jae came walking in, cradling a bouquet.

The whole way over, he had been memorizing the cue cards the production issued.

How to deliver the first line.

Which hand offers the flowers.

Whether to shake hands upon meeting.

Every step of the procedure was scrapped collectively the moment he raised his head.

“Baek Si-on sunbae, hello!”

Park Soo-young turned to the male idol who had materialized mid-bow.

Holding flowers.

A production microphone clipped to his chest.

She turned her face back to Baek Si-on.

Every unreasonable detail she had willfully ignored in the shock of the surprise came home in the same second.

The production truly could not afford Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on was not going to stay long on ‘WGM’ either.

The smile on Park Soo-young’s face slowly set.

“Oppa...”

Baek Si-on’s expression stayed normal.

“Mm?”

“You’re not...”

The second half of the sentence refused to come out.

Baek Si-on finished it for her.

“No.”

The staff behind the cameras finally lost it; laughter leaked from every direction.

Park Soo-young closed her eyes as her social-death highlight reel began to loop.

Both hands over her face.

“Ah, this is TOO much!”

“You just promised to work around all my schedules,” Baek Si-on kept needling.

Park Soo-young exposed one eye through the gap in her fingers.

“That’s void now.”

“Why?”

“Because the husband changed.”

Yook Sung-jae stood beside them, cradling the flowers.

He didn’t know whether to be glad about that sentence.

By the show’s procedures, he had finally obtained legal identity.

By the atmosphere, he resembled a staff member assigned to deliver the farewell bouquet to the outgoing husband.

❧ ❧ ❧

The prank effect was more than sufficient.

Baek Si-on rose.

If he stayed any longer, the real first meeting of Yook Sung-jae and Park Soo-young would be flattened completely.

Yook Sung-jae straightened up too.

“She’s yours now.”

The sentence made Park Soo-young’s expression considerably more complicated.

One minute prior: interim husband.

This moment: switched to the bride-family’s big brother, giving away a daughter.

“Don’t you bully her.”

Yook Sung-jae bowed at once.

“Sir!”

“She has a temper. Bear with it.”

Park Soo-young protested at once.

“Where is MY temper bad?”

“Who was it just now saying the production was too much?”

“That’s because you DECEIVED me!”

Baek Si-on looked back to Yook Sung-jae.

“You see?”

Yook Sung-jae held the flowers, daring neither to nod nor to shake his head.

On wedding day one, he had already received advanced training in surviving the marital cracks.

❧ ❧ ❧

After Baek Si-on left, the table’s atmosphere finally resembled a young-idol dating show.

Park Soo-young and Yook Sung-jae finally faced each other properly.

Yook Sung-jae hesitated, then offered the flowers.

“Hello.”

Park Soo-young took them with both hands.

“Hello.”

Neither of them knew what came next.

The wind rustled the wrapping around the bouquet.

Yook Sung-jae tried to break the awkwardness.

“You just... seemed disappointed?”

“I wasn’t!”

The answer came fast and loud.

Yook Sung-jae was silent a moment.

“If it were me, I’d probably be disappointed too.”

That answer embarrassed her instead.

She hurried to repair it.

“Really not — I know you.”

Yook Sung-jae finally recovered a little confidence.

“Know what about me?”

“BTOB.”

“And?”

Park Soo-young hugged the bouquet tighter.

“You sing well.”

“Which song?”

“……”

This marriage opened on its second crisis.

The crises, as logged

Crisis one: the bride had briefly held a virtual ex-husband of Billboard-champion caliber.

Crisis two: the bride’s entire memory of the current husband was the group’s name.

Marriage age at second crisis: one table, one bouquet, eleven minutes.

❧ ❧ ❧

The studio interview room.

Park Soo-young sat in the little black room, one camera rigged in front of her.

The PD’s voice came from off-camera:

“When you saw Baek Si-on-ssi — what did you feel?”

“I thought the production had won the lottery.”

“You actually believed it?”

“Of course I believed it. He was sitting right there — and he nodded.”

The more she said, the more aggrieved she sounded.

“Sunbae’s acting is THAT good — who could tell?”

The post-production caption already existed.

The caption, as slated

“Venice Best Actor deployed for romance fraud — a first.”

The subtitle unit: fast, merciless, proud of its work.

The PD pressed on:

“You also called him oppa.”

Park Soo-young’s head went down.

“Can that part be cut?”

The PD’s voice from outside the frame was firm.

“No.”

Park Soo-young closed her eyes.

“Then I’m finished.”

The PD asked the key question.

“When the real husband appeared — were you disappointed?”

“No.”

Unusually firm, that answer.

“Really not at all?”

Park Soo-young stressed it to the camera once more:

“Really not — Yook Sung-jae sunbae is also great.”

The PD pointed out:

“You’re virtual spouses already — still calling him sunbae?”

Her head went lower still.

One day into recording, and the address system had already been wrecked by Baek Si-on.

❧ ❧ ❧

Another interview room.

Yook Sung-jae.

The PD asked:

“Opening the door and seeing Baek Si-on-ssi — what did that feel like?”

“I thought I’d come to the wrong place.”

“And then?”

“I saw Joy sitting across from him, and I felt like I was here to deliver flowers.”

Laughter drifted in from the interview area.

The PD asked again:

“Any pressure, with Baek Si-on sunbae having appeared first?”

“Yes.”

Yook Sung-jae answered with total honesty.

“Before going in, I thought the thing to face would be first-meeting awkwardness. After going in, I discovered I also had to face the show-effect left behind by a global superstar.”

“Baek Si-on-ssi said don’t bully Joy. Your thoughts?”

Yook Sung-jae looked up.

“I’ll obey.”

“That readily?”

“He’d just finished the buzz cut. The way he said it really sounded like he’d actually come find me.”

The PD held in a laugh.

“Then — your first impression of Joy?”

“Very pretty. Very energetic.”

Yook Sung-jae paused.

“Also — very honest.”

“She knows BTOB.”

“That counts as honest?”

“BTOB is all she knows.”

The interview room broke apart laughing.

Yook Sung-jae sighed, head low.

And so the mutual damage of this marriage officially began.

❧ ❧ ❧

The first ‘We Got Married’ recording finally wrapped.

Park Soo-young bowed to the director, the field writer, and the camera crew one by one.

“Thank you for your hard work today.”

The director smiled with exceptional warmth.

“Soo-young did very well today.”

Several staff members nodded along.

Park Soo-young held the service smile while her back molars quietly clenched.

Did very well.

Translated: you got deceived especially well today.

From believing Baek Si-on was the husband, to switching to oppa, to agreeing to pre-dawn departures, overseas flights, and set visits — she had supplied enough material for the production to cut one complete love-brain growth arc.

Park Soo-young bowed once more.

Turned and got in the van.

The instant the van door closed, the smile on her face clocked out with it.

❧ ❧ ❧

Same day · night

The lock beeps — four heads, one door

The Red Velvet dormitory · the living room

The dorm lock beeped.

Four heads in the living room swiveled to the door at once.

Park Soo-young had recorded her ‘We Got Married’ first meeting today; the others had been curious all day.

Kang Seul-gi asked first:

“You’re back?”

Kim Yerim bounced off the sofa.

“Unnie, who’s the husband?”

Park Soo-young did not answer who the husband was.

She flung her bag onto the sofa.

And stood in the middle of the living room, puffing with indignation.

“Baek Si-on sunbae is SO mean!”

Four faces showed four different depths of puzzlement, all at once.

Kang Seul-gi frowned.

“What does Baek Si-on sunbae have to do with it?”

Kim Yerim’s eyes lit up instantly.

“Don’t tell me it actually WAS him?”

The remote clicked once in Bae Joo-hyun’s hand.

The television’s sound died completely.

Son Seung-wan stole a glance at the leader and brought her water cup to her lips.

The relationship map of this household was complicating at a speed she would rather not participate in.

“Tell us, tell us!”

Kim Yerim crossed the carpet barefoot and flung herself down beside Park Soo-young.

Kang Seul-gi scooted over too.

Park Soo-young inhaled deeply and launched the full dramatic retelling.

Arriving at the café; a broad-shouldered man seated with his back to the door; closing in to find a buzz-cut edition of Baek Si-on; the dream apparently come true; and then one question gauntlet after another, straight out of a job interview.

“And he kept NODDING!”

“I asked if his schedule was busy, he said busy, I said no problem!”

“He asked if three in the morning worked, I said yes!”

“He asked about overseas, I said if the company allows!”

“He asked about film sets, I said I can visit!”

“Then what?” Kim Yerim was already draped over the sofa back.

“Then Yook Sung-jae sunbae walked in holding flowers.”

The living room went quiet for a beat.

“Who?”

“Yook Sung-jae. Of BTOB.”

“HE’S the real husband?”

“Mm.”

“Then what was Baek Si-on sunbae?”

“The decoy.”

The living room went quiet again.

Then it detonated.

Kim Yerim went down first, curled into a ball on the sofa with laughter.

Kang Seul-gi hugged a cushion, laughing her eyes into two curved slits.

Bae Joo-hyun laughed the loudest.

Her signature laugh — the one the members privately called the “glass-wiping auntie laugh” — rang around the living room.

Park Soo-young sat in the middle of the four, her face darkening by the minute.

“Is it THAT funny?”

Bae Joo-hyun fought the laughter down.

“No.”

The instant she said it, the image replayed: Yook Sung-jae at the door hugging his bouquet, supervising the taping of Park Soo-young’s pledge of trans-national set visits.

The glass-wiping restarted.

Park Soo-young glared at her.

“Unnie, you laugh the LOUDEST!”

Bae Joo-hyun wiped the laugh-tears away.

“Because you actually believed it.”

“Who WOULDN’T have believed it in that situation!”

The more she turned it over, the less Park Soo-young accepted it.

“If YOU had pushed that door open, unnie, and Baek Si-on sunbae was sitting there saying he’s your husband — you’re telling me you wouldn’t believe it?”

Bae Joo-hyun’s laughter paused.

“Of course not.”

She said it with total certainty.

But Son Seung-wan silently swept her with another glance, registering cautious reservations about this “of course not.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The living-room hilarity gradually dispersed.

Park Soo-young sulked alone with her cushion.

Nobody consoled her.

Kim Yerim had her phone out, dutifully searching “who is Yook Sung-jae,” assembling the brother-in-law file.

Park Soo-young lifted her chin.

“Laugh away, all of you.”

She took the room in.

“Anyway, I also scored one vital piece of intelligence today — Baek Si-on sunbae’s ideal type.”

The surviving laughter in the living room cut off in a single stroke, like a master switch pulled.

The pull of this intelligence was obviously several magnitudes greater than “who is Yook Sung-jae.”

Kim Yerim scooted in at once.

“What kind?”

Kang Seul-gi was curious too.

“He said it himself?”

Park Soo-young finally had the room’s initiative back in hand.

“I asked him what kind of wife he likes.”

Bae Joo-hyun picked up her water cup from the table and took a sip.

Park Soo-young’s gaze swept across the three older girls and the maknae, confirmed to her satisfaction that every scrap of attention in the room was on her, and only then did she open her mouth:

“One who doesn’t do strange dances in the KBS corridor.”

As the words landed, the curiosity on Kang Seul-gi’s face curdled into blankness.

“Meaning what?”

Kim Yerim was blank too.

“What kind of ideal type is THAT?”

❧ ❧ ❧

Park Soo-young spread her hands.

“No idea.”

Bae Joo-hyun held her water cup.

The rim had just touched her lips — and her whole body lost motion.

She worked to look entirely uninformed.

But an extremely hard-to-ignore presence assembled itself beside her.

Bae Joo-hyun turned her face, slowly.

Son Seung-wan was staring at her.

The two gazes collided in the air.

Son Seung-wan: “……”

She relocated her attention to the television immediately.

Too late.

Bae Joo-hyun had already strung every clue together.

Son Seung-wan had seen.

This bratty little sister had sat on it until TODAY, without one word.

It did not make Bae Joo-hyun grateful for the discretion.

It raised a far more urgent question:

How much had she actually seen?

Bae Joo-hyun set the water cup down on the coffee table, slowly.

“Son Seung-wan.”

In the Red Velvet dorm, the leader calling your full name generally forecast some development that was not friendly.

Son Seung-wan’s spine snapped straight.

“Ye?”

Kang Seul-gi’s attention swung from Park Soo-young to the two of them.

Kim Yerim smelled a bigger melon.

Bae Joo-hyun stood.

“Inside. With me.”

Son Seung-wan stayed exactly where she was, hands placed with perfect propriety on her knees.

“Now?”

“Mm.”

Park Soo-young, cushion in her arms, eyed the pair with suspicion.

“What’s going on?”

“Some business.”

Kim Yerim was already kneeling up on the sofa.

“Can’t we listen?”

“No.”

Bae Joo-hyun walked toward the room.

Son Seung-wan still didn’t move.

As long as she didn’t stand, the interrogation perhaps could not officially commence.

Regrettably, this naive theory of law carried no weight against the authority of the captain.

Bae Joo-hyun slid the door open and turned back.

“Wendy.”

The voice, this time, had gone much softer.

It raised goosebumps on Son Seung-wan instead.

Resigned to her fate, she stood and dragged her feet toward the room.

Passing Park Soo-young, she collected an encouraging fist-pump.

It did not help.

At the doorway, Son Seung-wan attempted to negotiate leniency.

“Unnie, I really never said anything.”

Bae Joo-hyun rested her hand on the handle.

“So you really DID see.”

Son Seung-wan’s foot stopped outside the threshold.

A minute ago it had been one guilty glance.

Now the testimony had been completed with her own mouth.

In the living room, three voices sounded in unison.

“Ohhh!”

Park Soo-young hugged the cushion tighter.

Kim Yerim was already up off the sofa.

Kang Seul-gi caught up a beat late, a fresh thirst for knowledge on her face.

Son Seung-wan closed her eyes.

She might not be leaving that room today.

Bae Joo-hyun kept smiling and tipped her head toward the inside.

“In.”

The door swung slowly shut under the burning gazes of the other three.

In the last second before it sealed, Son Seung-wan’s final ineffective defense was still faintly audible.

“Unnie, I only kept quiet to protect you—”

Click.

The door locked.

Cushion in her arms, Park Soo-young suddenly realized something.

Baek Si-on’s so-called ideal type had not been an offhand invention.

She turned to the other two.

“Should we... go eavesdrop?”

Kim Yerim was already tiptoeing toward the door.

“Hurry.”

Kang Seul-gi was still hesitating.

Park Soo-young and Kim Yerim hooked her from either side and simply lifted her along.

The three of them pressed along the wall toward the bedroom.

Through the door, faintly, came Bae Joo-hyun’s voice.

“From the beginning.”
