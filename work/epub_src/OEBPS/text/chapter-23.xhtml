Chapter Twenty-Three

An Old Acquaintance and a New Number

June Outside the building Twenty-odd people with banners

Si-on didn't take the front entrance. Out front, twenty-odd people with banners held their phones up like it was a press line. A buzz-cut man in a flower-print shirt squeezing through the crowd into the DSP building — tomorrow's headline would write itself.

Not worth it.

He swung into the alley at the side instead. The back door was a grey fire door, not fully closed, propped on a flattened cardboard box. Beside it stood a man in a black polo, wearing a badge on a blue lanyard, the standard DSP issue. Early thirties, not much product in his hair but enough to tell he'd made an effort.

Si-on knew him. A group manager from the A'ST1 period — not the man who'd handled them directly, but the guy from the neighbouring group, same floor, someone you passed every day. Surname Park. Park-what, he couldn't dig up.

But the other man recognised him first. A cigarette pinched between his fingers, he froze mid-air, squinting for two seconds.

“…Baek Si-on?”

Si-on nodded.

“Park sunbae-nim. It's been a long time.”

He chose a safe greeting.

Manager Park blew out smoke, sizing him up. His gaze paused on the buzz cut, paused on the flower shirt, and finally settled on the face that had clearly thinned out.

“Enlisted, did you?”

“Three months ago.”

“Huh.”

Park drew the cigarette to his lips, took a drag, the tip flaring. The smoke was snatched up and scattered by a crosswind through the alley.

“Barely recognised you.”

He said it like he meant it.

But Si-on wasn't sure whether that “barely recognised you” was because the man's whole demeanour had changed after coming over, or because the name A'ST1 itself now needed an archaeologist to unearth. Almost certainly the latter.

“What brings you here? Not thinking of coming back to the company, are you?”

Lightly spoken. But not a casual question. This year Kris had run, Nicole had left, Jiyoung had left — the whole industry was riding a wave of contract exits. People were scrambling to get out, and here was someone voluntarily walking up to the door. Anybody would read a second layer into that.

Si-on nearly laughed. He glanced toward the front gate. Banners, hand banners, the chant — all of it faintly audible through the building's bulk.

“Park sunbae-nim, with all that out front, do I look like I came to clock in?”

Park choked on the retort. He looked down at the cigarette burning down to its filter, let out a short laugh — an admission that yes, the question had been a stupid one.

“Fair enough.”

Si-on didn't circle with him any longer.

“I'm looking for someone. Rainbow — Kim Jae-kyung. The number I had doesn't connect anymore. Do you have it, Park sunbae-nim?”

“Kim Jae-kyung?”

Park's cigarette hand drew a small circle in the air, as if flipping through an internal contacts list.

“…She hasn't been coming into the company much lately, stays at the dorm. What do you want with her?”

“Personal.”

Park didn't press. He turned the screen around. Si-on looked, tapped the number into his own phone.

“Thanks, Park sunbae-nim.”

“Don't mention it.”

Park crushed the cigarette out on an iron pipe at the base of the wall, flicked it toward the bin lid. Missed; it hit the ground. He didn't pick it up.

Si-on turned to go.

“Baek Si-on.”

Park leaned against the wall, head tilted, looking at him.

“If you really don't plan on coming back—”

He waved a hand.

“Never mind. Forget it. Go make the most of it.”

Si-on looked at him a moment. Didn't press. Nodded, and walked.

June The narrow alley A phone that no longer connects

The alley was narrow, the space between its two walls barely two metres. Sunlight only slipped in through the seam overhead, landing in a small square at his feet. Si-on leaned his back against a white box truck parked opposite and looked down at the number he'd just saved.

He dialled.

Three rings.

Picked up.

What came through first wasn't a voice but a clatter of knocking and hammering.

“Hello?”

“Jae-kyung, it's me, Baek Si-on. Out of the army.”

The hammering stopped.

“Si-on sunbae-nim?!”

Kim Jae-kyung's voice shot up half an octave.

“When did you get out?!”

Si-on glanced at the phone screen. Three eleven in the afternoon.

“…I've got some business. Where are you?”

“I'm at the dorm, rushing a batch of leather wallets… client's on a deadline, due the day after tomorrow.”

She paused.

“There's a café downstairs, or if you don't mind climbing, just come up. The others all went out for errands today, it's just me.”

“Send me the address.”

“You got it!”

He hung up. Ten seconds later a message popped in.

The address.

Si-on read it, exited, and stepped out of the alley to flag a cab. On the way, he had the driver stop at a corner fruit-and-vegetable shop, went in, and came out with a box of watermelon.

Pre-cut, sealed in clear plastic, two disposable wooden chopsticks banded to the lid with a rubber band.

You didn't show up at someone's door empty-handed. He'd been poor in the other life, but the manners hadn't gone.

✿ ✿ ✿

June Nonhyeon-dong, fourth floor Room 402, a doorbell

Rainbow's dorm was in Nonhyeon-dong. Fourth floor, no lift. Si-on lugged the watermelon with one hand, hauled himself up by the handrail with the other.

Room 402. He rang the bell.

The door opened. It was Kim Jae-kyung. Hair thrown up in a messy bun, flyaway wisps held back by a headband. A faded grey tee, sleeves rolled to the shoulder, a small strip of plaster wrapped around her left index finger.

Kim Jae-kyung

Singer · Dancer · Leader of Rainbow

Rainbow's leader, who still makes things by hand

Rainbow's leader and main dancer, a former JYP trainee who never stopped being a craftsperson — the leather bags she turns out carry no logo, only her own two hands.

She saw Si-on and, before her smile could finish forming, ran her eyes down him from head to toe. Then her eyebrows climbed.

“…Sunbae-nim?”

The Si-on in her memory was A'ST1's cute one. Round face, eyes that curved when he smiled, blowing hearts and doing aegyo on stage — fans tagged him “little brother,” “soft,” “puppy.”

The man in front of her —

Buzz cut. A hard jawline, a thin tan over the cheekbones. Flower-print shirt clinging damp and creased to him, sleeves binding the arms, a full size broader than she remembered. And the eyes.

It had been soft before.

It wasn't now.

Kim Jae-kyung gave a heartfelt sigh.

“The army really does rebuild a person.”

Si-on didn't take it up. He held the watermelon out.

“Take it. Now, business.”

She took the watermelon and stepped aside to let him in.

June Jae-kyung's room The handmade bag, on the sofa back

Inside, the smell of leather and glue hit you square in the face. If nobody told you this was a girl group's dorm, anyone walking in would assume they'd wandered into a handmade-leather shop off an alley in Hongdae.

Si-on stood in the doorway two seconds, letting his eyes circle the room. They landed, at last, on a bag slung over the back of the sofa.

Shoulder bag, leather, a colour somewhere between caramel and cinnamon. No logo, no extra hardware, just a single copper magnetic clasp at the mouth and a narrow hand-woven strap at one side.

Hand-stitched · no brand · one of one

A single-shoulder bag in tannery leather, the colour of caramel run through with cinnamon. No logo, no metal clutter — just a copper magnet clasp at the mouth and a narrow strap braided by hand.

hand-stitched seam · copper clasp · braided strap

The stitching is off by a stitch or two on the back panel. The arc is right, and so is the sheen. It's a thing made for no one in particular — which is exactly why it belongs to someone.

He took his eyes off the bag and looked at Jae-kyung. She was busy shoving components across the coffee table to clear a palm-sized spot, and setting the watermelon down in it.

“Sit, sunbae-nim. Shove that pile of leather over, don't mind it.”

Si-on didn't sit. He leaned on the doorframe, arms folded.

“I've got one thing to ask.”

“Go ahead.”

“Do you know any independent music producers? Singer-songwriter types, not attached to a company.”

Jae-kyung had been peeling the rubber band off the watermelon. She stopped. She looked up at Si-on, then around at the room full of leather, tools, and shavings. Then she spread her hands, helpless.

“Sunbae-nim, look at this place—”

She gestured with her chin at the pile of cutting knives and waxed thread on the dining table.

“Everyone I know right now is a Hongdae leather wholesaler or a hardware supplier. Not one music producer.”

Si-on nodded. No look of disappointment. No follow-up.

Jae-kyung wound the rubber band around her fingers, snapped it twice, then suddenly remembered something.

“But—”

“If you really want that kind of connection, try Hara sunbae-nim.”

“Goo Hara?”

“Mm. Hara sunbae-nim's been wanting to go solo herself for a while — she's been building up resources outside the company for the past two years. She's got a lot of independent producers and underground rappers saved up.”

Si-on considered it.

Goo Hara.

KARA.

Down on the street, the girls with the banners were shouting her teammates' names.

“All right. Give me her number.”

Jae-kyung pulled her phone from her pocket, flicked through, and turned the screen around. Si-on read it, saved it.

“Thanks.”

He put the phone back. Then, as if in passing, his gaze drifted to the bag on the sofa back again. He tilted his head.

“That bag—”

“Huh?”

Jae-kyung followed his eyes.

“That? That's just something I made to pass the time. The stitching's not even that good—”

“It's good.”

He walked over, lifted the bag off the sofa back, and turned it over to look at the back panel. A stitch or two off. But the sheen was right, the arc was right, and the braiding on the strap was clean.

Si-on slung the bag over his shoulder and tried it. Not too big, not too small.

Right for Eun-ah.

“My cousin just finished her trainee life. I'm getting her a present. Made by hand like this — you can't buy it anywhere.”

Jae-kyung stared at him, a little stunned.

“If you want it, just take it. We go way back—”

She didn't finish. Si-on pulled a wad from his pocket. Fifty-thousand-won notes, he didn't count, just peeled off a stack and set it down on the coffee table.

“No need.”

Jae-kyung's eyes went wide.

“Sunbae-nim—?! This bag's worth maybe three—”

“What it's worth is not up to you.”

He took the bag off his shoulder, tucked it under his arm, and walked to the door.

“The buyer decides.”

Jae-kyung opened her mouth to push the money back, then was afraid of offence; wanted to keep it, then thought it too much. She stood frozen in place, caught between the two.

Si-on already had the door open. One foot out, then a pause.

“You make good things. Stop calling it practice.”

The door shut. Footsteps sounded in the stairwell, level by level, growing fainter.

Jae-kyung stood there, looked down at the stack of notes on the table, then up at the sofa back. The bag that had hung there for three months — gone, just like that.

“…What a strange guy.”

She muttered it to herself, bent down, lifted the watermelon lid, and popped a piece in her mouth. Her eyes narrowed.

Sweet.

Really sweet.
