Chapter One Hundred Ninety Nine

Taking CJ to Ask SM for a Quote

March 30 · a bedroom, then the Gangnam Tax Office, then CJ CheilJedang · the day after the birthday A cat who scratched once and formally gave up · three minutes of emergency cosmetics · a signature session that grew a wooden frame · 3.2 billion won and a plaque · a deputy director’s résumé, quietly burnished · three sample cups and an idea two summers early · a text flown across an ocean: don’t be too happy Jin-ri’s apartment → Gangnam Tax Office → CJ CheilJedang R&D Center → Los Angeles, 3 a.m.

The first one awake that morning was Blin.

The Sphynx stretched in his cat bed, ambled to the bedroom door, and scratched the panel a few times with one paw.

The door didn’t open.

Blin held a three-second silence, turned, and withdrew to the living room.

It was the noise of this retreat that woke Jin-ri.

The first thing she registered was warmth. An arm lying across her waist from behind; breathing at the back of her head.

Very real.

Real enough that Jin-ri finally believed last night had not been a hallucination from wishing too hard.

She opened her eyes, studied the man beside her for a few seconds, and last night’s frames floated back up like crushed light.

Last night, as replayed behind her eyelids

The doorbell.

“Happy birthday.”

The song.

The kiss.

The elevator.

The words: stay.

And then—

Her heartbeat picked up speed again.

But she didn’t move.

Because she couldn’t tell whether Baek Si-on was awake.

If he was awake, she had no idea which face to wear.

If he wasn’t, she wanted to stay like this a while longer.

But very quickly, an extremely practical problem presented itself.

Her current appearance was probably not fit to be seen.

Which was absurd. The two of them had already gone past that step, and she was still worried about her morning face.

But Jin-ri was worried. She wanted the first thing his open eyes found to be beautiful.

So she carefully lifted his hand off her waist, eased the blanket back, and set her feet on the floor.

The moment they landed, her knees sent up a soft, aching protest. Seven falls at the training base yesterday, plus last night — in short, not one joint from waist to knee was willing to help her pretend nothing was wrong.

Her palm smacked the nightstand with a muffled thump of moderate volume.

Jin-ri spun to look at Baek Si-on.

Still on his side. Breathing unchanged.

She exhaled, braced on the wall, and shuffled into the bathroom emitting as close to no sound as physics allowed.

❧ ❧ ❧

The bathroom mirror reported: hair in mild chaos, lips dry, last night’s tear tracks still faintly at the corners of her eyes — and skin that was, annoyingly, luminous.

She kept the water at a whisper. Face washed, teeth brushed, Jin-ri stared into the mirror for three seconds — then pulled a cushion compact out of the drawer under the sink.

Three minutes later, having finally confirmed she stood inside the safe zone — the zone in which, if he said you’re wearing makeup, she could righteously say no — she crept back into the bedroom.

Baek Si-on held his original position.

Jin-ri lifted one corner of the blanket, installed herself inside his arm’s reach, adjusted, closed her eyes, and faked sleep.

Baek Si-on, in fact, had been awake since the cat scratched the door.

He simply hadn’t opened his eyes.

When he heard her wall-assisted shuffle to the bathroom, the whisper of water, the toothbrush meeting the cup, the drawer sliding open — Baek Si-on had a fairly precise picture of what she was doing.

Only when Jin-ri had burrowed back under the blanket and tuned her breathing light and even did he slowly open his eyes.

“You got fragrant.”

He buried his head in her hair.

Jin-ri’s fingers secretly strangled the sheet beneath the blanket.

Acting, she thought. I am the girlfriend of a Venice Best Actor. I cannot lose at fake-sleeping.

“Your heartbeat is very loud.”

“……”

Jin-ri finally could not sustain the performance.

She opened her eyes and glared at him, ferociously — for less than one second, at which point the proximity shattered all ferocity and left only a blush.

“When did you wake up?”

“When you got out of bed.”

“Then why didn’t you say so?”

“I thought you were executing a secret mission.”

“……”

Jin-ri pulled the blanket up.

“I was washing up.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“And incidentally applied makeup?”

“No.”

“Then did your eyelashes line up in formation by themselves after you got up?”

“……”

Jin-ri swatted him, furious — with roughly the force of last night’s entryway swats.

Baek Si-on didn’t dodge. One hand propped beside her, he looked down at her, and she looked back up.

Morning light leaked through the curtain gap and lay across his shoulder and the line of his face, tracing his profile with a very clean stroke.

Jin-ri abruptly wanted to touch it.

So she did. Her fingertips traveled from his brow bone down the bridge of his nose, then along the line of his jaw.

Baek Si-on didn’t move. He let her.

She said, small-voiced:

“How are you this good-looking even when you just wake up.”

“That kind of thing, you’re allowed to say louder.”

“…Eh?”

Jin-ri laughed — and midway through the laugh, his descending kiss caught it and kept it.

The morning kiss ran long.

Long enough that she was fairly certain all three minutes of that makeup had just been surrendered onto his lips.

Afterward Jin-ri lay on his chest, listening to his heartbeat, and after a while asked, suddenly:

“What time do you leave today?”

“Depends on when you let go of me.”

Jin-ri tightened her hold on his arm at once.

“Then you may be late today.”

“Do you know tax consultants bill by the hour?”

“Are you poor?”

“Not currently.”

“Then let them wait.”

Having said it, Jin-ri laughed first.

She had rarely been willful like this. More accurately: she had never lacked the ability — she had simply rarely been made to feel that she was allowed.

Baek Si-on watched her laugh and brushed the loose hair off her face.

“Bold, all of a sudden.”

“Because today is the day after the birthday.”

“Birthday privileges can be deferred?”

“They can.”

She nodded, serious.

“When a girl says they can, they can.”

Baek Si-on considered, then asked:

“Deferred until when?”

Jin-ri buried her face back into his chest.

“Until you leave.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Ten in the morning. The Gangnam Tax Office, Seoul.

By the time Baek Si-on’s car stopped at the entrance, Seo Eun-ju was already waiting out front.

“Today’s process is fast,”

she said, walking.

“The tax accountant pre-verified every document. You only need to sign in person.”

“No questions about the source of funds?”

“None. You’re not here to explain. You’re here to pay.”

The two of them walked through the front doors. At the sight of Baek Si-on, a first-floor staffer’s coffee nearly left his hand.

The next second, a team leader from the second floor came personally running down, his smile brighter than the lobby’s fluorescent tubes.

“Baek Si-on-ssi! Welcome, welcome. Please, upstairs — we’ve prepared a dedicated reception room.”

Baek Si-on glanced at Seo Eun-ju.

“Is this a privilege?”

“Not a privilege. Brown-nosing.”

“……”

The team leader led the way, greeting people and clearing a path as he went, and Baek Si-on followed him through the building with the unmistakable sensation of walking a red carpet.

The dedicated reception room was on the third floor.

Inside, already seated: the tax accountant, a section chief, and a deputy director who had visibly been summoned on short notice to lend the room weight.

The dedicated reception room, as prepared

Coffee — four kinds.

Fruit — three plates, cut.

One box of mints that appeared to have been purchased, at retail, from the convenience store across the street.

“Baek Si-on-ssi, please, sit.”

The deputy director rose to shake hands personally. Baek Si-on sat; Seo Eun-ju sat beside him. The tax accountant opened a folder and began.

“S.W Studio, in Baek Si-on-ssi’s name — consolidated settlement for fiscal 2014 and the first quarter of 2015, complete.”

“Give me the numbers.”

The tax accountant cleared his throat.

“S.W Studio’s taxable income for the period converts to roughly sixteen billion won. After lawful deductions — operating costs, performance production, staff salaries, legal expenses, charitable donations, vehicle and equipment depreciation —”

“actual Korean personal income tax plus local surcharges comes to two point four eight billion won, combined.”

Baek Si-on’s expression was nothing at all. Seo Eun-ju gave one small nod beside him.

The tax accountant continued:

“Additionally, a first-quarter tax reserve account has been provisionally funded at seven hundred and twenty million won, for the annual consolidated settlement and foreign-income credit reconciliation.”

“Three point two billion, together.”

Baek Si-on took the pen from Seo Eun-ju’s hand:

“Where do I sign?”

“Here, here — and here.”

On the third page, the deputy director abruptly rose, opened the cabinet at the side of the room, and took out a wooden frame.

The frame, as produced mid-signature

Inside: one sheet of stiff board, gold letters, printed large.

— EXCELLENT YOUTH TAXPAYER OF THE REPUBLIC OF KOREA · CERTIFICATE OF APPRECIATION —

“The first commendation plaque our office has issued this year,” the deputy director said, presenting it with both hands. “You win glory for the country abroad and fulfill your tax obligations at home. A model for our region’s young cultural-industry workers.”

Baek Si-on looked at the plaque.

Then he looked at Seo Eun-ju.

Seo Eun-ju said, expressionless:

“Sign for it. It can sit at the studio front desk. It looks better than the business license.”

The deputy director laughed: “Lawyer Seo is so witty.”

Baek Si-on signed the last page. The tax accountant closed the folder.

“That completes the process. The funds will be withheld within three business days. You’re free to go.”

The deputy director stood and walked them out personally.

“Any tax questions from now on — call me directly.”

He handed over a business card. Baek Si-on accepted it, read the title — Deputy Director, Gangnam Tax Office — and passed it to Seo Eun-ju, who gave it one glance and bagged it.

Outside the front doors, Baek Si-on’s phone buzzed once. The sender: S.W Studio finance.

The phone, as it buzzed on the steps

S.W STUDIO FINANCE — Seoul-region personal income tax withheld complete. Balance-change notice has been sent to your registered email.

Seo Eun-ju opened her bag, checked her own phone, and said:

“Next, CJ.”

“Mm.”

“On the way I’ll send Baek Eun-ah the scanned tax certificate.”

“She might go insane.”

“She’ll find out eventually.”

Seo Eun-ju put the phone away, then added, suddenly:

“Oh — the tax accountant asked me earlier which bracket your personal income rate was filed under.”

“What did you say?”

“I said the top bracket.”

“And then?”

“And then the deputy director said, ‘As expected of the nation’s pride. So conscientious.’”

“……”

The car waited at the curb. Baek Si-on pulled the door open, and looked up once more at the tax office building before getting in.

From this angle, the building was indistinguishable from any ordinary government office. But the respect he had felt inside it was nothing like what he had felt last year, in SM’s legal department.

He got in.

“To CJ.”

The car pulled out. In the side mirror, the tax office shrank. Seo Eun-ju flipped through her files, then said, abruptly:

“That deputy director — I checked. He may be promoted to office director next year.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“So?”

“So today you didn’t pay taxes. You burnished his résumé.”

“Gangnam doesn’t lack chaebols who pay billions. It lacks a world superstar at the height of his heat. He hands you the model-taxpayer plaque, takes one group photo into the National Tax Service’s internal briefing — that helps his promotion next year more than collecting an extra ten billion in taxes ever could.”

Seo Eun-ju closed the file, calm.

“You’ve burnished his record and his goodwill in the same visit. If you ever need anything in Korea, he will be more reliable than any presidential candidate you could meet.”

“…That sounds good.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Two in the afternoon. The CJ CheilJedang food-and-beverage R&D center.

The BodyArmor Asia joint venture had settled its framework in Los Angeles long ago. The toast had happened in LA; the landing had to happen in Seoul. So Baek Si-on hadn’t come today to discuss direction. He had come to push execution.

A row of sample bottles already stood on the conference table — tall, short, fat, thin — with sugar-content data, taste-test reports, and several trend charts of the Asian sports-drink market laid out beside them.

The head of CJ’s food-and-beverage division had arrived; marketing was there too; and BodyArmor’s technical people from the US hung on the wall screen via video link.

When Baek Si-on came in, the CJ division head stood to shake his hand.

The address was not “Baek Si-on-ssi.”

It was “Director Baek.”

Baek Si-on paused a beat — and didn’t correct it.

The meeting went straight to business. The R&D lead handed over three sample cups, labeled A, B, and C.

The sample table, as set

A — the US original formula.

B — the adjusted Asian reduced-sugar build.

C — the electrolyte-fortified build.

Baek Si-on tasted them in order and pointed at the cup marked B:

“How much sugar is down versus the original?”

“Thirty percent,” the R&D lead said.

“Lower still. People care about wellness now.”

“Even lower?”

The R&D lead had been taking notes; at this he set down his pen, hesitation plain on his face.

“Director Baek — lower, and the mouthfeel turns thin. The original’s sweetness is doing structural work. Pull the sugar and it drinks like something watered.”

Baek Si-on looked at him.

The man added immediately:

“Unless something else carries the flavor back.”

“Such as?”

“Fruit. Natural fruit as the flavor base, replacing part of the sugar’s structural support. Watermelon, peach, citrus, tropical — all validated routes in low-sugar sports drinks.”

Baek Si-on considered it, and nodded:

“That works.”

With the base formula settled, the conversation turned naturally to packaging and go-to-market. CJ had clearly prepared thoroughly. A page came up on the projector, and the marketing director tapped it:

“Director Baek as the main endorser is no problem. Your global recognition supports the master brand across all three lines — sports, music, fashion.”

Baek Si-on said nothing.

Praise of this species is always followed by a “but.”

And sure enough, the marketing director turned to the next page.

“But for the youth market, you alone make the brand too strong — and too far away.”

Seo Eun-ju read the English words on the screen and thought: admirably euphemistic.

Translated: Baek Si-on is now too expensive. He does not look like someone who would buy the second-bottle-half-price at a convenience store, standing next to high schoolers.

“So I recommend adding a girl-group co-promotion ambassador.”

Baek Si-on’s first reaction was very Korean.

“Girls’ Generation?”

The marketing director’s face performed tact.

“Girls’ Generation is of course wonderful. But the cost-performance is poor.”

For a moment Baek Si-on genuinely could not tell whether cost-performance meant the quote — or the phrase youth light-beverage line sitting badly next to the group’s average age.

The explanation came quickly. Girls’ Generation: national profile enormous, quote enormous, cooperation chains complicated — built for large, mature brands. The BodyArmor youth line needed freshness, an ascending arc, packaging play, fan participation.

“So we recommend Red Velvet.”

Baek Si-on’s eyes came up.

“Red Velvet?”

“Yes.”

The marketing director had clearly done his homework. In March, ‘Ice Cream Cake’ was released; Kim Ye-rim — Yeri — had joined, establishing the five-member system; a first music-show win had just been taken. The group had topics, controversy, ascending momentum, high willingness to cooperate, and enormous potential.

And, most importantly of all: it was cheap.

“And the name itself — Red Velvet — already has color in it, and dessert.”

Seeing Baek Si-on’s interest, the marketing director poured the idea out while it was warm:

“Say we do five fruit flavors. One member per fruit. Not a girl group holding bottles and smiling — the group’s structure becomes the product’s structure.”

The concept was still rough. No formal deck — an improvisation thrown out mid-meeting.

But Baek Si-on saw the future.

If the occasion had permitted, he would have poured this CJ marketing director a drink himself.

Some ideas look like nothing more than beverage planning.

But two summers from now, they might become a song of the summer.

“This direction works.”

The CJ marketing director’s shoulders dropped a visible centimeter, and he pressed on:

“Shall we approach SM under CJ’s name first? We’ll prepare a first draft of the quote and the portrait-rights scope.”

Baek Si-on shook his head.

“No need.”

The CJ side looked at him.

Baek Si-on picked up his phone.

“S.W Studio contacts SM first. CJ follows with the formal business process.”

“By S.W Studio?”

“Mm.”

He looked down and typed to Baek Eun-ah. It was still deep night in America. But this could go now, and be waiting for her morning.

The messages were short:

I approve the initial direction.

Baek Si-on

You’ll handle SM.

Baek Si-on

Don’t be too happy about it.

❧ ❧ ❧

Los Angeles.

Baek Eun-ah’s phone buzzed on the nightstand.

She had been nearly asleep. The Charlie Puth ‘Marvin Gaye’ radio promo these past days had wrung her out like seaweed dried by capitalism.

She fumbled for the phone and squinted at the screen.

The first: CJ, BodyArmor, the youth line.

The second: Red Velvet, co-promotion.

The third: she — interfacing with SM.

Baek Eun-ah’s eyes opened slowly.

Sleepiness was annihilated within three seconds.

She stared at the two letters for a long time.

A little over half a year ago, those two letters had been what kept her awake at night.

And now —

She was going to represent S.W Studio, carrying CJ CheilJedang’s Asian beverage project, and walk up to SM to ask:

Your rookie girl group Red Velvet — what’s the quote?

A little over half a year ago, the letters S-M were what kept her awake. Today she carries CJ’s beverage project to their front door — to ask the quote.

— Baek Eun-ah, 3 a.m., Los Angeles, reading it a second time to be sure

Baek Eun-ah looked at the phone and suddenly laughed out loud.

She threw off the covers, sat up, switched on the bedside lamp, and replied to Baek Si-on:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Eun-ah

I’ll try not to be happy.

Baek Eun-ah

But it’s a bit difficult.

In the CJ conference room, Baek Si-on read the replies, and the corner of his mouth moved a little.

The CJ marketing director was still waiting for his answer.

Baek Si-on set the phone face-down on the table.

“The head of S.W Studio will contact SM after she wakes.”

The CJ division head nodded.

“Then we’ll sync the proposal, the budget range, and the materials scope to S.W Studio’s business email.”

“……”

The meeting rolled onward. Baek Si-on leaned back in his chair and didn’t say much more.

Some accounts don’t need to be shouted back.

Nor do they need the old grievances dug out and counted, line by line, for the other side to hear.
