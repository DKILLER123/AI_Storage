Chapter Fifty-Four

The Man Who Scooped Justin Bieber

July 26 Afternoon Nonhyeon-dong, Kim Jae-kyung's dorm A name that detonated in the middle of a fitting

“Cousin——”

When Eun-ah pushed the door back open, she could feel a faint, peculiar smell hanging in the air.

But she didn't have time to examine it, because there was something much bigger stuffed in her head:

“Do you know Justin Bieber?”

The moment those words left her, all three of them turned and looked at her.

Justin Bieber.

That name in 2014 was roughly equivalent to a walking nuclear warhead.

Twenty years old. Canadian. Debuted at fifteen and ascended to sainthood on the spot. Over a hundred and fifty million records sold worldwide. In an era when "a billion" was still science fiction for most singers, he'd been the first male artist to break a billion views on YouTube.

But for the last couple of years, this warhead had been crashing down toward the ground.

Arrested for drunk driving. Sued for throwing eggs at a neighbour's house. Detained for racing through Miami. Those were the small things.

The thing that actually blew up was in April.

He visited Yasukuni Shrine in Japan, took a photo, posted it to Instagram, and captioned it with a smiley face.

The fury of Chinese and Korean netizens crashed over him like a tsunami.

Instagram · comment feed · 48 hours

“A criminal against history.” “Apologise.” “Get out of Asia.”

White House “We the People” · petition

Over 270,000 American petitioners demand the deportation of the “dangerous, destructive, substance-abusing” Canadian.

Those words had poured into his comment section at a rate of hundreds per second, flooding the servers until they choked. Eventually he apologised worldwide, deleted the photo, and posted a video statement in a tone of sincere contrition.

But the damage was done.

And even before all that, more than 270,000 American netizens had signed a petition on the White House website demanding that this "dangerous, destructive, substance-abusing" Canadian be deported. The White House was forced to respond formally, setting a record for the "We the People" petition platform's first official response on an entertainment topic.

Compared to that, Baek Si-on's Cheong Wa Dae petition breaking five thousand signatures…

Well.

That wasn't even a warm-up in front of Bieber.

“What about him?” Baek Si-on asked.

Eun-ah stood in the middle of the living room, gripping her phone, and swallowed.

“His manager, Scooter Braun, contacted Jung Jae-joon. He said Justin himself listened to Way Back Home and loved it. He wants you to write new material for him, in the same style.”

“……”

The room went quiet for about three seconds.

After three seconds, Kim Jae-kyung produced a sound somewhere between a scream and a gasp:

“Justin Bieber?! That Justin Bieber?!”

Choi Jin-ri's reaction was different.

She didn't scream.

But the hand holding the measuring tape slowly lowered, and her gaze moved from Eun-ah's face to Baek Si-on's face, and back again.

That was Justin Bieber, after all.

Scandal-ridden or globally apologised or not, that person still sat in the world's top three on Spotify monthly listeners.

And who was his manager, Scooter Braun? The man who'd discovered Bieber, signed Ariana Grande, one of the sharpest music managers on the planet. He wouldn't come knocking because a song was simply "nice."

Choi Jin-ri looked at Baek Si-on.

He should be thrilled.

But Baek Si-on stood in the middle of the room, hands at his sides, and the expression on his face wasn't excitement.

It was blank.

He really was blank.

Not because of the fact of "Justin Bieber heard my song" — though that alone was loud enough to shake the floor.

But because he'd suddenly realised something much bigger.

Tropical house.

On the timeline he remembered, this style had only genuinely broken into the mainstream in 2015.

The signal event was "What Do You Mean?" from Justin Bieber's Purpose album. The groove and melody of tropical house got grafted onto the biggest pop star on the planet, and overnight it went from a niche thing in underground electronic circles to a dominant force on the Billboard charts.

Before that.

Tropical house's standard-bearers were independent DJs like Kygo, Thomas Jack.

And now.

Baek Si-on had released Way Back Home in July 2014.

The YouTube channel MrSuicideSheep had titled the track:

YouTube · MrSuicideSheep · editorial pick

01 — “This track from an unknown Korean singer might be the best Tropical House song of 2014.”

Not “one of.” The best.

He'd only meant to copy a song he'd heard in his past life to scrape together a living.

And somehow he'd copied it too early.

So early that the very person who was supposed to define the genre was now coming to him for a song.

It was too absurd.

Absurd enough that Baek Si-on couldn't produce a single appropriate expression on his face.

“Cousin?”

Eun-ah was staring at him.

Kim Jae-kyung was staring at him.

Choi Jin-ri was staring at him.

The three of them waited for about five seconds.

Baek Si-on came back to himself.

His expression was as steady as if those five seconds of processed nothing had never happened.

“Let's finish the measurements first.”

Eun-ah was almost choked by those five words.

“Cousin! That's Justin Bieber!”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“Eun-ah, remember this: chasing after a thing doesn't make a deal.”

“For me, a well-fitting suit is the most important thing in front of me right now.”

Then he turned to Choi Jin-ri.

“Continue.”

Choi Jin-ri blinked, and looked back down to find the position of the leg measurement.

The pressure had inexplicably transferred over to the other person, the one who'd been recording numbers all along.

Kim Jae-kyung, of course, knew exactly where Baek Si-on was going to wear this outfit.

The Venice International Film Festival. The red carpet. Broadcast worldwide. Hundreds of cameras firing at him at once, thousands of media outlets spreading the photographs across the entertainment sections of the planet the next morning.

Every inch of fabric on Baek Si-on's body, every seam, every detail of the cut, would be scrutinised under high-definition lenses.

And that suit was made by her.

Kim Jae-kyung didn't dare go further down that thought.

But she couldn't stop herself from going further down it, either.

She was a proper graduate of a fashion-design programme, after all. Being an idol was just the day job.

And with Rainbow's situation what it was, better to… than to sit in a dorm waiting for a comeback notice that might never come.

“Jae-kyung?”

Baek Si-on's voice pulled her back.

“Mm! Here!”

She quickly dropped her head and filled in the numbers Choi Jin-ri had just called out.

Once the last of Baek Si-on's measurements was done, Kim Jae-kyung shut the notebook and went through every number from the top.

Choi Jin-ri's weren't done yet. The women's was the fussier one.

You had to account for whether the underarm fabric would catch or show skin when she raised her hand to wave on the red carpet; how deep the neckline could go to be most alluring without veering into anything too suggestive; at least thirty-plus measurements, all told.

“Sunbae, any preferences on the material?”

“Only the best.”

Kim Jae-kyung understood.

Respect the clothes first, then the man. You go out there, and you don't get looked down on by those white people.

“If we use nothing but the best… top-tier imported Italian wool, Loro Piana or Zegna cloth — just the fabric alone is six to eight hundred thousand won. Silk lining, hand-stitching thread, natural horn buttons, plus the other materials and the loss from pattern-making…”

She bit the end of her pencil, looked up:

“One suit, the hardware cost is roughly one-and-a-half to two million…”

“Five million.”

Kim Jae-kyung's mouth was still open, the next word not yet out, when it got cut off.

Five million, per suit.

Two suits, ten million.

If he went to one of those real high-end atelier houses in Cheongdam-dong for the same red-carpet suit, the starting price was twenty million won.

But for someone with no brand, no name, making handmade goods in a DSP dorm with a sewing machine she'd bought with her own savings —

that was the highest market-fair rate in the industry.

“Sunbae, this is too much. Three million would really be—”

“It's settled.”

Baek Si-on's tone held no room for discussion, and then he turned to Choi Jin-ri:

“Your company will let you go.”

“Don't worry.”

He added two more words.

Then he gave Kim Jae-kyung a small nod.

“Sorry to put you through the trouble. There's something I need to do, so I'll head off.”

The door swung shut on its spring.

The room was quiet for two seconds.

Eun-ah was the first to snap out of it:

“Cousin! What about the Justin Bieber thing?! Cousin! Wait for me!”

She was out the door in a whirlwind.

Left behind, Choi Jin-ri slowly rolled the measuring tape up, loop after loop, very carefully.

Her gaze fell to her own fingers.

The nails were still bare, not a single colour on them.

She suddenly thought.

For Venice, maybe she should get a manicure.
