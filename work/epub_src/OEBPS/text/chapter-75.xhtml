Chapter Seventy-Five

Justice Is Late, So Truth Can Be Too

August 27 9:00 a.m. Giudecca · Cipriani Hotel suite Fashion houses at the door, and a grey suit chosen in ten minutes

August 27. Venice. Giudecca Island, the Cipriani Hotel.

Nine in the morning. The suite doorbell rang on the dot.

The door opened from inside.

The person answering was a young man — black T-shirt, jeans, a pair of small ear studs.

Park Ji-hun.

He said nothing, stepped aside, and gestured them in.

The PR teams from several top luxury houses filed in, trailed by assistants pushing mobile racks and a dedicated tailor carrying a needle-and-thread kit.

Just as the PR lead opened her mouth to say "Good morning, we've brought—", Park Ji-hun had already hauled a suit off the second rack.

"Boss Baek, come try this on."

Baek Si-on emerged from the bedroom, wearing the hotel's white bathrobe, hair still wet from a shower.

"Check the size first."

Baek Si-on took it and went back into the bedroom.

In under three minutes, he was out again.

A grey double-breasted suit, white shirt, no tie. Trouser creases dead straight, the cuffs just covering the ankle.

Park Ji-hun circled him once.

His gaze ran from the shoulder line down to the waist, then from the sleeve length to the trouser length.

"Shoulder width's right. The waist needs to come in two centimetres. Sleeve's half a centimetre long, and the trousers are a touch long too."

He looked back at the tailor from the Armani team who was carrying the sewing kit.

"Sorry to trouble you."

The tailor came to, quickly grabbed the pins, crouched, and dotted two marks along the trouser hem. Then stood, walked around behind Baek Si-on, and made marks at the waist.

Park Ji-hun walked to the suitcase, pulled out a Jaeger-LeCoultre watch and a pair of Church's oxford shoes.

"Pair these with it."

He handed the watch to Baek Si-on and set the shoes on the carpet.

Baek Si-on put on the watch, bent down, and changed into the shoes.

Park Ji-hun stepped back two paces, swept him from head to toe, and nodded.

"Good."

Then he turned to the brand PR lead.

"Send it back to the room after the alterations. Before three this afternoon. Thank you."

"Ah… all right, no problem."

She squeezed out a professional smile and had her team begin packing the racks.

All of it in under ten minutes.

August 27 Midday Cipriani Hotel suite · living room Grey, and why — a lecture in two lights

In the suite's living room, Baek Si-on changed back into the bathrobe, sat on the sofa, and drank his coffee.

Baek Eun-ah couldn't help asking Park Ji-hun.

"With so many colours, why grey of all things? I hadn't even looked at the other suits before you'd settled on it."

Park Ji-hun, crouching on the floor tidying the accessory boxes the houses had left.

"I looked it up last night."

"In Venice this season, sunset is around seven-thirty in the evening."

"When Boss Baek walks the carpet, the daylight will be that warm gold. But the press area on either side — hundreds of cameras' flash firing at once — that's high-colour-temperature cool white."

"Layer the two lights, and the colour of the clothes shifts badly in the lens."

Baek Eun-ah blinked.

She'd never once considered this.

Park Ji-hun stood, setting the tidied boxes on the coffee table.

"Black looks dead under a strong flash. Navy throws off a strange stray light. Light colours are unkind to Asian skin — easily washed out."

"Only grey is the optimal answer."

"A neutral tone that won't distort under a mix of warm and cool light. The matte finish of the wool blend doesn't reflect, so no matter the lens it holds steady."

Baek Eun-ah went quiet a moment after hearing it.

"You can't leave."

"Huh?"

"I'm re-signing you!"

Park Ji-hun laughed once.

"Boss Baek not getting sick of me would be enough."

✿ ✿ ✿

August 27 Afternoon → evening Cipriani Hotel · the gown An eleven-step face, and a dress that took the whole afternoon

Compared to Baek Si-on's side, all swiftness, Choi Jin-ri's side had nothing of the speed.

The scale of a woman's red-carpet styling wasn't even on the same dimension as a man's.

The makeup had to be done in layers:

Primer, foundation, concealer, setting powder, contour, highlight, blush, eyeshadow, eyeliner, false lashes, lip.

Each layer had to wait for the one before to dry before it could be built up, or the wear time would drop sharply.

The hair took even longer. Just the wash-and-blow was forty minutes.

The styling team SM had flown in from Seoul also had to decide, based on the neckline of the dress chosen for that night, whether to pin the hair up, side-braid it, or let it fall — each option carrying a different curl and wave.

Jewellery, shoes, clutch, and nail colour all had to match the gown.

Three gowns in a morning of fittings.

After choosing, the alterations, pressing, and accessory adjustments consumed a further entire afternoon.

✿ ✿ ✿

August 27 6:02 p.m. Cipriani Hotel · the lobby A grey suit, a silver watch, and an elevator that goes down

The lobby on the ground floor of the Cipriani.

The marble floor was polished till it could show a person's reflection. Light from the crystal chandeliers poured down from three metres overhead, coating the hall in a warm gold.

Baek Si-on stood in the centre of the lobby.

Grey double-breasted suit, Church's oxford shoes on the marble, the leather polished by Park Ji-hun to a shine.

The Jaeger-LeCoultre on his wrist showed half a dial, the silver hands pointing at 6:02.

Baek Jeong-hun stood beside him.

The director had tidied himself up for once today. His hair combed back with wax, showing the face carved by post-production editing — the nasolabial folds two levels deeper than his actual age.

Lee Seung-cheol walked over from the other end of the lobby, steps quick.

"Director Baek, Mr. Baek. The boat's already waiting at the dock."

He glanced around.

"And Miss Choi Jin-ri?"

"Not down yet." Baek Jeong-hun checked his watch.

Lee Seung-cheol said no urging words.

A female actor's red-carpet styling involved three to five times the steps of a male actor's — being a few minutes late was the norm.

Especially since Choi Jin-ri's styling team had been flown in from Seoul by SM. Unfamiliar with the place, they'd take a few more minutes in the suite. Perfectly normal.

The people in the lobby waited each on their own.

Baek Eun-ah scrolled her phone by the floor-to-ceiling window. Park Ji-hun leaned against a pillar, turning over the photos of Baek Si-on's fitting he'd shot on his phone.

About three minutes passed.

Then the floor indicator above the elevator began to tick.

Five. Four. Three. Two. One.

Ding.

The elevator arrived.

The doors slid open from the centre, outward.

Choi Jin-ri stepped out.

The low murmur of conversation in the lobby broke, for one visible second.

A deep-blue Dior off-the-shoulder gown.

The satin sheen flowed slightly with every step she took, drawing a single clean contour line from shoulder to ankle, with no extra fold anywhere.

On the bare shoulders and collarbone, no adornment at all.

Nothing needed.

Those two stretches of shoulder line extending upward from the deep-blue satin were, in themselves, the finest ornament.

The Venice look red carpet · August 27

Him Grey double-breasted suit, white shirt, no tie. Trouser creases dead straight, cuffs just covering the ankle. A Jaeger-LeCoultre and a pair of polished Church's oxfords.
Her A deep-blue Dior off-the-shoulder gown. Satin sheen flowing with every step, a single clean contour from shoulder to ankle, nothing on the collarbone. Nothing needed.

The grey double-breasted, white shirt, no tie — red carpet, August 27.

The deep-blue Dior off-the-shoulder — the gown made by hand.

All of it chosen and altered in under a morning; the dress alone cost an afternoon.

Choi Jin-ri walked up to Baek Si-on and Baek Jeong-hun on her high heels, lowered her head slightly, and said, a little apologetically:

"Sorry. A little late."

Baek Jeong-hun was just about to say "no problem."

"Justice is late too."

Baek Si-on's voice cut in first.

Baek Jeong-hun's mouth stayed open in the shape of the words, tilted his head to look at Baek Si-on.

The expression read "what are you saying."

Choi Jin-ri blinked too.

Baek Eun-ah, off to the side, rolled her eyes once, caught on, and instantly supplied the second half:

"So Truth can be late?"

Baek Si-on didn't answer.

But he didn't deny it either.

Choi Jin-ri looked at him, pressed her lips, a smile not quite unfolded hiding beneath the matte-red lip colour.

Baek Jeong-hun shook his head, gave up on understanding the deep meaning of the sentence, and turned toward the entrance.

"Come on, come on — any later and the boat won't wait."

Lee Seung-cheol followed behind, arm extended toward the lobby exit.

"Everyone, this way."

The group crossed the lobby and out the hotel's rear door.

The hotel's private dock was just behind, a stone jetty running out about fifteen metres, at the end of which was moored a white water taxi.

Baek Jeong-hun boarded first.

Lee Seung-cheol followed.

Baek Eun-ah and Park Ji-hun boarded too, then SM's manager.

Baek Si-on walked to the end of the jetty and stepped up onto the boat's deck in one stride.

He steadied.

Then turned around.

Choi Jin-ri stood at the edge of the jetty.

One hand lifting the hem of the gown, the other hanging at her side, her gaze fixed on the gap of about thirty centimetres between the deck and the jetty.

Baek Si-on reached out a hand.

Choi Jin-ri looked up at him once.

Then put her hand in his and stepped over the gap.

The moment her heel landed on the deck, the boat rocked.

Not much.

Enough to tip her balance forward a little.

Baek Si-on's free left hand came up instantly, pressing at her waistline through the Dior satin.

Choi Jin-ri steadied.

But Baek Si-on's hand didn't withdraw right away.

It lingered a second longer.

Or two.

Choi Jin-ri raised her eyes.

The distance between the two of them was about twenty centimetres.

The Venice evening light came up from behind his shoulders, gilding the outline of his grey suit.

She looked at his face.

He looked at her eyes.

"Can we cast off?"

The boatman tilted his head at them, one hand on the helm, an expression impossible to tell whether it was urging or enjoying the show.

Choi Jin-ri reacted as if someone had hit an eject button.

She took half a step back, dropped her head, and sat down beside Baek Eun-ah.

Face turned toward the lagoon, not looking at anyone.

But from Baek Eun-ah's angle, she could see the tips of her ears were red.

Baek Si-on sat down opposite her, crossed a leg, and looked toward the far shore of the lagoon too.

Expression normal.

As if nothing had happened.

The boatman shook his head, muttered something unintelligible in Italian, and turned the key.

August 27 Dusk Venice lagoon → Lido Island pier Fifteen minutes, and the red carpet ahead

The boat ran about fifteen minutes.

The Lido pier came into view ahead.

People crowded the dock.

Baek Si-on could see the entrance to the red carpet.

A dark-red carpet ran from the pier up to the main door of the Palazzo del Cinema, both sides lined with waist-high metal barriers, and behind the barriers stood a wall of press photographers.

Hundreds of cameras' telephoto lenses rose like a row of black cannon muzzles, all trained on the direction of the carpet.

Flashes had begun firing sporadically, like distant stars testing the light early.

The boat came alongside.

The boatman tied the line to an iron post on the pier.

Baek Jeong-hun stood up first, stepped onto the stone steps, turned back, and reached out a hand to pull Lee Seung-cheol up.

Baek Eun-ah and Park Ji-hun also stepped ashore, standing at the side of the pier, out of sight of the press area.

SM's manager went up too, walked a few steps aside, and pulled out his phone to shoot.

Baek Si-on stood.

Walked to the bow.

One stride onto the gangplank, one more onto the stone paving of the pier.

He steadied.

Then turned.

Choi Jin-ri was still on the boat.

She looked at Baek Si-on, and Baek Si-on looked at her.

He reached out a hand.

The same gesture as on the Giudecca dock a moment ago.

Choi Jin-ri stood, lifted the hem of the gown, walked to the bow, placed her hand in his, crossed the gangplank, and stepped onto the pier.

The instant her heel landed on the stone.

The hundreds of cameras behind the barriers on either side pressed their shutters at once.

Click click click click click click click click click click.

The flashes flared from every side at the same moment, white beams crossing over the two of them, lighting the little patch of pier brighter than noon.

Choi Jin-ri's hand was still in Baek Si-on's.

She didn't let go.

Baek Si-on didn't let go either.

Surrounded by hundreds of cameras, hand in hand, they stood on the Lido pier, in the Venice dusk of late August.
