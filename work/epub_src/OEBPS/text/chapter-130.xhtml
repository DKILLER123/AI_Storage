The Unscientific Album Pre-Order

The next day · Seoul time 2:03 a.m. Seoul · asleep — except for the people who get paid to stay awake A chart refreshes on the other side of the planet, and half the Korean internet stops pretending to sleep.

Seoul. Two in the morning.

At the instant the Billboard website updated, Korea was still asleep.

At least — every normal person should have been asleep.

But entertainment reporters are not normal.

Finance reporters are not normal.

And the overnight editors at every major portal are the least normal of all.

At 2:03 a.m., when they refreshed the chart, their first reaction was not a scream.

It was the suspicion that their eyes were lying to them.

Billboard Hot 100. United States.

Number one.

Baek Si-on — Legend.

Ten minutes later, the first breaking story went out.

The same morning 7:40 a.m. Seoul · Gangnam · a coffee shop in the morning rush A barista with strong opinions, one national anthem, and an office worker who forgot she was going to work.

The customers in line froze for a second.

Then somebody laughed.

An office woman with an iced americano in hand looked down at the news on her phone and, without quite deciding to, hummed softly along:

“Won’t stop till we’re legends…”

She didn’t know why her blood felt a little hot.

She was just going to work.

In a few hours she would be facing her boss, the daily report, the KPI meeting, and the profound question of what to eat for lunch.

But at 7:40 in a Gangnam coffee shop, hearing that a Korean man had taken number one on the American Billboard chart — she still felt like:

Maybe today won’t be so bad after all.

Somewhere in the world, at least, somebody had actually torn a hole through the thing called “impossible.”

“Won’t stop till we’re legends—”

The same morning 8:30 a.m. Seoul · LOEN Entertainment A role that fits like a dark twin, and a pre-order page that moves like a bank run.

LOEN. 8:30.

“Ji-eun-ah!”

Lee Ji-eun was reading the character profile for the role of Cindy — the more she read, the more the part felt like her own shadow side.

She looked up at Jeong Han-teuk.

“What is it, oppa?”

“Baek Si-on took Billboard number one!”

Lee Ji-eun blinked.

She had already seen it. Her phone had been lighting up with notifications since seven.

But Han-teuk clearly needed to shout it out loud.

“Oh — and his album’s in pre-order!”

“Album?”

“His first full album!”

Han-teuk tapped open the page at speed and handed the phone over.

Human — The 1st Album Pre-order Edition.

The album cover filled the screen.

“Human” · Baek Si-on · the 1st Album · Pre-order Edition

Human

One man. One field. One beam of light that never quite reaches him.

The cover photo looks anything but mortal.

Lee Ji-eun looked at it.

The album name: Human.

An ordinary person.

But there was nothing ordinary about the cover.

Black and white. Baek Si-on standing in an empty expanse of field, the sky hanging low and heavy behind him. Wind lifting the skirt of his long black coat like a torn flag. He isn’t looking at the camera — face turned slightly away, jawline cold as a blade. Far off on the horizon, a single shaft of light falls through a break in the clouds, landing behind him without ever quite reaching him.

Lee Ji-eun stared at the cover for several seconds.

“Human?”

Han-teuk nodded.

“The album name means ‘ordinary person.’”

“……”

The album was called Human, but the photograph looked like a god.

She said quietly:

“Such a show-off.”

Han-teuk didn’t catch it.

“Hm?”

“Nothing.”

Lee Ji-eun tapped into the pre-order page.

The screen showed:

pre-order page · Korean music store

[Pre-order open]

[Pre-order Edition · unlocked tracks]

1 · Legend

2 · Way Back Home

3 · Way Down We Go

4 · Bones

Tracks five through nine sit behind black bars, numbered and unnamed.

Legend needed no explanation.

By now the entire country knew. Billboard Hot 100, number one. Those words were enough to make every gossip account on the peninsula perform a seamless U-turn from “violence-prone” to “light of Korea.”

Way Back Home was the song Lee Ji-eun knew best of all.

Because she had written the lyrics.

Way Down We Go was the commercial song for Hyundai Aslan. She had watched the TVC — the car had cost a fortune to shoot, and the man in it had cost more.

As for Bones…

Lee Ji-eun thought of the “Ayi-ya-yi-ya” that had already gone completely viral around the world.

So the pre-order tracks of Baek Si-on’s first full album were: the Worlds war anthem, a song she had a hand in, a car commercial song, and one unholy masterpiece that had taken over the internet off nothing but a misheard demo.

The spiritual state of this pre-order album was extremely Baek Si-on.

But —

What about Love Yourself?

Lee Ji-eun scrolled down and read the list again.

Still nothing.

A small frown creased her brow.

Why wasn’t Love Yourself among the unlocked tracks?

It was her second-favourite song on Baek Si-on’s album.

Her first favourite, naturally, was Way Back Home.

Not because she had written the lyrics. Because the song was good.

Objectively.

Second place was Love Yourself.

And absolutely not because she had appeared in the music video.

And certainly not because the video contained a shot of her and Baek Si-on kissing on the living-room floor.

It was simply a good song.

Lee Ji-eun studied the pre-order page for a moment.

It was probably a marketing decision.

Fine. He had a plan, then.

She kept scrolling.

And saw Han-teuk’s purchase record on the screen.

order confirmation · Han-teuk’s phone

Order number: 00386

Placed minutes ago, the moment the page opened.

Three hundred and change. His fingers were fast.

Three hundred and something. That was quick.

Lee Ji-eun took out her own phone.

Opened the pre-order page.

Log in. Buy. Pay. Confirm.

She might make fun of him for being a show-off, but as a “sunbae” buying a record to support a “hoobae’s” sales — that was only proper.

The order went through.

order confirmation · Lee Ji-eun’s phone

Order number: 014582

14,582.

She blinks. Counts the digits. Once. Twice.

Lee Ji-eun stared at the number on the screen and blinked, certain she had misread it.

She brought the phone closer and carefully counted the places.

Ten thousand.

Fourteen thousand five hundred and eighty-two.

“Oppa.”

She turned to Jeong Han-teuk.

“When did you buy yours?”

Han-teuk glanced at the time.

“Three minutes ago. The second the pre-order page opened, I went in and bought it.”

“……”

She looked down at her own order number again.

Three minutes ago, the number was in the three hundreds.

Three minutes later, it was past fourteen thousand.

In other words — in the time it had taken the two of them to exchange a couple of sentences, this first full album — whose complete tracklist had not even been revealed — had sold over fourteen thousand copies.

Was this scientific?

This was not scientific!

Korea’s physical record market had been shrinking for years!

Even for an EXO comeback, early pre-orders had to be pumped up by fan-club group-buys and fan-sign event quotas.

And Baek Si-on?

He didn’t even have a proper fan club with organised bulk-buying teams.

He didn’t even post selfies!

Three minutes. Fourteen thousand copies.

Where were all these people coming from?

Was the entire country skipping work today to sit at home buying albums on their phones?!

Later the same morning A music-show broadcast day Seoul · KBS · the first-floor shared waiting area A wall-mounted TV, forty idols in stage clothes, and one phone that keeps leaving its owner’s hands.

KBS. Music Bank broadcast day.

First floor. The big shared waiting area.

Same as always: dozens of young men and women in bright stage outfits packed onto foam mats and plastic folding chairs.

The wall-mounted television was playing a replay of the KBS morning-news special.

Baek Si-on. Billboard. Number one. The screen kept cutting between the Worlds opening ceremony and the Lakers season opener.

Everyone in the room was watching. Nobody dared turn the volume up too high — the senior acts’ waiting rooms were too close.

In the farthest corner of the room, three members of Red Velvet were huddled.

Today was the third week of Be Natural promotions.

The missing member was Park Soo-young, who was currently running the “borrow a phone” mission and sprinting back.

“Got it!”

Park Soo-young dropped onto the mat next to Kang Seul-gi.

Three heads came together over one screen at once.

It was a composition that had appeared who-knows-how-many times by now: every time something major happened involving Baek Si-on, Park Soo-young went and “borrowed” a phone, and three heads crammed over a single screen.

SM’s phone policy had, on this particular issue, ceased to exist in practice.

Park Soo-young opened Naver first.

Real-time search. The top ten was almost entirely Baek Si-on.

She scrolled down to the news about the album pre-order.

Tapped it open.

Human. “Ordinary person.”

And yet, in the photograph, Baek Si-on stood between the wilderness and the falling light, long coat whipped by the wind, cold as a statue no one had found yet.

Park Soo-young went silent for a second.

“How is this ‘ordinary’?”

Kang Seul-gi gave a serious review:

“Looks like a god on his way home from work.”

“……”

Irene said nothing. She reached over and scrolled down the screen.

The released tracklist.

The last five songs sat locked, titles blacked out, only numbers visible.

Son Seung-wan counted beside her.

Pre-order version info.

pre-order page · version details

Standard edition — ₩19,000

Contents: CD + photobook + lyric booklet + one bookmark

Versions: one. Just one.

There is no random photocard. No signed lottery. No “collect all four versions for a hidden benefit.”

Park Soo-young stared at the word “bookmark.”

“They don’t even print his face on the bookmark? And there’s only one version? No A-version B-version C-version D-version?”

“No idea…” said Son Seung-wan.

In the SM system, an album usually shipped in three or four versions. Different covers. Different photocards. Different bonuses. Buying three or four copies to complete the set was basic behaviour; buying ten or more was not unusual.

Baek Si-on had one version. ₩19,000. One CD, one photobook, one lyric book, one bookmark.

No random-card packs. No signed raffles. No “collect all four to unlock a hidden reward.”

It was so simple it looked less like a 2014 Korean music-market product and more like the decision of someone who had never learned how the idol industry was played.

Irene’s finger stopped at the bottom of the page.

pre-order page · live total

Current pre-orders: 31,427

The number is still ticking upward as they watch.

It has been on sale for minutes.

“God—”

Park Soo-young kept her voice down, but the shock in it refused to stay down with her.

“Over thirty-one thousand? It’s only been up a few minutes! Does Baek Si-on sunbae really have this many fans?”

“Maybe it’s not just fans.”

Irene stared at the climbing number and said quietly:

“He took Billboard number one today. For a lot of Koreans right now, buying this album isn’t following an idol anymore. It’s a way of taking part in history.”

“……”

Kang Seul-gi looked at the page and asked softly:

“Only a standard edition? No limited edition, no deluxe?”

“Let me look.”

Park Soo-young backed out of the pre-order page and searched for a while, until she found an all-English site.

S.W Studio Official Store.

Baek Si-on’s global official merch store.

The same day Later · Seoul time Seoul · Nonhyeon-dong · Big Hit Entertainment A three-billion-won bet, seen in a new light — and seven boys who finally understand what the door is for.

Big Hit.

Bang Si-hyuk sat in his office, reading the news.

Baek Si-on, Billboard number one.

Human, in pre-order.

Korean pre-order numbers exploding.

He pushed his glasses up.

Said nothing.

After a few seconds, he opened Big Hit’s internal financials. Then the training, schedule, and content plans for BTS over the coming months.

And he sat in silence for a long time.

Baek Si-on had put ₩3 billion into Big Hit.

At the time, that money had already been important enough.

Seen from today, its meaning was completely different.

Because this was no longer simply “a rising artist invests in a small company.”

This was a Billboard Hot 100 champion — betting on Big Hit before anyone else could.

If that fact ever got out, the investment market would revalue the whole company.

Bang Si-hyuk leaned back in his chair and, all of a sudden, smiled a little.

“Really…”

He said it under his breath.

“We have to move faster.”

In the practice room, all seven members of BTS were watching the news too.

“The one and only mortal god of the universe.”

Min Yoon-gi had his eyes closed.

“What the hell is a mortal god?”

“The album’s called Human.”

Jung Ho-seok finished the thought:

“But the cover doesn’t look very human.”

Park Ji-min laughed until he folded over.

Kim Nam-joon looked at the chart screenshot and shook his head, smiling.

He knew what this meant for them.

It didn’t mean they would get famous just because Baek Si-on had taken number one. Nothing worked that simply.

But this number one proved one thing:

Koreans did not have to stand inside their own market and wait for the outside to come and look at them. They did not have to let the Western industry define them as “Asian idols.”

Someone really had walked out of Korea, and with his own work, fought his way into the centre.

For every young person still standing in a practice room in this country, that meant more than words could say.

Like somebody had kicked a door open.

The road beyond it was still hard.

But at least now they knew the door could be opened.
