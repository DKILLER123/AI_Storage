Chapter One Hundred Twenty Five

A “Genius” Idea: Raiding a Wedding

Late October 10:27 a.m. Los Angeles · Beverly Hills · the Four Seasons A phone lit up with news that will re-price the word “small gift.”

Los Angeles.

The Four Seasons, Beverly Hills.

When Baek Si-on woke, the curtain was only half drawn.

California sunlight was slamming straight through the open half, like a neighbour with no concept whatsoever of “five more minutes.”

He reached out and groped for the phone on the bedside table.

The screen lit up.

10:27 AM.

Several unread messages.

At the very top, a KakaoTalk from Baek Eun-ah, the kind of excitement radiating off the words that looked like she might faint at any second.

KakaoTalk · Baek Eun-ah

Baek Eun-ah

Oppa!!!

Baek Eun-ah

I looked that watch up!!!

Baek Eun-ah

Patek Philippe 5208P!!

Baek Eun-ah

It’s the same model on the wrist of that big boss in Russia!

Baek Eun-ah

The list price is around a million dollars — but the catch is it’s not even something you can buy just because you have the money!

Baek Si-on: “……”

Back in Dubai, when he had seen that box, he had only glanced at the dial.

The craftsmanship was fine.

But watches were not really his field.

He had assumed it came to about two hundred thousand dollars.

Three hundred thousand, at most.

After all, it was a “small gift.”

And then —

A Patek Philippe 5208P.

The same model as the big boss of Russia.

The Gulf royals’ understanding of the word “small” was, evidently, pegged directly to the size of their assets.

He backed out of the chat with Baek Eun-ah.

Scrolled down.

KakaoTalk · Yoon Hye-ja

Yoon Hye-ja

Get some rest.

KakaoTalk · Choi Jin-ri

Choi Jin-ri

Glad you made it! Get some rest~ take care!

Both were replies to the safe-arrival messages Baek Si-on had sent them, separately, the moment he landed in Los Angeles.

He answered each in turn, then got up, washed up, and headed down to the hotel restaurant for a quiet late breakfast that doubled as lunch.

Late October 1:00 p.m. Los Angeles · Staples Center · service entrance A run-through for a return that isn’t a secret to anyone.

One in the afternoon. Staples Center.

Baek Si-on, Baek Eun-ah, Park Ji-hun, and the two bodyguards reached the arena’s rear entrance.

The floor director of the TNT broadcast team was already waiting.

Forty minutes went to ironing out the sequence.

The lift timings. The light-cue beats. The cue for Kobe’s entrance. The DJ’s script. The trigger positions for the special-effects flames. The camera-switching plan.

Once the rundown was locked, Baek Si-on went up and walked it twice.

The sound was fine.

The in-ears were fine.

There was enough friction on the stage floor — no slipping.

When he was done, Jeanie Buss, the Lakers’ president, came over to shake his hand.

Jeanie Buss

President · Los Angeles Lakers

Real · Staples Center · the afternoon before the opener

The president who ends a stranger’s rehearsal by walking over to shake his hand, take one photo, and send her assistant after his sister with a business card. Partnership talks, she notes, are worth having later.

One photo together.

Jeanie’s assistant asked for Baek Eun-ah’s contact, saying there might be partnership talks worth having later.

Baek Eun-ah took the business card and offered her own with both hands.

Everything confirmed, the whole party went straight back to the hotel to rest up for the real performance tomorrow.

Late October The next morning Los Angeles · the Four Seasons · suite Three suits, a stylist who checks seams, and a watch that must not outshine the occasion.

The next morning, the Tom Ford brand PR delivered the suits to the hotel suite — three of them in all, plus two spare shirts, ties, cufflinks, and dress shoes.

The brand PR was extremely courteous.

So courteous that Baek Eun-ah wondered if they had come to pledge allegiance rather than deliver clothing. After all, Baek Si-on was no longer merely Korea’s Venice-winning actor.

Game-night suits · Tom Ford delivered to the suite

1 · BlackThe plainest read — a slab of darkness that disappears into any crowd.
2 · Dark greyOne degree softer than the black, considered rather than ceremonial.
3 · The choice — midnight blueThe suit Park Ji-hun settled on after going through all three one at a time — worn with a white shirt and the platinum pair of cufflinks the brand had sent over. Under a white follow spot it would read as near-black on camera anyway.

After Baek Si-on tried it on, Park Ji-hun circled him once and pulled twice at the shoulders and the back: “No alterations.” For the wrist, a far more understated black-strap watch — the Patek Philippe 5208P stayed in its box. Tonight he was singing for Kobe, not measuring wristwatches against a Russian head of state. Baek Eun-ah looked at that “far more understated” watch and fell silent for a moment. In her book, nothing north of ten million won had any business being called understated. But fine — her worldview had already been rebuilt, once, by the Dubai royal family.

Late October 6:00 p.m. Los Angeles · Staples Center · the tunnel beneath The NBA red carpet runs underground, lined with shutters and the smell of flash.

Six in the evening. The underground parking channel at Staples Center.

The NBA’s so-called “red carpet” was not a carpet laid outside a hotel door.

It was an underground tunnel.

Players, stars, brand guests, singers, celebrities — this was how they walked into the arena.

Photographers lined both sides.

Long lenses and heavy artillery.

Flashes packed so thick you could not count them.

In the years to come, this tunnel would become a true fashion battlefield.

Whatever every player wore, whatever watch sat on his wrist, whatever bag he carried, the media would slice into ten articles each.

But for now, it had not yet been fully industrialized.

Yet the moment Baek Si-on walked in, it already carried that flavour.

“Baek!”

“Baek Si-on! Over here!”

“Legend!”

“Look this way!”

“……”

Baek Si-on paused for a couple of seconds.

Turned his head.

Full front.

Profile.

A raised hand.

And in.

Late October 7:25 p.m. Los Angeles · Staples Center · floor level A sea of purple and gold, and one man standing in the only light left.

Seven twenty-five in the evening. Staples Center.

The building was full.

A sea of purple and gold ran from the lower bowl all the way up to the roof.

The big screen kept looping the season preview reel.

The DJ dropped warm-up tracks in fits and starts.

The murmur of the crowd was like a pot of water on the boil — not quite there, but on its way.

Seven twenty-seven.

The season preview on the big screen finished.

The house lights cut, hard.

Twenty thousand people lost their sight at the same instant.

In the dark, the murmur shot up an octave within half a second.

And then —

a single distorted electric-guitar note ripped out of the main PA.

It rang under the Staples Center roof for about two seconds.

Then the shriek died.

Pure black.

Pure silence.

Then one white beam drove straight down from the very top of the arena and pinned the dead centre of the floor.

Baek Si-on stood inside the follow spot.

Midnight-blue suit, near-black in the white light.

A standing microphone in front of him.

He gripped the mic stand with one hand and raised his eyes.

The first round of screaming detonated through the building.

Not everyone knew who he was.

But there was not a person in the building who had not heard this song.

The music video for “Legend” had already crossed over. Kobe’s 8-and-24 double exposure inside it, LeBron’s pool challenge, the athlete copycats around the globe, the NBA fan wars — all of it had pushed the song into something like a shared symbol of the sports world.

The drums came in.

The handclaps folded over them.

Baek Si-on opened his mouth.

Here we go, here we go—

It’s about time that we set it off—

The first line landed and the building twitched like it had been touched with a live wire.

The second line fell and somewhere in the lower bowl, feet began to stamp.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Then more.

The whole floor of Staples Center began to shake with the beat.

Here we go, here we go—

It’s my turn to make history—

The big screen cut to footage from the “Legend” music video.

The cheers stacked higher and higher.

When I’m gone, they’ll remember me, yeah—

Injury can’t stop me—

As that line fell, the main screen swung to the players’ tunnel.

Nobody stood there.

But everyone in the building knew the man about to walk out of it.

Trouble can’t stop me—

The camera cut back to Baek Si-on.

He raised a hand and pointed into the stands, as if hurling the line at everyone wearing a 24 jersey.

A dreamer with the fever to be great was all I ever wanted—

The stamping in the building tightened into a single rhythm.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

The TNT broadcast cut to a wide shot in that moment.

A top-down pull. From directly above the roof, the figure in the follow spot at the centre of the floor was ringed, circle after circle, by the crowd.

Eight bars before the chorus.

Every instrument began to climb.

Baek Si-on lifted the microphone off the stand.

Got me singin’ like—

Bang bang!

At all four corners of the court, columns of gold flame shot up at once.

The whole building answered.

Bang bang!

Bang bang!

Every “BANG” met a burst of flame.

Baek Si-on stood where the firelight and the follow spot crossed.

Let’s fire the weapon—

Baek Si-on dragged the note out long.

Then he hurled the final line.

Won’t stop till we’re legends!!!

Do not stop, until we become legends.

In that same instant, the ring of arena lights, the main board, the floor projections and the dome beams all blazed to life at once.

Under the eyes of the whole building, Baek Si-on’s right hand swung hard toward the players’ tunnel.

The arena DJ’s voice detonated in sync:

“Ladies and gentlemen—”

The lights in the tunnel began to come up.

“Returning to the court, in the purple and gold—”

Deep in the tunnel, a figure appeared.

“Make some noise for number twenty-four—”

The figure began to walk.

Then to run.

“Kobe—Bryant!”

Kobe burst out of the tunnel.

Staples Center, in that second, reached its physical acoustic limit.

Twenty thousand people rose as one.

The roar came down from every side like a tidal wave pressing toward the centre of the floor.

“KOBE!”

“KOBE!”

“KOBE!”

Kobe ran to the middle of the court.

Baek Si-on waited for him in the follow spot.

The two men faced each other.

Kobe put out his right hand.

Baek Si-on put out his right hand.

A high five.

The camera happened to catch that exact moment.

On the big screen.

A Korean singer.

A Los Angeles legend.

A battle anthem charging at number one on the Billboard charts.

One high five.

The sound of the building ran completely out of control.

Late October Same moments Los Angeles · Staples Center · the visitors’ tunnel mouth Two Rockets watch a coronation and plan to end it.

On the far side of the court, at the entrance to the visitors’ locker-room channel, James Harden and Dwight Howard stood shoulder to shoulder, watching everything unfolding at centre court through the gap.

James Harden

Guard · Houston Rockets

Real · October 2014 · the visitors’ tunnel

The Beard with two drives and a drawn foul who reminds twenty thousand people that Kobe’s comeback night is not a documentary. He plans the upset from the mouth of the tunnel and does not plan on losing.

Dwight Howard

Centre · Houston Rockets

Real · October 2014 · the elbow at the centre of everything

Elbows out, hands spread, grinning wider the louder the boos get. His defensive rebound starts the fight, and his elbow — swung from two hundred and sixty pounds away — is what nearly ends Baek Si-on’s night.

“Fuck, they’re running this entrance like a coronation.”

Harden narrowed his eyes.

“We torch him tonight.”

Beat Kobe on his home floor, in front of twenty thousand people, with a war anthem like that blaring in the background — there was no better script than that.

“You mean Kobe?”

“What, you want to torch the guy singing instead?”

Howard shrugged.

“That works too.”

Harden: “……”

He looked back toward centre court.

Kobe was in a brief embrace with Baek Si-on.

As they separated, Kobe clapped Baek Si-on once on the shoulder.

Harden admitted it — the image was cool.

Cool enough to be annoying.

What annoyed him more was that the song genuinely went hard.

“If you really do torch him tonight, what do you think the media writes?”

Harden thought it over.

“‘Black Mamba’s comeback night, ruined by the Beard.’”

Howard laughed out loud.

“Then I’m in charge of grabbing twenty boards.”

Harden gave him a look.

“Make your free throws first.”

Howard’s smile froze for a beat.

“You’re no fun.”

“Just facts.”

Baek Si-on’s seat had been arranged in the VIP first row, right beside the Lakers’ bench.

A brown leather jacket.

A white V-neck tee underneath.

The frontman of Maroon 5.

“Best pre-game show I’ve seen all year. Not even close.”

Four spots behind “Legend” at number three.

In a certain sense, they were soldiers on the same battlefield now.
