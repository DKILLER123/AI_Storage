Chapter Two Hundred Twelve

Late, With Someone Behind Her

April 18

Morning — far too late in it

A hotel near Haeundae · the scene of a silenced alarm

The next morning, Choi Jin-ri woke in a temperature that was very happy and very dangerous.

Happy, because Baek Si-on was right beside her.

Dangerous, because she squinted one eye open, felt for her phone, read the time — and went from asleep to fully awake in something under a second.

“…I’m dead.”

She bolted upright, wearing the full panic of a person slapped awake by reality. On the phone screen, the time had sailed clean past the alarm she had set the night before.

No — wait.

Not sailed past.

It had never rung at all.

She frowned her way into the alarm screen, stared for two seconds, and turned to glare at Baek Si-on.

“Baek Si-on.”

No movement.

“Baek Si-on!”

Still no movement — but the corner of his mouth carried the faintest possible arc, filing the report that he had not been asleep at all.

Choi Jin-ri ground her teeth.

“You turned off my alarm.”

“Mm.”

He had finally spoken.

“Why?”

“You made me wait an hour yesterday.”

Choi Jin-ri had been fully prepared to detonate. At minimum, she had planned to pin this man to the mattress and bite him, as a public service clarifying that this was not how romance was conducted. But the moment “you made me wait an hour” left his mouth, her momentum lost most of its funding.

“That still doesn’t carry over to today — I’m going to be LATE.”

She pressed her lips together and started inching toward the edge of the bed. Her feet had barely touched the carpet when the next second arrived, and she was scooped back from behind, wholesale. The back of her head knocked into his collarbone — painless, but deeply undignified.

“Oppa, I am genuinely going to be late.”

“Mm.”

“The director is going to kill me.”

“Mm.”

She twisted around to glare:

“This is SO juvenile!”

“You weren’t all that mature while making me wait an hour.”

Choi Jin-ri stopped talking.

She had concluded that this man, once in love, ran a revenge appetite of frankly deranged calibration — and that she possessed no rebuttal with any legal standing.

Scooter Braun ↔ Baek Si-on · voice · the bomb’s afterweather · one-word answers

The phone buzzed on the nightstand while its owner was being held, struggling with zero persuasiveness. Baek Si-on fished it over one-handed and checked the display: Scooter. He answered.

“Talk.”

Scooter came through at machine-gun speed, though the payload was clear enough. ‘Furious 7’ had exploded — the North American opening weekend had charged straight into the top ranks. ‘See You Again’ went without saying: every chart that would take it had taken it, and radio rotation was approaching the density of oxygen. Baek Si-on’s American goodwill was, roughly, at its all-time high. Which was why a film project over there had requested a meeting with him by name. Then, item two, fired off without a breath: the Met Gala invitation had arrived. Early May. The Oscars of fashion. New York’s most accomplished posers, requesting his presence.

“Noted.”

“‘Noted’ means WHAT? When are you getting on a plane?”

Scooter, audibly apoplectic.

“Today.”

“Good.”

Click.

The hotel room went quiet again.

Choi Jin-ri, folded in his arms, had heard all of it clearly.

She knew, of course, that her boyfriend was formidable now. Hollywood film resources. A Met Gala invitation. Pick either noun at random and a top-tier Korean celebrity could milk press releases out of it for a year. And to him, the whole delivery had been one casually answered phone call.

She looked up at the sharply carved face and said, small-voiced:

“You’re flying back to the States?”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on tossed the phone back toward the nightstand and put his eyes back on her face. There was real reluctance in her chest — and she was, nonetheless, sensible about it. She pushed at his chest, gently.

“Then get up and go wash and pack, there’s a mountain of things to prepare. And I have to get to the set too.”

The moment she tried to push herself up, she was pressed back down. This time Baek Si-on rolled over and pinned her underneath him entirely.

“…Hollywood is waiting for you.”

Immovable.

“…The Met Gala is waiting for you.”

Equally immovable.

Choi Jin-ri switched tactics.

“…I really, truly do not have time for this.”

“You’re still short forty minutes,” Baek Si-on said.

“What?”

“The hour you owe me.”

“Can I get a discount?”

“You didn’t discount the outfit-choosing time when you did your makeup yesterday.”

“……”

Choi Jin-ri surrendered.

❝

The scolding could be scheduled for later. For now: enjoy this excessive, itemized, interest-charging favoritism.

— the debtor’s position, filed without protest

❧ ❧ ❧

By the time Choi Jin-ri stood in front of the bathroom mirror, it was ten in the morning. Clothes changed, hair redone. Presentable, technically. Baek Si-on, meanwhile, was leaning against the bathroom doorframe looking infuriatingly refreshed, holding a cup of the hotel’s fresh-ground coffee and drinking it at a loitering pace.

She looked at the face of a man to whom nothing whatsoever had happened, and wanted to pounce and bite him. Ideally hard enough to leave a clear, unmistakable tooth mark — one he would then have to deal with at the airport this afternoon, under a turtleneck, or with concealer, his problem, his logistics.

But at present she needed mild concentration to walk in a straight line, and there was no surplus energy budgeted for violence.

So she just glared at him. The glare carried exactly one sentence:

You wait and see.

Baek Si-on received the message.

And became selectively blind.

“Let’s go.”

“…Mm.”

April 18

Mid-morning

The road to the ‘Train to Busan’ set · a defense in three drafts

The whole ride to the set, Choi Jin-ri conducted psychological preparation.

Nearly two hours late. Yesterday, she had left early. Today she presumably did not look entirely normal either. Director Yeon Sang-ho was not the type to dress anyone down in public — but his cold face and his silence were, at intervals, considerably scarier than shouting. In her head she had assembled no fewer than three apology scripts.

The apology arsenal, as drafted in transit

Draft one — the sincere version: “I’m sorry, Director, I slept badly last night, there was a problem with my alarm.”

Draft two — the vague version: felt a little unwell, went to the hospital in the morning.

Draft three — the emergency reserve: ate something that turned against her.

Her internal ranking favored draft three on technical grounds: it was the only entry that simultaneously explained the lateness and the unsteady walking.

As it turned out, at the set, not one of the three drafts was needed.

Because Director Yeon’s attitude was entirely outside her forecasting envelope.

“Sulli’s here?”

The director’s very first look at her carried no dark face at all — the tone even came pre-warmed with concern.

“Did you rest well last night?”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

“…F-fine. Pretty well.”

“Good. Not much for you today — go get into makeup first, no rush at all.”

She nodded, dazed, and turned toward the makeup room.

The set, as recalibrated overnight

The PA who handed her water had appended a “Good morning” at no extra charge. The assistant director passed by and greeted her, smiling. Even the extras-zone coordinator, of the species that acknowledged nobody, dipped his head at her.

Choi Jin-ri sat down in the makeup chair and stared into the mirror for a good long while. She was not stupid, and the arithmetic completed itself quickly. It was not because she was exceptionally cute today. It was not because the ‘Train to Busan’ set had spontaneously declared a Children’s Day. It was because someone had come by last night — someone whose manner of addressing the director, whose reception protocol, and whose invisible residue of weight had required every person on this lot to re-verify one fact.

She looked at herself in the mirror. The makeup artist began the base layer, strokes feather-light. She closed her eyes.

❝

So this is what it does, having someone stand behind you and hold the line: the whole world actually gets a little softer. Even when he is no longer in the room.

— the makeup chair, 10:40 a.m.

Before flying to North America, Baek Si-on called Jung Jae-joon first. The line picked up with music still playing in the background.

Baek Si-on ↔ Jung Jae-joon · voice · the LA deployment · the dirty-work treaty

“I’m going to Los Angeles.”

“You’d better not be about to say you thought of another new song.”

Two seconds of Jae-joon silence.

“So what you mean is: you airdrop two lead singles, and all the dirty work and heavy lifting lands on me.”

“Production fees stay as agreed.”

“And then you dust off your pants and fly away.”

“Royalties stay split as agreed.”

“…Fine.”

“Don’t let DSP mess with the mixes.”

“What do you take me for, a first-day intern?”

Baek Si-on said nothing more, hung up, and scrolled to the flight information Baek Eun-ah had sent over.

April 18

Local afternoon

Los Angeles International Airport · arrivals

Pushing his luggage out into arrivals, Baek Si-on found his pickup was not Scooter.

It was Baek Eun-ah. Dressed considerably sharper than her Korea days — and, if anything, a shade tanner.

“Welcome back to the United States of America, Overtime Branch.”

“How’s Charlie doing?”

He fed the luggage into the trunk.

“Alive,” Baek Eun-ah said. “And living extremely well.”

“Anything on your desk that needs me?”

“Overtime pay.”

“Approved.”

Baek Eun-ah’s eyes lit up.

The van pulled away from the airport. Los Angeles sunlight came in slantwise through the window: the city, still the same city — palm trees along the road, traffic, billboards, restaurants that looked expensive and might not taste like anything.

At SB Projects, the conference room door had barely registered him when Scooter arrived at collision speed and enthusiasm, wrapped both arms around Baek Si-on, and thumped his back twice.

“My golden boy!”

He released him and looked him up and down.

“You’ve gotten thinner.”

“No, I haven’t.”

“You really have.”

Scooter frowned, grief-stricken.

“Has somebody been draining you dry?”

Baek Si-on was not entirely certain whether the “draining” on the table referred to schedules, or to something else.

If schedules: guilty as charged.

But if Scooter meant the other layer — Baek Si-on gave the matter serious internal review. Choi Jin-ri, for the record: her stamina reserves, her appetite for revenge, and her sustained-combat awareness all remained rated at the stage commonly described as quite adorable.

❝

“Drained dry” would be a defamatory overstatement. At most, the record would support: low-intensity cardio.

— the internal audit, closed without further discovery

Scooter dropped the subject, slung an arm around Baek Si-on’s shoulders, and steered him inside:

“Anyway — King.”

“Welcome back to America.”

“Now let’s talk about the new world this bomb blew open.”

The script was already lying on the table.

Baek Si-on looked down at the cover.

‘La La Land’.

He opened to the first page while Scooter ran the briefing.

The project, as tabled

Lionsgate and Summit Entertainment, a new project, budget around thirty million dollars. Director: Damien Chazelle — the ‘Whiplash’ breakout out of Sundance, already an Oscar winner, currently one of the most watched young directors in Hollywood.

The casting history: original male lead Miles Teller wanted six million; the director had four million on the table. Talks collapsed. The original female lead, Emma Watson, declined an uncertain mid-budget musical in favor of the ‘Beauty and the Beast’ mother lode.

The new female lead: Emma Stone — discovered by Chazelle himself, in the audience at a musical in New York.

At that point, Baek Si-on’s hand stopped on the page.

“Emma Stone?”

“You don’t remember?”

“The ‘Spider-Man’ one?”

“Last year at Venice — she walked the opening red carpet as one of the ‘Birdman’ principals.”

“……”

Baek Si-on’s memory pulled up the Lido Island of last autumn.

Venice, last autumn, as retrieved

The place where he took the Volpi Cup. The place where he and Choi Jin-ri held hands on a dock for the first time. The place where he stood on a dinner table singing, and later folded himself into a garden. The place where an American woman named Alexandra Daddario invited him up to Room 312.

As for Emma Stone — he searched honestly for several seconds. Opening film: ‘Birdman’. There did seem to have been such a person.

But in truth, those days his attention had been fully consumed by three items: the premiere, the prize, and Choi Jin-ri. The residual capacity had been reallocated to a visual impact of the Daddario class. The composition of the ‘Birdman’ delegation, the red-carpet placements, the seat numbers in the opening hall — zero retrievable data. Baek Si-on therefore elected not to engage with the topic.

“So — how exactly do they plan to make audiences accept an Asian man romancing Emma Stone across Los Angeles?”

The question was not preciousness on Baek Si-on’s part. Hollywood’s racial storytelling barrier was real. No need to reach for the distant past; the recent past sufficed. John Cho. Korean-American actor, solid craft, stable reputation. In ‘Selfie’ he played the male lead, romancing a white female lead — and American audiences didn’t buy it. The ratings stalled, the show was canceled, and a second season evaporated into silence. A pile of compounding causes sat underneath, but market prejudice was the main load-bearing one.

“Honestly, I haven’t fully mapped their internals either,” Scooter said. “Maybe the director has his own handling for it. And even if the deal goes nowhere, this meeting tells me how this system over here reads you — and how I’m supposed to walk you into it. But before all of that, there’s one basic question.”

“Do you actually like the character?”

Baek Si-on didn’t answer. He opened the script again and read from the first page.

He knew this film would go on to win many awards. He knew the original lead had played the part well. And he knew, with the same certainty, that he could do it better. The thing Sebastian carried — he knew it from the inside. Everyone around him certain he was wasting his time; only he himself certain the road was right. Wasn’t that precisely himself? Buying bitcoin. Investing in game companies. Backing Bang Si-hyuk. The number-one pledge donations. The free concerts. Every single step had read, from the outside, like a madman’s choices — and he had known exactly what he was doing. Sebastian, likewise.

❝

The only difference: Sebastian plays jazz. He plays the whole world.

— the casting decision, made somewhere around page one

“I like the character.”

Baek Si-on closed the script.

“Set the meeting.”
