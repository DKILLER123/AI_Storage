Chapter Forty-Seven

Korea's Lone Entry and the Net's Hunt for a Villain

July 24 Evening Every front page at once The shortlist, and the headlines it wrote itself

The pride of Korean film! Director Baek Jeong-hun's new work Green Bottle Fly in the Venice main competition!

The sole Korean hope vying for the Golden Lion! Kim Ki-duk and Hong Sang-soo both absent — who is this indie director?

From the Busan short-film section to the Venice main competition: Baek Jeong-hun, the biggest dark horse in Korean cinema.

The twelfth Korean film in seventy-one years of Venice to reach the main competition. What did it do to deserve that?

OSEN · Sports Chosun · Dispatch · Star News · Xports News

All of them, in the space of twenty minutes, ran the same breaking story.

Even KBS and MBC slid the item into the closing minutes of the evening news — the culture segment, in that formal tone reserved for a moment of national pride.

And then the Naver search re-washed itself, all within half an hour.

While all of Korea went looking for who Baek Jeong-hun was, Baek Si-on sat on the living-room sofa, staring at the livestream of the Venice website on his phone. The shortlist page was still loading slowly, the server probably being swamped by press from every corner of the globe.

He left the browser, opened his contacts.

Lim Geun-ho.

He dialled. One ring. Two. Three. Picked up.

Baek Si-on → Lim Geun-ho · evening · Nonhyeon-dong

The other end was loud — keyboards, voices, a printer spitting paper. The whole Dispatch newsroom was probably in a state of war-footing.

“Hello?”

“Director Lim. This is Baek Si-on.”

There was a pause on the line. It took about three seconds for Lim Geun-ho to square the name "Baek Si-on" with the young man with the crew cut who'd sat across from him yesterday afternoon, handing over a USB stick.

“Ah—Mr. Baek. Don't worry about your thing, the material's already being cut, and the copy's half-written. I'll publish it first thing tomorrow morning. Today I really can't spare a hand—”

“Are you right now looking up information on Director Baek Jeong-hun?”

The line went quiet for a moment. Lim Geun-ho didn't hide it.

“Yes. Korea has only this one film in the Venice main competition, and every media outlet is fighting for it. But the director's too clean — there's almost nothing about him online, not even a decent public photo. We sent two reporters to dig, and so far all we've got is that he's from the independent-film circle, made some shorts before, screened at Busan……”

Dispatch never did a second-place story by choice. Information other outlets could find, they considered beneath their standards. What they wanted was the exclusive. The angle nobody else had, the material nobody else could get, the point of entry nobody else had thought of.

But right now every outlet was on the same track, and whoever found Baek Jeong-hun first, whoever got the first interview, won.

Si-on waited until he'd finished, then switched the phone to his left hand.

“Stop digging.”

“What do you mean?”

“Baek Jeong-hun is my uncle. And the film in the Venice main competition — I'm the lead actor in it.”

There was about five seconds of silence on the line. But in those five seconds, Si-on heard the sound of Lim Geun-ho getting up from his chair, then footsteps. He'd probably walked to a quieter corner.

“……What did you say?”

“The film's called Green Bottle Fly. The director is my uncle. I play the lead, Sang-hun.”

“……”

A moment later, Lim Geun-ho's voice came out half through his nose:

“Daebak! So you really are an actor. And you went to that collection company for role research?”

His language had shifted. Thirty seconds earlier it had been "we'll deal with your thing tomorrow." Now it had switched to full honorific mode. Was Dispatch snobbish? Not exactly. More precisely, their nose for "news value" had just been recalibrated. Baek Si-on had gone from an anonymous nobody needing help clearing his name, to a source who could deliver the exclusive of the year. Naturally, the treatment changed.

“Yes.”

“This film's about the intergenerational cycle of domestic violence. The role I play grows up in a violent home and ends up one more cog at the bottom of the world. The collection work is only one face of it.”

He paused.

“I can't play a person I don't understand. The violence on the page is words. The violence you've lived is flesh and blood. My uncle bet everything on this film, and as an actor I have no right to be lazy.”

The other end went quiet again. Lim Geun-ho was probably digesting it. From a news-writing standpoint, this was a gift of a headline all by itself: the actor who went down into the underworld to live a life he could not fake.

The sound of furious typing came back over the line.

“Mr. Baek — no, Actor Baek. Can Dispatch get the exclusive on your uncle?”

“Yes. But I have one condition.”

“Say it.”

“The debt-collection piece runs today. Not tomorrow — today. Because it's already started to affect my family.”

“Understood.”

“Then I'll leave it to you, Director Lim.”

“See you shortly.”

“……”

He hung up. Glanced at the time in the top-right corner of the screen.

Six-forty-three.

He tucked the phone into his pocket, stood, changed his shoes, grabbed his keys, and headed out.

No taxi. His uncle's studio wasn't far from home — ten minutes on foot.

When he pushed the front door open, the sky outside was still light, that exact moment that sat between day and night. The colour was making one long, slow gradient from west to east. The west was burnt orange where the sun had burned down through it; to the east it faded into grey-blue, and further on, the first star had already come out.

Si-on walked along the pavement with his hands in his pockets. Past the convenience store, the glass door reflected his own silhouette. The ginkgo trees on both sides were a green so dark it looked black, the leaves pressed thickly overhead, rustling when the wind came through, like someone clapping from very far away.

He pulled out his phone, opened KakaoTalk, and found Kim Jae-kyung's chat. He typed a line.

"Can you make a dress?"

He tucked the phone back into his pocket and turned into the alley.

At the far end, the second-floor window of the old building where the studio was had a light on.

His uncle was in.

Si-on quickened his step.
