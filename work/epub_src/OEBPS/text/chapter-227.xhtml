Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Seven

Jeong Han-teuk Discovers the Wealth Code

A few days later · morning

A janchi table already steaming — incense lit

Namyangju · outside a studio lot, on the capital’s outskirts

On the outskirts of Seoul.

Outside a studio in Namyangju, a canopy had already been raised on the open ground.

The offering table stood dead center.

A boiled pig’s head sat upright upon it, several red envelopes already stuffed into its mouth, two thick envelopes pressed against its ears.

Flanking the head, in their order: fruit, rice cakes, dried fish, rice wine, and incense candles.

A neatly folded sheet of white paper lay propped at the front of the table — the production’s written prayer.

Smoke was already climbing out of the censer.

Staff from every department kept arriving.

On an ordinary film’s first shoot day, the busiest people on site are usually the crew.

On the first shoot day of ‘The Three Evils’, the busiest were the media, fighting for position.

A black van pulled up outside the canopy.

Baek Si-on stepped out.

The buzz cut charged into the press lenses before the rest of him did.

Shutter fire cracked across the barrier line.

Right behind, Baek Jeong-hoon’s cast assembled in full: Kim Hye-soo, Yoo Ah-in, Park Seong-woong, Lee Sung-min, Jo Woo-jin, Kim Seon-young, and the rest of the core supporting cast.

The parking lot grew denser and denser with cars.

As the last people arrived, the noise settled down.

Baek Jeong-hoon stepped to the offering table, lit three sticks of incense, and bowed to it.

Then he raised the rice-wine cup overhead and poured it out onto the ground before the table.

The rite, as ordered

The director: three sticks of incense, one deep bow, wine poured overhead.

His lips, moving — audible to no one.

Baek Si-on: red envelope into the pig’s mouth, one stick for the censer, palms pressed, one bow.

Kim Hye-soo, Yoo Ah-in, Park Seong-woong, Lee Sung-min: forward in sequence.

Camera, lighting, art, stunts, hair-makeup-wardrobe, producing: by department, in order.

Rice wine and incense smoke mixing. The red envelopes, stacking thicker.

The rite finished, Baek Jeong-hoon stood in front of everyone.

“Thank you for your work, all of you.”

“The next few months — safety first.”

“Are we ready?”

The departments’ answers surged in from every direction.

“Ready!”

Baek Jeong-hoon raised a fist.

“‘The Three Evils’!”

The whole crew shouted in unison:

“A smooth shoot—!”

The voices scattered into the air above the empty lot.

Then, the commemorative photo.

The photographer hoisted his camera and called:

“Over here! One, two, three!”

The shutters rattled through several rounds.

Baek Si-on stood at Baek Jeong-hoon’s right hand; Kim Hye-soo stood at his left.

Behind them, the frame was packed with every department’s staff.

That photo would be on the front page of every entertainment portal within hours.

The incense on the offering table was still burning.

And outside, the press could no longer hold still.

❧ ❧ ❧

The barrier line opened.

The reporters scattered in every direction like racehorses with the gates ripped off.

The largest pack made straight for Baek Si-on.

Kim Hye-soo, Yoo Ah-in, and Park Seong-woong were swarmed in circles of their own.

Several finance and film outlets cornered Baek Jeong-hoon, pressing about investment scale, overseas distribution, and the political material.

The rest roved among the supporting cast, trying to mine every answer for a headline word.

Shutters, voice recorders, microphones, shouts of “over here, please,” and “one more question” filled the whole lot.

❧ ❧ ❧

Jo Woo-jin had drawn the fewest reporters.

But every one of them knew, with surgical precision, exactly what to ask.

“Jo Woo-jin-ssi — you’ve just finished ‘Inside Men’ with Lee Byung-hyun-ssi, and now you’re on set opposite Baek Si-on-ssi.”

The reporter’s smile was friendly.

The question was not.

“Both are top-tier, internationally celebrated Best Actor calibre. With whom do you feel more pressure in a shared scene? Whose acting aura is stronger?”

The textbook leading question.

No matter how Jo Woo-jin answered, tomorrow’s headline had already been written in the reporter’s head.

The headlines, as pre-written

‘Jo Woo-jin says Baek Si-on outshines Lee Byung-hun’

‘Jo Woo-jin states plainly: Baek Si-on falls short of his senior’

‘Old and new Best Actors, face to face — a character actor tells the truth’

Three slots, one answer, zero risk to the paper.

Jo Woo-jin answered with great care:

“Both of them are teachers I have to look up to and learn from. I come to set every day with reverence, as if attending class.”

The reporter wouldn’t drop it.

“If you absolutely had to choose — whose pressure is greater?”

Jo Woo-jin handed the question straight back.

“The pressure mainly comes from me.”

“If my preparation falls short, I’d be nervous standing beside any fine actor.”

The note-taking reporter wrote with his head down.

Another one pivoted instantly.

“Can you tell us who you play in ‘The Three Evils’?”

Jo Woo-jin thought for a moment.

“A man whose job is handing things to people.”

“Hands them what?”

“Information. Sometimes, possibly, other things.”

“Good guy or bad guy?”

A faint, meaningful smile crossed Jo Woo-jin’s face.

“Until I reached the last page of the script, I also believed he was just a middleman.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Yoo Ah-in’s corner was far livelier.

His nightclub owner was the most important villain of the film’s first half — and Baek Si-on’s first true on-screen opponent.

“The role involves drugs, coercion, and violence. Are you worried the audience will recoil?”

Yoo Ah-in tilted his head and considered.

“If audiences walk out of the cinema hating him, that means I acted him all right.”

The reporters pounced:

“You reportedly share a large number of scenes with Baek Si-on-ssi. Facing an actor with Venice’s recognition — pressure?”

Yoo Ah-in glanced toward the far end of the crowd.

Baek Si-on had been cornered so completely that only a buzz cut remained visible above the microphones.

“Of course there’s pressure.”

Every recorder in the cluster shot upright.

“The pressure is mainly in the script. He really does beat me up.”

Laughter broke across the crowd.

Someone chased it.

“So in the film — who wins?”

“If I won, the movie might wrap itself up in half an hour.”

“What about a real fight?”

Yoo Ah-in slid his hands into his pockets.

“That question should go to the insurance company.”

The laughter got louder.

Several reporters were already composing their headlines.

The headline, as drafted

‘Yoo Ah-in admits he’s no match for Baek Si-on — says his own victory would end the film early’

The body text would explain everything. The headline’s only job was the bait.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on had the most people around him by far.

Microphones and recorders reached in from every angle, at hedgehog density.

But the style of the questions was noticeably different from the Jo Woo-jin and Yoo Ah-in corners.

Friendlier.

Looser.

If an ordinary actor answered badly, the press could always cut, nitpick, and steer.

If Baek Si-on answered badly, the outlet’s first worry was whether it would still be allowed inside his press zone in the future.

Professional ethics are elastic.

The bigger the star, the smaller the elasticity.

One entertainment reporter fought to the front row and opened on styling.

“Si-on-ssi — you’ve gone back to the buzz cut. It’s very close to your look in ‘Green Bottle Fly’, right after your discharge. A deliberate echo of your debut work?”

Baek Si-on raised a hand and rubbed the top of his head.

The stubble was too short to find any hairstyle in.

But the hand-feel was good.

“No. Probably Director Baek decided my hair would get in the character’s way.”

The reporters laughed.

Someone followed up.

“Did the stylist agree?”

“He’s in Paris now.”

“For work?”

“Or possibly because he couldn’t stand to look at this buzz cut anymore.”

The laughter got bigger.

The second question arrived.

“‘The Three Evils’ is your third feature as an official lead. In ‘Green Bottle Fly’, your character is beaten to death. In ‘Sado’ — well, everyone knows how Crown Prince Sado ends. And this leading man doesn’t look likely to survive either.”

“How do you manage to die in absolutely everything you act in?”

Baek Si-on’s eyes flicked toward Baek Jeong-hoon.

“I’ve been wondering the same thing. Maybe my uncle has some kind of issue with me.”

The press roared harder.

“So how does this one die?”

“Can’t spoil.”

Baek Si-on paused one second:

“But the odds of dying of old age are low.”

“Have you considered taking a role that survives to the end credits?”

“I have.”

“What genre?”

“A musical.”

The lot erupted in a sound that was half shock, half delight.

A musical.

Safe.

Nobody generally gets executed by firing squad mid-song.

Granted — if the director was still Baek Jeong-hoon, no guarantees.

❧ ❧ ❧

“This is your second feature lead under Director Baek Jeong-hoon. Does uncle-and-nephew make a director-actor pairing any easier?”

“No.”

Baek Si-on’s answer was crisp.

“He only gave me the full script four days ago.”

A collective gasp went through the press pack.

“Four days of prep?”

“Correct.”

“You didn’t protest?”

“I protested.”

“How did that go?”

“He said giving me four days’ notice already showed tremendous sincerity.”

The reporters swung en masse toward Baek Jeong-hoon.

Baek Jeong-hoon stood on the other side, serenely fielding questions like “does the cult money entering politics mirror reality.”

On the subject of “advance preparation,” the uncle and nephew clearly labored under a vast family disagreement.

“On set — do you call him uncle, or director?”

“Director.”

“And if the director cusses you out in front of everyone?”

“I go home and tell my mom.”

The lot dissolved into laughter again.

Whether uncle-and-nephew collaboration was easier had a clear answer now.

Probably not easier.

But there was always the sister-in-law to summon for family arbitration.

❧ ❧ ❧

Near the end of the scrum, a music-media reporter finally found a gap.

“Can you share specifics on the second tour leg in the second half of the year?”

Every recorder in the vicinity swung back toward Baek Si-on.

The tour clearly outweighed a film kickoff on the interest scale.

If he said two extra sentences, tomorrow’s front page would switch stories on the spot.

A superstar’s concert tour, after all, moves a city’s GDP.

Baek Si-on declined to take the thread.

“The tour will happen. Dates will be announced by the company in due course.”

He raised a hand and indicated the offering table and the ‘The Three Evils’ kickoff banner behind him.

“Today is ‘The Three Evils’’ ceremonial first shoot. If I stand on my uncle’s film set talking concerts, he may move my character’s death up by ten minutes.”

The laughter rose again.

Baek Si-on inclined his head slightly to the press.

“Thank you, everyone.”

He turned toward the production.

The reporters were still shouting behind him.

“Si-on-ssi, look this way once more!”

“Last question!”

“The really last one!”

Baek Si-on didn’t look back.

The buzz cut caught the sun; the back of him was sharp as a blade fresh off its edge.

In a few hours, wearing exactly this look, he would stand in front of the camera and play a fugitive who knew he was about to die.

Where he passed, the shutters kept firing.

The reporters bent over their photos and audio from minutes ago and began assembling headlines in their heads.

❧ ❧ ❧

Same day · later

Lights changed three times — one agent, idle, phone out

On the outskirts of the ‘Producer’ set

On the outskirts of the ‘Producer’ set.

Inside the set, the camera ran its dolly track while the newly installed director and cinematographer walked Lee Ji-eun through the scene.

Jeong Han-teuk sat on a folding chair in the waiting area, her coat and handbag beside him.

For the moment, an agent had no function to perform.

So he pulled out his phone and opened Naver’s entertainment section.

The page finished loading, and the recommendation rail was occupied, top to bottom, by a single person.

Naver · Entertainment

‘The Three Evils’ officially kicks off! Baek Si-on returns to the screen with a buzz cut — a super-luxury lineup with Kim Hye-soo and Yoo Ah-in revealed

Naver · Entertainment

Baek Si-on’s “dies-in-every-role” curse continues? His own answer: next time, a musical

Naver · Entertainment

Stylist exiled to Paris over the buzz cut? Baek Si-on answers it himself at the ceremony

Naver · Entertainment

…

Jeong Han-teuk tapped into the photo gallery.

The offering table, the pig’s head, the red envelopes, the cast photo.

The last frame of the gallery was the back of Baek Si-on, leaving the press zone.

Jeong Han-teuk enlarged the photo, then shrank it back.

A thought surfaced.

‘The Three Evils’ had started shooting today.

By the customary courtesy of the Korean entertainment world, artists and friends close to a production send a support gift on such a milestone.

A food truck, a coffee truck, a flower stand — format unrestricted.

The person need not appear.

But the courtesy must arrive first.

❝

Some favors never need the person involved to act in person. The signature only has to be right.

— the agent’s theorem, proven two days ago by one box of chocolate

Jeong Han-teuk’s mind ran for a few seconds.

He thought of the chocolate affair from two days back — an event that had established a highly practical theorem of agency:

some favors do not require the principal to lift a finger.

They require only the correct signature.

With that, Jeong Han-teuk opened his contacts, found an agency-support catering company that had served the industry for years, and dialed.

Jeong Han-teuk → the support-truck company · voice · the order placed as IU

“Can you take a rush order today?”

“Namyangju — the ‘The Three Evils’ film production.”

“Cater for one hundred and fifty-two portions.”

“...Somebody here eats three portions, yes — coffee, fruit, and ice cream, all of it.”

“Banner signature: IU.”

The voice on the line asked what message should go on the banner.

Jeong Han-teuk reopened that “dies-in-every-role” story.

“Make the main banner read...”

““Please safely survive to the wrap party. Third time too.””

The supplier read the whole order back once.

Jeong Han-teuk was very satisfied.

Given the relationship between Lee Ji-eun and Baek Si-on, the line was mean enough, and familiar enough.

One glance, and anyone would believe she wrote it.

❧ ❧ ❧

The studio lights had changed over three times.

The slate clapped shut again; the director finally called the break.

Lee Ji-eun came off the set.

Jeong Han-teuk met her with a bottle of water, cap already twisted off, straw already in.

Lee Ji-eun’s hand came out first:

“Phone.”

Hydration was forcibly demoted to second priority.

Jeong Han-teuk handed the phone over.

She had meant to check the reaction to ‘The Producer’ episode two.

But the moment the news app opened, the front page had been occupied by another face.

The push column, as occupied

#BaekSiOnNewFilmKickoff#

#BaekSiOnBuzzCut#

#BaekSiOnDiesInThreeFilmsStraight#

#BaekSiOnAnswersTourQuestion#

Lee Ji-eun stared at the rail and wrinkled her nose.

Her phone cared about Baek Si-on more than she did.

She was quitting the news app when she noticed one unread message still pinned at the top of the push column.

The sender was precisely the person occupying the front page.

Lee Ji-eun opened the chat.

Nine photos poured onto the screen in rapid succession.

Only then came the sentence after them.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Si-on

[nine photos — an orange-and-white support truck, canopy up, banner along the side: “Please safely survive to the wrap party. Third time too. — IU”]

Baek Si-on

Thank you for the support.

Lee Ji-eun studied all nine photos from first to last.

An orange-and-white truck body, a canopy on the roof, a menu board listing coffee and desserts.

The banner hung along the truck’s flank, its signature perfectly legible.

IU.

The support message was equally unmistakable.

“Please safely survive to the wrap party. Third time too.”

Her finger hovered above the input box.

A normal person would have asked, at this point, what the truck was.

She typed something else entirely.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Who took the photos for you?

Send.

No read marker appeared beside the message.

He had plainly thrown himself back into shooting.

She waited a while.

Still nothing.

Lee Ji-eun opened the nine photos again.

Who would have taken them?

Baek Eun-ah?

A co-star?

The catering supplier?

Or...

Choi Jin-ri.

Her thoughts paused on the last name for a moment.

She leafed through the photos once more.

Composition: standard.

Angles: fixed.

Several frames even showed the fixed promotional-shooting angle.

It must have been the supplier.

Not that brat of a peach.

Good.

Immediately afterward, she registered a far more ridiculous problem.

The truck was not from her at all.

Yet the first thing she’d cared about was who held the camera.

She decided to ignore the discovery.

Except that the presence beside her kept growing stronger.

Lee Ji-eun looked up and caught Jeong Han-teuk’s eyes darting evasively.

Her eyes narrowed, slowly.

“Han-teuk oppa.”

“Mm?”

She turned the phone screen toward him — nine support photos, aimed at his face.

“This. Don’t tell me it was you?”

Jeong Han-teuk knew the cover was blown.

“Mm.”

“Under my name?”

“Mm.”

“Why?”

A very distinct question mark materialized on Lee Ji-eun’s face.

Jeong Han-teuk answered as though it were self-evident.

“Isn’t it so you don’t feel you owe him one over the chocolate and end up running hot-headed to his set in person?”

Lee Ji-eun stared at him.

The righteous confidence began leaking air.

The chocolate had been his errand too.

The food truck was likewise his errand.

If Lee Ji-eun took one step further back down this chain, she might discover her agent manufacturing a friendly exchange between two parties neither of whom knew anything about it.

Fortunately, Lee Ji-eun’s attention stayed on the truck.

She locked the screen.

“Minding my business for me.”

“……”

Jeong Han-teuk felt somewhat shortchanged, deep down.

The labor, as unbilled

Rush-contacted the supplier.

Confirmed the production headcount.

Curated the menu.

Designed the banner.

Completed the courtesy chain on her behalf.

Half a day’s work. Words received: “minding my business.” Praise quotient: zero.

Lee Ji-eun stood to head back to the set.

Two steps out, she stopped.

“How much was it?”

Jeong Han-teuk failed to follow for a second.

“What?”

“The truck.”

“Three million two hundred and eight thousand won.”

Lee Ji-eun lowered her head and operated her phone for a while.

Then she walked on toward the shooting area.

Cindy’s cold shoulder-glide re-engaged.

The back of her looked no different from when she had walked out.

A dozen-odd seconds later.

A vibration came from his pocket.

Jeong Han-teuk pulled out his phone.

The bank notice had already popped to the center of the screen.

KB Kookmin Bank

[Deposit notice]

Credit: 5,000,000 won. Remitter: Lee Ji-eun.

Jeong Han-teuk stared at the string of digits.

Lee Ji-eun had collected a favor from Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on believed Lee Ji-eun cared about him unprompted.

The catering supplier closed a rush order.

The ‘The Three Evils’ production ate afternoon snacks.

And he himself had cleared one million eight hundred thousand won, net.

The five-way win, as booked

Lee Ji-eun: a favor banked, a suspicion quietly retired, cover intact.

Baek Si-on: one truck of snacks, one message signed IU, pride fed.

The supplier: a rush order, at rush rates.

The production: one hundred and fifty-two portions of morale.

Jeong Han-teuk: +1,800,000 won, and one theorem richer.

A transaction structure you could submit to a business school without blushing.

Jeong Han-teuk looked up; Lee Ji-eun was already back on set, head down, listening to the director’s notes.

Cold profile.

No different from the Cindy who was all mouth and impossible to soothe.

But five million won does not lie.

Her mouth said: minding my business.

Her transfer overpaid the invoice by one million eight hundred thousand.

Jeong Han-teuk looked down and checked his bank balance one more time.

The chocolate, once.

The food truck, once.

Both operations had returned above-market yields.

He had suddenly discovered that he seemed to be in possession of an entirely new wealth code.

❝

Route goodwill properly between Baek Si-on and Lee Ji-eun, and cash flow generates itself.

— the wealth code, as discovered in a folding chair outside Stage 4

Of course.

The business model’s greatest risk was equally clear.

The day the two principals sat down and seriously reconciled accounts, he might need to emigrate at once.
