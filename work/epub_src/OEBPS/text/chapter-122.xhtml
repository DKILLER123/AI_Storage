Chapter One Hundred Twenty Two

A Chaebol’s Sponsorship Settles a Boast

October 21 Morning COEX Convention Center · Hall A · Seoul Three Aslans on a turning dais, one song on a loop, and the whole industry holding its breath for a number.

The next day, Seoul. The main hall on the first floor of COEX had been taken over by Hyundai Motor. On the central dais three Aslans stood in black, white and deep grey. The turntable revolved slowly; the light fell from the ceiling and ran along the paintwork like satin.

Behind them, on the great screen, the Aslan’s first television commercial played on a loop.

TVC · Hyundai Aslan · Way Down We Go

Morning mist. A test track. A black body.

Baek Si-on, in a dark suit, walking in from the edge of the frame.

The opening of Way Down We Go pressed down, low and heavy.

Most of the media in the room had already seen the advertisement. Even so, watching it again on the launch-event screen, somebody here and there could not help murmuring.

Launch hall · murmur over the TVC

“This ad looks expensive.”

“It makes the car look expensive, too.”

“It’s not the car that got more expensive. It’s the man who carries the car up a grade.”

“……”

Nearly eight hundred people filled the floor below the stage: press, dealers, automotive bloggers, industry analysts, Hyundai executives, VIP clients of the brand — and Baek Si-on’s fans. The launch event was not open to the public, but Hyundai had reserved a hundred “brand experience officer” places inside the official invitations, distributed by lottery.

Hyundai Aslan · launch event

official invitations only · not on sale anywhere

Seats available100places

Four hundred and seventy to one — a lottery Korea would have queued for anyway.

✿ ✿ ✿

October 21 10:00 a.m. COEX Convention Center · Hall A · main stage The PowerPoint is perfectly adequate. Nobody is watching the PowerPoint.

At ten sharp the launch event began. First came the Hyundai Motor global CEO’s welcome video. Then the Aslan product presentation: configuration, powertrain, chassis, safety systems, interior materials, a value-for-money analysis. The slides were delivered by the product manager of Hyundai’s domestic marketing division. He was earnest, and the data was solid. But nobody in the room was paying attention to the deck. Everyone was waiting for one person.

The product presentation ended. The lights dimmed. The host’s voice came out of the speakers:

Hyundai Aslan · launch event · official call

“Ladies and gentlemen — will you welcome the brand ambassador of the Hyundai Aslan—”

The screen cut to the last frame of the commercial. The black Aslan swept through the test-track corner, one perfectly stable arc pressed into the light. Then the picture went black, and a single spotlight opened on the side of the stage.

“— Mr. Baek Si-on.”

Baek Si-on walked out.

The flashguns detonated at once. Applause. Screaming. And, from the brand-experience-officer section, a wall of sound that had nothing to do with a car launch and everything to do with a concert.

“Baek Si-on-ssi!”

“Over here!”

“This way!”

“……”

The host smiled and asked:

“Baek Si-on-ssi. As the first owner of the Aslan, how does it actually drive?”

Baek Si-on took the microphone.

“Very steady.”

The host waited three seconds.

Nothing more came.

A ripple of laughter went through the room. The host had clearly done his homework and knew this guest’s answer style could not be measured against an ordinary celebrity’s. He picked the thread up smoothly.

“Very much in keeping with the Aslan’s brand character.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Mm.”

Down in the audience, the Hyundai marketing director’s mouth twitched. The man really was a miser with words. Yet, strangely, the press ate it up. If any other celebrity answered like that, it would read as insincere. When Baek Si-on answered like that, it read as the shortest test drive review ever written by someone who had actually driven the car — because he did hold the key to the first production Aslan on earth.

Launch stage · the round of questions

“About the filming of the commercial — what was the moment that stayed with you?”

“The take where the mist came in on cue. The car did the rest.”

“Why did Way Down We Go feel right for the Aslan?”

“Because it doesn’t ask you to be excited. It asks you to already be somewhere.”

One question after another — the shoot, the song, the fit — and Baek Si-on answered all of them.

In the second half of the event the host left the stage. Crew wheeled out a standing microphone. Baek Si-on walked to it, and the room fell quiet. A few seconds later, the backing track began: the opening of Way Down We Go. He gripped the microphone stand and began, eyes lowered.

Oh father tell me…

The instant his voice came out, the journalists who had been writing things down stopped moving their fingers. This song was nothing like Legend. No stadium detonation. No Worlds-final adrenaline. It was lower. Colder. A particular kind of high-end gravity, falling. And precisely because of that, it fit the Aslan in an unexpected way — a car not for a young man’s hot-blooded impulse, but for a man who already occupies a certain place in the world.

★ Hyundai Aslan launch · Way Down We Go · live

One standing mic, eight hundred people, no pyrotechnics. The song does the lighting itself.

“Oh father tell me —”

“Do we get what we deserve?”

The low end pressed into the floor of Hall A like something physical. In the dark, several phones were filming without anyone noticing they had raised them.

The song ended. Applause. Baek Si-on stepped back half a pace, bowed briefly, and left the stage. The shutter sounds stitched together behind him.

Baek Eun-ah was waiting at the side-stage entrance.

“Oppa. Hyundai says after the event there’s a media group interview — about twenty minutes — then a dealer session, and after that you can leave.”

“Mm.”

Eun-ah studied his face.

“You look relaxed.”

“A launch event is easier than the World Championship.”

“…Fair.”

After all — a man who had struck the Korean drum before sixty-three thousand people and nearly lifted the roof off the whole stadium could treat an eight-hundred-seat indoor hall like karaoke in a living room.

Half an hour later the launch event was over. The press dispersed. The dealers dispersed. The brand experience officers went home happy, carrying signed posters, photo prints and Hyundai gift bags.

✿ ✿ ✿

October 21 Afternoon → 5:20 p.m. Hyundai Motor headquarters · the order war room A number that stops a meeting cold, then a number that makes it stop breathing.

In the afternoon, at Hyundai headquarters, the sales data started coming in batch by batch. After the launch broadcast ended, traffic to the Aslan website exploded. Dealer phone lines burned. The reservation system’s backend kept refreshing. After ten minutes, the number jumped again. After half an hour, people in the room stopped talking. After an hour, the only sounds in the meeting room were mouse clicks and breathing that kept getting faster.

At 5:20 p.m., the first-day order tally:

Four thousand units.

The Hyundai marketing director stood straight up.

“Check it again.”

The sales lead’s voice was a little unsteady.

“Checked. Duplicate and anomalous orders have been removed. Current effective orders are around four thousand and still coming in.”

Four thousand. For the Aslan, that number was no longer “the launch went well.” It was an explosion. What had Hyundai’s internal expectation been? Eight hundred to twelve hundred units — and even that had been optimistic. The Aslan’s positioning was too delicate. Put kindly, it “filled a gap in Hyundai’s mid-to-high-end market.” Put unkindly, it was a rebadged Grandeur wearing a Genesis-sized price tag. That it sold at all was strange. That it sold four thousand was absurd.

Hyundai Aslan · first-day order tally · 17:20

4,000+ units

First-day revenue projection₩160 billionin a day

Four thousand cars in a day for a model the market had quietly written off.

But the marketing director knew the truth under the number. Of those four thousand orders, how many were genuinely bought because the chassis was tuned well, the NVH was good, and the value held up against the competition in its class? Perhaps three hundred. Maybe fewer. And the rest?

Because of Baek Si-on. The car he drove was an Aslan.

Consumer research · the lesson nobody likes to read

There is a brutal conclusion buried in consumer research: when a brand ambassador’s personal charisma outruns the product’s own value anchor, the purchase decision stops being a rational calculation and becomes an emotional vote.

Is the Aslan worth forty million won?

Yes. And no. It depends on what you mean by “worth.”

If “worth” means the chassis, the engine, the spec sheet, the line-by-line comparison against rivals — then probably not. If “worth” means sitting down in the driver’s seat, closing the door, and seeing Baek Si-on’s profile in the commercial and the low frequency of that song flash through your head, and thinking — yes, this is the feeling I wanted — then it is worth every won.

The marketing director watched the still-jumping number on the screen and slowly sat down. At an average of forty million won per car, that was a hundred and sixty billion won in sales. In one day. And Hyundai’s endorsement fee to Baek Si-on? One point two billion won. Per year.

Hyundai Aslan · the arithmetic

Return on the feebeyond contract — closer to legalised robbery

One point two billion buying a hundred and sixty billion. A return on investment so lopsided it did not read like a commercial arrangement at all. It read like some kind of legalised robbery.

Yet the marketing director felt no excitement. He felt the opposite: a very cold dread.

Because Baek Si-on’s IP value had already outrun the grade that one point two billion was meant to pay for. It was October now. The contract had been signed in September — when Baek Si-on had just won the Venice Best Actor award, his heat at its peak, and one point two billion was the absolute ceiling of the Korean endorsement market. One month later, the man’s commercial value was rising at a nearly uncontrollable speed. And next year?

Hyundai marketing · internal note · do not circulate

Keeping the endorsement, or keeping the man?

Water flows downhill, people climb — the oldest rule in the market

The threat

Mercedes, BMW, Porsche, Lexus — even the European ultra-luxury houses — will come for him. A man who dragged a mid-to-high-end sedan through an awkward positioning to four thousand first-day orders, starring in a Mercedes S-Class or a BMW 7 Series or a Porsche Panamera or a Bentley spot? What does Hyundai hold?

The contract?

A contract ends. Money? Someone will always pay more. The chassis number 0001 Aslan? A sentimental key ring, not a chain.

The real answer

The thing a carmaker and a star most fear is not a short contract. It is a shallow relationship. Keep him long-term and he must never feel Hyundai is merely a fund that pays on time — he must be able to call on the company as the sturdiest capital ally in the Korean entertainment world.

Conclusion: make him need us the way a fortress needs a wall, not the way a client needs an invoice.

The marketing director paced the office twice. Then he decided to test the water. He picked up the phone and walked out into the quiet corridor beyond the meeting room. He dialled Baek Eun-ah.

✿ ✿ ✿

October 21 Early evening Hyundai headquarters · a corridor with a view of the city Capital, which fears nothing so much as a man who wants nothing.

The phone rang a few times. It connected.

Marketing director → Baek Eun-ah · the order numbers

“Good evening, Director-nim.”

Baek Eun-ah’s voice was all business.

“Agent Baek. Thank you for today. The launch event went very well.”

“Thank you. Have the order figures come out?”

The marketing director paused for a second. This girl was getting better and better at going straight for the important number.

“They have.”

“How are they?”

“Around four thousand.”

There was a short silence on her end. When Baek Eun-ah spoke again, she was audibly brighter.

“Congratulations.”

“It should be us congratulating Baek Si-on-ssi.”

The director kept his tone easy.

“To be honest, this number is far beyond our internal expectations.”

“The Aslan is a good car.”

“No need to be so modest, Agent Baek. Whether the car is good is one thing. Who sells the car is another.”

He paused.

“Is Baek Si-on-ssi free to take a call right now?”

“One moment, please.”

A little rustling at the other end. A few seconds later, Baek Si-on’s voice came on the line.

Marketing director → Baek Si-on · the same call, handed over

“Director-nim.”

“Mr. Baek. Thank you for today.”

“Thank you as well.”

The marketing director looked down the corridor at the glass at the end. Beyond it, the lights of the city were coming up below Hyundai headquarters. He chose his words carefully.

“The launch event went very well today.”

“Mm.”

“And the orders went very well too.”

“Eun-ah told me.”

“You may not know this, Mr. Baek, but the Aslan project has carried a lot of internal pressure. Positioning, pricing, market acceptance — we had our doubts.”

“No need to worry anymore.”

“Right.”

The director laughed once.

“At least today, there’s no need to worry.”

He did not come straight to the point. A thing like this could not be said all at once.

“I’ve always felt that you and the Aslan are a perfect match in temperament. Steady. Restrained. Not the kind that lets its edge hang out — but there’s a forward-driving force underneath.”

It was, in fact, a line lifted from the description Baek Si-on himself had given of the car’s musical character when the advertising song was being made. Baek Si-on heard it. Did not pick it up.

“Internally we’ve been discussing going beyond the TVC — longer-term brand content. Owner stories. Global schedules. Business travel. Even special projects tied to music and film.”

“Have Eun-ah and your team connect.”

“Of course. Of course.”

The director smiled and agreed — then finally steered a little closer.

“Your schedule should only get more international from here. If there’s anything Hyundai can help with, please don’t hesitate.”

At that point, it was no longer a hint. It was an open offer.

Sitting in the back seat of the van, Baek Si-on looked out the window. He thought of Lee Ji-eun. He thought of Park Ji-eun. He had been wondering how on earth he was going to make good on the connection he had casually invented. And now the resource had delivered itself to his door.

“Actually, there is one favour I’d like to ask.”

On the other end of the line, the marketing director perked up almost instantly.

“Please, go ahead!”

A favour was the best possible outcome. Capital feared nothing so much as a counterpart who wanted nothing. Once a man asked for something, the debt of gratitude was banked.

“I’d like to help a friend get a role. But the writer is something of a big name, and going through a normal audition could get complicated.”

The marketing director was an old fox of the marketplace. He understood at once.

“What you mean is… you’d like Hyundai to sponsor the production?”

In the Korean drama world, a writer’s power was genuinely immense. But even the most famous writer had to be courteous to a top-tier sponsor walking into the production with serious money behind him.

“Yes.”

“Which production?”

“From what I hear it’s still in the planning stages. A KBS production. The writer is Park Ji-eun. I believe it will be called The Producers.”

The marketing director ran it through his head in a second. KBS. Park Ji-eun. A top broadcaster and a hit-making writer — a sponsorship Hyundai could put its name on and never lose money from, and on top of that, a favour of enormous size handed to Baek Si-on. Two birds with one stone.

“Who are we placing?”

He asked it with total directness. At this stage of the conversation there was no point in circling.

“IU.”

“The female lead?”

“The second female lead.”

The marketing director laughed.

He had braced himself to be asked to pluck a star from the sky. Instead the request was a single apple from the lowest branch.

“Mr. Baek. This request of yours… is almost too polite.”

“Give me a moment. I’ll start arranging it right away.”

In the back seat of the van, Baek Si-on looked at the city lights sliding past the window and let out the smallest breath. The boast he had blown into a phone screen the night before — one carefully aimed favour had begun to pay for it.
