Chapter Forty-Six

Eleven a.m., Rome Time

July 23 Evening Yeonnam-dong, Baek Si-on's flat A visitor, a bento, and the word "Venice"

Once they were inside, Baek Si-on squatted down, reached into the bottom shelf of the shoe cabinet, and slid out a pair of guest slippers, setting them at Choi Jin-ri's feet.

Jin-ri looked down at them.

Grey. Cloth soles.

“Thank you.”

As she changed shoes, Si-on had already taken the convenience-store plastic bag from her hand and carried it into the kitchen. He tore the plastic off the pork-cutlet bento and the other cardboard box, dropped them into the microwave, and set it for two minutes.

Coming back out, he reached past her and set the air-conditioning to twenty-four degrees.

“Anything to drink?”

Jin-ri pulled her gaze off the black-and-white photograph on the wall by the entrance — the one of Baek Jeong-hwan, Si-on's father — and looked at him.

“Water's fine.”

Si-on opened the refrigerator, took out a bottle of mineral water, walked over, and handed it to her.

Jin-ri took it with both hands.

“Thank you.”

The voice was a little more natural than before, now.

The cap and the mask were still on. But the sunglasses had come off, hanging from the collar of the T-shirt.

“Ding——”

The microwave chimed.

Si-on went to the kitchen, brought out the two heated bentos, and set them down on the dining table. The plastic lids had lifted a little from the steam, and a sweet, slightly scorched smell leaked out of the seams.

Jin-ri's eyes moved between the sofa and the table.

The sofa was closer.

But she chose the table instead. She pulled out the chair across from Si-on and sat down, chin on her hand, watching him eat.

The same picture as that nameless little shop in Yeonnam-dong. The same as that morning in the SM canteen. As if everything hanging on the hot search had nothing to do with him.

“Have you got your dress ready?”

The question came out of nowhere, and it threw Jin-ri for a loop.

Dress?

She blinked twice, letting her mind run a full lap before it caught up.

Ah. The Venice Film Festival.

That night of barbecue at her place, he'd said something about it. Told her to stop worrying about whether it would get in, and start thinking about what dress to wear.

But.

“The shortlist's only announced tomorrow, isn't it?”

It wasn't that Jin-ri lacked faith in the film. She'd lived through what Baek Jeong-hun made — every take, every frame, every time he said "again" with that set of his jaw. She knew what it cost.

But this was Venice. An A-list international festival. Directors from every corner of the world fought each other to get a film in. Just from Asia alone, a hundred-plus features competed every year for a handful of places. Line up every recognised Korean director in a row and Baek Jeong-hun might not even make the end of it.

First feature submitted, and it would be lovely to get in.

To state it so flatly, like it was already a fact…

But Jin-ri didn't push it.

She looked down at her hands on the table.

Nails cut short, clean, not a trace of colour. During promotion season, the stylist had always done her nails for her. Nothing loud — usually a bare pink or a pale bean-paste, matched to the stage outfit. Each time, after it was done, she'd turn her fingers under the light and watch the way it slid across the surface of the polish.

She didn't need that now.

Someone on hiatus doesn't need a manicure.

Someone on hiatus doesn't need a dress.

“……I probably can't go.”

The chopsticks over the pork cutlet didn't stop.

“Because of the company?”

“Mm.”

SM's statement had been explicit: all scheduled activities suspended, to concentrate wholly on rest and recovery. If she showed up on a red carpet in Venice in a gown while she was officially "suspended," it was the same risk as coming to see Si-on in Yeonnam-dong. Only the exposure was ten thousand times bigger than a flat in an apartment block.

“Do you want to go?”

Jin-ri looked at him.

She'd heard this sentence before. Outside Baek Jeong-hun's studio, he'd asked her whether she wanted to play Yeon-hui. She'd said yes. And he'd taken her at her word and fought to make it happen.

And now?

SM wasn't releasing her. What was he going to do?

Jin-ri didn't know.

But she did know he wasn't asking for her opinion. He was confirming the stance. Once confirmed, the thing after it, he'd handle himself.

“Yes.”

“Mm.”

Si-on answered, and went back to eating with his head down.

The room went quiet.

Only the hum of the air-conditioner and the soft scrape of chopsticks against the edge of the plastic box filled the silence.

Jin-ri rested her chin on her hand and watched him eat.

He ate fast. So fast that while she was still searching for a next topic, the first bento was already finished. The second was dragged over, opened, and continued. Four minutes. By the time both boxes were empty, she still hadn't come up with a subject.

Si-on stacked the two plastic boxes, carried them into the kitchen, and went about it with a fastidious sort of care — plastic box and cardboard box separated, the sauce packets set aside, the recycling done to the letter.

Jin-ri sat at the dining table, watching the silhouette of him tidying in the kitchen, and knew she had no reason to stay any longer. The bento was delivered. The person was seen. He'd eaten. She should go.

July 23 Evening The street below A taxi, and the line he'd once given her

When Si-on came out of the kitchen, he found Jin-ri already fully suited up and standing at the entrance.

“I'll walk you out.”

“Okay.”

“……”

The two of them went downstairs. The front door pushed open, and the evening heat of late July pressed in.

Si-on stood at the curb and raised a hand, hailing a taxi. It pulled over. Jin-ri opened the rear door, bent to get in, and then stopped. She turned back, looked at him through the sunglasses.

“If anything comes up, you can message me.”

Si-on went still.

He'd heard this sentence before. He was the one who'd said it to her.

“Okay.”

Jin-ri said nothing more. She bent down into the car and shut the door.

The taxi pulled away.

Merged into the flow of traffic. The tail-lights grew smaller in the dusk.

Si-on stood at the curb with his hands in his pockets, watching the car round the corner and disappear.

July 23 Night The study A professor, a petition, and a glass of milk

Professor Yoon Hye-ja found out anyway.

A colleague had sent her a link to the Cheong Wa Dae petition on KakaoTalk, with a single sentence: Is this your kid?

When Si-on came by to bring her milk, her laptop screen showed exactly that page — the national petition on the presidential website.

He glanced at the count.

Just over two thousand.

Not a lot, and not a little. Enough to make the news once. Still a long way from the two-hundred-thousand threshold that would oblige the government to respond.

“Mom.”

“Mm.”

“I'll handle it.”

Yoon Hye-ja turned to look at him. She didn't ask what happened. She didn't ask what he planned to do. She'd taught for so many years that she knew exactly how much of a burden a barrage of questions laid on a person who wasn't asking for them.

“I know.”

“Then get some rest.”

“Mm.”

Si-on set the milk down on the table. Before he closed the door, he glanced back at the computer. The page had already switched over to the faculty system. She was preparing her lecture.

He shut the door.

The sun would rise as it always did.

July 24 Morning Across the internet The abuse, and then the clock hitting eleven in Rome
July 24 Morning Seoul · a screen, a blog, and a repost The abuse didn't stop. It got louder.

The abuse didn't stop. It got louder.

At nine, a Naver blogger with three hundred thousand followers published a long post titled How a violent debt collector laundered himself into an independent musician. Three thousand words, not one solid piece of proof, but worded so precisely it never crossed into libel while still being enough to steer every emotion in the room.

At ten, the official account of the Korea Music Content Association reposted a statement on "fair competition in the music source market." It named no one. Everyone knew who it meant.

At eleven, the Cheong Wa Dae petition ticked up to five thousand.

Cheong Wa Dae · national petition

For a full investigation and ban of "chart-rigging singer" Baek Si-on

5,012 signatures

Response threshold200,000

Enough for one news cycle. Far from a mandated government reply.

Six o'clock in the evening, Korean time.

Eleven o'clock in the morning, Rome time.

The 71st Venice International Film Festival streamed the announcement of this year's main-competition shortlist, live from its own website.

The list was long. Twenty films. Thirteen countries and regions. One of them from Korea.

Within half an hour — before the list was even fully read out — the Korean media had collectively lost its mind.
