Chapter Seventy-One

The Pre-Tax Number Tastes Best

August 25 11:30 p.m. Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong Cold noodles, a ledger, and a number that shines pre-tax

That night. Yeonnam-dong. Home at half-past eleven.

Professor Yoon Hye-ja had left two bowls of cold noodles and a plate of cucumber kimchi in the kitchen, sealed under cling film, with half a watermelon in the fridge besides.

Baek Si-on carried a bowl of noodles to the table and ate noisily, slurp by slurp.

Across from him, Baek Eun-ah opened the laptop, pushed her own noodle bowl aside, and began to tap away at the keyboard.

She was doing the accounts.

Commercial gig receipts · nineteen shows

Shows performed19
Fee per show₩20,000,000
Gross₩380,000,000
Agency cut (15% of gross)−₩57,000,000
Cover-licence fee (19 shows, bundled)−₩8,500,000
Net take-home₩314,500,000

Pre-tax. And that is the number we want to look at.

Baek Eun-ah typed that figure into the first row of the Excel sheet, set the font to twenty points, bolded it, stared at it for three seconds, and felt deeply satisfied.

Then she started deducting.

The agency that had handled the festival bookings took a fifteen percent pass-through cut, per industry custom.

The commercial-performance licences for "Moves Like Jagger" and "Happy" required a per-use fee paid to the original rights holders.

Each song had a different price per show, but after the third gig Baek Eun-ah had negotiated a bundled rate with the rights agency.

The cover royalties for nineteen shows, total: eight million five hundred thousand won.

The final take came to three hundred fourteen million five hundred thousand.

Baek Eun-ah stared at that number.

Then she pulled up the memo app and found the record from months ago, when Baek Si-on had won the World Cup betting on a lottery ticket.

Three hundred sixty million.

The income from nineteen shows had come up only forty-odd million short of what he'd won on that ticket.

Of course.

This was the pre-tax figure.

Korea's top marginal comprehensive-income tax rate was thirty-eight percent, and with the local income tax added on, the real effective burden ran to around forty percent.

But that was next year's tax season. Tonight, he didn't want to think about it.

Tonight he only wanted to look at the pre-tax number.

The pre-tax number tastes best.

Baek Si-on drained the noodle broth, pushed the bowl aside, wiped his mouth, and glanced at Baek Eun-ah.

The girl's eyes, reflecting the screen glare, shone like two little bulbs, and the corners of her mouth were split nearly to her ears.

Tired, sure.

Nineteen shows. Nineteen rounds of coordinating venues, confirming equipment, arguing with the organizers about speaker specs, arranging the makeup room for Park Ji-hun, and handing Baek Si-on water, towels, and the mic.

Her work had been no lighter than his.

Baek Si-on reached out and ruffled the top of Baek Eun-ah's head.

"I'll give you fifty million."

"Huh?" Baek Eun-ah thought she'd misheard.

"Salary."

Under the harsh customary rules of the Korean entertainment business, a rookie manager only a few months into the job would count herself lucky to get a base pay that just met the minimum cost of living.

Even the senior managers who'd brought up idols in their companies could get, at most, ten percent of the artist's income as their cut in the contract.

And Baek Si-on had simply sliced her a big, solid chunk of real money at the top-end fifteen percent.

He'd never been stingy with strangers either — let alone with his own cousin who ran herself ragged for him.

"Cousin—!"

Baek Eun-ah shot up from the chair, charging around the table to hug him.

Baek Si-on straightened his arm, palm flat against her forehead, holding her at arm's length.

"Still jealous of them?"

Baek Eun-ah froze.

She knew who "them" meant.

Irene. Son Seung-wan. Kang Seul-gi. Park Soo-young.

The four girls still in the SM dorm.

"No."

Not one bit jealous anymore.

She'd been in the working world for three months, and her first fifty million won was already in hand.

And Irene and the others — how many years would they have to grind inside a system with no freedom at all before they could save up fifty million that was truly their own?

Maybe a year.

Maybe two.

Maybe by the time that day came, Baek Eun-ah would already be on her fourth or fifth fifty million, buying a house.

Thinking of Irene reminded her of her own escape from SM, which naturally led her thoughts to her cousin, fiercely defending her and repaying the thirty-two million for her.

She pursed her lips.

"Cousin…"

"Mm?"

"Just give me eighteen million."

"What do you mean?"

"That thing… before…"

Baek Si-on looked at the girl across the table, and his mind followed her to that afternoon in the blazing summer heat, when she'd run her legs off through the streets of Seoul buying tickets for obscure football matches.

"Good. You've grown up."

He didn't refuse, didn't say he wasn't short on money.

In this world, knowing to take money you'd earned and use it to settle the old debts of your own accord — that was the first step for an adult building an independent character.

Hearing that assessment, Baek Eun-ah wrinkled her nose at him in immense self-satisfaction.

"Hehe~"
