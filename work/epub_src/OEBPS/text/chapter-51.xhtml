Chapter Fifty-One

The Half-Exclusive at a Japanese Restaurant

July 25 Evening Cheongdam-dong, Gangnam-gu — a small Japanese restaurant The third shop down an alley, and a deal

Cheongdam-dong, Gangnam-gu.

A Japanese restaurant that didn't announce itself from the street. The third shop down the alley, its signboard already missing a light. You stepped past it twice before you noticed it had a door at all.

Son Nam-won had arrived ten minutes early.

The private room was done in tatami. The wooden partition slid open to reveal a low dining table, two cushions, a potted bamboo in the corner, and an air conditioner humming away with the patient indifference of a machine that had long ago given up on helping anybody feel comfortable.

He sat cross-legged. Switched to kneeling. His knees complained, and he went back to cross-legged.

When Baek Si-on had asked him to meet, the first thought that had crossed his mind was I'm about to get hit.

The second thought was is it too late to run.

The third thought was that the consequences of refusing might be worse than the consequences of showing up.

The wooden partition slid open again.

Baek Si-on walked in.

A baseball cap. A black T-shirt. Both hands empty.

That alone let Son Nam-won let some of the air back out of his shoulders.

At least there was no baseball bat.

He pushed himself up off the table, wincing:

“Baek-ssi, I—”

Baek Si-on waved a hand and settled onto the cushion across from him.

“Relax.”

He took the cap off and set it on the table.

“I'm not here to settle a score, and I'm not looking to make trouble for you.”

Son Nam-won stayed bent over, his lower back emitting a sound that was not dignified.

“I'm here to talk about working together.”

“Work… working together?”

Son Nam-won slowly straightened and blinked.

That word, coming out of the mouth of a man his own article had buried alive, had the unmistakable ring of a trap.

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on raised a hand for tea. The waitress set the door back, shut it behind her, and was gone. He slid one of the cups across the table.

“You know about Choi Sulli's situation?”

“I… I know.”

Know. That wasn't the half of it.

The press release announcing Choi Jin-ri's suspension had been OSEN's front-page feed. The several follow-up analyses that trailed it like a wake had been written by men Son Nam-won had put on the job. The on-stage demeanour controversy during the promotion cycle, the f(x) "one down" talking point, the flame war on the fan forums — he'd followed every inch of it.

“I need you to write a story for me. About SM stopping Choi Sulli from attending the Venice Film Festival.”

A very peculiar expression crossed Son Nam-won's face.

“Hold on.”

He raised a hand, like a student who'd fallen behind the lesson.

“Choi Sulli?”

“Mm.”

“The Venice Film Festival?”

“Mm.”

“What do those two have to do with each other?”

Fair question.

When the Venice website had published the selection list, the page had carried only the barest information:

71st Venice International Film Festival · official site

Main Competition — The Green Bottle Fly (South Korea) — dir. Baek Jeong-hun

The number of people in the whole country who knew Choi Jin-ri had acted in this film might well have been under thirty.

Son Nam-won was plainly not one of them.

Baek Si-on looked at the question marks arranged across that face, fished his phone out of his trouser pocket, opened a photograph, laid it flat on the table, and pushed it across.

Son Nam-won bent his head.

The photograph was the wrap shot of The Green Bottle Fly.

About a dozen people stood in it. Most of them Son Nam-won didn't recognise. But the two in the centre — those two he knew.

On the left, Baek Si-on. Thinner than he was now, with a smear of unremoved fake blood on his face, running from the temple down across the cheekbone.

On the right, Choi Jin-ri. Hair pulled into a low ponytail, barely any makeup, eyes curved up into two crescents, a row of teeth showing, her left hand throwing a V sign.

Nothing like the "stage demeanour controversy," the "exhausted and suspending all activities" Choi Sulli on the trending list.

Son Nam-won stared at the photograph for five seconds.

Looked up.

“The two of you. The leads of this film?”

“Mm.”

Son Nam-won's mouth opened and then closed.

His brain was turning at speed.

The male lead of the Korean film selected for the Venice main competition = Baek Si-on. He knew that. Dispatch's report had confirmed it.

But the female lead = Choi Jin-ri. That, he had not known. Nobody in the entire Korean media world knew.

Baek Si-on didn't give him long to sit with it.

“The festival opens in September. For any film in the main competition, the director and the leads walk the red carpet, attend the world premiere, and sit through the press conference. That's the baseline for how this thing works.”

“And you know exactly what state Choi Jin-ri is in right now.”

At last, Son Nam-won's mind started to engage.

Did walking the Venice red carpet count as a performance activity?

Of course it counted.

Not just counted — it was the highest tier there was. A global broadcast, hundreds of international press trailing her, photographs filling every entertainment front page on the planet within twenty-four hours.

“Is this… an exclusive?”

Son Nam-won's news instinct was waking up.

“A half-exclusive. The fact that Choi Jin-ri is the female lead — right now, you're the only journalist in Korea who knows it.”

A half-exclusive.

A first-mover advantage.

That was a scarcity OSEN didn't get handed every day.

Inside Son Nam-won, the sting of being publicly humiliated by Dispatch and the pull of this meat on the table were swinging hard on either end of a scale.

“So… how do you want it written?”

The moment those words were out, the scale had already tipped.

Baek Si-on watched him.

“Simple. Use the angle that SM stopped its own artist from attending the Venice Film Festival, and push it in the direction of 'a chance to bring glory to the country, thwarted.'”

He paused.

“As for Sulli herself… I'd like you to lean into what you're best at.”

“What I'm best at?”

“Turn her into a victim.”

The Industry's Eye · on the word “victim”

The word “victim” in news writing has a very specific target. The moment the public settles on calling someone a victim, the party applying the pressure automatically becomes the perpetrator.

You don't report on a victim. You manufacture one — then the rest follows on its own.

But once the rush had passed, Son Nam-won's brain started doing arithmetic.

“Baek-ssi… you know what SM Entertainment means to the Korean media world.”

“More than half of OSEN's entertainment copy feeds on the SM system. Girls' Generation, EXO, f(x), SHINee — the comeback teasers, the exclusive leaks, the album pre-release listens, they're all sent to us point-to-point by SM's PR division.”

He didn't finish the sentence.

Baek Si-on understood.

Cut the feed, lose the exclusives.

But that wasn't his problem.

“This story belongs to you until I walk out that door. The moment I'm out of it, it belongs to any outlet that's willing to run it.”

Baek Si-on said it softly, and Son Nam-won's expression stiffened.

The arithmetic had started.

Dispatch's thunderclap of an investigation had already taken his earlier piece about the "violent debt collector" and ground it face-first into the pavement, over and over, without mercy.

If he didn't soon throw out a nuclear headline — something bigger than "Baek Si-on gets cleared" — to pull the eyes away, then he, Son Nam-won, as entertainment editor-in-chief, would be the man on the hook for "losing two major exclusives in a row."

The CEO doesn't yell at the reporter.

The CEO just replaces the editor-in-chief.

But if he agreed, if it ran, then SM's press feed was cut.

That was a real loss.

SM's press releases carried at least three-tenths of OSEN's entertainment-page traffic.

But — SM wasn't the only mountain.

On the JYP side. Miss A was in the middle of a promotional cycle, GOT7 had debuted not even six months before, and 2PM's army-enlistment timeline was a ready-made long-form topic. Park Jin-young himself was a walking archive of news material, capable of generating a stir every few weeks without breaking a sweat.

On the YG side. A BIGBANG comeback was always the year's biggest traffic bomb, and while 2NE1 had internal friction brewing, the friction itself was the news. Yang Hyun-suk's various controversies — dig one shovelful and you hit a headline.

And then there was Big Hit.

That small company had pushed out a boy group called BTS the year before, still flailing around in the mud of the small- to mid-sized agency tier.

But Son Nam-won had done entertainment news long enough that his nose was sharper than a dog's.

Those boys had something in them that he couldn't quite name, and it didn't look like the kind of thing that stayed in the mud.

Lose one — SM.

And there was still JYP, YG, Big Hit, and a whole stack of small and mid-sized companies waiting to be mined.

Press feeds could be rebuilt.

But once a man was fired, everything was too late.

“Can I use this photograph?”

Son Nam-won pushed the wrap shot back across the table.

Baek Si-on gave a single nod.

Son Nam-won's shoulders came down. He fished his phone out of his pocket, opened a recording app, and set it on the table between them.

“Then let's start. From the beginning — how did Choi Jin-ri get into this film.”

Baek Si-on looked at the little red button of the recording app winking on the screen.

And began.

“This past March——”
