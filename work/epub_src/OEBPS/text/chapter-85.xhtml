Chapter Eighty-Five

Performance Art at Arrivals

September 7 still KE927 · cruise First class · the partition of the next seat A reading light, six chuuni lines, and a threat involving the emergency exit

In fact, someone really could stop him.

On the other side of the partition of the next seat, Choi Jin-ri quietly opened her eyes.

Perhaps the halo of the reading light beside her had pricked her eyelids. Or perhaps, from the moment the plane had taken off, she had not been asleep at all.

She tipped her head a little.

Just in time to see Baek Si-on staring, unblinking, at the notebook in his hand.

The expression was very focused.

Curiosity started before reason did.

What was he writing?

Did it have anything to do with her?

The second thought came up, and Choi Jin-ri cursed herself in her head.

Peeking at what someone else was writing — how was that different from being a pervert!

But her body was already moving.

The head came over, slowly.

Neck stretched, chin tipped up a little, eyes stealing a look toward the notebook in Baek Si-on’s hand.

The collar of the airline pyjamas had slipped to one side from lying too long, most of a collarbone out. She did not attend to it.

At this moment Baek Si-on was sunk in a certain strange satisfaction brought by those six lines he had written, and the peripheral-vision system failed.

Because when a person is extremely focused, the brain automatically shuts the channels that perceive the surrounding environment, and concentrates all processing resources on the single point being looked at.

The nose, however, did not.

A very faint smell drifted over.

Baek Si-on’s nasal cavity received the signal. The brain completed the match in half a second.

Choi Jin-ri.

Looking, beside him.

Snap—

He closed the notebook at the speed of light.

Baek Si-on turned his head.

Choi Jin-ri’s head was less than thirty centimetres from him.

Two pairs of eyes met in the warm light of the reading lamp.

“Is it lyrics?”

She blinked once.

“?”

“What you wrote in the notebook — those are lyrics, right? You’re going to put out a new song?”

In fact both of them knew perfectly well.

Those extremely chuuni lines about “passed down through the ages” and “become a legend” had nothing whatsoever to do with the rhyme scheme of a pop lyric, not with an eight-foot pole.

But Baek Si-on had no choice.

Admitting these lines were rap lyrics to be chanted over a beat was still better than admitting he was staying up in first class writing a teenage boy’s diary.

“…Mm.”

“Then I’ll look forward to it~”

Having said that, Choi Jin-ri’s head pulled back, and she nested into her own seat again.

Baek Si-on looked at the side of her face, already withdrawn.

“You’re laughing, aren’t you.”

“I’m not.”

Choi Jin-ri turned her head to face the bulkhead on her side.

Only the back of a head for him.

“You are laughing.”

“Really not.”

The back of the head spoke, the voice steady enough to be suspicious:

“I’m looking forward to your new song, very seriously.”

“…”

He could not be bothered to keep pulling this kind of nutritionless nonsense with Choi Jin-ri.

He stuffed the notebook deep into the pocket on the side of the seat.

Very deep.

Deep enough that he sincerely hoped this damned hardback would go straight through the special steel plate of a Korean Air cabin and drop vertically into a Pacific trench thirty thousand feet below.

Out of sight, out of mind.

The hum of the engines took the whole space back.

About five minutes on.

A humming came from beside him, there and not there.

The tune was improvised, not a song, but the lyric Baek Si-on heard very clearly.

“I will become a legend~ I will write my own history~”

Baek Si-on’s eyelid jumped.

“My name~ will be passed down through the ages~”

“Choi Jin-ri.”

The humming on that side stopped.

“Sing that again and I’ll throw you out the emergency exit.”

Choi Jin-ri’s side was quiet for a few seconds, then she laughed out, completely.

This was perhaps the most unrestrained, least image-managed laugh she had had in recent times.

“…”

“Good night.”

The tail of Choi Jin-ri’s laugh came in.

✿ ✿ ✿

About half an hour on.

Baek Si-on still had not slept.

“I will become a legend.”

“My name will be passed down through the ages.”

After Choi Jin-ri had hummed them into trot, the texture of those two sentences in his head underwent a subtle change.

From “diary” to “lyrics.”

What if they really were written as a song?

The thought came up, and he first vetoed it, on instinct.

Writing a song specifically to rave about his own great achievements was a behaviour far too shameful, completely beyond the limit his face could bear.

Unless…

Inside these extremely chuuni lyrics, he hard-stuffed a little of other people’s inspirational stories.

Let the listener think the song was singing everyone’s dream.

As for him.

He would just sneak a little private goods in.

Confuse the ear.

Mm.

This commercial plan sounded like it had a bit of something.

Go back and talk to Jung Jae-joon.

Thinking that, sleep finally caught him.

The breath began to lengthen.

Consciousness sank, layer by layer.

September 8 3:00 pm Incheon International Airport · Terminal 1, international arrivals Thirty outlets, three majors, and a badge tied to a forehead

September eighth.

Three in the afternoon.

Incheon International Airport, Terminal 1.

The exit of the international arrivals hall was already packed beyond passage.

Park Jae-won stood at the very outer rim of the crowd, a shotgun mic clenched in his hand, that three-thousand-won Insight badge from a Hongdae quick-print shop hanging around his neck.

He was a hundred and seventy-three centimetres.

Among Korean men, not short.

But standing now in this steel forest composed of long guns and short cannon, he felt like a child who had just entered kindergarten.

He counted.

Left to right, the media marks he could see were at least thirty.

OSEN, Sports Chosun, Star News, Dispatch…

These were entertainment media. He knew them. No surprise.

The surprise was the rows behind.

KBS.

MBC.

SBS.

The news divisions of the three majors were all here.

Each house at least two cameras, ENG reporters fully kitted with lights and sound, the posture no different from interviewing a member of the National Assembly.

Further back.

Chosun Ilbo.

JoongAng Ilbo.

Dong-A Ilbo.

The culture-desk reporters of Korea’s three major papers had come too.

Even YTN and Yonhap were there.

Park Jae-won looked both ways again.

Beside the media zone, in the public area the airport security had fenced off with iron barriers, about two or three hundred fans were packed in.

He took his gaze back from the fan zone and looked down at his own kit.

One shotgun mic.

One photographer.

One badge.

Park Jae-won looked up again at the black wall in front of him, composed of telephotos and ENG cameras.

Could he squeeze in?

Would his question be heard?

In the middle of doubting himself.

He suddenly remembered, one morning a few days ago, his own boss Son Nam-won sitting in front of a computer and saying, in a profoundly mysterious tone, that promise.

“As long as he sees you carrying an Insight badge, he will absolutely stop and answer your question.”

Park Jae-won’s gaze went back and forth twice between the surging crowd and his own chest.

If he hung it on his chest.

In this arrivals line of a few hundred people shoving each other, this palm-sized plastic card would absolutely be blocked dead by the backs of the reporters in front.

Then it could only appear in the place on earth least likely to be blocked.

So he took the blue lanyard off his neck.

Held both ends of the lanyard, looped it over the top of his head, and tied a dead knot at the back of his skull.

Then felt the card on his forehead, confirming it was not crooked.

This should be conspicuous enough.

Unless that newly minted Best Actor was not only short-sighted but also physically blinded, he could not possibly miss this extremely visually polluting marker.

Beside him, a Sports Chosun stills photographer turned his head, looked at him once, and turned back.

What use was a stunt like that? As if seeing a card stuck to your forehead would make him answer your question first?

The woman with a DSLR on the right noticed too.

She took the camera down from in front of her face, looked at Park Jae-won for two seconds, then pretended to look down and adjust focus, but the corner of her eye swept his forehead, unmistakably, twice.

Park Jae-won took all those eye-knives as they came.

In media.

Face was the cheapest consumable.

Click-through was the only dignity.

September 8 3:15 pm Incheon · international arrivals, the glass doors A shutter avalanche, three streams, and a man who would not stop walking

Three fifteen in the afternoon.

The automatic glass doors of the international arrivals exit slid open to both sides.

The flash that had waited a full two hours exploded in that instant like an avalanche, all together.

A few hundred high-powered flashes hard-lit the wide hall of Incheon Airport into an operating-theatre grade of shadowless-lamp site.

The density was higher than the Venice carpet.

Because the photographers in Venice shot to a rhythm, two or three a second, composition and timing.

Korean entertainment media did not do that.

A dozen a second.

Full auto burst.

Hold the shutter and do not let go.

The sound was like a crowd cracking sunflower seeds at the same time.

Walking at the very front was Baek Jeong-hun.

The culture-desk reporters of several old, authoritative papers stuck to him at once.

These people did not care about gossip.

They held recorders, backing up under the shoves of security, throwing out hard questions about the European independent-circuit system, the genealogy of violence aesthetics, and what the Lion of the Future meant for Korean film history.

Baek Jeong-hun had not yet come out of the exhaustion of a long-haul flight, and could only be walked by two airport security, playing tai chi as best he could.

Coming out of the corridor next was Choi Jin-ri.

The entertainment media and second-tier fashion magazines that had not grabbed the most central positions instantly dumped all their fire on her.

The machine-gun questions were all around the brand sponsorships on her body, her state of mind on returning to the centre of public opinion, and the closing look-book.

The several large SM bodyguards travelling with her formed a human wall with extreme professionalism, walking her forward an inch at a time.

The two streams split off.

Then.

The ultimate storm-eye of the arrivals line came out last.

Baek Si-on was in an extremely ordinary black T-shirt, the visor of a cap pulled very low, a backpack slung across his right shoulder. The Volpi Cup was wrapped inside it.

The shouting of the thirty-odd security holding the line was, in that second, instantly drowned by the insane sound-wave of a few hundred reporters and fans.

“Mr. Baek Si-on! What do you want to say to the domestic audience first, after taking the prize?!”

“Mr. Baek Si-on! Congratulations on the Volpi Cup! Can you say two sentences?!”

“Baek Si-on! Baek Si-on! This side! Look this side!”

“What’s your first feeling, back in Korea?!”

“What’s the next plan?!”

“Si-on oppa—!!!”

“Best Actor—!!!”

“…”

Baek Si-on lifted the visor a little, showing the full brow and eyes. His feet, however, did not stop.

Could not stop.

The rule of this kind of arrivals corridor was completely different from a carpet.

On a carpet you could stop, hold a pose, leave the reporters enough time.

In an arrivals corridor, the moment you stopped, the flow behind would jam dead, the scene would go out of control, security would collapse.

So you could only take interviews while walking.

“Baek Si-on-ssi! Congratulations on the Volpi Cup! How do you feel right now?”

Baek Si-on glanced at the first mic shoved in front of him.

KBS.

“It hasn’t fully sunk in yet. It’ll probably take a few days to digest, slowly.”

The KBS reporter walked two steps with him, the ENG camera operator walking backward beside them with the rig on his shoulder.

Another mic came in from the right.

MBC’s.

“Mr. Baek, the mother you mentioned in the speech, Yoon Hye-ja — did she see the live stream? What was her reaction?”

“She told me to know when to stop.”

“What?”

“She cares quite a lot about personal privacy.”

The reporters laughed.

Several camera lenses shook. The operators holding the rigs laughed too.

✿ ✿ ✿

About halfway down the corridor.

Baek Si-on had already answered seven or eight outlets, talking as he walked, the rhythm controlled reasonably well.

Sound came at him from in front, from the left, from the right, all at once. Mics kept sprouting from the gaps in the iron horses like mushrooms after rain.

“Mr. Baek, what’s the first thing you want to do, back in the country?”

“Eat.”

“Any Korean food you’ve been wanting?”

“My mom’s cooking.”

“Any plans for the next work?”

“Not decided yet.”

“…”

Park Jae-won was squeezed at the tail of the line.

The biggest problem with a moving arrivals is that everyone is walking, and if you do not keep up fast enough, in three seconds you will be thrown to a distance the mic cannot reach.

The mic-wall in front, composed of the three majors and the three papers, had no gaps.

The big-outlet reporters had long legs, big voices, excellent kit.

Their ENG operators carried professional shoulder cameras, the lighting people held LED fill boards, the sound people held furry shotgun mics.

Three or four to a group, like a precision interview tank, walking, shooting, recording, extremely efficient.

Park Jae-won had one shotgun mic in his hand.

The photographer behind him was shooting and jogging after him, gasping.

“Faster, faster! We can’t keep up!”

Park Jae-won bit down, sped up, and started squeezing forward from the right of the line.

The badge on his forehead bounced with his running.

He turned sideways past Star News’s photographer.

Turned sideways past OSEN’s sound person.

In front was JTBC’s wall of people.

A photographer walking backward and a lighting person with an arm-rig, a reporter with a mic standing in the middle, three people shoulder to shoulder, blocking the right half of the corridor dead.

Park Jae-won drew a deep breath.

From the roughly ten-centimetre gap between the lighting person and the sound person, he forced his body through.

The lighting person looked back at him once, but had no time to take it up, turned, and kept walking.

Park Jae-won squeezed into the second row.

Baek Si-on was less than three metres ahead, answering a question from a JoongAng Ilbo reporter.

“…I’m very grateful for the jury’s recognition.”

The reporter followed up:

“You are now the first Best Actor in Korean film history at any of the three majors. How do you feel about that?”

“The way is long, and I shall search high and low.”

The reporters in the front row took that sentence into every mic and every recorder.

Park Jae-won held the shotgun mic and pushed the foam head out through a gap in the second row.

Not high enough.

The shoulder camera of the photographer in front blocked most of him.

He went up on his toes.

Still not enough.

He drew a deep breath, lifted the shotgun mic over his head, and opened his mouth at the same time.

“Mr. Baek Si-on! Insight reporter Park Jae-won! May I ask—”

The voice was drowned in a dozen mics shouting at the same time.

No one heard.

Baek Si-on did not react.

Park Jae-won’s heart sped up.

The end of the corridor already showed the exit of the terminal hall.

The van was parked outside.

Thirty metres left, at most.

Thirty metres walked, Baek Si-on in the car, the door shut, over.

He would not have a second chance.

Park Jae-won lifted the shotgun mic another five centimetres.

The badge on his forehead had started to slide with sweat. He pressed it back with his left hand, then, with the loudest voice of his life, shouted one sentence.

“Mr. Baek Si-on!!! Insight!!!”

This time, Baek Si-on’s feet stopped.

The security line at the very front stamped on the brakes with him.

The reporter pack behind, carrying heavy kit, piled into each other on the inertia. All kinds of swearing and wailing rose at once.

Baek Si-on swept a look toward the direction the voice had come from.

Second row.

A young man who looked twenty-five or twenty-six, a shotgun mic in his hand, a badge stuck to his forehead… no. Tied to it.

Like a clown who had taken a wrong turn at a large sacrificial rite.

Absurd.

An eyesore.

And extremely professional.

“The reporter with the badge tied to his head. What’s your question?”

The arrivals hall, which a second before had been boiling over, went into a collective dead silence that lasted a full three seconds.

The gaze of the whole floor turned, as one, in the direction of the newly minted Best Actor’s gaze, and looked, in collective astonishment, at Park Jae-won, who at this moment was in a state of severe crash.

Feeling those few hundred sharp lines of sight that could have flayed him alive, Park Jae-won swallowed a large mouthful of saliva.

“Uh… that… Baek Si-on-ssi, first, congratulations on the Volpi Cup! I wanted to ask, you thanked a lot of people in the speech. Can you say, specifically, who you most want to see right now?”

The voice was much steadier than expected.

Perhaps after adrenaline had secreted past a certain threshold it produced, instead, a strange calm.

“My mom.”

Having said it, Baek Si-on stepped again, and kept walking.

The reporter pack behind surged up like a flood suddenly ungated, filling the place where Park Jae-won had stopped.

Park Jae-won was squeezed to the edge of the corridor.

He watched the crown of the black cap rise and fall in the crowd, getting farther, and at last disappearing behind the automatic doors of the terminal exit.

Then he turned to look at the photographer behind him, who was crouched beside the iron horse, gasping like a dog.

“Did you get it?”

“Got it.”

The photographer lifted the camera and showed him the playback.

In the frame, Baek Si-on was looking toward the lens, his mouth frozen on the two syllables of “my mom.”

Park Jae-won nodded once.

Reached up and felt the plastic card on his forehead.

Untied the lanyard from the back of his skull, looped it back around his neck, the badge hanging on his chest.

Back where it belonged.

He picked up the shotgun mic and walked toward the terminal exit.

Two steps in, the corner of his mouth bent.

The sajangnim had not lied to him.

This badge really was something.
