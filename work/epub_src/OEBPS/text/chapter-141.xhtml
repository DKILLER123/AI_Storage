Chapter One Hundred Forty One

Romance Dies of Wardrobe Management

November 8 · night The same hour the medal settled against the blue fabric Yeouido · KB Financial Group headquarters · the top-floor conference room Six departments, one long table, and a screenshot the entire country has already seen.

At the same time.

In the top-floor conference room, the heads of brand, legal, compliance, digital channels, and risk management sat along both sides of a very long table.

On the projection screen, frozen mid-frame, was tonight’s most widely shared screenshot.

the conference-room projector · tonight’s most-shared screenshot

Baek Si-on, in the blue gonryongpo, the Silver Crown Cultural Medal on his chest, standing at the centre of the National Theater stage.

Frozen there while the rest of the country celebrated.

The PR director stood beside the screen with a laser pointer in his hand.

“The public feedback is very clear.”

He clicked to the next page.

The first exhibit was SK Telecom’s official tweet. The second was a passer-by’s video of the Hyundai Motor convoy. The third was a text excerpt from the acceptance speech itself.

“Both SK Telecom and Hyundai Motor were named out loud by Actor Baek Si-on in the acceptance speech. Tomorrow morning, both of those companies eat a very strong wave of positive coverage.”

“Which is to say: in tonight’s most valuable public cultural event, we were absent.”

Nobody in the conference room said anything.

Several of the executives looked distinctly unwell.

One of them rubbed the space between his brows.

“Why weren’t we part of this operation?”

“……”

The PR director opened his mouth.

Stopped for two seconds.

And then said, with great honesty:

“It’s not as if we could have sent an armoured cash truck to pick him up?”

The room went grave-silent.

A digital channels head, younger than the rest, failed to hold it in and coughed into his chest.

The head of risk management glared at him.

The PR director knew how absurd it sounded, but the more he turned it over, the more aggrieved he became.

A convoy of bulletproof-vested cash-in-transit guards, walking a man in a gonryongpo into the National Theater? Wouldn’t that have been the prequel to a bank robbery?

The young head of digital channels finally couldn’t help himself, and said quietly:

“Actually… we can still fix this.”

Everyone looked at him.

He cleared his throat and did his best to look like someone who had not just been laughing.

“Starting tomorrow morning, every digital screen at every KB branch in the country switches to a congratulatory display. All at once.”

The PR director picked it up immediately:

“KB Kookmin Bank congratulates its brand ambassador, Actor Baek Si-on, on receiving the Silver Crown Cultural Medal of the Republic of Korea.”

The head of brand nodded.

“Workable.”

The head of legal pushed up his glasses.

“The wording is fine. Just be careful not to imply the medal has any direct connection to KB.”

The head of compliance also nodded.

“Words like ‘President’, ‘government’, ‘national honour’ — use them carefully. Congratulations, yes. Binding them to us, no.”

The head of digital channels kept going:

“The app splash, the ATM screens, the website banner, the social media main images — all of them can switch together.”

The head of brand asked:

“Which picture do we use?”

The PR director looked at the projection screen.

What was on it now was the livestream frame of the acceptance speech.

the conference-room projector · the acceptance-speech frame

Blue gonryongpo.

The Silver Crown medal.

A standing microphone.

Behind him, the stage lights of the National Theater.

Good, said every face at the table. And not explosive.

It photographed beautifully. It was not explosive.

The head of brand thought for a moment.

“The one where the President pins the medal on, then.”

The head of legal shook his head at once.

“A photograph with the President in frame means confirming wire-service licensing and confirming the Blue House’s usage boundaries. Putting the President directly into a bank’s commercial advertising is a world of trouble.”

The head of compliance delivered the finishing cut.

“And we are not a government agency.”

The PR director sighed.

“Then we crop to the acceptance-speech photo?”

The head of digital channels frowned.

“The speech photo is good. But SK and Hyundai were already named in the speech, out loud, by him. If we use that one, we’re reminding everybody that KB wasn’t.”

The conference room went quiet again.

The line hurt.

It was also true.

In tonight’s famous scene, SK had owned the sky. Hyundai Motor had owned the ground. The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency had even owned the traffic order.

KB Bank alone was like a relative who had missed the red envelope because of network lag.

At that exact moment, the PR director’s phone buzzed.

Ding.

A Naver news push.

He looked down at it.

Then his brows slammed together.

Naver News · push notification

“An Arrogant Man of Privilege, or a Pure Madman for the Craft?” — Baek Si-on’s Forty-Minute Extreme Crossing

Other people in the conference room had received the push too.

“What is this?”

“This headline…”

“The whole country is praising him, and somebody picks tonight to push back?”

The head of brand’s face darkened.

“So desperate for traffic they’ve gone mad?”

The PR director opened the article.

Source: Insight. Editor-in-chief: Son Nam-won.

Several expressions in the room turned delicate for a second.

Insight’s nose for anything involving Baek Si-on had lately been beyond unreasonable.

So when Insight published on Baek Si-on, you did not read the headline.

You read the content.

The PR director scrolled down.

The article opened swinging.

“Privileged class,” it said. “A police escort.” “A helicopter spanning Jeonju and Seoul.”

It looked loaded for bear.

Then the second paragraph started, and the smell changed.

“……”

The PR director kept scrolling.

The more he read, the more speechless he became.

The title spat at you. The body worshipped.

How was this pushing back?

This was baiting every doubter in the country through the front door, sitting them down in a chair, and making them read a full argumentative essay on the subject of exactly how dedicated Baek Si-on really was.

The young head of digital channels said quietly:

“Clickbait.”

The PR director rolled his eyes.

“But it works.”

Insight understood traffic.

First, “an arrogant man of privilege” — to hook everyone who wanted to yell at him.

Then, “a pure madman for the craft” — to reclassify everything he had done.

And finally, SK, Hyundai, the police, and the production crew, all folded into a single narrative.

You couldn’t attack it.

You couldn’t praise it without feeling cheated by the headline.

Deeply immoral.

Also deeply media.

The PR director kept scrolling.

Until he reached the photo.

His finger stopped.

the article’s photograph · long lens, from the far side of the helipad

A silver-white SK helicopter, just down.

Beside the pad, a Hyundai Aslan convoy drawn up in formation. Outside the cordon, police and staff holding the line.

At the cabin door, a man in a blue gonryongpo, bending his way out of the helicopter — the rotor wash shoving his hem back, the head-crown perfectly straight, the chest not yet pinned.

A man air-dropped out of the Joseon dynasty into modern Seoul.

One frame. The whole absurd, dedicated, chaebol-linked, state-honoured, entertainment-industrial night, compressed.

For one instant, nobody in the conference room spoke.

The acceptance-speech screenshot had been good.

But this one was news.

This one was an event.

The head of brand slowly sat up straight.

“That one.”

The head of legal was looking at the image too.

“An Insight news photo means licensing.”

The PR director already had his phone out.

“I’ll call them.”

The head of risk management hesitated.

“That photo has SK and Hyundai in it.”

The head of brand looked at him.

“Which is exactly why we use it.”

“Everyone else is already on the field. If we put out an ordinary congratulatory image now, it only shows we couldn’t keep up. Use this one, and at least KB is back inside tonight’s story.”

The head of digital channels added at once:

“Then the copy gets one more line — our thanks to every person who kept the time, kept the trust, and kept the honour on their post tonight.”

The head of compliance nodded.

“Acceptable.”

The PR director dialled.

“Hello, Insight? This is KB Financial Group, brand and public relations.”

Something came back down the line that the room couldn’t hear.

The PR director glanced at the photo on the projection screen.

“Yes — we want to buy the licence for tonight’s photo of Actor Baek Si-on coming off the helicopter.”

A few seconds.

The PR director’s expression changed.

“…The price?”

Everyone in the conference room looked at him.

The PR director was silent for two seconds.

Then he covered the mouthpiece and said, across the length of the table, to the head of brand:

“They say three media outlets, two brands, and one textbook publisher are already asking about this photo.”

“……”

A textbook publisher?

The young head of digital channels said quietly:

“The meme said textbook cover. Somebody took it seriously.”

The head of brand closed his eyes.

“Buy.”

The PR director nodded.

“Buy.”

A beat.

“Buy it now.”

This time, KB refused to look like a company with no internet.

November 8 · the same night A red sea of taillights, and a phone that has been quiet all evening Seoul · the back seat of a nanny van, homeward through the city Heels kicked off, a coat over the knees, and one chat window sitting at the top of the screen all day.

On a nearly empty Seoul road, Choi Jin-ri sat in the back of the nanny van and finally kicked her heels off.

Her ankles ached to the point of numbness.

The Fashion King premiere, the stage greeting, the media photo line, the follow-up interviews — all of it was finished.

Her manager was in the driver’s seat; he checked the rear-view mirror.

“Tired?”

“Not really.”

“Get some rest when you’re home.”

“Mm.”

Choi Jin-ri nodded, laid her coat over her knees, and looked down to unlock her phone.

The KakaoTalk screen lit up.

At the very top, still, was Baek Si-on.

She opened the chat.

Typed.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Choi Jin-ri

Hard day today, Silver Crown Crown Prince.

sent · no reply yet

After typing it, she stared at the line for two seconds.

It felt a little too familiar.

And it felt exactly right.

The matter of Baek Si-on receiving a state medal in the crown prince’s court robes of Sado had already had the whole country calling him “the Crown Prince” for an entire night.

One more person doing it could hardly count as going too far.

Choi Jin-ri pressed send.

Message sent, she laid the phone face-down on her knees.

Three seconds.

Five seconds.

Ten seconds.

No reply.

She thought about it and decided this was normal. Baek Si-on was probably still backstage at the National Theater right now, ringed in by officials, sunbaes, reporters, staff, and no telling how many people who wanted a photo with him.

Choi Jin-ri leaned back against the seat and let out a soft breath.

Outside the window, the Seoul night retreated one pane at a time, red taillights strung out into a broken line.

About seven minutes later, the phone buzzed.

Choi Jin-ri flipped it over instantly.

Baek Si-on had replied.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · seven minutes later

Baek Si-on

Hard work on the Fashion King premiere too.

Choi Jin-ri

I watched the livestream.

Baek Si-on

Mm.

Choi Jin-ri read it, and her eyes slowly curved.

It wasn’t that nobody had ever congratulated her before.

Staff, the director, fans, the members — they had all said it.

But this one was different, because tonight Baek Si-on stood in the brightest place in all of Korea.

Everyone was looking at him.

Everyone was talking about him.

And he had not forgotten that today, she had fought a battle of her own.

That pleased her more than any pretty sentence could have.

There it was again, too. That “Mm.”

In tonight’s good mood, even the “Mm” looked a lot less punchable than usual.

She kept going.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · the same chat, continued

Choi Jin-ri

You looked really handsome today. In that outfit, receiving the medal.

She typed the compliment, and the moment it went out she felt a flicker of embarrassment.

She did not recall it.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · the same chat, continued

Baek Si-on

The outfit is the production’s.

Choi Jin-ri

……

Choi Jin-ri

I meant the person.

She closed her eyes.

Romance dies of wardrobe management.

And she was still smiling.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · the same chat, continued

Baek Si-on

Thank you. You looked lovely today too.

Choi Jin-ri froze.

She looked back down at the screen.

Made sure she had not misread it.

He had complimented someone. Voluntarily!

No coaching. No correcting. None of her “you’re supposed to say…”

He had typed it himself.

Her ears slowly went hot.

She wanted, very badly, to fire back a whole wall of words.

But that would look far too eager.

So she stared at the screen for a full half minute, and then replied with enormous restraint:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on · the same chat, continued

Choi Jin-ri

I know.

Choi Jin-ri

Rest early, Silver Crown Crown Prince.

Baek Si-on

You too.

Choi Jin-ri looked at those two words, and the small pride in her chest bubbled up again.

Good.

At least it wasn’t “Mm.”

Massive progress.

She hugged the phone to her chest and laughed into it for a while.

The van kept rolling toward Seongbuk.

Outside, the city lights slid across her face.

Tonight she had not stood beside him.

But he had not left her behind, either.

November 8 · the same night, elsewhere A man who cannot change his clothes Seoul · the National Theater of Korea · the backstage rest room One official photo call still owed, five group chats blinking, and two unread messages that matter.

On the other side.

In the backstage rest room of the National Theater, Baek Si-on could not change into a suit, and could not yet take off the crown prince’s court robes either.

Because the official photographers were still owed one more set of group photos.

Baek Eun-ah had a tablet in her arms and was running five conversations at once — SK, Hyundai, the organizing committee, the studio, and the PR group.

Baek Si-on sat in a chair, looking down at his phone.

He had left Choi Jin-ri’s chat window, and just below it sat one more unread.

Lee Ji-eun.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Lee Ji-eun

Congratulations.

Baek Si-on

Thank you.

Very short.

Not like her usual style, which was to seize any opportunity to snipe at him twice.

On her side of the screen, the typing indicator came up.

A few seconds later, the message arrived.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun · the same chat, continued

Lee Ji-eun

Your speech today had real class.

Baek Si-on

Thank you.

Lee Ji-eun

Especially the Tom Ford Gangster line.

Baek Si-on

Don’t.

Lee Ji-eun looked at those two words and finally laughed out loud.

So he could feel shame too.

Good.

Very good.

She kept typing.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun · the same chat, continued

Lee Ji-eun

I drank the saffron water.

Baek Si-on

How was it?

Lee Ji-eun

It was awful.

Baek Si-on

Then don’t drink it.

Lee Ji-eun read that line, and her fingertips stopped.

Perfectly ordinary words.

And somehow, looking at them, she felt comfortable.

Not “hold your nose, it’s for your health.” Not “keep it down, it’s good for you.” Just: if it’s awful, don’t drink it.

She lowered the phone a little and looked at her own blurred reflection in the window.

Today she had only meant to send one line of congratulations.

Somewhere along the way, one flat sentence of his had gone and made something inside her go soft.

She let out a slow breath.

Typed.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun · the same chat, continued

Lee Ji-eun

Then you rest early tonight too.

Baek Si-on

Mm.

Lee Ji-eun did not hold that “Mm” against him.

Because she knew: at this level of busy, he had still answered her.

That was enough.

November 9 · morning A city that woke before the trending list went to sleep Seoul, nationwide The internet has been in a very stable state of madness since roughly the moment a blue gonryongpo walked into a theater.

November 9.

Seoul woke up, and the trending list still hadn’t.

More precisely: from the moment Baek Si-on had walked into the National Theater in a blue gonryongpo the night before, the Korean internet had settled into a very stable state of madness.

Naver’s real-time search board had barely moved all night.

Ten slots. One crown, one helicopter, one convoy, one apology, one photograph, one effect. Nothing had moved since midnight — the country had slept in shifts.

At eight in the morning, people walking past the KB bank branch noticed it at once: the electronic screen over the entrance had changed.

KB Kookmin Bank · branch entrance LED · 08:00

Loan rates. Credit-card promotions. Fund recommendations. All gone.

In their place: the gonryongpo, the helicopter, the money shot of the year.

KB Kookmin Bank congratulates its brand ambassador, Actor Baek Si-on, on receiving the Silver Crown Cultural Medal

The line crawls across the bottom. It does not stop crawling.

A man who had come in to reissue his passbook stood at the entrance staring for a good long while.

His expression was complicated.

An auntie beside him studied it earnestly for a while too.

“That’s the kid, isn’t it — the what’s-his-name, the Best Actor one?”

“Mm.”

“He has such an honest face.”

Inside, at the counter, a clerk was helping a customer with their business. The customer signed with one hand and stared at the lobby screen with the other half of his attention.

“Your bank’s ambassador is really something now?”

The clerk smiled with tremendous professionalism.

“Yes.”

She said it with her back a little straighter than usual.

After all, when KB had signed Baek Si-on, the advertising position had also been “professional, dependable” — the safest possible logic for a financial brand.

Now look.

Professional and dependable, still in stock.

Plus one Silver Crown Cultural Medal, complimentary.

The customer picked up the pen and said, with feeling:

“Then your eyes were good.”

The clerk smiled.

Thought: thank you.

Never mind that a few days ago, someone internally had nearly tried to terminate the contract.

But that had been an intern’s affair.

Interns are forever young.

Forever useful.

Forever leaving a firm silhouette in the public-relations history of Korean big business.

At the same time, the KB mobile app had changed its splash screen too.

KB StarBanking · splash screen

You open the app. Not your balance. Not transfers.

Honour comes from trust. Trust connects the future.

And first, before any of it: Baek Si-on.

An office worker, freshly awake and hoping to check whether his salary had landed, stared at his phone for three seconds.

“I just wanted to see whether the company had paid me.”

He had not seen money.

He had seen the Crown Prince.

He closed the splash and went into the balance page.

KB StarBanking · balance

Balance: ₩38,420

He was silent for a moment.

Then he tapped back to the home page and looked at Baek Si-on one more time.

“Same country. Both of us Korean. How is the gap this wide?”
