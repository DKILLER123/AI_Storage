Chapter Fifty-Three

The Measuring Tape Reading Less Than Ten Centimeters Away

July 26 2:50 p.m. Nonhyeon-dong, downstairs from Kim Jae-kyung's dorm Three people, two dresses, and one very long tape

Nonhyeon-dong, in front of Kim Jae-kyung's dormitory block.

2:50 in the afternoon.

By the time Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah arrived, Choi Jin-ri was already there — leaning against the shade of a tree next to the entrance, scrolling her phone.

A black baseball cap. A black mask. Amber-tinted sunglasses.

The standard-issue "please don't recognise me" kit.

But the hair coming out from under the cap was different from before. Last time it had been greasy and limp. Today it was washed — and curled.

She wore a white square-neck top, the fabric reading as a cotton-linen blend, and a pair of pale high-waisted wide-leg trousers. At her ankle, a thin silver chain.

Eun-ah closed the distance quickly and took Choi Jin-ri's arm in both hands, pulling her off the wall.

“Eonni! So pretty!”

Choi Jin-ri yanked, nearly dropped her phone, and stuffed it back in her pocket.

“No, I'm not……”

The denial was automatic. But the hand that was free tucked a strand of hair behind her ear, exposing a silver stud, and the gaze behind the sunglasses passed over Eun-ah's shoulder to look at what was behind her.

Baek Si-on was walking over slowly, carrying a watermelon.

The last time they'd met, she'd thrown his own words back at him — if there's ever anything, you can message me. But since then, not a single new message in their KakaoTalk thread.

Now they were meeting again, and she wasn't sure what the opening was supposed to be.

“Aren't you going to greet me?”

Baek Si-on's opener left her blinking behind the sunglasses.

“Good…… good afternoon?”

“The honorific?”

“H…… honorific?”

“Call me sunbae.”

“……”

Choi Jin-ri blinked twice behind the lenses.

Sunbae.

By group debut year, f(x) did indeed come after A'ST1.

But! She — Choi Jin-ri — had debuted in 2005, as a child actress, in the SBS historical drama The King and I.

2005.

What was Baek Si-on doing back then?

Probably sitting in some middle-school classroom doing his homework.

By debut year, by industry seniority, she was the sunbae.

A sunbae calling a hoobae "sunbae"?

That equation never came out right, no matter how you solved it.

But when she looked at the man in front of her — carrying a watermelon, expression so matter-of-fact that there wasn't an ounce of room for negotiation in it —

Fine.

“……Sunbae, hello.”

The voice leaked out muffled from beneath the mask, the end of the sentence dragging with a distinct, reluctant undertone.

Baek Si-on nodded. The expression was satisfied.

Watermelon in hand, he turned and went up the stairs.

✿ ✿ ✿

July 26 Afternoon Nonhyeon-dong, Kim Jae-kyung's dorm, 4th floor The four hundred-and-second door, and a sewing machine

Fourth floor, room 402.

Kim Jae-kyung opened the door.

High ponytail, bare face, a grey cotton lounge set, Hello Kitty slippers.

“Sunbae.”

She gave Baek Si-on a small nod, then her gaze moved past him to the two behind him.

“Let me introduce you.”

Baek Si-on stepped half a pace aside to clear the line of sight:

“My cousin, Baek Eun-ah. And Choi——”

Kim Jae-kyung's eyes lit up all at once.

“I know, I know, you don't have to.”

She pulled the door wide and stood out of the way.

“Come in, come in.”

The three of them changed into slippers and went in.

The moment Eun-ah was through the door, the first thing she did was hoist up the tan leather bag she'd been carrying and wave it at Kim Jae-kyung.

“Jae-kyung eonni! You made this, right!”

Kim Jae-kyung took one look at the bag and recognised it.

“Ah… that. I made that pretty roughly——”

“Roughly about where!”

Eun-ah flipped the bag over, pointing at the stitching along the side.

“Look at this edge. A machine couldn't even sew a line this straight. I took it to the department store and the saleslady thought it was bespoke Italian.”

Kim Jae-kyung was getting a little flustered by the praise, so she pivoted.

“Sulli sunbae-nim, you're so much prettier in person than on television……”

Choi Jin-ri had just taken off the sunglasses and mask, and was holding her cap in her hands, trying to figure out where to put it.

“Not at all.”

She smiled, and her voice had loosened considerably from what it had been downstairs:

“Just call me Jin-ri.”

“No, no, Jin-ri sunbae-nim——”

“Call me Jin-ri.”

“Jin-ri!”

Kim Jae-kyung accepted it, fast.

In under two minutes, the three women had completed the entire social process, from first meeting to honorific upgrade, with a speed that was genuinely startling.

Baek Si-on had sat down on the sofa, opened the watermelon box he'd carried up, and forked a slice into his mouth.

Eating watermelon.

In the most literal sense.

Kim Jae-kyung was pulling Eun-ah over to look at a half-finished piece on the sewing machine, and Choi Jin-ri drifted over too. Three heads squeezed together, chattering like a nest of birds that had just gone off.

Kim Jae-kyung talked fabric, patterns, the three-dimensional cutting she'd been researching lately. Eun-ah was all "whoa," "amazing," "how did you do that."

Choi Jin-ri was quieter, but now and then she'd reach out and touch the texture of the material.

Baek Si-on forked a third slice of watermelon.

He'd noticed something.

Once three women got together, he automatically became part of the furniture.

He didn't mind.

Chewing watermelon in silence was fine.

He was mid-chew when Eun-ah's phone suddenly went off from her pocket. She pulled it out, glanced at the caller ID, answered, and listened for a couple of seconds.

“Who?!”

The voice shot up half an octave.

All three of them turned at once.

Eun-ah quickly covered the receiver, waved a hand at them, mouthed a silent nothing, nothing, and hurried to the door, slipping out.

Kim Jae-kyung turned her gaze back, picked up the measuring tape off the table, looped it once in her hand, and looked at Baek Si-on, who was eating watermelon.

“Let's measure. Sunbae first?”

Baek Si-on set the watermelon box on the coffee table and stood.

He walked to the open space in the middle of the room and held his arms out naturally.

Kim Jae-kyung came over with the tape.

“Shoulders first.”

She stood behind him, laid the tape across the bone at his left shoulder, pulled it to the right, read the number, and murmured it to herself as she noted it down.

“Arm length.”

From the shoulder to the wrist. Another measurement recorded.

“Now the chest.”

Kim Jae-kyung came around to his front, both hands holding each end of the tape, about to pass it around behind his back.

This action required both her arms to thread past his sides, meeting behind him to close the tape.

Objectively, it was an embrace.

Her hands had just reached the height of his chest.

The Fitting in progress

1 · The tape Kim Jae-kyung's arms reach past his sides, about to wrap around behind him and close the measurement.
2 · The moment Two hands at his chest height, almost an embrace with a tape measure as the only thing in between.

The room goes quiet. Nobody breathes.

“I'll do it.”

The voice came from beside them.

Both of them turned.

Choi Jin-ri stood two steps away. The person who'd been sitting quietly in the chair, watching, had stood up and walked over at some point without anyone noticing.

“For the measuring, I can help. You take the numbers, I'll run the tape. That's easier.”

Kim Jae-kyung looked at the calm, flat expression on Choi Jin-ri's face, then at Baek Si-on's arms, still raised in the air.

“Sure.”

She handed over the tape and turned toward the workbench to get the notebook and pencil.

“Then I'll write it down.”

Choi Jin-ri took the tape, walked over to him, tilted her head up just slightly to look at him for a moment.

Then she laid one end of the tape over his left shoulder blade, and threaded her arm around from his right side, closing the tape across his chest.

The trajectory was identical to an embrace.

Except there was a tape measure between them.

Choi Jin-ri's fingers pinched the point where the tape crossed at his chest, and she looked down to read:

“One hundred and five.”

Baek Si-on looked down at the crown of her head.

“Noted.”

Kim Jae-kyung wrote it down on the other side.

“Waist.”

Choi Jin-ri drew the tape out from behind him, crouched down, and ran it around his waistline.

Kim Jae-kyung sat at the table recording the numbers, stole a glance at Baek Si-on's expression, then quickly dropped her eyes back to the notebook.

The corners of her mouth were pressed tight.

Pressed very, very tight.
