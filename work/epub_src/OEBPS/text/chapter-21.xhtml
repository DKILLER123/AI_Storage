Chapter Twenty-One

The Two Flavors of Chicken Breast

June 12:15 The café near SM Choi Jin-ri in the doorway

Twelve fifteen.

When Choi Jin-ri appeared in the doorway of the café, Baek Eun-ah was slouched over the table scrolling her phone, while Baek Si-on stared out the window still chewing over the melody.

“Eonni!”

Eun-ah spotted her first and shot to her feet, waving.

Jin-ri crossed toward them. She'd changed. The sweat-soaked T-shirt from that morning was gone, replaced by a pale-grey short sleeve with a ring of white trim at the collar, and a pair of dark denim. Her hair was down, bangs pinned to one side to bare her forehead. Her face —

Eun-ah leaned in to squint.

“Eonni, you did your makeup!”

“I just… threw something together.”

Jin-ri's voice shrunk as it trailed off, eyes drifting sideways.

Eun-ah laughed and looped her arm through Jin-ri's, pulling her toward the table.

“Eonni, you look so pretty like this.”

At the table, Eun-ah hollered across at Si-on.

“Cousin, Eonni Jin-ri's here!”

Si-on came back to the present, turned, saw her, and rose from the chair.

Jin-ri nodded at him, lowering her voice.

“Sorry to keep you both waiting.”

Si-on was about to say “it's fine” when Eun-ah beat him to it.

“A meal with Eonni is worth waiting for, no matter how long! Right, cousin?”

She wheeled around and gave Si-on a wink.

A very emphatic wink.

Si-on looked at her.

“…Mm.”

Jin-ri stood a step away, and, very quietly, the corner of her mouth curved.

June The stairwell Still circling that song

The three of them left the café and headed for the stairwell. Eun-ah walked ahead with Jin-ri hooked on her arm, chattering. Si-on trailed behind, hands in his pockets, unhurried.

Still circling that song.

Better to stay clear of anything from the past three years, he decided. But which of the songs still to come would suit him?

His gaze drifted up at Eun-ah's laugh drifting back down the landing. The two of them had already rounded the corner onto the second floor.

The moment the canteen doors swung open, the din of voices washed over them. Long tables, round tables, anywhere a person could sit was occupied. Someone balanced a tray while hunting for a seat; someone queued at the window; someone ate one-handed while scrolling; a group of three hunched together, trading bites and gossip.

The three wove through the crowd to the serving window. Behind the glass sat a row of stainless steel warming troughs — rice, soup, three or four hot dishes, kimchi, salad.

Jin-ri stood at the counter and glanced back.

“Without an employee card it's awkward, so let me cover this.”

Si-on didn't argue. He picked up a tray and started ordering.

“Pork cutlet, two servings.”

“Japchae, one.”

“Spicy stir-fried pork, one.”

“Kimchi stew, one bowl.”

“Pan-fried mackerel, two.”

“Rice, two bowls— no, three.”

The lady's ladle flew back and forth between the trough and the tray, and the longer it flew, the more complicated her expression got.

Behind him, Eun-ah buried her face in her hands.

Jin-ri stood at his shoulder, watching the little mountain stack up on the tray. Her mouth twitched, but she said nothing.

Si-on finished and turned, tray in hand. Eun-ah and Jin-ri followed, each carrying their own — Eun-ah a bowl of cold naengmyeon, the red broth bright with shaved ice, two boiled egg halves and a few slices of pear floating on top; Jin-ri a salad of greens, cherry tomatoes, boiled chicken breast, and a pale, unseasoned block of tofu at the edge.

The three found a four-seater against the wall. Eun-ah sat beside Si-on, Jin-ri across.

Si-on set his tray down flat, picked up his chopsticks, and dove in. First bite, pork cutlet — crisp outside, tender inside, the sauce sweet-and-sour. He narrowed his eyes, and took another.

“Cousin, this kimchi's been pickled just right, try it.”

Eun-ah offered him kimchi from her naengmyeon bowl. As her chopsticks came back, they swerved mid-air, grazed the tray, and lifted a whole piece of pork cutlet straight into her mouth.

“Delicious~”

She chewed, eyes drifting back to the tray.

Si-on's chopsticks paused.

Then he dragged the tray a little closer to his own side.

Eun-ah: “…”.

Si-on didn't look at her. He attacked the rice.

Jin-ri watched the two of them from across the table and thought of that night. The signless little shop in Yeonnam-dong — Si-on had sat the same way, opposite her, eating with total focus, eating with something almost healing in it.

Watching him made her want to eat, too.

Jin-ri looked down at her own salad, forked up a cube of chicken breast, and chewed.

Dry.

Bland.

But, for some reason, not so hard to swallow after all.

Two flavors of chicken breast — hers: dry, bland. His: the flavor of a man who's decided tonight isn't the night he sleeps.

The lady at the window has seen a lot of trays in her life. This one is the reason she'll tell her daughter about it.

June SM employee canteen Two flavours of chicken breast

The three of them were mid-meal.

“Sulli?”

Someone called her name. Jin-ri turned. It was Girls' Generation's Kim Hyo-yeon, tray in hand, standing at the side of the table and beaming at her.

Kim Hyo-yeon

Singer · Dancer · Girls' Generation

Born Kim Hyo-yeon · on stage, Hyoyeon

Girls' Generation's main dancer and one of SM's older seniors — cheerful, relentless, and the one who would drag Jin-ri out toward the industry's nightlife.

“Eonni.”

Jin-ri set down her fork, stood, and gave a small bow.

“Sit, sit, you're eating.”

Hyo-yeon pulled out the empty chair beside Jin-ri as if it were the most natural thing in the world, and, in a throwaway tone, complained.

“I messaged you on Kakao yesterday — why didn't you reply?”

Jin-ri thought for a moment. She had, in fact, received a message. But she'd just come off a dance practice, so worn out she could barely keep her eyes open. She'd thought “I'll reply in a bit” — and then fallen asleep.

“Sorry, Eonni. I was so tired yesterday I passed out the moment I got back to the dorm.”

“No worries, I figured.”

Hyo-yeon waved it off, leaning in.

“Anyway, there's a party tonight, some friends from the industry put it together, pretty lively. Come with me.”

Party.

The word ran through Jin-ri's head. A nightclub, the music, a crush of bodies, someone drinking, someone dancing, someone holding a phone up filming all of it.

She didn't want to go.

What she feared most these days was a crowd — let alone a neon-lit club thick with alcohol and the gaze of strangers.

But the words lodged in her throat. This was a sunbae. A Girls' Generation sunbae. Debuting earlier, standing higher, someone she bowed to and greeted politely whenever they passed in the company. Could you refuse a sunbae's invitation?

“I—”

Jin-ri's gaze flicked across to the opposite seat.

Si-on sat there, head down, chopsticks lifting a piece of pork cutlet to his mouth.

He didn't look up.

He didn't look at her.

As if he'd heard none of this at all.

She pulled her eyes back, wrestled with herself for a few seconds, and spoke.

“Eonni, I've been so tired lately, maybe—”

“Ah, that's exactly why you need to unwind!”

Hyo-yeon steamrolled straight through the refusal, cheerfully relentless.

“Have a drink, meet some new people, you'll feel better. It's settled, then. I'll pick you up tonight.”

Jin-ri went quiet. The sliver of courage she'd scraped together shattered under the sunbae's matter-of-fact certainty.

“She said she doesn't want to go.”

Just as Hyo-yeon was about to stand and leave, a voice cut across the table. Jarring. Sudden.

Hyo-yeon turned, looking at the buzzed-head man in the flower-print shirt on the far side, and pointed at her own nose, utterly baffled.

“You talking to me?”

Si-on nodded.

Hyo-yeon's temper flared in a heartbeat.

“Who are you?”

“Who are you?”

Si-on tipped his chopsticks in her direction.

“Dragging an idol who's about to make a comeback with a new concept album — who's been killing herself in practice until she's about to collapse — out to a club party in the middle of the night.”

Hyo-yeon's brows knit, mouth opening to fire back. Si-on didn't give her the opening.

“You don't care whether she leaks the new album's styling concept. You don't care whether she still has the energy to rehearse with her members tomorrow.”

“What is it you want?”

“Is SM your company?”

For a beat, the canteen went silent.

The neighbouring tables glanced over on the sly.

Eun-ah's chopsticks froze mid-air, half a mouthful of naengmyeon in her cheeks, eyes gone wide and round, staring at Si-on. Her cousin. The cousin who'd just finished his enlistment, just wrapped his film, just dragged her out to buy lottery tickets that won her more than two hundred million won.

And here he was, chopsticks pointed at a Girls' Generation sunbae, laying into her line by line.

“You—”

Hyo-yeon's face cycled through every shade of red. No stranger had ever pointed a finger at her nose and bawled her out.

She glanced around. Some people were watching. Some were pointedly not watching.

Then she swung back, glared hard at Si-on, and, before she left, hurled a parting shot over her shoulder at Jin-ri.

“Sulli. These are the kinds of friends you keep?”

With that, she hoisted her tray and stalked off without a backward glance, feet pounding hard.

While Si-on watched Hyo-yeon's retreating back, Eun-ah's chopsticks made a casual, border-crossing expedition toward the largest pork cutlet left on his tray. The instant the tips were halfway across — Si-on, without even looking, lifted the entire stainless steel tray above his head.

Eun-ah's chopsticks closed on emptiness.

Watching Hyo-yeon storm off, Jin-ri turned back, looked at the two cousins still locked in a silent war over a cutlet, and spoke, a little uneasy.

“Um—”

“It's fine.”

Si-on, assuming she meant to thank him, set the tray back down and cut in.

“…What I mean is, Eonni Hyo-yeon isn't actually a bad person.”

Jin-ri's fingers twisted together, her voice shrinking.

“She's just a bit careless, that's all. She usually looks after me — she probably didn't think it through…”

Si-on's chopsticks paused for two full seconds.

He didn't take it up. He just started eating again, twice as fast.

Jin-ri watched, fingers twisting tighter. She was used to pleasing others, but that didn't mean she couldn't read a room. The easy, loose energy that usually hung around Si-on had gone, visibly, cold.

Not three minutes later, Si-on scraped the last of the rice from the tray and swallowed.

“Thanks for the meal.”

He drew a tissue, wiped his mouth, stood, and lifted the clean-scraped tray. He looked down at Jin-ri.

“I've got things this afternoon. I'll be off.”

He turned and walked toward the collection point. Eun-ah, a strand of naengmyeon dangling from her lips, glanced from a frozen Jin-ri to the broad back already several metres off.

“Eonni—”

Eun-ah bit the noodle clean, fumbled for her canvas bag.

“…Let's do it again soon!”

She grabbed her half-finished tray and trotted after him.

“Cousin! Cousin, wait for me—”

June SM employee canteen The crowd, and the talk

In moments the two of them were swallowed by the canteen's busy crowd. Only Jin-ri remained at the wall-side table, alone.

The noise around her was still loud — trainees discussing the afternoon's evaluations, staff grumbling about a brutal schedule. But to her, all of it suddenly sounded very far away.

She looked down at the pale diet salad on her tray. Fingers stiff, she picked up the fork and stabbed another cube of boiled chicken breast, brought it to her mouth, and chewed slowly.

Still dry.

Still bland.

But this time, as the raw, fibrous grain spread across her tongue, she finally tasted it.
