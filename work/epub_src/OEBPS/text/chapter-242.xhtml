Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Two

Three Gazes at the Premiere

Late July · the day of arrival

One international arrival · one rerouted itinerary

Incheon International Airport · arrivals hall

Incheon International Airport. The arrivals-hall doors slid open and the noise of people and airport announcements poured in together.

Alan Walker came through with a computer bag on his back and a suitcase in tow. The exit area was packed — waiting families, drivers holding name signs, fans with slogan towels, all of them jammed against the railings. He swept the crowd once, and a white pickup sign entered his field of view fast: his own name printed on it in English. The girl holding it was young — white shirt, dark trousers, hair tied back in a clean, sharp bunch.

Alan wheeled the trolley over.

“Hello.”

Baek Eun-ah lowered the sign and confirmed his name and flight in English first. Identity: correct. She put out her hand.

“Welcome to Korea, Mr. Walker. Tough journey, I imagine.”

Her pronunciation had come a long way in a year. Alan answered, a bit stiff:

“Alan is fine, please.”

“All right — Alan.”

She waved the staff over for the luggage. Alan let go of the trolley handle, and his eyes went over her shoulder, sweeping the pickup crowd in a full circle. The face that recurred across news clips, stages, and Worlds VODs was nowhere in it. Eun-ah read him perfectly.

“Mr. Baek Si-on is at the ‘Sado’ production press conference and media interviews today — he can’t get here yet. He asked me to apologize for him. Tonight, once his work is done, he’ll come to the studio to meet you in person.”

Alan nodded.

“That’s fine — work comes first.”

Understanding was understanding, but the disappointment in his voice was not hidden tightly. He had crossed half the planet, and one of the things he most wanted to verify was that the man behind that invitation email truly existed.

“But before the hotel, we’ve arranged one other stop.”

“What?”

“Faker.”

The fatigue was sliced in half, as if by the airport’s automatic doors.

“Now?” Alan asked.

“If you’d rather rest at the hotel first, we can move it.”

“No need.”

The answer came far too fast for a man fresh off a transcontinental flight.

❧ ❧ ❧

The van pulled out of Incheon, and the scenery outside began to move. Before coming to Korea, Alan had crammed an entire viewing of ‘My Love from the Star’. In his imagination Seoul was all glittering high-rise apartments, uniformly sleek shopping streets, the Han River lit up on both banks, and impeccably dressed male and female leads — ideally with an alien or two descending from a rooftop. What actually entered the window first: vacant lots. Then warehouses. Highway sound walls. Whole stretches of low gray buildings. And interchange ramps going who-knew-where.

Alan stared out the window as his expression slid from anticipation to confusion. Eun-ah noticed.

“What’s wrong?”

“It’s not like the drama.”

“Which drama?”

“‘My Love from the Star’.”

Eun-ah: “......”

She gave genuine thought to how to explain the relationship between drama location scouts and an airport expressway to this foreign guest.

“That drama didn’t film this stretch of road.”

She added:

“Also, we don’t usually station aliens next to the highway.”

Alan: “......”

The car drove on toward Seoul. Only when the towers climbed up over the skyline, the Han flashed silver, and the district ad-boards began lining the road did Alan recover a modest fraction of his K-drama filter.

❧ ❧ ❧

The same afternoon · before the studio

One club visit · one signed jersey · one rank kept private

The SKT T1 clubhouse

The SKT T1 clubhouse. Official photos from past tournaments hung along the corridor walls; trophies stood in glass cases, metal bases engraved with years and event names; signed peripherals sat on display stands; and from the direction of the training room came the dense clatter of keyboards.

Alan followed Eun-ah inside. A young man in a team jacket was coming out of the training room. SKT at this moment ran the two-mid rotation of Faker and Easyhoon — two contrasting styles giving the coaching staff more tactical options, and the team’s results hummed along accordingly. Which is why Lee Sang-hyeok stepping out of the training room to meet Baek Si-on’s foreign guest disrupted nobody’s preparation rhythm.

Alan had prepared a number of opening lines. In front of the actual person, exactly one survived:

“Hello.”

Lee Sang-hyeok shook his hand politely.

“Welcome to Korea.”

“Thank you. I watched last year’s Worlds live — I was sorry not to see you in the finals.”

Eun-ah translated it across. Lee Sang-hyeok’s expression barely moved.

“Then this year I’ll work my way back.”

Eun-ah finished translating, and Alan nodded, slowly. That sentence and the song he had flown here to work on were, as it happened, about the same thing: legends fall too — what matters is standing back up.

Lee Sang-hyeok asked:

“What role do you play?”

“Mid lane.”

“What rank?”

“......”

Alan went quiet. Lee Sang-hyeok didn’t press — which preserved the last shred of this international guest’s European-server dignity.

The base tour circled once, and at the door Lee Sang-hyeok handed over a team jersey signed by the whole SKT roster. Alan received it with both hands. The two of them took a photo in front of the club’s logo wall; after the shutter, Eun-ah signaled that time was up. Alan shook Lee Sang-hyeok’s hand goodbye — and outside the clubhouse doors he looked back once toward the training room. The keyboards had already started up again.

❧ ❧ ❧

Hotel. Luggage down. Shower, change. Eun-ah took him to a Korean BBQ place nearby — his first lettuce-wrap, the whole parcel crammed into his mouth at once, and his face went through a small war of expressions. She asked whether he wanted the chili paste. He tried one dip. Then silently pushed the paste to the far end of the table.

❧ ❧ ❧

Evening · into the night

Three chairs · one remix · one midnight delivery run

Jung Jae-joon’s studio

Evening. Jung Jae-joon’s studio. The door pushed open — Baek Si-on was already in the control room, still in his ‘Sado’ press-conference suit. Alan stopped in the doorway. Baek Si-on rose.

“Met Faker?”

Alan nodded and took his hand.

“I did. Thank you.”

“Then it’s time to work.”

Baek Si-on turned to the other chair, where a man freshly back from Japan was dialing in equipment.

“Jung Jae-joon — my producer.”

Jung Jae-joon pulled one earcup off and put out a fist. Alan hesitated a beat, then carefully bumped it.

“You’ve had a day. Sit.”

Alan took the third chair. Three people, one row of screens. The project loaded and the stems fanned out across the display. What followed involved no pleasantries and no compromises — only pure musical collision.

The window went fully dark. Two men and one boy had spent hours sanding the electronic reconstruction of ‘Legends Never Die’ when the studio’s outer door abruptly swung open — corridor air rushed in, trailing the rustle of convenience-store plastic bags. Choi Jin-ri — cap and mask on, several large bags of late-night food and drinks in hand — stood at the door with half her head poked in.

“Am I interrupting?”

Four heads turned at once. Jung Jae-joon noticed the bags first. After a run past midnight, food mobilized a producer’s enthusiasm far more efficiently than the midnight ambush of Baek Si-on’s girlfriend.

“If you’re carrying food — no.”

Jin-ri lifted the bags.

“Then I’m not interrupting.”

She came in, shouldered the door shut, and set the food on the lounge table. Baek Si-on pulled off his headphones and stood.

“What brings you here?”

“Somebody wrapped his film and stopped coming home.”

She and Eun-ah unpacked the bags together, item by item — the voice soft as ever, the resentment in it not soft at all.

“I came to confirm whether he still intends to keep this girlfriend.”

“For now, yes.”

Her hands stopped; she looked up at him.

“For now?”

“Renewal depends on the quality of the late-night delivery.”

“You’re renewing based on snack quality?”

She swatted him. Baek Si-on caught her hand mid-swat and drew her toward the console.

“Sit.”

She had come only to deliver food, she meant to say — but her body sat, quite honestly, into the chair still warm from him. Baek Si-on turned to Alan and introduced in English:

“Sulli — Choi Jin-ri. Actress.”

Then to her:

“Alan Walker. DJ.”

She gave Alan a small bow.

“Hello.”

Eun-ah translated, and supplied the footnote:

“He’s the DJ my cousin found in Norway — he’s doing the remix version of ‘Legends Never Die’.”

Jin-ri said to Alan:

“Thank you for coming all this way to Korea.”

Eun-ah carried it over; Alan waved both hands — the honor was all his, being on a world-championship theme. The mutual courtesy parade ended itself. The fried chicken on the table would not sacrifice its crust to international etiquette much longer.

Jin-ri’s attention returned to Baek Si-on. His buzz cut had grown out a fair amount, much softer than in his first days on ‘The Three Evils’. She ran her hand front to back through it, then back against the grain with her palm.

“Finally doesn’t look fresh out of prison.”

She ruffled it once more and delivered a serious review:

“Though the texture has gotten worse.”

Baek Si-on let her complete the full quality inspection on top of his head while he concentrated on conquering the late-night spread. Alan understood none of the words. He understood everything else. A woman allowed to ruffle a world superstar’s head at will — that answer required no translation.

The food got divided fast: fried chicken, gimbap, fish-cake soup, and tteokbokki covering the table. Eun-ah walked Alan through the names and the heat levels of Korean late-night food, with a special warning that the tteokbokki sauce was far more dangerous than it looked. Alan, remembering the chili paste’s delayed fuse from lunch, prudently selected the safest-looking gimbap.

Food demolished, work resumed. Jin-ri sat beside them at first, listening with real appetite — the sub-bass of the Drop rolling out of the monitors, mixed with the bursts of shop-talk between Baek Si-on and Jung Jae-joon. But she had run a full schedule that day and then kept watch until midnight for this visit; her battery was done. She moved to the lounge sofa. The suit jacket Baek Si-on had taken off lay over the armrest; she pulled it over naturally and hugged it in her arms. The looped playback and the low discussion turned gradually into white noise. Her head nodded lower and lower — and at some point, she was asleep.

❧ ❧ ❧

The next thing she sensed arrived blurred. The music had stopped. Someone was clearing the table. Some of the lamps had gone dark. Then her body abruptly lost the sofa. Jin-ri’s eyes opened, groggy. The ceiling was moving. No — the ceiling wasn’t moving. She was. She was suspended in Baek Si-on’s arms. The suit jacket still hugged in her arms, one sleeve trailing, swaying gently with his stride.

It took her a while to manage, quietly:

“Finished?”

“Mm.”

She tipped her face and, over his shoulder, saw Alan and Jung Jae-joon still at the console, saving the project.

“Did I interrupt your work?”

Baek Si-on carried her toward the door.

“You’re entitled to.”

The corner of her mouth curled in the dimness; both arms went around his neck and her face buried into the side of it.

“Then I’ll come again next time.”

“Mm.”

Two steps from the door, he stopped. Turned back.

“Alan.”

Alan looked up from the monitor. Baek Si-on asked in English:

“Are you free on the evening of the third?”

Alan glanced first at Eun-ah, confirming this was not a work question.

“Nothing scheduled.”

“My film ‘Sado’ holds its VIP premiere. If you’d like, come see it.”

Alan was visibly surprised. He knew Baek Si-on had been promoting the film everywhere; he had no idea what it was actually about.

“I don’t speak Korean.”

“The distributor has an English-subtitled print.”

Baek Si-on added:

“But before the premiere — don’t go looking up this piece of history.”

Alan was even more puzzled.

“Why?”

“I want to know how much of it a European viewer with zero knowledge of the history can understand. Afterward, tell me what you understood.”

A pause.

“And which parts you didn’t understand at all.”

Alan considered, then nodded:

“I’ll come.”

Invitation, settled. Baek Si-on turned back toward the door, Jin-ri in his arms. Jung Jae-joon called after them:

“Come listen again in the afternoon — your ears are no good right now.”

“I know.”

Eun-ah said she’d take Alan back to the hotel. The door shut. Three people left in the studio. Alan looked at Eun-ah:

“Does he often carry his girlfriend out of the studio like that?”

Eun-ah, expressionless:

“This is my first time seeing it.”

Jung Jae-joon added from the console:

“Mine too.”

Alan nodded. So he had arrived in Korea on day one and witnessed a limited-edition scene.

❧ ❧ ❧

The elevator sank. Jin-ri was awake by now, arms still looped around his neck with no intention of coming down. She tipped her face up.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“Am I heavy?”

He weighed the person in his arms.

“Manageable.”

She found the vague answer offensive.

“What does ‘manageable’ mean?”

“I can make it to the parking lot.”

“Hmph.”

The elevator doors opened. He carried her to the van, opened the rear door, set her into the seat, leaned in to pull the seatbelt across. The buckle clicked home — and she caught him by the collar and pulled the just-straightening man back down. When the kiss broke, her breathing sat slightly off, her forehead still resting against his.

“Inspection finished?” Baek Si-on lifted a thumb and wiped away the smudge her lips had left on his.

“Inspection of what?”

“Whether the girlfriend stays.”

Her eyes curved.

“Confirmed.”

The accumulated resentment of the day had now received very thorough after-sales service. He was closing the door when she suddenly remembered something.

“The premiere is on the third, right?”

“Mm.”

“You just invited the Norwegian.”

“Mm.”

She stared at him.

“And me?”

Baek Si-on leaned back down to her.

“Are you coming?”

“Is that an invitation?”

“Otherwise why would I ask?”

The corner of her mouth was already climbing, and still she pressed:

“You’re not asking just because you happened to overhear me?”

“No.”

“Then why?”

Baek Si-on took no detour.

“Because I want you there.”

The mouth corner surrendered entirely, and still she chased one more line:

“As what, exactly?”

“On the red carpet — actress Choi Jin-ri. After the credits — my girlfriend.”

Thoroughly charmed, she finally stopped cross-examining.

“Then I’ll have to dress beautifully.”

He straightened up.

“When have you ever not?”

She was, from there, completely happy. The van door closed. In the driver’s seat, the professional started the engine as if the rear seat had transmitted nothing at all. The headlights came on and lit the way to the parking-garage exit.

August 3 · seven in the evening

One poster wall · one guest list · three names too close together

Samseong-dong · COEX · the Megabox premiere of ‘Sado’

August the third, seven in the evening. Samseong-dong, COEX, the Megabox. The elevator opened directly onto a colossal poster: Baek Si-on’s Crown Prince Sado with a long blade in hand, standing outside the palace gate, the blade’s aim ending at a single candle-lit window. A viewer who didn’t know the history could read an entirely new story out of it — the prince will rebel; the king will die. As for that rice chest every Korean already knew about, the distributor had buried it without a trace; not one plank of it would be shown early.

The corridor, as dressed

Character standees lined both walls of the walkway to the theaters — Song Kang-ho, Baek Si-on, Moon Geun-young, Jeon Hye-jin, in costume and in order.

At the corridor’s end: the lobby. The press pit roped off, a step-and-repeat plastered edge to edge with distributor, sponsor, and partner logos; dozens of photo-journalists wedged inside, tripods and long lenses as far as the eye reached.

Right of the board: the guest check-in desk — staff with name lists and seat maps, verifying every invited artist’s name, agency, and entourage count. The venue was not sealed; fans and ordinary moviegoers pressed behind the metal barriers outside, phones and posters and autograph books held high over the crowd.

“Si-on-oppa!”

“Over here!”

“‘Sado’ is going to be huge!”

“......”

Whenever the creative team passed, the chanting drowned the lobby’s background music whole.

Baek Eun-ah stood by the check-in desk running the run-of-show with the distributor’s floor manager, a VIP guest list in hand sorted by expected arrival. The front of it was all normal: directors and actors who had worked with Lee Joon-ik; Song Kang-ho’s industry friends; artists invited by Moon Geun-young and Jeon Hye-jin; Showbox’s key partners; critics, cultural figures, overseas sales reps. Her finger slid down the page.

It stopped.

Three names, not far apart.

“Choi Jin-ri.”

“Lee Ji-eun.”

“Red Velvet — all five.”

The list, as dreaded

Eun-ah stared at that page for a long time. One thought remained: it’s over.

Tonight’s film was ‘Sado’. But the person who truly needed to be locked in a rice chest to cool down might be her.

Another burst of flashes went off at the board. She looked up: Song Kang-ho, Baek Si-on, Jeon Hye-jin, and Moon Geun-young stood shoulder to shoulder, waving on cue, making hearts, rotating positions at the photographers’ command. The picture looked like one loving family portrait. Anyone who knew the history could hardly miss what this family’s actual relations were: a father meaning to kill his son; a son raising a blade against his father; a wife joining the denunciation of her husband; a birth mother consenting, for the sake of the royal grandson, to her own son walking toward death. The most stable family in the Joseon royal house, arguably.

Eun-ah glanced at the “harmonious family” in front of the board, then down at the three names in her hand — and decided that reality’s relationship complexity wasn’t much behind the film’s.

Tonight’s film was ‘Sado’ — but the first candidate for the rice chest was standing at the check-in desk, holding a guest list.

— the premiere, as curated by fate

❧ ❧ ❧

The creators’ photo call ended and Baek Si-on stepped back from the board. The staff were about to walk him into the theater when the fans outside kept calling his name. He thought for a moment, asked a staffer for a marker, and turned toward the fan zone. The roar behind the railing surged at once; the bodyguards closed in instantly.

“Don’t push.”

He took the first poster handed over the barrier, signed it, passed it back.

“There’s time for more later.”

“Hold your phone tight — if it drops in, it’s gone.”

Autograph books, albums, magazines, and film posters kept arriving. He signed what he could, and for every To-signature asked after the spelling. Eun-ah watched from a short distance — that broad back serenely signing posters, of all things. The nerve. Tonight’s guest seats held his girlfriend, his most dangerous almost-something, and the brand endorser quietly crushing on him — and he had the presence of mind to work the barrier one poster at a time. The internal editorial wrote itself.

“Eun-ah.”

Someone called from behind her. She turned. Choi Jin-ri and Lee Ji-eun had just checked in and were walking toward her arm in arm, beaming.

Eun-ah: “......”

She had prepared for the worst. The worst meant: the two of them arriving separately, exchanging air-smiles at regulation distance, with herself assigned as messenger, usher, and manager of eye-contact frequency. She had prepared three seating-reshuffle plans. Instead, the two of them arrived like best friends on an arranged movie night. If Eun-ah hadn’t personally witnessed the earlier incidents, she might nearly have believed it.

Jin-ri reached her, let go of Ji-eun’s arm, and turned a full circle, the skirt flaring with the spin.

“Well?”

She meant tonight’s styling. Eun-ah moved fast:

“Jin-ri-unnie, you look beautiful tonight.”

Jin-ri nodded, satisfied. Lee Ji-eun stood alongside, smiling, her gaze settling calmly on Eun-ah — and that patient way of watching made the fine hairs on Eun-ah’s spine stand up.

“......”

Why was she the one absorbing this on her cousin’s behalf? Eun-ah turned to Ji-eun:

“Ji-eun-unnie, you look wonderful today too.”

Ji-eun’s brow moved a fraction.

“Too?”

The guest list abruptly turned scalding in Eun-ah’s hands. One word — the ORDER of one compliment — and the water between these two women could lift. She re-engineered the sentence at speed:

“Jin-ri-unnie’s styling suits a film premiere perfectly, and Ji-eun-unnie’s complexion is especially good tonight.”

Jin-ri glanced aside at Ji-eun.

“Funny — hers sounded the more sincere.”

Ji-eun smiled faintly.

“Probably because I didn’t force her to answer me first.”

Eun-ah’s temple began to throb. There was a time she could flirt on her cousin’s behalf using nothing but his phone — one message used to send Jin-ri fleeing, red to the ears. Facing the two of them herself, she finally learned how wide and cruel the gulf is between playing substitute and playing live. Sending messages for someone else, she only ever produced output. Now she had to absorb the feedback. From across the check-in desk, someone called her name — and for the first time in her life, a work summons sounded beautiful.

“One moment, you two — the staff will take you for photos and seating. I have other guests to receive.”

She left hugging the list, at a pace well over regulation walking speed. Lee Ji-eun watched her go.

“Is she hiding from us?”

Jin-ri answered:

“Maybe she’s just busy.”

“Who do you think she’s afraid of?”

Jin-ri considered.

“Shouldn’t be me.”

Ji-eun picked it up:

“Nor me.”

They looked at each other, drew no answer from either face, and turned away in the same instant — both of them looking instead at Baek Si-on, still signing at the barrier. He stood with his head down, the pen crossing one handed-over poster after another.

Lee Ji-eun looked away first. Tilted her face aside. The undisguised way Jin-ri looked at Baek Si-on had all landed in her eyes — tender, radiant, practically announcing to the lobby whom her heart was storing. Ji-eun spoke, feather-light:

“Stop staring. Even if you keep staring, he can’t come hug you right now.”

Jin-ri, cut off mid-gaze, went pink to the ears.

“Who’s staring...”

Ji-eun didn’t expose her. The topic turned instead.

“Seriously though, Jin-ri-ah — don’t you owe me a proper thank-you today?”

Jin-ri’s face filled with question marks.

“Mm? Thank you for what?”

“I recently blocked a very dangerous peach blossom for you.”

Confusion turned to alertness.

“What peach blossom?”

Lee Ji-eun entered narration mode. She told the whole story, vivid and complete: how, days ago at the ‘Infinite Challenge’ rehearsal in Pyeongchang, she had “without a single casualty” sniped Bae Suzy’s attempt to get close to Baek Si-on through a song called ‘Dream’ — and, for credibility, tossed in the Milan Fashion Week prehistory of Suzy’s earlier approach. She even produced her phone and pulled up a photo of the three of them together at some public event.

“So — shouldn’t you thank me?”

Jin-ri studied the photo on the phone and went quiet for a while. She believed it, ninety-nine percent. Because the information chain locked together perfectly: f(x) had been practicing flat-out for the ‘4 Walls’ comeback; Krystal Jung, having grown close to Kim Jong-in, occasionally picked up EXO-internal news — including one item: the company was considering using Byun Baek-hyun for a male-female duet with JYP’s Bae Suzy. Time, place, motive — every gear meshed.

The “danger” of Bae Suzy, Jin-ri knew too well. Suzy was not the type who only dared glance shyly from a waiting room, unable even to start a conversation. Hers was the dangerous kind: beautiful, and aware of what she wanted — and when she decided to close distance, she did not retreat to some “safe distance” merely because the target already had someone beside him. What Jin-ri feared most was precisely this breed: capable, resourced, and unafraid to manufacture her own opportunities. And Ji-eun’s interception had struck exactly that core concern.

Jin-ri said, with total seriousness:

“Thank you, unnie.”

Lee Ji-eun raised a brow a fraction. She had prepared full contingencies for follow-up interrogation.

Why so eager?

What did you get out of it?

Are you eliminating your own competitors?

Instead, Jin-ri had simply thanked her... This peach was, at times, sensitive to the point of headache, and at times honest beyond all expectation. None of it stopped Lee Ji-eun from accepting the gratitude with perfect calm.

“Don’t mention it. It’s what anyone would do.”

Delivered exactly like a good older sister exhausted on her little sister’s romantic behalf — as if she had entirely forgotten that she had “edited” the story into the version Jin-ri most wanted to hear. Jin-ri knew nothing of what had happened in Milan that night. She only felt that Ji-eun-unnie looked unusually agreeable today. A dangerous feeling — fortunately, Ji-eun didn’t let her enjoy it long.

“But don’t celebrate too early.”

Lee Ji-eun looked back toward Baek Si-on, unhurried.

“What do you mean?” Jin-ri asked.

“Peach blossoms outside are easy to spot.”

She gathered her gaze back and set it on Jin-ri’s face.

“The truly dangerous ones are usually the people who’ve always been nearby.”

Jin-ri examined her with an extraordinarily subtle look — the suspicion written right there on her face: are you talking about yourself? Lee Ji-eun read the anti-thief stare instantly, felt not one gram of guilt, raised a hand, and tapped Jin-ri’s forehead with a knuckle.

“What are you thinking?”

Then she tipped her chin toward the other side.

“There.”

“They’re here.”

Jin-ri turned where she was shown. The five of Red Velvet had just walked into the lobby — Kim Yerim leading, Kang Seul-gi, Park Soo-young, and Son Seung-wan behind her. Bae Joo-hyun walked last. Her attention had already drifted, all on its own, to the loudest fan zone in the venue. And of course — of all the moments — that was exactly the scene Jin-ri’s eyes landed on.

As a woman who loved Baek Si-on, she read the delight in Bae Joo-hyun’s eyes at one glance. The emotion behind that delight looked far, far too much like her own — the one she wore whenever she looked at him.
