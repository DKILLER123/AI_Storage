Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Seven

The Pretty Noona Buying Dinner

The retake · a private dance studio

Tucked into a comparatively quiet street

No sign downstairs · only a floor-number plate

No sign hung downstairs.

The entrance carried only a metal plate with the floor number, beside which parked a few unmarked business vans.

The van stopped at the curb.

Bae Joo-hyun put on her mask and sunglasses and pressed the black cap down lower.

A loose sun-protection shirt draped over her, thin wide-leg trousers falling to the shoes, a backpack behind her holding dance clothes, water, and a towel.

The manager turned back from the driver’s seat.

“Want me to go in with you?”

“No need.”

Bae Joo-hyun pulled the door open.

A private lesson.

The fewer people, the lower the chance of a leak.

Inside, the elevator reached the designated floor; the corridor was very quiet.

Two tall men stood outside the dance room.

Bae Joo-hyun recognized them.

Baek Si-on’s bodyguards.

The two had clearly received notice of the visit, but with Bae Joo-hyun covered head to toe, one still stepped forward politely.

“And you are?”

Bae Joo-hyun pulled down her mask.

No need, like last time at the studio gate, to be asked “Red Velvet” three times by the security uncle, nor to dig out a cropped old group photo to prove herself.

This time she only had to show her face.

The bodyguard stepped aside and pushed open the dance room’s double door for her.

❧ ❧ ❧

Crisp tap sounds came out at once.

♪

Tap · the solo homework take

Baek Si-on · practice room, marked line

Tap.

Tap-tap.

Tap.

The rhythm was tight, the soles striking the wooden floor overlapping the accompaniment.

Bae Joo-hyun stood in the doorway.

In the center of the room, Baek Si-on, in black practice wear and tap shoes, was walking a complete step combination along the marked line in front of the mirror.

More eye-catching than him was Baek Eun-ah.

She held a camcorder, chasing the shot in a posture extremely hard to describe in normal language.

Sometimes half-squatting.

Sometimes sliding sideways, body turned.

To keep Baek Si-on’s feet in frame at all times, she even glided a stretch along the floor, a movement somewhere between a crab’s migration and a shaman’s dance.

The posture was bizarre, yet professional.

Korean music-show and industry fancam operators worked precisely this way — half-squat, sidestep — every day.

Bae Joo-hyun, a music-show host and professional idol, was no stranger to the method.

Only, the one who used to squat and sidestep below the stage was a professional cameraman.

Now it was Baek Eun-ah, and the picture carried a certain pathos of a manager forced to expand her job description.

She stood in the doorway a while longer, and finally realized, late.

She had assumed today’s “retake” would go like this:

Baek Si-on, arms folded in front of him.

Waiting for her to finish the whole forty-point homework, then picking faults item by item — rhythm, turns, partner coordination.

As it turned out, Baek Si-on too had to hand his recording to someone else for grading.

Which meant today there was no lofty teacher and no failing student come to retake.

Only two people who hadn’t managed to hand in complete homework, happening to converge in the same dance room.

The realization stirred in Bae Joo-hyun an emotion that probably shouldn’t have appeared.

Schadenfreude.

So the Venice Best Actor also had homework to hand in, and the one filming it was his own cousin.

Life really was fair.

❧ ❧ ❧

The accompaniment entered its last section.

Baek Si-on finished a run of continuous steps, his body stopping on the final accent squarely before the mirror.

Arms spread, the movement cleaned off.

The music ended.

Baek Eun-ah held the filming posture two seconds more, confirming the movement was fully captured before lowering the camcorder.

She looked down to check the footage.

Scrubbed from the start to the middle, then focused on the fastest footwork.

“The feet all made it in.”

Baek Si-on caught his breath a little.

“That sounds like the bare minimum.”

“Last time you danced out of frame three times halfway through.”

“That was you not keeping up.”

“So this time I nearly broke my back.”

Baek Eun-ah, hand on her lower back, was about to keep accusing her cousin’s tap dance of the occupational injury it had done her lumbar spine, when a figure entered the corner of her eye.

She turned.

“Joo-hyun-unnie’s here.”

Baek Si-on turned toward the door too.

“You’re here?”

Bae Joo-hyun nodded and walked into the dance room.

“Ne, sunbae.”

She first glanced at the practice video in Baek Eun-ah’s hand, then looked down to confirm the tap shoes on Baek Si-on’s feet.

The schadenfreude she had pressed down finally leaked out a little.

“Sunbae has to hand in homework too?”

“I hear pleasure in your tone.”

Bae Joo-hyun denied it outright.

“No!”

Baek Si-on didn’t point out that her eyes were already curved into smiles.

“Good if there isn’t.”

Bae Joo-hyun added a line in her head.

Anyway, you have no proof.

Baek Si-on changed out of the tap shoes, sat on the bench, and switched back to ordinary practice shoes.

“Did you bring dance clothes?”

Bae Joo-hyun patted the backpack behind her.

“Brought them.”

Baek Si-on gestured toward the inner side of the room.

“There’s a changing room over there.”

“Ne.”

Bae Joo-hyun passed in front of the two and pushed open the changing room door.

The door closed.

❧ ❧ ❧

The changing room wasn’t large.

A bench against the wall, a few lockers beside it, and a full-length mirror facing the door.

Bae Joo-hyun took off her cap and mask.

Her hair had been pressed into a curve under the cap; she smoothed it with her fingers, restoring the bangs to their original direction.

She set the bag on the bench.

Unzipped it.

First took off the wide-leg trousers and sun shirt, then drew out the dance skirt she had prepared in advance.

After changing, she did a simple turn in front of the mirror.

The hem swung lightly with her body, circling a round at her legs.

She stopped.

Checked left and right.

The clothes fit.

Her hair was already tied.

Bae Joo-hyun put her hand on the doorknob, then drew it back.

She returned to the bench, opened the backpack’s side pocket, and took out two lip glosses:

the two lip glosses · from the side pocket

LAVUE 01 — “First-Love Peach”

LAVUE 02 — “Vampire Cherry”

She first twisted open No. 01 and, with the lip brush, laid a thin layer of peach-pink-orange over her lips.

Once the surface set slightly, she opened No. 02 and dotted the dark red lightly onto the inner upper and lower lips.

Bae Joo-hyun pressed her lips together at the mirror.

Then realized the motion would ruin the layering she had just formed, and quickly fixed the corners with a fingertip.

Before leaving, Bae Joo-hyun checked the makeup once more and put her hand back on the doorknob.

She paused again.

Turned back to the mirror and smoothed the hem down a little.

Too deliberate wasn’t good.

Having smoothed it, she felt the waistline was hidden.

Pulled it back up.

Bae Joo-hyun stared at herself in the mirror and finally realized that if she kept adjusting, the people outside might suspect she had remade an entire outfit in the changing room.

Only then did she pull the door open and walk out.

❧ ❧ ❧

The changing room door was pushed open.

Baek Eun-ah turned her head first.

A light dance top traced her lines; the dark practice skirt ended above the knee.

The shoes had changed to a pair of chunky-heel dance shoes, the heel moderate — just enough to pull, on camera, the height gap between the two from “walking a child” back into “acceptable” range.

The touched-up lip makeup looked especially lively under the dance room’s ceiling lights.

Baek Eun-ah first gave ample support:

“Wow, Joo-hyun-unnie, you’re so pretty today!”

“You went in a student here to retake, and came out looking ready to shoot a film!”

Bae Joo-hyun had already done ample psychological preparation in front of the changing room mirror.

But praised to her face like this, she was still a little embarrassed.

“It’s not that exaggerated.”

Modest in words, her fingers — under cover of tidying her hair — had already drifted toward Baek Si-on, standing in the center of the practice room.

Whose evaluation she really wanted to hear — not even a strand of hair could hide it.

She had already designed her responses in advance.

If he said she looked nice, she’d say it was for the filming effect.

If he said the lip color was nice, she’d remind him it was LAVUE’s two-color layering.

If he noticed nothing at all.

Then it would only prove this man’s taste didn’t match the makeup brand he himself had founded.

Baek Si-on was indeed looking her over.

Bae Joo-hyun kept her surface calm, waiting for the line she had already prepared a response for.

What she heard was:

“Can you dance in high heels?”

“……”

The answers she had prepared all lost their use at once.

Dead memories began to attack her.

Bae Joo-hyun quickly pressed the harmful memories back down.

“I’m fine!”

The answer was firm.

Baek Si-on confirmed once more.

“Really?”

The doubt activated Bae Joo-hyun’s pride on the spot.

She admitted that, in her earliest days after debut, she had indeed had an unpleasant experience with high heels.

But that was already over a year ago.

Through countless music stages, commercial shows, rehearsals, and shoots, high heels had long become standard equipment for a female idol’s work.

However you put it, she was a proper idol out of the SM trainee system.

And Red Velvet’s leader, besides.

How could she be defined forever by one old accident?

Bae Joo-hyun lifted her chin slightly.

“I’m a professional idol.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“A forty-point professional idol?”

“……”

One arrow through the heart.

The professional dignity she had just built was pierced on the spot by forty points.

Damn that forty!

Had she known this score would be used to question her professionalism, she wouldn’t have handed in the practice video with a variety-show mindset of “just finish the moves.”

Bae Joo-hyun bit her lower lip and walked straight to the center of the dance room.

The hem swayed lightly at her legs with each step.

The dance-shoe heels struck the floor, the sound steady and crisp.

She really had debuted late.

But age never meant weak skill.

Forty points was only because she hadn’t been serious last time.

Now Baek Si-on doubted her.

Then let him see with his own eyes what a forty-point professional idol looked like when she got serious.

❧ ❧ ❧

Seeing this, Baek Si-on rolled his ankles and wrists, then walked to stand before Bae Joo-hyun.

“Let’s run it once first.”

Bae Joo-hyun nodded.

“Okay.”

Baek Eun-ah picked up her phone, retreated beside the mirror, ready to play the music and watch the two’s blocking.

‘La La Land’s dance system mixed tap, waltz, jazz, and the partner dances common to Hollywood’s golden age.

What Baek Si-on had just recorded alone was the tap basics homework.

What he and Bae Joo-hyun had to hand Damien was this — a partner waltz test.

The waltz hold — standard, and intimate

The two face each other.

One hand clasped.

The woman’s other hand rests on her partner’s shoulder.

The man places his right hand near his partner’s left shoulder blade.

Baek Si-on stood before Bae Joo-hyun and raised his left hand in invitation.

Bae Joo-hyun looked at the palm suspended in the air, and the pride the “forty points” had stirred leaked half away in that instant.

She had been so busy proving she could dance.

She had forgotten that a partner dance is called a partner dance because it needs two people very close.

Bae Joo-hyun forced herself to move.

Half a step forward.

Right hand raised, to take the palm he held in the air.

The instant her fingertips touched his palm.

A warm sensation traveled over her skin, and her fingers drew back half an inch on reflex.

Baek Si-on lowered his head, looking at her withdrawn hand.

“Static?”

Bae Joo-hyun’s expression management faced its greatest test of the day.

“…… No.”

“Then what are you dodging?”

“Adjusting my hand position.”

She gave herself no more room to hesitate and pressed her hand up on her own.

Baek Si-on closed his fingers at once.

His palm was much larger than Bae Joo-hyun’s.

Once the two hands truly fitted, hers was almost wholly enclosed inside.

The palm’s warmth, the contact of knuckles, and the strength in Baek Si-on’s grip all became extraordinarily clear.

A presence so strong she could hardly pretend this was ordinary practice.

Bae Joo-hyun silently repeated in her head:

This is homework.

This is a partner-dance test to hand a Hollywood director.

It has nothing to do with a heartbeat.

Her palms are sweating because the dance room is too hot.

❧ ❧ ❧

Once the handclasp was set, Bae Joo-hyun raised her left hand to rest above Baek Si-on’s shoulder.

Baek Si-on’s right hand closed at once, falling toward her back.

The instant his palm landed below her shoulder blade, his fingers met a horizontal strap hidden under the dance practice wear.

Baek Si-on’s hand stopped at once.

Last time, an accident had happened at this very spot.

That time’s ending was a crisp “slap” and several days of social death for Bae Joo-hyun.

Of course he remembered.

Baek Si-on’s palm slid quickly down from the shoulder blade and finally settled at her waist.

Bae Joo-hyun’s body dodged sideways on instinct.

The dance frame broke at once.

Baek Si-on’s hand stopped in mid-air.

“Dodging again?”

Bae Joo-hyun obviously couldn’t admit the real reason: because your hand suddenly went to my waist!

“Not ready yet.”

“Oh.”

Baek Si-on put his hand back toward her waist.

Bae Joo-hyun shrank a little again.

“Ticklish?”

His hand followed.

She kept dodging.

He kept following.

The two shifted left and right several times.

The originally standard waltz preparatory pose gradually turned into some kind of lateral chase with no accompaniment.

❧ ❧ ❧

The lateral chase ended.

Baek Si-on finally settled his hand at Bae Joo-hyun’s waist.

She didn’t dodge again.

She bit her lip, moved her attention off the warmth at her waist, and focused on the music about to begin.

Baek Eun-ah pressed play.

♪

Waltz · the partner-dance test

‘La La Land’ rehearsal · slow tempo

A gentle waltz accompaniment unfolded through the dance room.

Baek Si-on took the first step.

Bae Joo-hyun followed.

At first, the two were indeed a little uncoordinated.

Baek Si-on’s stride was naturally larger than Bae Joo-hyun’s; to avoid throwing her off, he had to consciously rein in his steps.

Bae Joo-hyun, given their stride and height gap, had to judge more keenly the guidance he sent through palm and waist.

On a turn, she walked half a step too many.

Backing up, his center of gravity pressed too far forward.

The two missed the melody’s landing before the mirror, then found it again on the next beat.

But after the first few bars, the problems vanished quickly.

Bae Joo-hyun’s dance foundation was far more solid than that forty-point homework had shown.

Her backward steps were steady.

Her turns were clean-arc.

The hem spread at her legs and gathered back, fully keeping Baek Si-on’s guiding rhythm.

Baek Eun-ah, standing beside the mirror, had been ready to list the problems needing adjustment in her head.

After watching a while, she found she had nothing to do for the moment.

The accompaniment entered its last section.

Baek Si-on, after a turn, brought Bae Joo-hyun back in front of him.

The music ended.

Bae Joo-hyun stood steady.

The heeled dance shoes flat on the floor — no wobble, no twisted ankle.

She caught her breath a little, then lifted her face.

“How was it?”

She wasn’t asking, of course, whether she had remembered the moves.

What she really wanted to confirm was: do you still think I’m only forty points?

Baek Si-on gave a direct assessment.

“Very good.”

Bae Joo-hyun waited a while.

Found it was only two words.

“Only ‘very good’?”

“Stable movement, accurate rhythm, no problem with turns or following awareness.”

He reassessed the performance just given.

“Danced seriously, forty points really was too low.”

The smugness at Bae Joo-hyun’s lips finally couldn’t be pressed down.

“Then how many points?”

Baek Si-on gave a new score.

“Eighty-five.”

“Oh.”

❧ ❧ ❧

After a short rest, the second rehearsal began.

The two were already used to each other’s rhythm and stride.

Baek Si-on no longer had to deliberately slow his pace and guidance; Bae Joo-hyun no longer had to rely on memory to judge direction at every step.

Once the movement turned natural, the atmosphere a waltz truly needs began to appear.

Baek Si-on advanced, and she retreated with the flow.

He raised a hand to guide, and she completed the turn along their clasped palms.

The practice skirt spread at her legs, then fell naturally with the turn back.

The height gap remained, but because Bae Joo-hyun wore heeled dance shoes, her frame was slightly lifted, forming in the mirror a stagger well suited to the lens.

Her frame was slender.

Baek Si-on’s shoulders, back, and arm lines had strength.

One led.

One followed.

The picture was unexpectedly harmonious.

Baek Eun-ah raised the camcorder, adjusting the angle the way she had filmed the tap.

Moved two steps left.

Squatted a little lower.

Just about to unfold that lumbar-killer camera work, she found she didn’t seem to need to keep migrating sideways.

As long as the two stayed fully in frame, the composition already held.

❧ ❧ ❧

The second rehearsal ended; neither raised a movement problem.

Baek Eun-ah confirmed:

“Record the official version now?”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Record it.”

She walked to the position fixed in advance, opened the recording function, and first confirmed both their full bodies were in frame.

“Ready.”

Bae Joo-hyun adjusted her breathing.

Baek Si-on raised his left hand again.

She stepped half a step forward, put her right hand into his palm, and rested her other hand on his shoulder.

The two entered the preparatory pose.

Baek Eun-ah counted down.

“Three, two, one.”

The music began.

The official recording started.

❧ ❧ ❧

The first few beats went smoothly.

Until Baek Eun-ah, to keep up with the two’s direction of movement, passed in front at an angle with the camcorder.

The instant the lens entered Bae Joo-hyun’s peripheral vision, professional habit took over her body at once.

Her face angle adjusted unconsciously, her gaze chasing the camera.

For a female idol, this reaction needed almost no thought.

Whether a music stage, a commercial show, or an ad shoot, the moment the red light came on, she should find the camera first.

But a partner waltz needed her attention to stay on her partner throughout.

So, on the next guiding move that brought her back in front of him, Baek Si-on said two words low:

“Look at me.”

Bae Joo-hyun only then realized she had just chased the camera.

Enduring the discomfort of “the camera is right there, yet I can’t confirm whether I’m being filmed well,” she pulled her attention back off the camcorder.

It landed on Baek Si-on’s face.

His pupils reflected her.

Everything in the dance room seemed to vanish — Baek Eun-ah holding the camcorder vanished too, and the playing music became a very distant background.

The two’s steps still landed on the beat.

But Bae Joo-hyun could no longer tell whether what drove her turn now was the waltz’s guidance, or her heartbeat.

❧ ❧ ❧

When the music stopped, Bae Joo-hyun hadn’t even noticed.

She blinked.

Blinked again.

Her awareness finally withdrew from that overly focused gaze.

But Bae Joo-hyun didn’t know where to put her eyes, and finally could only let them fall to their clasped hands.

“You… you can let go now.”

Hearing this, Baek Si-on simply released her palm, drew back the arm around her waist, and stepped half a step away.

Her body regained its freedom.

Bae Joo-hyun, though, secretly chafed in her heart.

Why did I open my mouth.

Baek Si-on hadn’t said he wouldn’t let go.

Reminding him a little later wouldn’t have affected the development of the American film industry.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Eun-ah looked down to check the recorded file.

“Captured it completely, nothing out of frame.”

“Want to watch it once?”

Baek Si-on nodded and walked to her side.

Baek Eun-ah tapped playback.

By normal positioning, three people watching one small screen, the most reasonable arrangement was:

Bae Joo-hyun — Baek Eun-ah — Baek Si-on.

Bae Joo-hyun did walk this way.

But when she actually stopped, her steps had circled half a step extra.

She ended up stopping on Baek Si-on’s other side.

The arrangement became:

Baek Eun-ah — Baek Si-on — Bae Joo-hyun.

The camcorder was still in Baek Eun-ah’s hand.

Which meant, to see the playback clearly, Bae Joo-hyun could only lean toward Baek Si-on.

She leaned over quite naturally.

The reason was airtight: the screen is too small — how can three people see clearly without crowding in?

As for why she didn’t stand on Baek Eun-ah’s other side?

That side probably had bad feng shui.

❧ ❧ ❧

The playback began.

On screen, Baek Si-on guided Bae Joo-hyun through the first set of turns.

Baek Si-on first minded his own center of gravity and hand position.

Then, feeling a warm touch along his arm, he turned his head.

Bae Joo-hyun was holding the earnest expression of checking homework, as if crowding this close were purely for the technical progress of the musical film.

Baek Si-on thought a moment, reached over, and took the camcorder from Baek Eun-ah’s hand.

He moved the screen in front of himself, then tilted it slightly right.

This way, the picture sat exactly between him and Bae Joo-hyun.

She no longer had to look past Baek Si-on at the screen in Baek Eun-ah’s hand.

Standing where she was, she could see perfectly clearly.

“Can you see it now?”

“Mm.”

The two stood shoulder to shoulder like this, watching the partner dance in the playback.

Baek Eun-ah, standing beside them, first looked down at her own empty hands, then looked up at the camcorder her cousin had deliberately tilted toward Bae Joo-hyun.

Finally, her attention fell on Bae Joo-hyun, who could already see the screen clearly yet still stayed pressed against Baek Si-on.

She blinked slowly.

The video was filmed by her.

The camera work was hers.

Once the playback began, she — the camerawoman — was instead squeezed out of the best viewing spot.

Was this vast practice room too small to hold Baek Eun-ah?

❧ ❧ ❧

Watching to the end, Baek Si-on handed the camcorder back to Baek Eun-ah.

“Cut the irrelevant bits at the start and end.”

“Stabilize where the camera shakes. Once it’s exported, send it to Director Damien.”

Baek Eun-ah looked down at the device back in her hands.

So this dance room did have room for her.

Not only room — it very much needed her.

Baek Eun-ah opened the editing software and typed a casual note into the export field.

“Need to add end credits?”

Baek Si-on asked: “What?”

“Special thanks to filming, camera work, editing, and Baek Eun-ah, squeezed out of the playback area.”

Bae Joo-hyun silently stepped half a step aside, trying to prove she hadn’t taken part in the squeezing-out.

Baek Si-on, as if he hadn’t heard how much resentment was packed into the line, reminded her:

“Check the clarity before exporting.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

The video had no obvious problems.

Which meant Bae Joo-hyun’s task today was done.

By reason, she should now change back, bid Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah goodbye, and return to the dorm.

But truly reaching this ending, she was a little unwilling to leave.

Today’s time together was shorter than she had imagined.

The actual dancing was only a few runs.

And that “look at me,” plus the hand not yet released after the music ended, kept recurring in her head.

Bae Joo-hyun asked casually, as if offhand:

“Sunbae, don’t you have to keep doing the ‘Sado’ roadshow today?”

“Resting today.”

Her heartbeat quickened a beat at once.

A promise that had existed before surfaced quickly from memory.

“Didn’t I say before I’d treat you to a meal, sunbae?”

“So?”

“Since we both happen to have time today, want to go now?”

The instant she asked, Bae Joo-hyun regretted it a little.

Proposing a meal alone right after the retake ended — was the order too hasty?

But the words were out; taking them back was stranger than saying them.

“Can we?”

Baek Si-on looked down at his sweat-soaked practice wear and wiped his brow.

“Sure.”

Bae Joo-hyun hadn’t had time to be happy yet.

“But I’ll shower first.”

“Wait a while.”

Bae Joo-hyun nodded at once.

“Okay.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on took his change of clothes into the dance room’s attached shower.

The door closed.

The sound of water came from inside soon after.

Only Baek Eun-ah and Bae Joo-hyun were left in the dance room.

The editing software was still open.

Baek Eun-ah kept processing the video with her head down; Bae Joo-hyun stood beside her, now and then confirming from the corner of her eye what she was doing.

In the end, Baek Eun-ah broke the quiet first.

“What’s unnie planning to treat cousin to?”

“Haven’t decided yet.”

“Did your settlement come through?”

Bae Joo-hyun: “……”

“It did.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded, relieved.

“Then no problem.”

Bae Joo-hyun hesitated a moment, then asked:

“Eun-ah, want to come along?”

Baek Eun-ah kept dragging the timeline, head down.

“If unnie invites me sincerely, I could consider it.”

Bae Joo-hyun fell silent.

Of course she didn’t want to invite her.

But to refuse at once would make the purpose of treating Baek Si-on alone too obvious.

Baek Eun-ah waited a while and received no follow-up.

She looked up on her own.

“Seems it isn’t very sincere.”

Bae Joo-hyun tried to salvage it:

“No, it’s just…”

“I still have work.”

Baek Eun-ah smiled a little.

“You two go.”

Bae Joo-hyun felt relieved inside, but had to keep a regretful look on her face.

“Then next time, together.”

“Okay.”

Baek Eun-ah lowered her head again.

As for which day this “next time” would fall on, most likely, in Bae Joo-hyun’s memo, its scheduling approached never.

❧ ❧ ❧

The video finished exporting.

Baek Eun-ah confirmed the file played normally, packed the equipment, and left the dance room first.

Bae Joo-hyun returned to the changing room.

Took off the practice skirt and heeled dance shoes.

Changed back into a white short-sleeve, thin wide-leg trousers, and the sun shirt, and put on the collab training shoes Baek Si-on had given her.

After changing, she stood before the mirror to check.

The base makeup was fairly intact.

The lip makeup had faded a little.

She lightly touched up a layer with No. 01 “First-Love Peach,” then blotted the excess shine with a tissue.

Then she took a small bottle of perfume from her bag.

She had meant only one spray at the neck, but after her finger fell, she hesitated and added a little at the wrist.

The two wrists touched lightly together.

She lowered her head and smelled.

Too strong?

Bae Joo-hyun stood before the mirror a long time.

In the end she didn’t wash it off.

Only pulled her cuffs down a little.

Sweating from dance practice.

Wearing perfume was perfectly normal.

❧ ❧ ❧

Bae Joo-hyun pushed open the changing room door.

Baek Si-on had finished showering too — a clean black short-sleeve with casual trousers, his wet buzz cut only roughly towel-dried.

When the two drew close, he caught a very faint scent.

“You wore perfume?”

Bae Joo-hyun’s heart tightened.

“I sweated from practice just now; I was worried I’d smell.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Smells nice.”

Bae Joo-hyun suddenly felt that all the time she had just spent agonizing in front of the mirror had been worth it.

She lowered her head to tidy her backpack strap, pressing down the corner of her mouth.

“Thank you.”

Baek Si-on picked up his cap.

“What are we eating?”

Bae Joo-hyun countered:

“Is barbecue okay?”

“Sure.”

She chose a barbecue place not far from the Red Velvet dorm.

Good flavor.

Private rooms.

The staff were used to nearby celebrities and wouldn’t make a fuss.

The van stopped in the restaurant’s parking lot.

The two went straight into the private room through the back door.

Bae Joo-hyun took off her cap and mask and set her bag on the chair beside her.

The server brought the menu.

Since she was repaying a favor, she obviously couldn’t seem stingy about the budget.

The menu was pushed in front of Baek Si-on.

“Order whatever.”

Baek Si-on lifted his face.

“Sure?”

“Sure.”

Baek Si-on really began flipping through the menu.

Hanwoo tenderloin

Marinated beef short ribs

Pork belly

Cold noodles

The server, holding the electronic order pad, recorded item by item.

Bae Joo-hyun could keep her smile at first.

When the menu reached the third page, the smile began to crack.

“Sunbae, do you usually eat this much in one meal?”

“You said order whatever.”

Baek Si-on was still flipping.

“It’s not too late to regret it now.”

“I don’t regret it.”

Bae Joo-hyun paused a moment.

As if to prove she really could afford it, she added a line:

“My settlement already came through.”

Baek Si-on’s menu-flipping hand stopped.

Then he ordered two more plates of meat.

Bae Joo-hyun: “……”

Should she not have volunteered her personal finances?

Never mind.

Two more plates of meat at least proved this meal was worth treating.

The server put away the order pad and confirmed one last time:

“Would you like alcohol or drinks?”

Bae Joo-hyun turned to Baek Si-on.

“Sunbae, do you want to drink?”

“No.”

“Because you have work tomorrow?”

“Alcohol doesn’t taste as good as cola.”

Bae Joo-hyun paused, then the corner of her mouth curved.

“So sunbae has a child’s taste after all.”

Baek Si-on: ?

“Only children like sweet drinks.”

Baek Si-on turned to the server.

“Bring two bottles.”

“Cola?”

“…… Beer.”

Bae Joo-hyun added from the side:

“Make them cold.”

The server recorded it, repeated the dishes once more, and left the private room after confirming everything was correct.

The door was gently closed.

Bae Joo-hyun finally couldn’t hold in her laughter.

“Sunbae really doesn’t have to force it.”

“I’m correcting your mistaken idea of adult taste.”

“Then I’ll look forward to it~”

❧ ❧ ❧

Bottled water the restaurant provided sat at the table.

Bae Joo-hyun twisted off the cap and first rinsed her own cup, chopsticks, and small dish one by one.

Then she took Baek Si-on’s utensils and washed those too.

Baek Si-on watched a while.

“Who taught you that?”

“Yoona-unnie.”

Im Yoon-a, filming on the mainland before, had picked up the habit of scalding cups and plates with water or hot tea, and later sometimes did it on the side when eating with the company’s juniors.

Bae Joo-hyun had watched a few times and learned it.

A cultural export that traveled half a circle and finally returned to a Korean barbecue restaurant.

“Good.”

Bae Joo-hyun set the washed utensils back in front of Baek Si-on.

Chopsticks and spoon laid side by side, the direction matching her own place.

The server knocked and brought kimchi, pickled radish, lettuce, and two frosty bottles of Hite beer.

Baek Si-on glanced at the beer bottle, which bore Hyun Bin’s advertising image.

Bae Joo-hyun noticed too.

Once the server left, she asked naturally:

“Sunbae, why don’t you take liquor ads?”

Korean liquor brands usually chose the hottest actors, singers, or sports stars of the moment as endorsers.

Given Baek Si-on’s current national recognition and commercial influence, no brand could fail to approach him.

Baek Si-on picked up the opener, opened two beers, and handed her one.

“My influence is too big.”

“If I endorsed liquor, it would make some underage fans want to imitate.”

Korean law could require stores not to sell alcohol to minors, but couldn’t stop a fifteen-year-old girl, because her favorite idol shot an ad, from growing curious about alcohol.

Bae Joo-hyun understood.

“Sunbae is afraid of leading children astray?”

“Mm.”

Bae Joo-hyun, studying the utter lack of enthusiasm in the way he poured himself a drink, said:

“Actually, part of the reason is that sunbae himself doesn’t like drinking, isn’t it?”

“That too.”

To those who like it, alcohol is nectar.

To those who don’t, it is only a liquid.

Baek Si-on was somewhere in between.

He could drink when necessary.

But if you discussed flavor alone, he really would rather choose cola.

Bae Joo-hyun reached a conclusion:

“Still a child’s taste after all.”

Baek Si-on set down the bottle.

“Are you that much older?”

Bae Joo-hyun finally seized a rare advantage.

“I’m born in ’91.”

“’91?”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on, used to judging relationships by debut time and industry seniority, really hadn’t calculated Bae Joo-hyun’s age carefully.

She looked young.

Wasn’t tall either.

Plus her status as a rookie junior — it was easy to overlook that she was actually older than him.

“Then should I call you noona?”

“No need.”

Bae Joo-hyun refused extremely fast.

“Noona” would make her feel Baek Si-on was deliberately flagging the age gap, and easily boxed the two into an older-sister-younger-brother etiquette.

She didn’t want to be treated as an older sister to be respected.

Bae Joo-hyun chose her words carefully:

“We’ve already met privately many times.”

“From now on, we can drop the honorifics, right?”

This was the condition she really wanted to raise.

To give up the age advantage Korean etiquette granted, and trade away that “sunbae” that always kept an industry distance between them.

Baek Si-on heard the meaning.

“Sure.”

Bae Joo-hyun held the cold beer.

Given permission, she was actually more nervous than when she had made the request.

The words circled her tongue once and finally came out.

“Si-on-ah…”

Baek Si-on: “……”

This form of address usually came from elders like Yoon Hye-ja, or from seniors older and closer than him.

In his private circle, Choi Jin-ri called him “oppa.”

Lee Ji-eun mostly skipped even his name, just a single “ya.”

Baek Eun-ah called him “cousin.”

Jung Jae-joon was in the habit of calling him “boss.”

The Western side uniformly called him “Baek.”

Bae Joo-hyun was the first to call him by this address.

It sounded strange.

Bae Joo-hyun, seeing no reaction, assumed he hadn’t heard clearly, and called again.

“Si-on-ah?”

Baek Si-on pressed down the slight discomfort.

“Mm.”

Bae Joo-hyun’s eyes curved into smiles at once.

Just trading one form of address.

The line between the two marked “sunbae” and “junior” was quietly stepped over.

She lowered her head and filled her own beer.

“Have a drink?”

“Mm.”

Bae Joo-hyun raised her glass.

“Here’s to your American shoot going smoothly.”

The toast was faintly familiar.

Baek Si-on, without thinking hard, raised his glass too.

“Here’s to your comeback blowing up.”

“Cheers!”

The rims touched lightly.

Bae Joo-hyun tilted her head and drained the cold beer in one go.

Setting the glass down, she let out a refreshed breath:

“Aah—!”

A hot summer.

Just finished dancing.

Then a glass of ice-cold beer downed.

The chill and the malt flavor sliding down her throat together — it really was very comfortable.

Baek Si-on held his own glass, not yet drunk.

“This is the adult taste?”

“Now you know, right?”

Baek Si-on didn’t answer; his attention fell to Bae Joo-hyun’s lips.

She noticed.

“What is it?”

Baek Si-on raised a hand and pointed at his own upper lip.

“Here.”

Bae Joo-hyun understood at once.

Beer foam had stayed at the edge of her upper lip.

Baek Si-on had assumed she’d wipe it with a tissue or the back of her hand.

Bae Joo-hyun instead stuck out the tip of her tongue and lightly licked the bit of white foam away.

She did it naturally, seeming to carry no special meaning at all.

But in a private room, the two sitting face to face.

One of them, having licked her lips, lifted her face; the other hadn’t yet withdrawn his attention.

“Why are you looking at me like that?”

“Cute.”

Bae Joo-hyun started slightly.

Baek Si-on added:

“I mean that move you just did.”

The explanation was very rigorous.

But “cute” had already been said.

The qualifier afterward didn’t reduce its killing power much.

Bae Joo-hyun lowered her head, pretending to pour herself another drink, but the curve at the corner of her mouth couldn’t be pressed flat at all.

❧ ❧ ❧

The server knocked again.

The charcoal grill was carried into the private room.

Then the ordered meat and side dishes gradually filled the table.

The hanwoo’s marbling was especially clear under the ceiling lights.

Bae Joo-hyun, facing the table full of food, finally gained a more concrete understanding of her own “order whatever.”

She instinctively picked up the tongs and scissors.

Taking care of her younger members had become a habit.

When Son Seung-wan, Kang Seul-gi, Park So-young, and Kim Ye-rim sat in front of her, the grilling was always her job.

But Baek Si-on reached straight toward her.

“Give them here.”

Bae Joo-hyun didn’t react.

“What?”

“The tongs and scissors.”

“I’ll just grill.”

“There’s no rule that the one treating has to do the grilling.”

Bae Joo-hyun handed the tools over.

Baek Si-on picked up the first piece of beef and set it on the grill.

Sizzle—!

The meat touched the hot grill, fat quickly bubbling fine, the scent of charcoal and beef spreading through the private room.

Bae Joo-hyun lifted her cold beer and took a sip.

Summer.

Cold beer.

A private room.

The person grilling for her across the table was the one she had quietly cared about for a long time.

She leaned into the backrest, the corner of her mouth curving.

Life, really.

At least in this moment, it was pretty good.
