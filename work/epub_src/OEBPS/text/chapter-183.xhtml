Chapter One Hundred Eighty Three

A Moving City’s GDP

January 2–3 · Churchill back to Los Angeles, then straight into money One fake sleep that fools nobody · one necklace ruled neither hidden nor shown · one debrief conducted from a destroyed hotel bed · five percent of a drink, and fourteen cities asking him to please come spend money The snow road out of Churchill → thirty-five thousand feet over the Pacific → the Four Seasons, a law office in Century City → the tour war room A window betrays a smile; a guide admits the truth about Yellowknife; a best friend certifies a heart-arsonist; a founder hands over five percent and the whole of Asia; and a man who thought he was scheduling concerts learns he is, technically, a walking stimulus package.

The heater in the SUV on the way back was running full blast.

On the way in, Choi Jin-ri had pressed her forehead to the window like a small animal insatiably curious about the world, determined to file away every snow-bent pine in her eyes.

On the way back, she pressed her forehead to the window too. At first.

But the window glass was too honest. It reflected, with perfect clarity, the corner of her mouth that refused to stop curving upward.

She stared at her own reflection, abruptly overcome with embarrassment — so she closed her eyes and pretended to sleep.

The car swayed once. She tipped, conveniently, sideways. Her shoulder landed on Baek Si-on’s shoulder. It swayed again, and she gradually burrowed into the crook of his arm, found the most comfortable angle, and curled up.

Baek Si-on looked down at her. Eyes closed, breathing even, rhythm steady — complete with several micro-expressions of jolted awake, then slept on, timed to the road’s bumps.

Her acting had improved. It had not yet improved enough to fool a Venice Best Actor.

He didn’t expose her. He simply drew his hand out of his pocket and settled it on her shoulder, so she could lean more securely.

In the front passenger seat, one bodyguard studied his radio. The other stared out the window with total commitment, as though a polar bear might charge out of the snowfield at any moment demanding an autograph. The professional standards of both men reached, in this moment, their personal all-time peak.

Pierre drove, glancing at the mirror now and then. He looked at the girl curled into Baek Si-on’s side, then at Baek Si-on’s still-unremarkable face, and couldn’t help muttering in English:

“Sir — a suggestion, if I may.”

“Go ahead.”

“Next time you bring a girlfriend for the aurora — choose Yellowknife. Higher odds of the lights, glass-domed viewing cabins, no freezing outside, and no polar bears.”

Baek Si-on considered it.

“Why didn’t you recommend it this time?”

Pierre’s eyes found him in the mirror, perfectly at peace.

“Because Churchill’s hosting fees are pricier.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Wonderful.

The honesty of capitalism is, and will always be, colder than the wind on the Canadian frontier.

❧ ❧ ❧

Churchill airport. Pierre finished with the luggage and shook Baek Si-on’s hand.

“Have a safe trip.”

He glanced at Choi Jin-ri, who was arranging her scarf nearby, and added, more quietly:

“And may the clover keep working.”

Baek Si-on nodded his thanks and turned toward the gate. Choi Jin-ri hurried up alongside. Two steps in, her hand crept over and, through the thick of both gloves, took his hand.

He looked down at it. Didn’t pull away. Closed his fingers back around hers.

Choi Jin-ri smiled — a bigger one than the window had caught. But this time she wasn’t embarrassed.

Hand in hand, stepping on packed snow, they walked up the airstair one step at a time. Behind them lay Churchill’s flat white horizon; overhead, the sky was turning a morning gray-blue. The aurora had long since scattered.

But something, it seemed, was planning to outlast it.

❧ ❧ ❧

The flight was quiet. Choi Jin-ri was asleep the instant she touched the seat — genuinely, this time. She had been too excited, too cold, and wrung out between an aurora and a kiss; the whole night had run her like a machine on spin cycle. Baek Si-on spread the blanket over her and rested with his eyes closed for a while himself.

As they neared Los Angeles, the flight attendant came down the aisle softly.

“Sir — we’ll be landing in about twenty minutes.”

He opened his eyes and nodded. Beside him, the announcement woke Choi Jin-ri too; she rubbed her eyes and sat up, groggy.

“We’re here?”

“Soon.”

She looked out the window. The Los Angeles coastline was already visible in the distance, sunlight spilling through the cloud seams, the sea almost too bright to look at.

“I’m going to change.”

She took her bag to the lavatory and traded the pajamas for something rated for Los Angeles temperatures. Fixing her collar, her fingertip caught on the thin silver chain at her neck — the green four-leaf clover glowing soft and wet-looking under the cabin light.

She stared at it a long time. Then the deliberation began.

Wear it over the clothes? Too dangerous. Professor Yoon Hye-ja was waiting in Los Angeles. She didn’t think the professor would press hard — but if the question when did you buy this ever came, she did not currently possess an answer that could survive cross-examination.

Take it off and hide it in her bag? Absolutely not. This was a keepsake of the heart! He hadn’t made it official — but this kind of thing, a girl is entitled to declare unilaterally.

Declared, and it is in force.

After much struggle she tucked the necklace inside her collar — deliberately leaving one short segment of silver chain visible.

❝

Not hidden, not shown — Schrödinger’s necklace. You say it’s there, it’s there. You say it isn’t, you simply haven’t been looking carefully enough.

— an airplane mirror, somewhere over the Pacific

She checked the mirror. Satisfied. A secret, plus a small ambition of being discovered. Entirely reasonable.

❧ ❧ ❧

On the ground, the convoy dropped Choi Jin-ri at the Four Seasons first. The car stopped at the door and she lingered, in no hurry to get out.

“I have work.”

“…Oh.”

“Dinner together tonight?”

Her eyes bent into crescents at once.

“Okay~”

She pushed the door open, stood at the hotel entrance, and waved at the car. Baek Si-on nodded. The car pulled away slowly, and she watched it until it turned the corner and disappeared completely before she went inside.

In the elevator, her expression management held. In the corridor, still holding. Card into the room lock. Door shut.

The next second, she dove face-first into the bed and kicked both legs against the mattress like a pedal drum.

“AAAAAAHHH!!!”

The pillow swallowed most of it — from the hallway it would have sounded like a muffled wooo. She rolled over, rolled again, and renovated the hotel’s immaculate bed into a disaster zone.

Not enough. This degree of celebration was not nearly enough. She needed a fellow human to receive the joy now overflowing her container, or she was liable to detonate on the spot from unused emotion.

Choi Jin-ri sat up, snatched her phone, and dialed Hara.

Choi Jin-ri → Goo Hara · video · the debrief · the disaster zone formerly known as a hotel bed

Hara’s face filled the screen — makeup off, mid-skincare. One look at Choi Jin-ri’s expression, and the cotton pad stopped moving.

Ya. Choi Jin-ri. Looks like you didn’t just pass Professor Yoon’s interview — you took home the top-tier offer.

Kissed?

Choi Jin-ri buried her face in both hands. Two seconds passed. Then, from behind the fingers: one heavy, deliberate nod.

Hara slapped the table.

Ya! Choi Jin-ri! Full report, not one word missing!

I was going to tell it with some restraint…

Restraint lasted one sentence. She ran from the snowball fight to the clover, from the clover to the necklace, from the necklace to the glove over her mouth — and at “sausage-mouth,” Hara sprayed a mouthful of water clean across her room.

Hold on. Hold on.

Hara wiped her mouth.

So you worked up the courage to kiss him — and he taught you a lecture on low-temperature physics first?

Eonni, stop laughing.

I’m not laughing.

Hara inhaled, deeply.

Then laughed again.

Sorry. Can’t help it.

Choi Jin-ri glared. Hara waved both hands in surrender.

Okay, okay, I’m listening seriously. Then what?

Then he moved his hand away, and said — a thing like this shouldn’t make the girl go first.

And then… we… we kissed.

At which point she buried her face in the pillow all over again. The laughter on Hara’s face drained out slowly. She clicked her tongue.

Baek Si-on, that man — idle, he looks like a money-making machine with the feelings removed. The moment he actually moves, he’s a straight-up heart-arsonist. Jin-ri-ya, this lifetime of yours is planted in his hands.

No rebuttal came. It sounded like a curse, but her current state was, honestly, closer to willingly buried. And then, as the thrill and the blush receded, first-love rookie anxiety came creeping up over the ridge. She flattened her grin, set her chin on the edge of the pillow, and looked into the screen.

But, Eonni.

Mm?

We haven’t confirmed anything. He never said he likes me, never said what we are. We just… kissed.

Hara looked at the girl marinating in her own head, and sighed, helplessly fond.

What decade-old script is your brain running?

Huh?

He dropped a stack of trips that were real money on the table, flew across half a continent in the middle of the night, and stood in the Canadian cold with you. That already outweighs any dry little confession.

She patted toner into her cheek, unhurried.

Be happy. Some relationships should be allowed to grow a little before you laminate them with labels. Safer that way.

Choi Jin-ri looked down at the small clover at her chest. After a few seconds she nodded, softly.

Mm.

Hara’s normal tone snapped back into place.

Of course — if he dares play amnesia on you, you tell me.

What would you do?

I’ll fly to Los Angeles and slap that feeling-free money-machine face of his awake.

Choi Jin-ri finally burst out laughing again.

He won’t.

Tch. Look at you, defending him already.

I’m not!

Sure. Not.

After hanging up, she hugged the pillow and rolled one more lap across the ruins. Then she fished the clover necklace back out of her collar, held it up to eye level, and issued a formal proclamation:

Choi Jin-ri · official designation, entered into the record this day

“From today on, you are Exhibit No. 1.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Elsewhere. Los Angeles, a law office in Century City.

Baek Si-on sat in a conference room, signing the BodyArmor contract.

Across the table sat Mike Repole, the brand’s founder — a veteran of the American drinks business, visibly high-voltage, talking fast, eyes lighting up over shelf space and distribution like a man who had just wrestled an entire aisle of premium real estate out of a supermarket’s cold dead hands.

The terms had been essentially settled.

BodyArmor × Baek Si-on · the term sheet, as signed in Century City

Original equity: 5%, founder tier — dry stock, from the source.

Asia rights: joint-venture authority for the brand across the entire region.

Structure, per the founder: US side keeps the core concentrate and the formula standard. Water, bottles, filling lines, warehousing, convenience chains, supermarket channels — all of it local, through an Asian partner of his choosing. Unit cost floor: down at least two-thirds. Bottle frontage: one Kobe, guaranteed to move product.

“What do the JV rights mean, exactly?”

Baek Si-on tapped the contract. Mike Repole had visibly been waiting for the question:

“Simply put — we let you find a local partner in Asia, stand up a joint company, and let it run production, bottling, distribution, and sales.”

He understood — and saw the problem at once.

“Imported retail, with tariffs, transpacific freight, cold chain, and channel markups stacked on top? It doesn’t move.”

Mike nodded.

“Exactly. So the core clause is the local-bottling license. You find a consortium with mature drink lines and retail reach, and the cost per bottle drops by at least two-thirds. And with your face and Kobe’s on the bottle, I’d bet the young Asian consumer tries it.”

Baek Si-on looked down at the contract. The structure was genuinely interesting. Channels, pricing, and partners for the whole Asian market would sit in his own hands. If it worked, sales participation on top. If it didn’t, he still held five percent of the company from birth.

The car wasn’t luxurious yet — the navigation might not even work properly. But the driver was Kobe. That alone would make plenty of people willing to take a seat.

He signed the signature page. Mike signed. Contracts exchanged. Handshakes all around.

“Welcome to BodyArmor, Mr. Baek.”

“To a pleasant partnership.”

“It’s going to be a very pleasant one.”

Leaving the law office, Scooter finally switched topics to the thing that mattered.

“Drinks, parked. Now let’s discuss the thing that’s actually going to make me pull my hair out.”

“Which is?”

“The North American tour.”

He turned his tablet around.

“I announced yesterday that you’re prepping a proper tour. As of this morning, fourteen cities have sent formal invitations. Six are offering venue subsidies, three will shave part of their entertainment tax, and two want to fold you into city-marketing campaigns.”

Baek Si-on was a little surprised.

“Subsidies that generous?”

American states guarded tax and venue fees with a jealous grip. Entertainment-tax relief for a foreign singer was not a common concession.

Scooter shrugged.

“An audience that flies across state lines, books hotels, buys merch, checks out restaurants, posts everything, and carries your city name up the trending list while they’re at it — that’s worth more than any tourism campaign a board could buy.”

Baek Si-on looked at the tablet’s dense list of city names — New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, San Francisco, Toronto, Vancouver, Seattle, Atlanta, Houston — every one trailing a string of figures: fees, subsidies, venue capacities, draft dates.

The North American tour · invitation ledger, day one

Formal invitations: 14 cities and counting.

Venue subsidies: 6. Entertainment-tax relief: 3. City-marketing tie-ins: 2.

Each city name trails its own bribe — money, tax breaks, capacity, dates. Reading of the record: he is not touring as a concert. He is touring as short-term tourism revenue with a microphone.

“You wouldn’t just be bringing a concert. You’d be bringing the tourism income with it.”

“So I’m a mobile economic stimulus package?”

“More or less.”

Baek Si-on was silent for two seconds.

“Then they should present me with a commendation banner.”

Scooter blinked.

“A what?”

Baek Si-on thought about how to translate it.

“A silk banner with gold tassels. East Asian governments award them instead of money. It says ‘champion of the people’s hearts’ on it.”

Scooter: “……”

He looked back down at the cities offering very real, very un-silk sums.

“I’m gonna stick with the American version, thanks.”

“Cash.”
