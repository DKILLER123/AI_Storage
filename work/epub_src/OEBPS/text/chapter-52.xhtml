Chapter Fifty-Two

Baek Si-on, Used as Firewood for Reform

When Baek Si-on left, he didn't ask Son Nam-won who the anonymous source behind the debt-collection story was.

Not because he didn't care.

Because it was the rule of the trade.

A journalist protects a source the way a lawyer protects a client. It's a given. You ask and you won't get an answer anyway — you'd only look like you didn't know how the game was played.

And besides, he was here to talk business.

Whatever needed digging, there'd be time to dig later.

✿ ✿ ✿

July 25 Late evening Cheongdam-dong, the alley outside the restaurant A phone that kept buzzing, and a decision

The wooden partition slid shut. Beyond the glass door at the end of the corridor was the Cheongdam-dong night.

He pushed the glass door of the restaurant open, and the late-July heat hit him in the face.

He walked a few steps down the alley toward the main road, then stopped, hit by something he'd almost forgotten, and pulled out his phone.

Several unread messages were waiting on KakaoTalk.

Three voice messages from Baek Eun-ah. He didn't tap them.

The very bottom one was from Kim Jae-kyung.

It had arrived at 9:12 that evening — while he'd been in the room with Son Nam-won. He hadn't looked at it since.

He opened it.

Kim Jae-kyung

Kim Jae-kyung

Dress?

Kim Jae-kyung

Men's or women's?

Kim Jae-kyung

How soon do you need it? Handmade work can't be rushed.

9:12 p.m. · unread

Three messages, spaced about a minute apart. That told him she hadn't typed them in one burst; she'd been composing, deleting, composing again, thinking as she went.

Baek Si-on stood under the streetlight, thumb hovering over the input box, and typed.

Kim Jae-kyung

Baek Si-on

Both. A month.

Kim Jae-kyung

Okay, sunbae-nim. Come to my dorm tomorrow for measurements, I'm here after three.

Baek Si-on

Good.

9:33 p.m. · sent

Kim Jae-kyung was a night owl too, clearly. The reply came in under fifteen seconds.

✿ ✿ ✿

July 26 8:20 a.m. Yeonnam-dong, Baek Si-on's flat A door unlocked, and a storm in sneakers

The next day.

8:20 in the morning.

Baek Si-on's door lock turned from the outside.

“Cousin!”

The voice cut straight through the flat from the entryway and slammed into the bedroom.

Baek Si-on rolled over and dragged the blanket over his head.

Footsteps, closing in. The bedroom door shoved open, the sound of it smacking the wall arriving at the exact same moment as Baek Eun-ah's voice.

“Why didn't you answer me last night?! Three voice messages! Not one heard!”

“……I didn't see them.”

Eun-ah stood at the foot of the bed and stared at the lump of duvet for three seconds.

“Hmph.”

Then she pulled out her phone and pressed it flat against the duvet, right beside the bump that was his head.

“Fine, don't answer. But you have to look at this.”

“……What?”

“Melon put out an announcement.”

Baek Si-on's eyes weren't even fully open yet, and his hand was already reaching out.

Melon · official

Platform notice · 26 Jul 2014

Regarding the results of the investigation into recent music-source data controversy, and the announcement of platform mechanism adjustments

I. On the playback-data controversy surrounding singer Baek Si-on's digital single “Way Back Home”:

After a line-by-line audit by the Melon data-security team of the full playback logs covering the contested window (23:00 on 21 July to 07:00 on 22 July), the findings are as follows. The playback increase for “Way Back Home” during this period was primarily driven by return traffic from overseas IP addresses, and this aligns closely with the propagation routes on overseas platforms such as YouTube and SoundCloud. After comparing device IDs, account registration information, and playback behaviour patterns, no evidence was found of bot inflation, zombie-account loop playback, or any other form of artificially manipulated data. The playback data for this song is genuine and valid. Melon hereby apologises to singer Baek Si-on and to the wider user base for the information lag caused by the investigation period.

II. Regarding adjustments to the platform charting mechanism:

One. The current real-time chart is abolished. Two. A new 24-hour daily chart is established, calculated on cumulative playback from 00:00 of the current day to 00:00 of the next, updated once each day at 10:00. Three. The weighting of the new chart will incorporate play count, unique listeners, and completion rate simultaneously, so as to more accurately reflect a song's real audience size. This adjustment is intended to provide a fairer competitive environment for all musicians. We thank the users for their understanding and support.

— Melon Operations Team

Baek Si-on finished reading the first section, and his thumb slipped further down the screen.

He put the phone down.

Leaned back against the headboard.

Eun-ah watched his face, trying to find an emotion on it worth reading.

“Cousin?”

“Mm.”

“You're cleared! Melon said the data's fine! You're innocent!”

“Mm.”

“Are you just going to keep going 'mm, mm'? Can't you at least react?”

Baek Si-on ignored her.

He was thinking about something else.

The chart-inflation scandal his own song had stirred up had become the spark that pushed Melon into reform.

The problem of the real-time chart being held hostage by fandom was something the platform knew better than anyone. But touching that cake meant touching the interests of every agency. Half of an idol group's music-source results came from fans streaming for them. You take away the real-time chart, and SM, YG, JYP would be the first to push back.

So Melon had held the clarification for days — not because it was waiting to see which way the wind blew, but because it was waiting for the fire to burn big enough. If the fire wasn't big enough, the reform had no legitimacy.

He had been the firewood.

That, actually, he didn't mind.

A tool, then. Fine. So long as the result was good, so long as the clean name came back and the chart was fixed and no one else would ever be tagged with "chart fraud" for an odd spike in the middle of the night.

But there was one other thing that stung.

Last night he'd ground his back teeth and called Lee Ji-eun "sunbae," begging her to push LOEN to put out a clarification quickly.

Judging by the wording of this announcement, the rigour of the investigation, the completeness of the reform plan — none of it had been squeezed out by one phone call last night.

Which meant.

Even if he hadn't made that call, the announcement would have gone out this morning all the same.

That "sunbae" had been wasted.

The thought landed, and Baek Si-on set the phone on the nightstand, and buried his face in the blanket.

“What are you even thinking about, cousin?”

Eun-ah squatted by the bed frame, both hands resting on the edge, chin propped on the backs of her hands, the whole picture of a dog waiting for a treat over a fence.

“Thinking about what breakfast my mother left me.”

“……”

Eun-ah took a deep breath, swallowed every sentence she'd been about to say, and got up to go heat food in the kitchen.

✿ ✿ ✿

July 26 Morning Yeonnam-dong, the kitchen Ribs, egg rolls, and a change of plans

On the kitchen table sat a breakfast wrapped in cling film. Pork rib soup, a rolled omelette, a plate of seasoned spinach, two bowls of rice.

Five minutes in the microwave.

The two of them sat down and ate.

Eun-ah sat across from him, poking at her omelette with her chopsticks, scrolling her phone and reading out the comments — the ones, now that the tide had turned, speaking up in support of her cousin.

Baek Si-on listened to a few lines, then, out of nowhere, said:

“Get hold of Choi Jin-ri. Three o'clock, we're going for measurements.”

“Measurements? Measurements for what?”

“The dress.”

“The dress?”

Eun-ah blinked twice. Then it hit her.

“……Oh!”

The Venice Film Festival!

“But… Jin-ri eonni is being shelved by her company.”

Baek Si-on picked up a piece of omelette and put it in his mouth.

“It'll be lifted.”

Eun-ah really wanted to press, how will it be lifted, but a part of her already knew the answer wouldn't come.

So she just opened KakaoTalk and found Choi Jin-ri's thread.

“Three o'clock, right?”

“Mm.”

Eun-ah's thumbs went fast.

“Where?”

“Hongdae. Kim Jae-kyung's dorm.”

“Kim Jae-kyung? The one from Rainbow?”

“Mm.”

Eun-ah looked down and kept typing.

Choi Jin-ri (Sulli)

Baek Eun-ah

Jin-ri eonni, are you free at three? My cousin wants to meet you.

sent

She stared at what she'd written for a second.

The phrasing felt a little odd.

Ah, whatever.

Sent's sent.
