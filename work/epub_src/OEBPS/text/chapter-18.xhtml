Chapter Eighteen

The Diving Header Smashes Out 360 Million

June 14 3:30 a.m. Hongdae, the open-air tent An empty screen, a drunk crowd

June 14th, the wee hours. An open-air pojangmacha tent outside Hongdae. The early-summer night wind was shredded by a couple of roaring industrial fans. A fifty-inch TV hung on the wall, showing the 2014 World Cup group stage — the Netherlands against Spain.

Seven or eight plastic tables sat in front of the screen, every one full. At the next table a shirtless middle-aged man knocked back a beer and bellowed loud enough for the whole street to hear.

“Ssibal, the defending champs play a group match? That's a beating. I put twenty thousand on Spain with a one-goal handicap.”

Someone at his table agreed.

“Solid.”

Eun-ah went pale. She turned to look at Si-on. Her cousin was eating cold noodles, lifting the chopsticks, feeding himself, chewing with focus.

“Cousin… you're really not nervous?”

Si-on looked up at her.

“The noodles are good. Want a bite?”

Eun-ah decided not to speak. Han-teuk was busy talking himself down. Twenty thousand won — if he lost it, he could call it buying Mr. Baek a meal —

“GOAL!!!”

The tent exploded. Xabi Alonso had scored the penalty. 1–0, Spain ahead. The shirtless man waved his beer bottle and howled.

“See?! The champions are the champions!”

Eun-ah covered her face. Han-teuk gritted his teeth.

“It's fine, only twenty minutes in…”

He glanced at Si-on. Si-on finished the last of his cold noodles, tipped the bowl to drink the broth, set it down, and raised a hand for the waiter.

“Another mixed cold noodles, extra sauce.”

Eun-ah: “…”. Han-teuk: “…”.

Minute forty-four. Daley Blind whipped in a cross from the left. Van Persie started his run. He didn't jump. He launched. His whole body levelled out, parallel to the turf, like a javelin hurled at the goal, and he buried his head in the ball. It looped over Casaillas's fingertips and dropped into the net.

The tent went quiet for three whole seconds. Then everyone spoke at once, but nobody finished a sentence.

All swearing, all exclamations, and the scrape of chair legs on concrete. 1–1. The shirtless man at the next table had his beer bottle frozen mid-lift, forgotten. Han-teuk pulled the ticket from his pocket, looked at it, folded it, put it back. Then pulled it out and looked again. Eun-ah was gripping Si-on's sleeve, nails digging into the fabric. Si-on looked down at his own sleeve. Said nothing.

Then the match turned into a slaughter. Eight minutes into the second half, Robben tore through the whole backline and blasted into the far corner. 2–1. Nine minutes later, a scramble off a corner, a header. 3–1. The shirtless man had gone quiet. One of his friends lit a cigarette; it took two tries. Eight more minutes, Van Persie stole the ball and tapped it into an empty net. 4–1. Eight minutes after that, Robben again. 5–1.

Full time. The tent was as silent as a funeral home. Han-teuk pinched his own thigh. Ouch. Pinched again. Still ouch. He held the ticket up to the bare bulb and turned it over and over. The shirtless man turned and looked at the paper lit through from behind.

A slaughter. The champs were the champs — until they weren’t.

“You… bet the Netherlands?”

Han-teuk nodded mechanically. The man picked up his bottle to drink, found it empty, and set it back down. Eun-ah grabbed Si-on's arm.

“Cousin! Costa Rica plays tomorrow, right?!”

“Mm. Four in the morning.”

“If that wins too—”

She stopped herself before daring to finish. Si-on finished it for her.

“Three hundred sixty million.”

Eun-ah let go and leaned back, staring at the wobbly bulb overhead, not speaking. Han-teuk stood up suddenly, chair legs scraping the concrete. Si-on looked up.

“Where are you going?”

“To church.”

“?”

“Pray. Make a wish. Burn incense. Whatever — I have to do something.”

He strode out. After a few steps he turned back, grabbed the remaining beer off the table, drained it standing, slammed the bottle down, and walked off again. Eun-ah watched him vanish into the mouth of the alley.

“Cousin, I think he's lost it.”

“It's fine. He needs time to process.”

✿ ✿ ✿
June 15 3:30 a.m. The same tent The morning of the match

The next day, 3:30 in the morning. The same tent. The place was nearly empty — two or three tables of stragglers. Costa Rica vs Uruguay kicked off at four, and with neither side a title favourite, not many had the conviction to stay up. When Si-on walked in with a dark-eyed, sleep-deprived Eun-ah, Han-teuk was already sitting there. Si-on gave him a longer look. Black clothes, high collar, a cross pinned to the collar front.

“The outfit—”

“Borrowed from the church. Feels safer.”

“…”

Eun-ah dropped into her seat and slammed her head onto the table, making the surface thump.

“God, I'm so sleepy. If we lose, cousin, you're carrying me home.”

“Fine.”

Si-on turned and called the waiter.

“A cold noodles with an egg, and a plate of dumplings.”

Eun-ah: “… You can eat this?”

“Breakfast.”

“…”

The match began. It was dull. Both sides passed it back and forth in midfield, and Eun-ah's head started dropping toward the table again.

“Don't sleep.”

Si-on gave her a nudge.

“I'm not sleeping… I'm just closing my eyes for a second…”

Minute twenty-three. Uruguay won a penalty. Cavani converted. 1–0. Eun-ah sat bolt upright and looked at Si-on. Si-on dipped a dumpling in sauce and put it in his mouth.

“Seventy minutes left.”

Han-teuk pressed the ticket between his palms, shut his eyes, and his lips started moving. Si-on leaned in to listen.

“…Hail Mary, full of grace…”

He was actually praying. Half-time, still 1–0. Eun-ah slumped over the table, refusing to look. Han-teuk kept whispering. Eight minutes into the second half, Costa Rica won the ball high up the pitch, Campbell took it on the left and let fly from distance — skimming the turf into the far corner. 1–1.

Han-teuk's mouth stopped. Four minutes later, a corner, a header at the back post, off the underside of the bar and in. 2–1, ahead. Han-teuk pressed his hands together and his mouth started again, three times faster. Si-on listened. No — that wasn't Hail Mary anymore.

“Amitabha Amitabha Amitabha…”

“Weren't you just praying?”

“Double insurance!”

“…”

Minute eighty-three. Costa Rica countered, three on two, slid it to the middle and tapped it into the empty net. 3–1. The whistle went. On the television, the Costa Rica players were hugging each other, kneeling on the grass. The hero Campbell pointed to the sky with both hands.

The run no one saw coming. “Double insurance,” Han-teuk called it.

Eun-ah flung her arms around Si-on's and shook it.

“Cousin! Cousin cousin cousin cousin—!!”

“Mm.”

“Three hundred sixty million! Three hundred sixty, right?!”

“Mm.”

“AAAAAAAH!”

When she'd finished, Han-teuk finally got a word in. He looked at Si-on, wanting to ask so much. How did you know. Are you even human. Should I believe in Buddha or in you. But what came out was:

“…Where to next?”

Si-on stood, pulled the wad of tickets from his pocket, knocked them straight against the table.

“Cash them in.”

He turned and called toward the counter.

“Could you keep an eye on the table? We'll be back to settle up.”

The waiter nodded mechanically. It was only after the three of them had gone, swallowed by the mouth of the Hongdae alley in the small hours, that he caught on.

Settle up? Settle what up?
