Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Three

One Screen, Three Rules of Love

August 3 · the premiere · the photo call

Two arms linked · one scrapped fisherman script

COEX Megabox · the step-and-repeat

A few months ago, on seeing a pretty girl quietly watching her boyfriend, Choi Jin-ri would have gone home and flipped through a whole night of news, and on top of that studied every public interview Bae Joo-hyun had given since debut.

But now she was oddly calm.

The reason was simple.

The LAVUE lip-gloss endorsement on Bae Joo-hyun’s lips — one word from her and Baek Si-on would withdraw it without a second’s hesitation.

That privilege was like an insurance policy with her name written on it.

But Choi Jin-ri also knew this:

Favor is fine, privilege is fine — confirming it once in a while is security.

She would not, on the strength of Baek Si-on’s indulgence, treat that indulgence as a chip she could overdraw without limit.

So compared with the pretty junior sister off there still watching Baek Si-on’s back, the person more worth figuring out right now was the good unnie beside her.

Why had she suddenly warned her to watch Bae Joo-hyun?

And what reaction did she want her to have?

Lee Ji-eun was still waiting.

Waiting for Choi Jin-ri to show tension, wariness, or at least a thread of unease.

What she got was Choi Jin-ri narrowing her eyes slightly, looking toward the five girls for a few seconds, then turning her face:

“I’m nearsighted.”

Lee Ji-eun’s wait was cut off by a line entirely outside the plan.

“What?”

Choi Jin-ri looked that way again, squinting harder.

“Too far to see clearly. Who is that?”

Lee Ji-eun fell silent.

At roughly the same distance, Choi Jin-ri had just now picked Baek Si-on out of dozens of people by the railing in a single glance.

Now swap in Bae Joo-hyun, and suddenly she was nearsighted.

Selective optics · as measured

Vision for Baek Si-on: 1.5.

Vision for Bae Joo-hyun: 0.3.

Selective myopia.

Lee Ji-eun went along with it and introduced her anyway:

“The endorser for Baek Si-on’s lip-gloss brand, and your company’s junior sister.”

Choi Jin-ri put on a look of sudden understanding.

“Oh, Irene.”

And that was the end of it.

The fisherman script in Lee Ji-eun’s head was scrapped on the spot.

The full plot she had preset was:

The fisherman script · storyboarded by Lee Ji-eun

Choi Jin-ri notices Bae Joo-hyun watching Baek Si-on → develops crisis awareness → starts guarding the pretty junior from the same company → the two women check each other → she, the good unnie who warned her early, sits to the side and reaps the fisherman’s profit.

Step one, jammed.

Choi Jin-ri simply hadn’t taken the bait.

And that calm couldn’t come from dullness, either.

Sensitive, this peach could read a complete emotional trajectory out of one down jacket, one bouquet, and one form of address.

She just didn’t care.

Or, more precisely: for now she did not regard Bae Joo-hyun as a competitor dangerous enough to matter.

Where the confidence came from, Lee Ji-eun couldn’t tell.

But she knew she had miscalculated.

Just as she was about to reset her strategy, a staffer on site walked over.

“Sulli-ssi, IU-ssi, you’re both up for photos shortly.”

The staffer checked the list, then asked:

“Will the two of you shoot together or separately? If separately, per the schedule, Sulli-ssi goes first.”

The order at a premiere backdrop follows no absolute rule.

Seniority, standing, arrival time, and the organizer’s running order all exert influence.

But in the entertainment press’s lenses, whoever comes out later is more easily read as the headliner, or the bigger name.

Lee Ji-eun didn’t want to provoke Choi Jin-ri over something this small.

Even less did she want the “righteous unnie who blocks peach blossoms for you” persona she had just built to be torn down by a photo order.

“Together.”

Choi Jin-ri turned her face.

Lee Ji-eun took her arm, smiling quite naturally.

“Let’s go.”

Choi Jin-ri didn’t refuse.

“Good.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The two walked to the backdrop arm in arm.

The reporters clearly hadn’t expected this pairing.

After a brief stunned beat, the shutter sound grew dense enough to blur into white noise.

Press pit · the photo call

IU-ssi, look to the middle!

Sulli-ssi, a little closer!

Can the two of you make a heart?

Lee Ji-eun and Choi Jin-ri glanced at each other, then at the same moment raised the hands nearest the center, each forming half a heart.

Their fingers met in the exact middle to complete the shape.

The media zone erupted at once.

The shutter sound climbed another gear.

❧ ❧ ❧

The fan zone suddenly broke into screams louder than before.

Hearing the shift in the wave of sound, Baek Si-on lifted his head and followed the fans’ line of sight over.

Two people stood at the backdrop.

Lee Ji-eun wore a pale pink dress with a light white cropped jacket thrown over it.

The whole look kept that fresh, petite feel she carried; her smile was very sweet.

But Baek Si-on had seen her picking things apart in the recording room, keeping ledgers, and making people re-record twenty times — the fragile-sweet-little-sister image, on her, counted as limited-hours service.

Choi Jin-ri was an entirely different style.

Tall, well-proportioned, she didn’t need to pose at the backdrop to carry the relaxed ease of a magazine pictorial.

One sweet.

One striking.

Standing shoulder to shoulder, neither really overshadowed the other.

Beautiful, each in her own way.

Baek Si-on’s gaze rested for a moment, then came back.

The fan in front of him, though, was still on tiptoe craning toward the backdrop, phone raised to shoot.

Fan zone · overheard

So pretty!

They made a heart together!

Quick, shoot, shoot!

Baek Si-on set his pen down and quietly scanned the fans behind the railing.

The fan at the very front read it first.

She immediately pulled her phone back from the backdrop and obediently aimed it at Baek Si-on.

“Oppa, I’m looking at you!”

The few beside her caught on too.

“Only oppa!”

“Oppa is the coolest!”

“We weren’t distracted!”

Only then did Baek Si-on lower his head to sign again.

❧ ❧ ❧

The same scene landed in Bae Joo-hyun’s eyes as well.

The whole process was short, yet somehow a little childish.

A superstar standing at the center of the world suddenly finds his fans’ attention stolen by two pretty women.

So he silently demands of them: look at me.

Bae Joo-hyun couldn’t hold it in.

“Haha.”

The laugh arrived with no warning.

Four heads around her turned at once.

Kang Seul-gi asked first:

“Unnie, what are you laughing at?”

Bae Joo-hyun was still sunk in that little contrast from a moment ago, and for a beat didn’t realize the members hadn’t seen the same thing at all.

“Didn’t you see it?”

Kang Seul-gi grew more baffled:

“See what?”

Kim Yerim guessed:

“Ji-eun sunbae and Sulli sunbae making a heart together?”

The words at Bae Joo-hyun’s lips slammed to a halt.

If she explained one sentence more, she would expose the fact that ever since entering the hall she had been secretly keeping track of Baek Si-on.

“……Nothing.”

Kang Seul-gi wasn’t reassured.

“Really nothing?”

“Mm, I just suddenly thought of something funny.”

The explanation was thoroughly perfunctory.

So perfunctory that Park Soo-young’s gossip radar had already started working.

“What thing?”

“Forgot.”

“You just laughed and already forgot?”

“It was very funny, so I forgot fast.”

Park Soo-young: “……”

That chain of causation clearly hadn’t survived any serious scrutiny by their leader.

Kang Seul-gi turned to Son Seung-wan, trying to get a different answer from the most reliable main vocal in the group.

Son Seung-wan was the only one in the group who knew for certain about Bae Joo-hyun’s feelings toward Baek Si-on.

That laugh from the captain just now was, nine times out of ten, about Baek Si-on.

But Son Seung-wan was not about to confirm it out loud.

Curiosity also needs to stay alive to be satisfied.

So the instant an inquiring look surfaced on Kang Seul-gi’s face, Son Seung-wan had already arranged on her own features the exact same bewilderment.

❧ ❧ ❧

After finishing their photos, Lee Ji-eun and Choi Jin-ri stepped down the stairs in high heels, guided by staff.

Still arm in arm, they were ushered into the theater.

Outside, the photo flow continued.

A few more groups of guests passed.

A staffer called out:

“Red Velvet, please prepare.”

The four who had been murmuring about why Bae Joo-hyun suddenly laughed went quiet at once.

The manager quickly checked their outfits.

Skirt hems.

Shoelaces.

Earrings.

No problems.

“Let’s go.”

The five girls followed the staffer into the photo area.

Their styling today carried on the fruit concept the BodyArmor ad had established.

At a film premiere where most guests chose black, white, gray, or dark colors, their sudden appearance brightened the whole photo zone.

❧ ❧ ❧

Photos done, the five stepped down.

A staffer reminded them to watch the cables underfoot so they wouldn’t trip, then pointed them toward the theater entrance.

The necessary route happened to pass the area where Baek Si-on was signing for fans.

The distance closed.

Bae Joo-hyun deliberately let her heels click out crisp sounds.

But he still had his head down.

The pen tip crossed the poster.

Name finished, poster handed back to the fan, the next poster already passed over.

Bae Joo-hyun walked past him from behind, and until the theater entrance blocked the fan zone from view, not one greeting came from behind her.

The smile at her lips held perfectly; her teeth, though, clenched lightly once.

This man, when working, truly would not deviate from what he was doing for anyone.

She had witnessed it before when visiting the set.

But knowing is one thing.

Actually walking past him in clothes she had carefully prepared, and being utterly ignored, still pressed tight against her chest.

“Unnie, this way.”

Kang Seul-gi called from ahead.

Bae Joo-hyun answered, and pressed the disappointment down beneath her smile.

This debt she decided to record for now.

The bad boy’s list of crimes gained one more count.

❧ ❧ ❧

Outside, the guests had basically all arrived.

The premiere’s person-in-charge reminded over the walkie-talkie:

Walkie · house channel

Pre-screening greetings in five minutes. Creative team please enter as soon as possible.

Baek Eun-ah heard it and headed for the fan railing.

There was still a short queue in front of Baek Si-on.

“No more signing — the director and Song Kang-ho sunbae are already inside.”

The words landed.

Behind the railing, the few fans who hadn’t reached the front instantly shot her aggrieved looks.

That grievance needed no translation at all.

So close!

It was literally so close!

And this manager of all people had to come drag oppa away!

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

She was plainly trying to keep the whole premiere on schedule.

How had she become, in a blink, the wicked manager who tears idols and fans apart?

Baek Si-on finished signing the last poster in his hand and capped the pen.

“The screening is starting soon — I need to head in.”

The fans behind, though disappointed, also knew a film’s schedule couldn’t be delayed.

Baek Si-on went on:

“After release, the creative team will attend twelve theater stage greetings. Specific dates and ticket times, the distributor will post on the official site. Anyone who didn’t get a signature today can follow the later schedules.”

Someone in the crowd cried out, wronged:

“What if we can’t grab tickets?”

Baek Si-on tilted his head.

“Sales haven’t opened and you’ve already decided you can’t grab them?”

“Oppa’s tickets are hard to grab right now.”

A chorus of agreement rose behind the railing.

Movie tickets, concert tickets, public-event passes — anything tied to Baek Si-on tended not to keep a sales page alive for long.

The fans possessed rich experience of failure on this front.

Baek Si-on thought for a moment:

“If you can’t get into any of the twelve…”

Behind the railing went instantly quiet.

Everyone waited for the rest.

“After the next album releases, I’ll consider holding a dedicated fansign.”

Behind the railing boiled over at once.

“Really?”

“Oppa, no taking it back!”

“We already recorded it!”

Baek Si-on glanced at the phones already raised.

“Which is why I said ‘consider.’”

The fans didn’t care at all.

In their dictionary, “consider” equals “agreed,” and “agreed” equals “event in preparation.”

“Event in preparation” equals: they can start researching fansign outfits now, then go meet their idol looking perfect.

Someone even began shouting:

“When’s the next album coming out?”

“The songs aren’t written yet.”

“Then oppa, go write them quick!”

Baek Si-on: “……”

A moment ago they couldn’t bear for him to leave.

Now, for a fansign, they were already hurrying him back to the studio.

He waved to the fans one last time.

“Thanks for coming today. Be safe on the road.”

The fans answered in unison:

“Ne——”

Only then did he follow Baek Eun-ah toward the theater.

A distance out, Baek Eun-ah couldn’t help saying:

“Why promise a fansign on the spot?”

“I only said consider.”

“They’ve already taken it as real.”

“Then we’ll hold it.”

Baek Eun-ah’s steps paused a beat.

“When?”

“After the next album releases.”

“When is the next album?”

“Don’t know.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

An album with not even a release date, paired with a fansign nobody knew when would happen.

And yet the fans were already happy in advance.

At least she no longer had to be the wicked manager who tears idols and fans apart.

That sense of relief deserved an entry in today’s work log.

❧ ❧ ❧

The screening hall was already full.

The front rows belonged to investors, the distribution company, film-industry guests, and invited artists.

Farther back sat the lucky audience members drawn by lottery to attend the premiere.

The host confirmed the creative team were all seated, then took the stage.

Megabox COEX · pre-screening greetings

Thank you all for joining the premiere of ‘Sado’ despite your busy schedules.

Before the official screening, let us welcome the film’s creative team to the stage for greetings.

Applause rose from the seats.

Lee Joon-ik, Song Kang-ho, Jeon Hye-jin, Moon Geun-young, and Baek Si-on climbed onto the stage from the side in turn.

The host handed the microphone to Lee Joon-ik first.

Lee Joon-ik briefly thanked the guests, mentioned that the film had gone through a long road from prep to shooting to final completion, and hoped the audience could set aside the familiar historical ending for a while and meet the father and son, Yeongjo and Sado, anew.

Song Kang-ho took the microphone, thanked the crew, and invited the audience to understand Yeongjo from the double identity of father and king.

Jeon Hye-jin’s and Moon Geun-young’s remarks were both short.

They offered no advance explanations of their character choices, only hoping the audience would reserve judgment until after the film ended.

Last, the microphone reached Baek Si-on.

He hadn’t yet spoken.

From the back rose a single scream.

“Baek Si-on!”

As if someone had pressed a switch.

Immediately, voices from every direction surged up at once.

“Baek Si-on!!!”

The filmmakers and actors in the front rows turned to look back.

A moment ago it had been a solemn, serious historical-film premiere; the instant Baek Si-on picked up the mic, the hall switched into a concert annex.

Baek Si-on waved once at the audience.

The cheering climbed another level.

He waited a while.

The wave of sound showed no intention of stopping, so he set his index finger lightly against his lips.

“Shh.”

The wave began to lower.

Those still screaming in excitement were nudged by companions and quickly quieted too.

The hall was left with only the faint floor-noise of the stage speakers.

Lee Joon-ik stood off to the side, thoughtfully committing the scene to memory.

If they worked together on the next film, he might consider letting Baek Si-on double as the floor manager.

❧ ❧ ❧

The remarks ended.

The creative team stepped down; the hall’s lights dimmed slowly.

After a brief darkness the screen lit, and the title ‘Sado’ surfaced.

Baek Si-on sat in the creators’ row, feeling it was strange.

While filming he had only known that in a given shot he should be in pain, suppressed, or angry.

But after the edit, one silence from Song Kang-ho, one lowered head from Jeon Hye-jin, joined to half a second of his own hesitation, composed causalities he had never been able to feel whole on set.

The film’s first half showed a son trying to meet his father.

Young Sado kneels in the hall reciting texts.

Boyhood Sado is forced to answer Yeongjo’s examination before the whole court.

Grown Sado stands on the steps in the crown prince’s ceremonial robes, waiting for his father to give him one nod.

But the harder Sado strove to approach the shape his father expected, the farther the distance between the two grew.

Time advanced.

Sado married.

The royal grandson was born.

On screen, the palace interior was bright.

Moon Geun-young’s Lady Hyegyeong held the newborn, staying close beside Sado.

The child was wrapped in soft swaddling, only a small face showing.

Sado knelt and bowed.

Lady Hyegyeong bent low as well, steadying the child in her arms.

‘Sado’ · on the big screen

Father.

There was a rare hint of expectation on Sado’s face.

His first half-life had won no affirmation from Yeongjo, but the child perhaps could.

Sado vaguely hoped this small life could become a new connection between father and son.

But Yeongjo only glanced once, then waved his hand, ordering the child carried away.

The screen gave Baek Si-on a brief close-up.

The propped-up smile was still there; the light in the eyes faded bit by bit.

❧ ❧ ❧

After the child was carried off, Sado raised another matter.

‘Sado’ · on the big screen

Mother’s birthday approaches. This son wishes to prepare a modest feast at the secondary palace.

Yeongjo had not yet responded.

The woman seated at his side spoke first.

Park So-dam’s Royal Noble Consort Mun was already with child, her palm resting lightly on the swell of her belly.

Royal Noble Consort Mun · on the big screen

The palace’s expenses have been tight these years, and Lady Yeong has always been a frugal person.

Now that she has reached the age of preferring quiet, why trouble the whole palace over a single birthday?

Each sentence, taken alone, sounded like concern for the palace budget.

Put together, the meaning was perfectly clear:

A concubine already past her prime and fallen from favor — why hold a birthday feast to remind everyone of what she once had?

Royal Noble Consort Mun ranked below Lady Yeong.

Yet because of Yeongjo’s present favor, she dared to step over the crown prince and openly slight his birth mother.

Sado’s gaze moved to Yeongjo, waiting for his father to stop it.

Yeongjo lifted his teacup and drank.

The silence itself was indulgence.

The last warmth on Sado’s face vanished entirely.

❧ ❧ ❧

The picture snapped over; the score turned urgent and killing.

The price of leaning on favor came swiftly.

On screen, Royal Noble Consort Mun knelt in another palace, her skirts spilled behind her.

Two attendants held her down.

The wooden staff came down hard on her calves.

Crack—!

Royal Noble Consort Mun’s body locked taut; a piercing scream rang through the inner palace.

The second blow followed at once.

Just then the palace doors were pushed open.

Yeongjo stormed in, first ordered the attendants to stop, then demanded of the Grand Queen Dowager why she had punished Royal Noble Consort Mun on her own authority.

The Grand Queen Dowager countered:

Grand Queen Dowager · on the big screen

Today she dares slight Lady Yeong; tomorrow will she dare disregard the queen and the royal law as well?

Yeongjo did not see it as an order problem.

In his view, his mother punishing the woman he favored was a denial of his power as king.

The two quarreled more and more fiercely.

Royal Noble Consort Mun knelt weeping; the attendants pressed their faces to the floor.

In his fury Yeongjo flung out a line:

Yeongjo · on the big screen

If Mother thinks I cannot even manage my own harem, then this throne — I may as well not hold it!

Let the crown prince take it!

He turned and pointed at Sado kneeling outside the hall.

Sado’s face went pale in an instant.

Outside the hall the sky was heavy.

A storm was coming.

❧ ❧ ❧

To ordinary viewers, this stretch showed both the perversity in Yeongjo’s temperament and one more catalyst driving the father-son bond toward ruin.

But Choi Jin-ri’s attention stopped on Lady Yeong.

A woman gives her whole life to a man holding absolute power.

Yet the moment that man withdraws his favor, within the vast system she ranks below even a spoiled young plaything.

Choi Jin-ri suddenly thought of LAVUE.

When Baek Si-on prepared to launch his own cosmetics brand, he had not invited her to endorse it.

At first, she had felt a little uncomfortable.

She was his girlfriend, after all.

Her fashion resources, her public image, her fame — all plainly qualified for that endorsement.

Yet he had chosen Bae Joo-hyun.

And she had followed her own commercial line, opening talks with Estée Lauder.

Now, from Lady Yeong, Choi Jin-ri suddenly understood another layer.

If Baek Si-on truly saw her only as the most convenient resource, he could have used affection to push her fee down.

He could have made her front for the new brand, spent the high-end commercial value she had just built, and packaged it all as “supporting her boyfriend’s career,” so every cost looked entirely reasonable.

But he hadn’t.

Lady Yeong staked her whole life on Yeongjo.

Baek Si-on had never asked her to stake her life on him.

She had once thought favor meant priority.

Now she understood one layer more.

True favor also includes not consuming.

The two of them were already together, and Baek Si-on still wanted her to hold value, work, and a future independent of “Baek Si-on’s girlfriend.”

When that understanding settled, Choi Jin-ri tilted her head, in the darkness, toward the creators’ row.

The hall was too dark to make out more than the outline of that buzz cut, but the corner of her lips curved lightly all the same.

❧ ❧ ❧

What Lee Ji-eun read from the same scenes was colder than Choi Jin-ri’s reading.

Lady Yeong must have been favored when young.

If she never had been, she would not have borne Sado, nor been ennobled as Lady Yeong.

But once time passed, the old favor was worth nothing.

The “special” in Yeongjo’s mouth seemed forever valid only for whoever was newest at that moment.

Lee Ji-eun’s gaze moved slowly.

First to Choi Jin-ri beside her.

Then, across several rows, to the area where Red Velvet sat.

She suddenly noticed this law cut both ways for her.

If men all tire of the old and love the new—

Then for Choi Jin-ri, she, Lee Ji-eun, was the later arrival; but for her, Bae Joo-hyun was the newer one.

Yesterday’s newcomer would sooner or later become tomorrow’s old one.

Lee Ji-eun pressed her lips together lightly, then reached a somewhat pessimistic conclusion:

A man’s promises are not necessarily reliable.

Rather than agonize over whom Baek Si-on looks at today, better to plant her own name inside his songs.

A person kept in a song, at least, cannot be replaced by time.

❧ ❧ ❧

In the back rows, Bae Joo-hyun kept thinking of Royal Noble Consort Mun beaten bloody.

As arrogant as she had been entering, that thoroughly wretched was she being punished.

Receiving favor was not itself a fault.

Her fault lay in taking Yeongjo’s favor, too early, for her own power.

Low rank, shallow roots — the only thing to lean on was the king’s present willingness to shield her.

Yet she had already begun crossing hierarchy to openly mock Lady Yeong, whose seniority and rank both outranked her.

As if Yeongjo favoring her today meant the whole palace must favor her too.

And one order from the Grand Queen Dowager made her recognize again exactly where she stood.

Bae Joo-hyun suddenly felt the scene was like a risk notice delivered across two hundred-plus years.

She had indeed received a great deal lately.

Endorsements, magazines, cameos, recordings, set visits, photos in the van.

If a few kindnesses now bred the delusion that the whole world ought to revolve around her, the line Baek Si-on had once tapped her with in the van would come true at once.

“You don’t actually think the whole world revolves around you just because you’re pretty, do you?”

Bae Joo-hyun recalibrated her strategy in her heart.

What mattered now was catching the chances Baek Si-on offered and turning them truly into her own ability and results.

Once she carried enough weight, then she would judge what the two of them actually were.

As for now.

Hold steady, farm up, don’t rush to feed the enemy a kill.

❧ ❧ ❧

On screen, Sado still knelt where he was, forced to absorb his father’s retreat-as-advance political trap of promotion-as-destruction.

Night wind stirred his robes.

Dark clouds already pressed over the palace walls.

In the darkness, Choi Jin-ri, Lee Ji-eun, and Bae Joo-hyun each stayed silent.

Nobody knew that from one court tale two hundred years old, they had just read out three entirely different rules of love.

And Baek Si-on in the creators’ row did not know that before the film even reached the rice-chest scene, people in the audience were already using Yeongjo’s harem to re-examine him.

❝

One screen, three women, three rules: favor means not consuming; promises live in songs, not in glances; and favor mistaken for power is a debt the palace always collects.
