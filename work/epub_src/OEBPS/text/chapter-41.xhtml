Chapter Forty-One

What Sulli Heard

July Night Choi Jin-ri's apartment The apartment had gone smoky and warm

When Choi Jin-ri came out of the bathroom, the living room had already gone hazy with smoke.

The fat of the pork belly had been hissing on the grill for the better part of an hour. What the heat pressed out of it ran down into the drip tray at the bottom — a slow, grease-thick stream — and the whole apartment was carrying a smell that was salty and sweet and scorched at the edges, the kind that makes a stomach sit up and take notice whether it was hungry or not.

She'd changed into a pink silk nightdress. Her hair, rubbed half dry with a towel, hung down over her shoulders.

Her face was bare.

Her eyes were still a little swollen.

But it was improved, a good deal, from where it had been when the door opened.

“Come on, come on.”

Goo Hara patted the cushion beside her.

Jin-ri stepped around the low table and sat down on the floor next to Hara, legs folded, knee catching the table leg. She had barely settled when a hand arrived in front of her.

Baek Eun-ah's hand.

In it was a leaf of lettuce, everything rolled up inside it like a parcel: a piece of pork belly just off the grill, a sliver of garlic, a ring of green chilli, and a smear of sauce underneath. Folded neatly. The way someone who's been doing it for other people for years does it without thinking.

“My cousin grilled it. Try it.”

Jin-ri looked at the lettuce wrap, then at the expression on Eun-ah's face which said, in very clear letters, tell me it's good. She took it and bit a small piece.

The juice of the pork belly came out the instant her teeth came together.

Salty. Fresh. Kicked up by the chilli and the garlic, and by the time it reached the back of her tongue it had turned into a single warm, heavy thickness. The crisp of the lettuce and the soft of the meat tangled together, and after two chews it was gone.

“Good?”

Jin-ri's eyes drifted across the low table, to the other side.

Baek Si-on sat on the sofa, chopsticks pinched over a piece of short rib, laying it down on the grill. His whole attention was on the grill plate, and he might as well have been the only person in the room; he hadn't looked up once.

She pulled her gaze back.

“Mm.”

Eun-ah let out a small "heh" and started making the next one.

✿ ✿ ✿

July Night The low table The rotation of the grill, and the bottles

The meat on the grill turned over. Si-on lifted a few finished pieces with the chopsticks.

Two pieces of short rib first. Onto Goo Hara's plate.

Then two pieces of chicken thigh. Onto Eun-ah's plate.

Then everything else still on the grill into his own small dish.

After that he laid a fresh strip of pork belly down, and in the pause he reached for the bottle of soju at the corner of the table and unscrewed the cap.

“Hara.”

Goo Hara was in the middle of putting meat in her mouth and made a muffled "mm?" around it.

Si-on had already poured into her cup.

Hara swallowed quickly, set her chopsticks down, and brought the glass up with both hands — one cupping the bottom, one resting against the side. The standard junior's way of receiving a pour.

“You don't have to be so formal.”

Si-on poured his own to the brim and lifted it.

“I've never found the right moment to thank you properly.”

He pushed the glass forward a little.

“Truly. Thank you. I'll finish mine, you do as you like.”

Head back.

One swallow.

The glass clicked down on the table.

Hara didn't hesitate. She took hers down whole, too.

✿ ✿ ✿

July Night The low table The second round, poured back the other way

Then she grabbed the bottle off the table and poured the next round herself — turning the exchange around the way Koreans do it.

Si-on turned his body sideways and held his cup out with both hands, dipping his head a little.

When Hara had finished, she refilled her own, and lifted it.

“Stop saying thank you. Honestly, I don't think I did anything.”

Hara knew about the song, of course. She'd known the moment he'd started on it. On the day it came out, July seventh, she'd gone to Melon herself, searched for it, listened twice, and — on a whim — saved it. When she saw it sitting at number eighty-nine on the real-time chart she'd felt a quiet swell of pride, the way a person does when a thing they helped set in motion moves a step without them.

And then it had started to sink.

Ninety-three. One-oh-seven. One-forty-eight. And then out of the top two hundred altogether.

She'd checked it every few days, and every time she opened Melon and saw the number had dropped another notch, a little more of some nameless guilt settled inside her. Logically she knew it had nothing to do with her — a song sinks or it swims on its own. But she was the one who'd introduced the producer, and the writer. When the song ran out of air, she couldn't shake the feeling that those two phone calls of hers had been wasted.

Last week she'd nearly messaged Si-on to ask how it was going. Typed a few characters. Deleted them. There'd been nothing to say.

So when she lifted this glass and said "I didn't do anything," it was the truest sentence she'd said all night.

“Are you hoping I'll feel guilty and die, so you can inherit the copyright?”

Hara very nearly spat the soju out.

She got a hand up just in time, coughed twice, and then — against every bit of her control — laughed out loud.

“What are you talking about?!”

Si-on tipped back the rest of his glass.

“This song got made because of you. You helped. But what happens to it is my business, not yours.”

It was said lightly. Hara heard the weight in it anyway.

She opened her mouth to say something, and a hand shot across the table and cut her off — Eun-ah, phone held up, screen pressed into Hara's face.

“Unnie, don't listen to him putting on a show! That song is huge overseas, even the Germans are praising it!”

The phone light caught Hara's eyes and she leaned back to get it in focus. Six figures. A play count six digits long.

Beneath it, a wall of comments in English.

nomoreseoul28

This can't be by a Korean idol. Who is this? I need this on repeat.

berlin_uh

I want this song to be heard all over the world, and at the same time I want to hide it away as my own secret.

soundcloud_ghost

Listened six times already. Whoever made this — please don't stop.

“What app is this?”

“SoundCloud. The biggest independent-music platform in the world.”

Eun-ah said it as if she were introducing a member of her own family.

“The YouTube channels posted it too. Two big channels, almost seven hundred thousand plays between them. The whole comment section is asking who this is.”

She scrolled down through a few comments and pointed at the middle one.

“Look at this one. A German wrote it — ‘I want this song to be heard all over the world, and at the same time I want to hide it away as my own secret.’”

Hara looked at the line of English, and one eyebrow climbed.

“For real?”

“Why would I lie?! I've got the screenshots saved, over a hundred of them!”

The more Eun-ah talked the more worked up she got, thumb flying across the screen.

Jin-ri had turned her head to one side to see the screen too.

She leaned in close.

Hara felt a drift of shampoo fragrance come over from the right. She glanced at her.

Jin-ri was staring at the English comments, eyes narrowed slightly, mouthing her way through the words.

“Have you heard this song?” Hara asked.

Jin-ri's eyes came up off the phone. She nodded once.

“And? What do you think?”

Jin-ri thought about it.

“……It's good.”

Hara waited two seconds.

That was the whole answer.

She looked at Jin-ri.

“You're a professional singer, and the most you can say about a song is ‘good’?”

Jin-ri was caught out.

“It's just…… good.”

From somewhere off to the side, Eun-ah made a "pfft" sound.

Hara wasn't about to let it go.

“Good where? The melody? The lyric? Or the person singing it?”

Jin-ri dropped her head, and the ears hidden behind her loose hair slowly began to take on a little colour.

“The melody's catchy. The key change in the chorus sits comfortably. The arrangement has good space in it, and the vocal-to-mix balance is controlled — nothing's fighting each other……”

The living room had gone quiet.

The grease on the grill was still hissing.

Hara looked at her.

Eun-ah looked at her.

Even Si-on's hand, moving a piece of meat, slowed by a half-second.

Jin-ri seemed to realise she'd said too much. She hurriedly picked up a piece of meat from her plate and stuffed it into her mouth, cheeks bulging, adding around it, in a muffled voice:

“That's all. It's just. Good.”
