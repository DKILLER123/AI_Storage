The “Yes” Worth Six Hundred Million

May 15 · night

Lights out — brightness at minimum

The Red Velvet dormitory · one screen still on

The dorm’s lights were all out.

Night-lamp glow leaked through the door gap, just enough to trace the outlines of blankets.

After Soo-young’s hooligan-husband showcase, the five had drifted off to their rooms carrying unequal portions of fatigue and laughter.

Screen brightness: lowest setting.

She opened Melon.

If it was nonsense, he was.

She added a new tag to Baek Si-on’s internal profile: utilitarian.

Her fingers went back to the keyboard.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Irene felt a wad of cotton lodge itself somewhere near her heart.

Melon real-time No. 2 was the best chart result of their career.

The whole dorm had been electrified by that number all night.

And Baek Si-on probably couldn’t work up the appetite to even check the boards.

Because the No. 1 sitting on top of ‘Oops’ was his own third Billboard champion.

The gap wasn’t enough to make her feel small as a person.

But it made the little joy she’d wanted to share with him look, suddenly, very small.

Rolled over.

Rolled back.

Her brain had begun running without authorization.

Tomorrow — would it be just the two of them?

Was he going to play her a new song?

Could it be that he wanted her to buy lunch?

On the last one, she decided: entirely plausible. Lunch was promised — hers to pay, next time.

She picked the phone back up and started searching restaurants near Nonhyeon-dong.

Three pages of recommendations in, she collided with a fatal problem: she had no idea what Baek Si-on liked to eat.

May 16

Morning — outfit number three

The dormitory · the full-length mirror, occupied

The next morning, in front of the full-length mirror, Irene had already changed three times.

The survivor: a pink-and-white floral V-neck short-sleeve blouse, and a pair of pure-white high-waisted denim shorts.

She stood at the mirror a while.

Then picked up a pair of small pearl studs and held them to her earlobes.

Put them on. Found it too deliberate. Took them off.

A minute later, put them back.

Final check in the mirror: floral blouse, white shorts, pearl studs, light makeup, bangs ending exactly at the eyebrows.

Acceptable.

Soo-young had at some point materialized at the door, shoulder on the frame, arms crossed.

“Unnie has a schedule today?”

“No.” Irene stuffed her phone into her bag, not looking at her.

“Then why the perfume?”

“I’m stopping by producer Jung Jae-joon’s studio.”

The answer was factually airtight.

But Soo-young plainly did not buy its completeness; her eyes were locked on the white denim shorts performing their leg-lengthening miracle:

“Coming back for lunch?”

Irene ignored her, got her shoes on, and pushed out the door.

May 16

Late morning

Nonhyeon-dong · Jung Jae-joon’s studio, control room B

When Irene pushed the studio door open, Jung Jae-joon was seated at the mixing console in huge headphones, track waveforms spread across the screen before him.

Behind the recording-booth glass, Baek Si-on stood at the microphone in monitoring cans, the cable running down from his right ear to a splitter at the base of the boom arm.

He was singing.

Irene sat down in the chair beside Jung Jae-joon.

The sound from the other side of the glass, restored through the monitors, filled the room.

The style was nothing she had predicted. Bright, bouncing hard, a lazy-but-cocky sway riding inside the beat. The chorus melody was violently catchy — two listens and it owned you.

Baek Si-on’s state at the mic was different from the version of him she usually saw, too. His whole attention had collapsed into the sound; he finished a line, judged its fit against the beat himself, and if it displeased him he simply threw a hand signal and demanded the take again.

And Jung Jae-joon was not polite to him about it, boss or no boss.

Irene set her bag on her knees and listened quietly.

The collar of the floral blouse fluttered once in the air-conditioning, and the pearl studs caught the little indicator lights of the control-room gear.

She had changed three times this morning.

Sitting here now, listening to the voice on the far side of the glass, she decided the effort had not been entirely meaningless.

At minimum, this exact moment offered a very attractive Baek Si-on.

Even if he was on the far side of a glass, with no idea she was on this one.

❧ ❧ ❧

The booth door opened.

Baek Si-on came out, pulling off his headphones.

Irene sat where she was and waited for him to say something.

Something like: were you waiting long?

Or: you look nice today.

At absolute minimum, a you’re here.

Result:

Baek Si-on raised a hand and pointed at the booth.

“In.”

Irene’s expectations were bisected on the spot.

“Me?”

“Is there a second unemployed person in this room?”

The remaining half was gone too.

She rose and walked into the same booth where, last time, her underwire had been snapped for science.

On the stand in front of the microphone sat a single sheet of A4.

She had expected dense staff notation and chopped syllables, or at least a passage with a beginning and an end.

The printed page held one line:

“Boom shaka laka boom”

Through the glass, her eyes found Baek Si-on in the control room.

He keyed the talkback.

“Can you read it?”

Irene looked down and confirmed the page’s contents once more.

“…I believe I can.”

The English difficulty of these lyrics had, after all, not yet exceeded the reach of mandatory Korean education.

“Sing.”

Irene put the cans on.

The beat started — the stickiest loop from that chorus.

She opened her mouth at the microphone.

“Boom shaka laka boom—”

The talkback key clacked open.

“Too soft.”

Second attempt.

“Boom shaka laka boom!”

“Too cute. Bring it down.”

Third attempt: the rhythm didn’t land.

Fourth: the tail note dragged.

The takes, as coached

Take 1 — too soft.

Take 2 — too cute; bring it down.

Take 3 — off the beat.

Take 4 — tail dragged.

Takes 5, 6… — two-to-three-word notes each; Jung Jae-joon occasionally adds one technical adjustment.

Greenlit at fifteen minutes: “That works. Good job.”

Irene had spent the whole night turning the invitation over in her head.

And now she stood here, in dead earnest, singing “Boom shaka laka boom” into a microphone for ten minutes.

Life was, indeed, full of surprises.

Some of them looked nothing like the ones you’d imagined.

At last, fifteen minutes in, Baek Si-on’s voice came through the talkback one more time:

“That works. Good job.”

Irene pulled off the cans and came out of the booth.

At the console, Baek Si-on was doing something to the session.

She leaned in beside him.

On the screen, the track she had just recorded had been copied into several copies and stacked.

Baek Si-on dragged a few parameters.

The process, as rendered

Pitch: up. The rhythm: sliced into short, bouncy fragments. The stacked tracks: one more effect layer on top, and the monitors began producing a sound both shrill and spring-loaded.

It sounded like a flock of yellow capsule creatures lined up at the edge of a basketball court, bouncing in perfect unison at the tops of their lungs:

“Boom shaka laka boom!”

Irene’s expression traveled from curiosity to blankness.

“Is… is that my voice?”

Baek Si-on watched the screen with perfect calm.

“Yes.”

Her finger pointed at the monitors, vaguely.

“How did it become THIS?”

Jung Jae-joon offered one line of professional explanation from the side:

“Pitch-up, slicing, track stacking — plus a touch of resonance effect.”

That was not, of course, what Irene had asked.

What she had asked was: why did a morning of careful preparation end with her turning into a yellow capsule creature on the record?

Baek Si-on heard the finished effect through, and nodded, satisfied.

“Suits it perfectly.”

Irene turned her face toward him.

“Suits WHAT exactly?”

Baek Si-on looked over — gaze sweeping past the floral blouse and coming to rest on her eyes.

“Your energy yesterday, when you were dancing.”

Irene: “……”

She understood at last: the revenge for that antique blacklist routine was nowhere near finished.

The revenge, as tracked

It had grown legs. It had sprinted from the KBS corridor all the way into the recording studio.

And here, successfully, it had evolved into a flock of Minions.

Baek Si-on saved the pitch-shifted, sliced vocal files into the project folder and pushed the remaining mixing and mastering straight to Jung Jae-joon at the console.

“What’s the song called?” Jung Jae-joon asked.

“‘Jumpshot’.”

Jung Jae-joon typed a few keys, flashed an OK sign, put on his noise-canceling headphones, and entered a state of total, professional deafness to the world.

The chair at the console spun half a circle; Baek Si-on stood.

Two steps.

Irene, behind him, had not followed.

He stopped, and beckoned.

“Come here.”

She followed him to the sofa area in the back half of the control room.

Baek Si-on sat. Irene sat beside him.

The distance they left between them was wide enough to fit one more Soo-young.

Baek Si-on turned his face; his gaze settled on her.

Specifically: locked onto her lips.

Irene’s breathing lost its footing instantly.

The distance was already close.

And now she was being stared at, like this, by a man with this much gravitational pull.

She lowered her eyes, uncomfortable, and lifted a hand to tuck a stray strand of hair slowly behind her ear.

The pearl studs came into view.

And just as the atmosphere began sliding toward the pink channel, Baek Si-on turned away, reached into the black briefcase at the end of the sofa, and produced two lip glosses in clear tester tubes.

One: a clean, translucent peach coral.

The other: a deep, black-cherry red.

He held both out to her.

“Try them.”

Irene looked down at the two tubes. Then up.

“For me?”

“Not yet.”

His answer was scrupulously honest: “Tester samples.”

Irene: “……”

So all that staring had not been the sudden discovery of her morning’s careful preparation.

She had been evaluated as a conveniently shaped swatch card.

She took the two tubes, and her mood sank below the level it had reached after being converted into a Minion.

Baek Si-on kept unpacking: a palm-sized folding mirror, a pouch of disposable lip brushes, sterile swabs, cotton pads, and lip makeup remover.

The kit, as unpacked

Folding mirror, palm-sized.

Disposable lip brushes, one pouch.

Sterile swabs.

Cotton pads.

Lip makeup remover.

Tools: complete. Preparation: total. Planned before he ever left the house.

Baek Si-on began dictating the method.

First remove her current lip color. Then First Peach over the whole lip surface, tracing the outline clean — especially the peaks and the center of the lower lip. Wait a few seconds for the light base to half-set. Then Vampire Cherry, dotted on the inner side of top and bottom lips.

Irene listened with great attention.

And when he finished, she asked a question with no connection whatsoever to makeup.

“Why are you so practiced at this?”

“I’ve watched someone apply it.”

“Who?”

“Eun-ah.”

Irene made a small “oh.”

The cotton pad erased her existing rose-beige.

In the mirror, her lips returned to their natural color — a faint, shallow pink.

Baek Si-on sat beside her, his gaze tracking every movement of her hands.

Being watched froze her wrist stiff.

“Could you not stare the entire time?”

“If I don’t watch, how do I judge the effect?”

The logic was completely correct.

Precisely because it was correct, it left no room for rebuttal whatsoever.

She could only unscrew First Peach, load a swab, and carry it toward her mouth.

But whether it was the oppression of that gaze beside her, or a lifetime’s habit of leaving this to makeup artists, the swab refused every instruction the moment it was hers to command.

The first attempt, as botched

The light shade went on uneven. The lip line breached the border. In her hurry to dot the dark shade inward, her lips pressed together without permission — and light and dark churned into a single catastrophe.

The mirror showed no water-light fruit-juice gradation whatsoever. It showed a woman who had gnawed half a dragon fruit beside a campfire.

Baek Si-on studied the masterpiece for a while.

“Too much.”

Irene knew it was wrong.

But she refused to admit she couldn’t even apply a lip gloss.

“I think it’s fine.”

“You look like you just fed on someone.”

Irene glanced down at the handwritten temporary name on the tester tube.

“It’s literally called Vampire Cherry.”

“That’s a product name, not instructions for use.”

Irene: “……”

Baek Si-on picked a clean swab off the table and turned sideways to face her.

The stretch of sofa between them compressed, in the turn, to less than one palm’s width.

He didn’t touch her.

The swab stopped in midair.

“Me, then?”

Irene blinked — and nodded, a nod of the smallest possible amplitude.

“Mm.”

The disaster site re-cleaned, Baek Si-on held a fresh swab in one hand and the gloss tube in the other.

The swab, loaded with product, traced slowly along her lower lip line. The peach coral spread, point by point.

At first Irene watched his hand.

Before long, her gaze had climbed his fingers without authorization.

The climb, as logged

Wrist. Forearm. Shoulder.

Then it stopped, on his face.

Baek Si-on tilted his head a fraction to check the symmetry of the two sides — and she tilted hers, a fraction, in the same direction.

Less than twenty centimeters sat between their faces.

Close enough that she could catch the faintest scent off his collar.

Her lips pressed together, once, from nerves.

“Don’t press.”

Irene relaxed her mouth immediately.

Baek Si-on switched swabs, loaded a trace of Vampire Cherry, and placed it only on the innermost strip of her top and bottom lips, the narrow zone near the mouth’s center. The deep red settled onto the peach base and, riding its moisture, feathered gently outward into a thin gradient. He pressed the transition line with the clean end of the swab, once, twice.

The light shade owned volume and translucence. The dark owned the color and the focal point.

The two layers finally fused.

He finished one side, tilted to check the other —

and his gaze, lifting a fraction past the lips, discovered a circumstance unrelated to the product.

Irene’s cheeks and the tips of her ears had gone entirely red — closing in on Vampire Cherry’s own swatch performance.

“Hot in here?”

Irene answered instantly:

“Mm.”

She knew, even as she said it, that the excuse would not survive an audit.

The studio ran air-conditioning several degrees below the street.

So she appended:

“The lights might be too bright.”

Baek Si-on’s eyes went up to the ceiling.

The lighting audit, as conducted

Fixture: ordinary LED ceiling panel. Output: soft, diffuse, even.

Roasting capacity: none, by any known physics. He did not expose her.

He handed the folding mirror back to her.

“Look.”

The mirror’s result was a different species from her own attempt.

The lip edge: translucent, clear peach. Deepening inward, and at the center a stain of dark red like a cherry bitten through at its ripest.

Above all: the contrast with her face. Her presence ran cold and slightly distant — and this color had folded a layer of soft allurement into the cold outline without asking permission.

Baek Si-on’s gaze rested on her lips for a few seconds, and he nodded once.

Irene used the mirror’s reflection to smuggle a look at his expression — to determine whether the good he saw was the gloss, or the girl wearing it.

The mirror was too small and the angle too poor; she got the bottom sliver of his chin and nothing else.

She snapped it shut and put on magnificent indifference.

“Well?”

“The product is fine.”

She waited two seconds.

Nothing followed.

“That’s it?”

Baek Si-on tipped his head.

“What else?”

Irene wanted to seize him by the collar and ask: you leaned that close and stared that long, and the final verdict is the product is fine?

But she had a dignity to maintain.

So she picked a less aggressive formulation:

“Does the color suit me?”

His gaze came back to her face — and this time it finally carried something she wanted.

“It suits you.”

“Better than whatever you had on this morning.”

That offhand little sentence proved he had noticed the morning’s preparation long ago.

He simply hadn’t said.

She produced a light “oh” and pretended the evaluation weighed nothing whatsoever.

At which point Baek Si-on took out his phone.

“Look here.”

Irene turned her face aside on instinct — and the shutter had already fired before her expression finished adjusting.

He glanced at the frame, then zoomed in and aimed the lens at the gradient on her lips.

“Don’t move.”

Her shoulders locked.

Another small shutter sound. On the phone screen, the water-light on her cupid’s bow compressed into a tiny bright point, the peach-to-cherry gradient finer than the naked eye had managed.

Two photos done, he handed her the phone.

“Look first.”

Irene took it.

The first shot was the frontal — and in the instant the shutter fell she had been looking straight at Baek Si-on.

The eyes in that photo carried a softness she herself had never been told about.

Irene handed the phone back.

“What are you going to do with these?”

“Show the brand team the real-world effect. All right?”

“All right.”

Baek Si-on sent both photos into the LAVUE project group.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on did not stay to enjoy his employees’ workplace self-rescue.

He locked the phone and turned to Irene.

“Do you have a personal cosmetics endorsement right now?”

Irene had not expected the topic to jump from lip swatches to here.

She shook her head.

“No.”

Red Velvet wasn’t a year old. Group-level opportunities were only beginning to arrive; personal commercial work sat at effectively zero. The brand contacts that did come were group packages, short events, or product gifts with no exclusivity worth the name.

Baek Si-on nodded once.

“Then do you want one?”

Irene failed to follow for a second.

“Sorry — what?”

He tipped his chin at the two clear tester tubes on the table, the ones without so much as a printed wrapper.

“Endorse them.”

Irene’s gaze made a full round trip between his face and the tubes.

“Me?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because you’re good-looking.”

She had braced for a commercial analysis.

He used the shortest possible answer instead — leaving her genuinely unable to determine whether he meant the color suited her, or simply that he found her good-looking.

Irene fought to stay composed.

Her reason, however, had not won a single round since she entered the studio today. And the temperature on her ears, having just barely subsided, climbed back up behind them, point by point.

She looked down and took a tube in hand, pretending to study its body with tremendous scholarly focus.

The sample carried a single handwritten number. There was nothing to study.

Baek Si-on didn’t wait for her to recover.

“Three years. Six hundred million won.”

Irene’s head came up.

The offer, as tabled

Term: three years — 600 million won total.

Year 1: 100 million. Year 2: 200 million. Year 3: 300 million. Tiered, ascending.

Exclusive lip-color cosmetics binding for the full three years.

Other beauty categories not banned outright — base makeup, skincare, fragrance, or multi-brand deals require advance clearance for direct conflicts.

Her side: she cannot sign it alone. SM’s legal, the revenue split, and the master plan all own a piece.

The terms were loose to the point of favoritism.

And Irene’s first reaction on hearing them was:

“I can’t decide this by myself.”

SM’s legal, the commercial split, the company’s overall planning — an artist had no authority to nod this through on the spot.

Baek Si-on, of course, knew.

“I’m asking whether you’re willing.”

The question stepped past the company and the contract and landed, again, on Irene herself.

The distance on the sofa hadn’t changed.

She looked at him. He looked at her.

“Willing.”

The word was out before Irene understood what was wrong with the picture.

The picture, as constituted:

The scene, as audited by its own witness

A man staring at her, asking are you willing. Her, red to the ears, answering willing. One “officiant” present throughout — back turned, noise-canceling headphones on, professionally pretending not to exist.

No ring. Two unlabeled tester tubes on the table, wrappers included in neither.

What kind of endorsement negotiation was this? She hit the brakes inside her own head.

Three years, six hundred million won. Exclusive lip binding. No ring. Two tubes without packaging.

Irene looked down fast and picked the tester tube back up.

To drag the conversation back from the cliff edge, she asked the safest question she could construct.

“What’s the brand called?”

“LAVUE.”

She paused.

“What?”

Baek Si-on slowed down and said it again.

“LAVUE.”

Irene re-assembled the syllables in her head.

La-vue.

It sounded like Love you.

The problem was that she had answered “willing” perhaps ninety seconds ago — and now the man had said a word that sounded like Love you, directly to her face.

For Irene, who had over-thought her daily quota several times before noon, this sat somewhat outside her rated tolerance.

Baek Si-on added:

“French. La Vue — the sight. The thing seen.”

She should have returned to commercial language right there.

But her mouth arrived before her reason:

“It doesn’t mean anything else?”

Baek Si-on’s gaze paused on her face.

“What did you hear?”

She shook her head fast, denial at full power.

“Nothing.”

Then, quietly, she repeated the brand name once.

“LAVUE.”

The syllables rolled off lips still stained cherry-dark at the center.

It still sounded like Love you.

Baek Si-on withdrew his gaze.

“Got it memorized?”

Irene nodded.

“Memorized.”

That particular pronunciation was, truly, very hard to forget.
