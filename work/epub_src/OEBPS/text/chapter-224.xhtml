Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Four

Just Asking His Taste, How Did We Land on Abs

Coming out of Jung Jae-joon’s studio, Irene assumed the day’s business was over.

She had recorded one basketball-cheer harmony line.

Tried two lip glosses that hadn’t even launched.

And, in passing, received a verbal endorsement offer worth six hundred million won over three years.

The morning had already been absurd enough.

A normal person would have gone back to the dorm, burrowed into the blankets, and replayed the exact expression Baek Si-on had been wearing when he said because you’re good-looking.

But the van hadn’t been driving long when the familiar building reappeared outside the window.

KBS.

“Who else is there to see?” she asked.

“Kim Eun-sook-writer-nim.”

“I’m going too?”

“It’s your business on the table. You’re not going?”

Irene: “……”

The LAVUE contract hadn’t even been delivered to SM yet. Her manager didn’t know. Lee Soo-man didn’t know.

Baek Si-on, meanwhile, was already paving her first exposure resource.

The conveyor, as ridden

The speed of his arrangements gave her the feeling of being strapped to a conveyor belt.

Pause button: not installed.

❧ ❧ ❧

May 16 · midday

Same day — the conveyor, continuing

Yeouido · KBS · ‘Descendants of the Sun’ project meeting room

In the meeting room, Kim Eun-sook looked up, and her attention landed on Irene first.

Yesterday’s blonde girl, punished to the corner to draw squares, was today sitting properly at Baek Si-on’s side.

Her treatment had been upgraded at speed.

At minimum, she now had a chair.

“Hello, Writer-nim. Sorry to disturb you.”

Kim Eun-sook nodded and returned the greeting.

“Please, sit.”

Today, she knew who Irene was.

The reason was ‘Oops’.

The BodyArmor commercial had aired in the golden slot directly before the ‘Producer’ premiere, and ‘Oops’ had climbed to No. 2 on Melon’s real-time chart.

The online discussion had maxed out overnight: the dopamine visuals, the five fruit flavors, the cuts splicing Baek Si-on into the Red Velvet frames, the simple, brainwashing chorus and the ad dance.

CJ’s offline channels had moved just as frighteningly fast.

Yesterday, the vending machines on the KBS floors had stocked only the usual beverages.

This morning, BodyArmor had already been crammed into them, the machines plastered with Red Velvet promo stickers.

Commercial, digital single, convenience stores, vending machines, bus placements — nearly everything had landed within the same day.

Which raised Kim Eun-sook’s expectations for the OST Baek Si-on had promised considerably.

An ad song thrown off half-casually had carried a brand-new girl group to No. 2 on Melon.

If it were her new drama, first-tier actors, a full-scale marketing push, and Baek Si-on singing it himself — no one yet knew what height that could reach.

But today’s agenda was placements and cameos, so she pressed the expectation back down.

Baek Si-on didn’t spend long on pleasantries.

“The three-hundred-million-won plan — I can accept it.”

Kim Eun-sook gave a small nod.

Two hundred million for the in-drama PPL and the makeup-and-styling collaboration, plus just under one hundred million for Kim Ji-won’s short-term lip-cosmetics licensing.

Just as Kim Eun-sook moved to confirm, Baek Si-on spoke again.

“But there’s one additional condition.”

“Go ahead.”

Baek Si-on’s gaze tipped slightly to the right.

“I’d like my endorser to cameo in the drama as a brand counter girl.”

Kim Eun-sook’s eyes followed his gaze and arrived at Irene.

“This one?”

“Irene. Bae Joo-hyun.”

Irene sat straight, hands folded on her knees.

Expression management: mostly successful.

The questions inside her head, meanwhile, had already formed a queue.

My endorser?

His?

When she finished listening, Kim Eun-sook opened the script in front of her and riffled her fingers back and forth through the pages.

Writing a placement was easy.

Writing one that felt natural was hard.

An actor mid-scene suddenly squaring the product to the camera and reciting its full list of effects — the audience would know at once that the sponsor had come to collect rent.

Placements like that earned money, and boos in the same breath.

At last her finger stopped on a flashback between Yoon Myung-joo and Seo Dae-young.

“There’s a scene.”

Kim Eun-sook said:

“Seo Dae-young’s ex-girlfriend is getting married, and Yoon Myung-joo goes with him to crash the wedding. Before the wedding, she needs makeup that can bury the ex-girlfriend on sight.”

She tapped the fingertip against that passage of the script.

“Here, we add a scene of Yoon Myung-joo passing a cosmetics counter.”

The scene, as slotted

A wedding about to be crashed. A counter on the way to it. Two unlaunched glosses, standing by to arm the leading lady.

Kim Eun-sook glanced up at Irene once: “The lines don’t need to be many. Two or three is plenty.”

She tossed out a direction offhand:

The counter girl recommends two lip glosses.

First line: ‘If pretty is all you need, this one is enough.’

Yoon Myung-joo asks: ‘Then what’s the other one for?’

The counter girl answers: ‘If you want to win today, wear both.’

That way the product hasn’t merely appeared in the frame — it has joined Yoon Myung-joo’s expression of character, and settled, in passing, the question of why anyone would layer two glosses at all.

That is what an effective placement looks like.

Irene sat alongside, entirely dazed.

“Am I really acting in this?”

“I spent three hundred million. I’m not paying you to go in and wipe the counter.” Baek Si-on said.

Irene: “……”

The corner of Kim Eun-sook’s mouth twitched; she turned a few pages further and stopped at the army-related plot.

“There’s one more slot.”

She sketched the design briefly.

In the drama, there is a scene where a girl group puts on a consolation-performance at an army base. Yoo Si-jin and Seo Dae-young — always gravely proper in front of the leading ladies — instantly join the entire hall of soldiers in cheering.

Kang Mo-yeon and Yoon Myung-joo witness their men screaming for a girl group on live television, fall into synchronized jealousy, and trigger a stretch of romantic-comedy warfare between the couples.

The scene needed a real girl group to begin with.

Now that Irene was cameoing anyway, and Red Velvet had exactly the heat of ‘Oops’ behind it, all five appearing together was the most natural choice available.

Kim Eun-sook lifted her head.

“If Red Velvet is willing.”

Irene’s mouth opened, then closed.

The package, as priced

Meaning: she, personally, gets a brand-counter cameo — and Red Velvet, all five, picks up a full in-drama stage on top.

Buy one. Get one.

Baek Si-on, of course, read Kim Eun-sook’s goodwill without difficulty.

Throwing in a full-group Red Velvet consolation performance was, at bottom, a signal of sincerity:

she accepted the three-hundred-million-won collaboration, and she hoped Baek Si-on would put real care into the OST.

So Baek Si-on turned the topic toward the part he owed.

“Writer-nim — do you have specific requirements for the OST?”

Only now did they enter the phase Kim Eun-sook had truly been waiting for.

She thought about the picture in her head, and said:

“Bright tempo. Sweet feeling. The male vocal and the female vocal answering each other, like a conversation.”

“The feeling of the lyric is ‘whenever I see you, my heart races all over again.’ It needs to work across a first meeting, the ambiguous stage, a reunion, and a love still climbing.”

Baek Si-on took out his phone and typed a few keywords into his memo.

The brief, as keyed in

Bright.

Male-female duet.

Reunion.

Heart races again at every meeting…… or just ideas off the visuals?

Military uniform and white coat.

Upbeat.

“Do you need a female vocal?” — “Best if there is one.” Noted down too.

Irene sat beside them, and at the words male-female duet, her fingers twitched lightly on her knees.

The two parties signed a letter of intent on the spot, locking the broad framework.

The skeleton, as signed

LAVUE pays three hundred million won.

Kim Ji-won uses LAVUE lip gloss in the drama.

Irene cameos as the brand counter girl.

Red Velvet cameos as the full-group army consolation performance.

Baek Si-on provides one OST, personally produced.

Formal fees, copyright ownership, actor licensing, shooting schedule: to be negotiated separately. Signatures: Kim Eun-sook, Baek Si-on.

The skeleton was officially standing.

Out in the corridor, Irene’s footsteps had gone slightly floaty.

The day wasn’t even over.

She was about to become a cosmetics brand’s endorser, cameo in Kim Eun-sook’s new drama, and then drag all of Red Velvet back in for a second cameo.

The speed at which Baek Si-on had advanced her life today was outrunning SM’s.

After walking a stretch, she finally circled back to the thing that had been sitting on her mind.

“Sunbae.”

“Mm?”

“That OST just now — are you planning it as a male-female duet?”

Baek Si-on tilted his head toward her.

“Mm.”

“Who are you thinking of for the female vocal?”

“Are you interested?” Baek Si-on stopped walking.

Irene nodded.

“Yes.”

She had already recorded one Minion line today. Surely she’d earned a chance to prove she could sing in a normal voice.

Baek Si-on did not answer directly.

“Did you practice yesterday’s dance?”

Irene’s shoe soles scraped across the floor.

She had, of course, practiced.

The practice, as logged

Partner: Park Soo-young, conscripted.

Venue: the dorm living room.

Genre: a married-couple partner dance featuring generous violence and a severe risk of traffic accidents.

Results achieved: stepped-on feet; total loss of balance; an audience of three younger members, heckling throughout.

“……I practiced a little.”

“Get the dance down first. Then we’ll talk about singing.” Baek Si-on resumed walking.

Irene very much wanted to ask why singing an OST required passing a dance examination first.

There was no causal relationship of any kind between the two skills.

But Baek Si-on was already heading for the elevator, and clearly did not intend to provide pedagogical support.

She suspected he was simply bullying her on purpose.

Irene could only follow.

And stamped the verdict in her heart one more time: bad boy.

May 16 · early afternoon

Post-signature — one passenger, one cousin, one sworn-to-silence driver

Underground parking · the LAVUE van, back seat

In the underground parking garage, Baek Eun-ah was already waiting by the car.

Her mission today: represent LAVUE at SM to negotiate Irene’s personal endorsement contract.

“Joohyun-unnie!” Baek Eun-ah waved her hand up at her.

“Eun-ah~”

Irene flashed her a sweet smile in return.

Baek Si-on handed the letter of intent to Baek Eun-ah.

She flipped through a few pages, then pulled the car door open.

Before getting in, Irene turned around to say her goodbye.

“Goodbye, sunbae.”

Baek Si-on offered one reminder.

“Practice the dance.”

The polite smile on Irene’s face short-circuited for a moment.

“……Understood.”

The door shut.

The car pulled out of the underground garage.

Irene sat in the back seat, the streetscape receding slowly in the sunlight outside the window.

She thought about yesterday’s Italian lunch.

It had already been agreed that next time the meal was her treat.

And to treat someone, you had to know what they ate.

Asking point-blank “what does your cousin like to eat” would be far too obvious.

She decided to take a detour.

“Eun-ah.”

“Mm?”

“Does Baek-sunbae usually eat…… is he picky?”

Baek Eun-ah was still leafing through the letter of intent, and answered offhand:

“Not picky.”

“He eats anything?”

“Pretty much. He’d put away three bowls of rice with nothing on the side but kimchi.”

The corner of Irene’s mouth moved.

Truly not picky.

Extremely down-to-earth, even.

“Eating that many carbs — how come he never gains any weight?”

“He works out every day.”

The sentence itself was utterly ordinary.

And Irene merely followed it, on pure reflex:

“Oh. Then he should have abs, right?”

The instant the words left her mouth, the air density inside the car changed.

Baek Eun-ah lifted her face from above the documents.

Irene realized too that her logic had just leapt from eating to abs — a span approaching the distance from Seoul direct to New York.

She scrambled to patch it.

“I mean…… conditioning. Don’t idols all have to stay in shape? He’s shooting a movie and everything……”

Baek Eun-ah gave an acknowledging sound.

“True.”

Irene had just let out her breath —

when she heard the question:

“Joohyun-unnie wants to see them?”

“……What?”

“Abs.”

The temperature of Irene’s face went straight for boiling point.

“I don’t!”

Baek Eun-ah’s expression didn’t waver by a single unit.

“I never said you did.”

Irene: “……”

In the front seat, the driver’s eyes never strayed from the road, both hands locked on the wheel — a statue that had donated its hearing to science.

The front seat, as sworn

Statement, heard in full. Opinion, permanently withheld. Eyewitness plan: none.

Professional. Very professional. The kind of professionalism this industry runs on.

Hounded by Baek Eun-ah’s gaze, Irene couldn’t sit still; she simply turned her face to the window.

The glass reflected the layer of two-tone water-light gloss still on her lips, not yet removed.

It looked very pretty.

But right now she wanted nothing except for the word ‘abs’ to be left behind on the road with the rest of the scenery.

Then Baek Eun-ah, beside her, suddenly added:

“He does.”

Irene didn’t catch up in time.

“What?”

“Abs.”

“……”

The answer, as delivered

Latency: a full minute behind the question. Payload: two words.

Evidence: personally verified, daily. Usage: purely to watch her cousin-in-law’s endorser combust.

Quiet returned to the car.

Irene swore to herself: before this vehicle reached the SM building, not one more syllable connected to Baek Si-on would escape her mouth.

At minimum — absolutely never in front of this particular cousin.
