Chapter One Hundred Five

Baek Eun-ah’s Morning Mail Hell

October 1 6:47 am PDT Los Angeles · hotel room Not an alarm. Mail. Then mail that had learned to be a machine gun.

Baek Eun-ah was shaken awake by a phone. Not a clock. Mail. One. Two. Three. Then the thing lost its manners and became a gun that did not know how to empty.

Buzz. Buzz-buzz. Buzz-buzz-buzz-buzz—

She buried half a face in the pillow. The right hand came out from under the duvet and patted the nightstand three times. First: the hotel notepad. Second: the water bottle. Third: the phone.

The brightness made her squint.

Six forty-seven.

Mail unread: 47. KakaoTalk unread: 23. SMS: 9.

She decided she was still asleep and had misread the numbers. Rubbed an eye. Sat up inside the duvet. Unlocked. Opened the box. Started to swipe.

KB Kookmin Bank · Brand

[Urgent confirmation] Possible visual extension of Mr. Baek Si-on’s Vogue work

Not the magazine’s frames — they knew those belonged to Vogue and to the photographer. They wanted a new set: the same altitude, the same international air, tuned to a bank. High-net-worth centres. Private-bank rooms. A handful of wealth events that liked a wall to look expensive.

Long letter. Short meaning. They had paid for a Venice Best Actor. They had just noticed a Vogue man in the global pool, and they wanted that man in the branch.

Hyundai Motor

[Aslan launch copy] Confirmation of “Vogue global content-pool feature” language

Three versions attached. Conservative. Ordinary. And the one marketing had written while the room was still standing: “From Venice to Vogue, from film to music, Baek Si-on and Aslan together define the international manner of a new Korean man.”

The last line, if her cousin saw it, would have “together define” cut out of the world.

SK Telecom

[New proposal] “Global Roaming / Connected World” TVC upgrade

The old brief had been young, tech, future. They wanted the upgrade: roaming, a connected world, work that happened in other cities. The reasons were written as if reasons could be stacked until they became a right: Venice, a North American release, Vogue, Los Angeles footage, a public face that already looked like a map.

They had signed a Best Actor. They had woken up next to a man the world was using as a socket.

The brain came on one lamp at a time. The brands already under contract had priced a Venice cup. Overnight he had grown a second price nobody had written down: a Korean man sitting inside Vogue’s global pool. That value was not in the paper. They all wanted to spend it anyway.

Four through nine were the same three houses at execution level. Dates. Specs. Approval trees. Work that did not need a decision before breakfast.

Ten changed the weather. The senders were names that had never written to her.

Jaeger-LeCoultre

Dinner · Geneva · if the diary allows

The brand had admired, during Venice, the “masculine image rich in the texture of time” he had given their watch.

Texture of time. Did these people not feel their own teeth when they typed?

The rest of the stack rhymed. Dinners. Shows. “A first conversation toward brand friendship.” Dating, in other words. Come sit. Drink. Watch a show. Let the world’s fashion cameras find you in the front row. If the noise was enough, if the papers were enough, if the temperature matched — then talk. If not, the meal had been a meal. Rivers, lakes, goodbye.

By the twenty-third letter her thumb had slowed. Not sleep. A fact she had never had to hold while she managed KB, Hyundai, SK.

Not one of those houses had mentioned money. Only invitations. Dinners, shows, tastings, “cultural exchange.” Fashion resource. She could talk fee. She did not know how to talk position. Fee had a market and a lawyer. Position had no number. You sat at Cartier and not at Chanel — what did Chanel hear. Preference for a rival? A later door closing? Dior’s front row the same week Saint Laurent asked — you picked one. What did the other one do with the picking.

She lay back into the pillow. Two weeks ago five national brands on the same morning had felt like a ceiling. She understood now that her cousin did not keep ceilings. He walked into them and took the plaster down and stood on the next floor.

Aish. The road was long, and it had just learned a new country.

She lifted the phone again. Left the box. Opened Kakao. Twenty-three unread. Most of them one person.

KakaoTalk · Hwang Soo-ah

Hwang Soo-ah

Eun-ah, Love Yourself, right? Confirm for me this one isn’t going to change again.

Eun-ah’s face did a small guilty thing. They had booked her for Legend. Bong Joon-ho had taken Legend. Then a cover of Can’t Take My Eyes Off You. Then the cover died and Bieber handed over Love Yourself. At that rate of weather, PTSD about being stood up was a professional diagnosis.

Hwang Soo-ah

If it isn’t changing, a few things I need from you:

1. Roughly when is Mr. Baek back in Korea?

2. Does this MV need a woman in it?

3. If it does — who.

Eun-ah?

Did you sleep?

Forgot the time difference. It’s the middle of the night there… sorry! Reply when you’re up.

Baek Eun-ah

Director Hwang, good morning! I’m really, truly sorry — the project kept changing direction and we’ve put you through it.

Deleted. Too small.

Baek Eun-ah

Director Hwang, good morning. Love Yourself is the MV. It will not change. Shoot dates, whether we put a woman in, who she is — I need a last word from Mr. Baek.

If you’re free we can video and put concept, look, and needs on one table. What time works on your side?

+30 sec

Hwang Soo-ah

OK

Nine tonight, Korea. As long as it doesn’t change. It really isn’t changing, right?

Baek Eun-ah

Really!!!

Three exclamation points. She left Kakao. Looked at the box again. Forty-seven. She had read twenty-three. Twenty-four still waiting. She put the phone face-down on the bed. Lay for half a minute.

The other bed made a sound. Seo Eun-ju was awake.

“What time is it?”

“Almost seven.”

“You’re up this early?”

“Not because I wanted to be.”

Eun-ah lifted the phone and shook it once.

“It shook me up.”

Seo Eun-ju took the water from the nightstand, drank.

“Did something break?”

“Not break, exactly.”

She passed the phone.

“Accurate version: cousin took another ceiling down.”

Seo Eun-ju took it. KB. Hyundai. SK. Then Jaeger, Cartier, Saint Laurent, Armani, Dior, Prada. A dozen letters later she handed it back. The face had not done much. The eyes had come the rest of the way on.

“Vogue is on the stands?”

“Mm. Korea looks like it already went up.”

“That’s normal.”

“This is normal?”

“For them it is.”

She threw the duvet off, went to the window, pulled the curtain half. Los Angeles morning came in and switched the room from grey-blue to warm white.

“Signed brands want the new international heat folded into the old paper. Unsigned houses want to be friends first. The moves are textbook.”

“The problem is I don’t know how to handle it.”

The more she said it the more it sounded like a joke that had stopped being a joke.

“Eonni. Do you ever feel eighteen is a cruel age to put this in front of a person?”

A few months ago the job had been taxis and waiting outside a gym. Then LOEN. Then KB, Hyundai, SK. Now Europe’s high jewellery, Swiss watches, Paris shows, New York dinners, all of it coming down the same chute. This was not growth. This was putting an eighteen-year-old in a max-level dungeon in starter cloth and telling her to kill the dragon.

Seo Eun-ju turned. Looked at the girl who still had wake-up in her mouth.

“You can also choose not to face it.”

Eun-ah blinked.

“And then?”

“Someone will face it for you.”

Five seconds. Then the head went down and the box opened again.

“Eonni.”

“Mm.”

“When you’ve washed, help me sort these houses first-pass. Legal risk. Exclusivity risk.”

Seo Eun-ju had the toothbrush. At the bathroom door she looked back.

“All right.”
