Chapter Thirty

This Is Clearly a Tibetan Mastiff

The same morning After the page LOEN · Ji-eun’s office, then the lift One week. Royce. A mastiff, not a puppy. Peach.

“Give me a week.”

“All right.”

Then the room went quiet.

Lee Ji-eun looked down, turned a page, drew two lines in the blank with the pen, as if putting her thoughts in order.

Baek Si-on sat opposite, didn’t move.

The quiet lasted about twenty seconds.

Lee Ji-eun’s pen stopped.

She lifted her head, looked at Si-on once.

She didn’t speak, but her eyes said it plain:

We’re done. You’re still planted here. For what?

Si-on was of course not admiring the office fit-out.

He was waiting.

Waiting for her to bring up pay.

Writing lyrics for someone was not volunteer work.

Market, he didn’t know.

But a lyricist at IU’s level, the number out of her mouth would not be small.

He had to hear the figure first, then decide whether to pull money from a pocket or talk a split.

Lee Ji-eun clearly had no intention of talking that.

Han-teuk at the door felt the field change, cleared his throat:

“Si-on, shouldn’t you take our Ji-eun for a meal? She did agree to write for you.”

The line came out naturally, as if finding Si-on a step.

In fact he was finding both of them a step at once.

Lee Ji-eun spoke first.

“No need.”

She closed the notebook, set it aside.

“If you want to thank someone, thank Hara eonni. I’m helping her. Not you.”

Si-on heard it.

She was purely doing Hara a favour.

He nodded once, stood from the chair.

“Then I won’t take more of your time.”

Said, turned, walked out.

The door not fully shut, a gap left.

Footsteps in the corridor, unhurried, going away.

Han-teuk stood still, the look going once between Lee Ji-eun and that gap.

“I’ll see him out?”

Lee Ji-eun mm’d, already opening the notebook again.

Han-teuk turned out, closed the door behind him.

✿ ✿ ✿

The lift.

When Han-teuk caught Si-on, he was standing there waiting.

The same place as last time. The same pose.

Both hands in his pockets, watching the floor number jump.

“Si-on.”

“Mm.”

“I’ll tell you something.”

Han-teuk leaned in, a hand by his mouth, the voice pressed very low:

“She likes chocolate.”

Si-on tipped his head, looked at him.

“Next time you come, bring some chocolate. Works better than pay.”

Han-teuk finished, added one more line:

“Don’t buy it too expensive. That makes her uncomfortable. Mid-range is fine. Brand-wise… Royce nama she eats the most.”

Si-on looked at him two seconds.

“I know. Thank you.”

A ding.

The lift arrived.

Door open.

Si-on stepped in, turned to face Han-teuk, hit one.

“Don’t see me off. Go back to work.”

Han-teuk stood outside, waved once.

The door shut.

Han-teuk watched the number jump from seven to six, six to five, all the way to one.

Then he turned back.

When he pushed the office door he heard Lee Ji-eun’s voice.

Not talking to him.

On the phone.

Lee Ji-eun → Goo Hara · after · the office

“Eonni. I did the favour.”

Lee Ji-eun in the chair-back, phone pinched between ear and shoulder, the other hand drawing circles on the notebook.

“But I’ll say it once. I helped because of you. It has nothing to do with that person.”

From the other end, Goo Hara’s voice came faintly, not clear, the tone carrying a question.

“What do you mean what? The whole thing was extremely unpleasant!”

The other end went a little higher, something like “that shouldn’t be.”

“What shouldn’t? Eonni, do you have some misunderstanding of him? That person’s attitude is outrageous, no honorifics at all, walked in like it was his house, got pushed and pushed straight back. I have never in my life seen someone asking a favour behave like that.”

The more she talked the more she wound up. The pen spun faster.

“And — eonni said he was a flower-boy puppy, right?”

The pen hit the desk.

“Where’s the puppy? That’s a Tibetan mastiff!”

The other end went fully silent.

Lee Ji-eun opened her mouth to go on, then the corner of her eye caught the door.

Han-teuk standing there.

The door open.

He had come in and she hadn’t heard.

They looked at each other one second.

“Eonni, tonight.”

Lee Ji-eun hung up, put the phone on the desk, packed the face from a moment ago away, put on a face that said this was already over.

But on the bridge of her nose a thin sweat still sat, from talking too hard just now.

Han-teuk pretended not to see.

He shut the door, went to his desk, sat, opened the computer.

Quiet about ten seconds.

“You’re close with that debt collector?”

Lee Ji-eun’s voice floating from behind.

Han-teuk turned the chair.

“Sort of… we know each other.”

“You know a debt collector?”

Han-teuk heard it. There was something under the line.

Thought, decided to explain anyway, or Si-on’s image in Lee Ji-eun’s head would take root for good on “debt-collector rascal.”

“He’s not a debt collector.”

“Mm?”

“He’s an actor. The collection company was living the part, for a role.”

Lee Ji-eun against the chair-back, both hands crossed on her stomach, the face a “keep making it up.”

“It’s true. And the Kim Se-jeong thing — at the time he was protecting them, the mother and daughter. The company was going to send someone harsher. He moved in himself, to stand in front. Later he destroyed the IOU with his own hands, and the money he put in to cover them was his own. I shot it.”

These things he had only worked out later himself.

At the time, locked by the throat on the floor of a half-basement, Han-teuk had not thought this man was any kind of good person.

Lee Ji-eun thought back.

It had in fact been Section Chief Bae Jong-han who told Han-teuk to go shoot. She had just been too busy then, and forgotten to watch.

“An actor?”

“Mm.”

“What’s he been in?”

“That I don’t really know… but he seems to have just finished an independent film.”

Han-teuk thought, pulled one piece out of memory.

“With Choi Seol… Jin-ri.”

The chair made a very short sound.

Lee Ji-eun’s body had suddenly gone forward, the spring in the back compressed and then coming back.

“Peach?”

Her voice half a step higher than a moment ago.

Han-teuk jumped a little at the reaction.

“Mm… Choi Jin-ri. I heard it’s an independent film. I don’t remember the director…”

“He did a film with Peach?!”

“Seems so.”

“When did they shoot?”

“Should be not long ago.”

Lee Ji-eun put her back into the chair, looking at the ceiling.

Peach.

Choi Jin-ri.

Now someone was telling her that the man who had just been in her office, who had made her angry enough to want to throw a pen, had shot a film with Peach.

This information needed a minute.
