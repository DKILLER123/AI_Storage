Chapter Forty-Four

A Twenty-Million-Won Editing Fee

July 23 8:00 a.m. The bathroom, then the corridor The headline that arrived before the toothpaste
July 23 Eight in the morning Baek Si-on's flat, Mapo · washstand Five strokes along the left molars, then the right

Baek Si-on stood at the washstand brushing his teeth. Five strokes along the left molars, then he switched to the right.

His cousin's voice detonated through the door.

“Cousin!!!”

There it was again.

The same volume and pitch as yesterday. That had been the Melon number one.

This one didn't sound like that.

Eun-ah reached the bathroom doorway with the phone practically pressed into his face. On the screen was an OSEN push notification:

OSEN · exclusive

[Exclusive] The mysterious overnight-chart newcomer Baek Si-on — revealed to have worked as a violent debt collector? What lies beneath the veil of independent music!

Si-on's mouth was full of foam. He squinted at it for a few seconds, leaned to spit, and took the phone, scrolling down.

The article was short.

An OSEN reporter, citing an "anonymous insider," claimed that after Baek Si-on was discharged from the military, he'd been employed by a private debt-collection firm, doing door-to-door collections.

The article never used the word "violence" past tense to condemn him. The headline did. The body only wrote that "multiple parties involved have reported intimidation and threatening behaviour during the collection process."

No specific date. No place. No hard evidence of any kind.

None of it mattered.

In the court of public opinion, the headline was the verdict.

He scrolled to the comments.

seoul_ghost

Hahaha, so a no-namer suddenly pops up rigging charts and it turns out he's a debt collector trying to be a star?

chart_watcher

Violent debt collection + midnight chart-rigging. The perfect combo. This guy deserves to be on the charts?

u_homegirl

HOW does IU even know this person?? Get a statement out NOW and distance yourself!

true_indie

Independent music? Hilarious. More like independent debt collection.

Eun-ah was staring at him, her whole body like a kettle that had boiled over and lifted its own lid.

“Cousin! What's going on?! Is it the boss of that collection company selling you out?!”

Si-on set the toothbrush into the cup, turned on the tap, and rinsed. Spat. Wiped his mouth.

“Let me think.”

He hung the towel on the rack and walked to the living room. Eun-ah was right behind him, her breathing loud.

He sat down on the sofa, arms crossed, staring at the ceiling.

The collection company.

No.

Private debt collection. A grey zone, skirting the edge of the law. The bosses live more cautiously than rats. The company is registered under someone else's identity, the office is leased out of a room in a shantytown, and the workers aren't even signed to formal contracts — wages are paid in cash.

And the thing those people fear above all else?

Light.

Exposure.

If the media lifts the curtain, then within a week the complaint letters of dozens of debtors, the labour arbitration, even criminal proceedings, pile onto a desk at the prosecutor's office.

Voluntarily feeding your own employee's information to a media reporter?

That's drawing an arrow on your own hideout and writing, police, this way.

Only a madman would do it.

So where had the leak come from?

He hadn't worked at that firm long. The number of people who knew he'd done this kind of work was small. Besides his family, only Han Teuk and the few others.

The people inside the collection company knew, true. But those people didn't even remember his real name.

That information chain was very short.

But someone had found the gap in the short chain.

Si-on's brow slowly pulled together.

He didn't like this feeling.

The collection work itself — he wasn't afraid of it being dragged up. Living it for research, working a temp job — he'd broken no law, and with the Kim Se-jeong family he'd been helping people.

What unsettled him was the timing.

The chart-rigging storm hadn't even settled, and hot on its heels came a dark-history exposé.

The two attacks had arrived less than twelve hours apart, as if they'd been queued up in order. First the achievement, then the character. Stacked together, they didn't make one plus one equals two. They made a fusion reaction.

✿ ✿ ✿

July 23 Morning The sofa, a phone, a decision The two phones, and the call

Any third party wanting to speak up for him at this point ran headlong into the same gate:

Are you sure you want to stand up for a former debt collector?

Including LOEN.

Including IU.

Si-on picked up his phone, opened the contacts, found Han Teuk's name, and dialled.

Baek Si-on → Han Teuk · midday · Seoul

“Si-on? What's the matter?”

Han Teuk had probably already seen the news.

“That video you shot last time, helping Director Bae — the one of me handling the Kim Se-jeong mother-and-daughter contract. Still have it?”

“Yes.”

“Can you put a pixel blur over the boss's face, and blur the company name, address, door number — anything that could trace back to the collection firm?”

“How fast do you need it?”

“Can it be quick?”

“An hour. It's just a mask in the editing program.”

“I'll come to your office.”

“Good.”

The line went dead.

Si-on stood, went into the bedroom, changed his trousers, and picked up the keys and wallet from the table into his pocket.

Eun-ah stood too, bag already slung over her shoulder — and was stopped.

“You've got a new job.”

“What?”

“Go to Ewha. Keep my mother company.”

Eun-ah blinked.

“If she hasn't seen what's online, don't let her know. Carry her bag, pour her water, walk her from the classroom to the parking lot.”

“And if she has seen it?”

“Tell her it's fine.”

Eun-ah opened her mouth to say "I'll come with you." But she saw the look on her cousin's face, and it turned into:

“Okay.”

Si-on nodded, pulled the door open, and stepped out.

July 23 Midday A bank, then LOEN Entertainment Two hundred million, and a USB stick

Stop one. The bank.

Si-on slid his ID and bank card across the counter, signed, and waited fifteen minutes.

He withdrew two hundred million won in cash.

The money filled a carry-on duffel bag.

Stop two. LOEN Entertainment.

By the time he arrived, Han Teuk hadn't come down yet.

He sat on the chair in the first-floor lobby, waiting. The two receptionists in white shirts recognised him — he'd signed the visitor log last time. One of them glanced his way a little longer than she should have; she'd probably seen the headline this morning too.

He didn't pay it any mind.

Twenty minutes later.

"Ding."

The elevator arrived. Han Teuk jogged out and, seeing Si-on, handed him the USB stick.

Si-on took it and turned it over.

“The blur's on?”

“All of it. The boss's face, the company name, the door number, the street signs in the shot that could give the location away. Everything's masked.”

Si-on nodded and slid the stick into his pocket.

Then he bent down, pulled the duffel zipper, and drew out a thick stack of cash.

Fifty-thousand-won bills. Han Teuk estimated four hundred notes as his eyes fell on it.

“Editing fee.”

Si-on pressed the money into his arms.

Han Teuk caught it reflexively with both hands.

“Wait…… it's just a few pixels, a couple of clicks in an editing program and you're giving me twenty million?”

“Too little?”

“It's too MUCH!”

Han Teuk lifted the money to push it back into Si-on's hands. But Si-on had put his hands in his pockets, physically shutting down the receiving channel.

“If you feel you don't deserve it, then pass on some of what you know as a manager to my cousin. She's new at it. There's plenty she needs to learn.”

Han Teuk's mouth opened. It fought for a second.

Teaching Baek Eun-ah to be a manager wasn't hard work, and he was willing to do it. But taking someone's twenty million and then going to teach that person's cousin — turn that logic over however you wanted and it still read like collecting tuition.

“But—”

“Do you have the number of the editor-in-chief at D-Sub?”

The subject had already been bent away.

Han Teuk's brain ran for a second.

“I don't. Director Bae does.”

“Ask him for it and send it to me.”

Han Teuk watched the expression on Si-on's face for a moment.

D-Sub.

Dispatch.

The nuclear weapon of the Korean entertainment industry.

“All right. I'll send it to you on KakaoTalk in a minute.”

“Mm.”

Si-on hoisted the duffel bag and turned toward the lobby door.

He'd gone three steps. Stopped.

“Oh. And my cousin's a minor. Keep your hands to yourself.”

“YAH! It's really—!”
