Chapter Seventy-Two

The Snore Was Eun-ah's Smoke Screen

August 26 In flight · 11.5 hours Korean Air KE927 · Incheon → Paris CDG First class, 1-2-1, and a partition that stays down

Korean Air KE927.

Incheon to Paris CDG.

Flight time, eleven and a half hours.

First class sat at the very front of the cabin.

1-2-1 layout.

[Window A] — aisle — [Centre D][Centre E] — aisle — [Window F].

Between D and E there was a motorised partition that could be raised and lowered. Raised, the two sides were invisible to each other. Lowered, it became a side-by-side double space.

Baek Si-on took D.

Baek Eun-ah took E.

Park Ji-hun took F.

Choi Jin-ri was in F of the first row.

About forty minutes after takeoff, the seatbelt light went off.

When the attendant pushed the trolley by, Baek Si-on asked for a glass of ice water.

Baek Eun-ah asked for orange juice, drank two sips, set the cup on the tray, then fished an eye mask and a tablet out of the seat-pocket pouch.

"Cousin, I'm going to the restroom."

"Mm."

Baek Si-on didn't look up. He was paging through a Paris travel guide he'd grabbed at an airport bookstore.

Though the destination was Venice, the layover at CDG would be a while, and he wanted to see if there was anything worth wandering near the airport.

Baek Eun-ah stood with the eye mask and tablet, and crossed the aisle.

She didn't go to the restroom.

She walked straight to seat F at the front.

Choi Jin-ri had already changed into the grey cotton pajama set first class provided, curled in the seat, a thin blanket over her knees, phone held up in front of her face as she scrolled.

Baek Eun-ah bent down and tapped the partition twice, lightly.

"Jin-ri eonni."

Choi Jin-ri lowered the partition, tilted her head to look.

"Eun-ah? What's up?"

Baek Eun-ah leaned in a little, her face arranged into an expression of great suffering.

"Eonni, do you sleep well?"

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

"All right… why?"

"My cousin snores."

Baek Eun-ah scrunched her brows into a knot, as though she'd been wronged somehow.

"Really loudly. I just can't—"

Choi Jin-ri, idol for so many years at SM, had flown first class more times than Baek Eun-ah ever had.

The electric partition between D and E muffled over seventy percent of the sound between the two sides. Add the noise-cancelling ear plugs the airline provided, plus the engine's own steady low-frequency hum.

Even if Baek Si-on snored in a duet, seat E was basically deaf to it.

But Choi Jin-ri didn't run that calculation at all.

Or rather, in her subconscious, the very impulse to calculate never arose.

"Oh."

Choi Jin-ri straightened the blanket beside her.

"Then I'll swap seats with you."

Baek Eun-ah's round face unfolded instantly.

"Eonni you're the best!"

The tone dripped with sweetness.

Choi Jin-ri folded the thin blanket twice, tucked the phone into her pajama pocket, stood on the cabin-slippered carpet, grabbed her small carry bag, checked the seat area to make sure nothing was left, and headed into the middle section carrying the blanket and bag.

August 26 In flight First class · seats D & E, partition down Page seventeen, and a pair of seats wide open to each other

Baek Si-on had turned to page seventeen, "Ten Hidden Photo Spots on Montmartre."

Movement came from the seat beside him.

He tilted his head naturally.

Choi Jin-ri was side-on, laying the blanket across seat E, bending over, hair falling from her shoulders.

First-class pajamas were a loose cotton fabric, dark grey, with a tiny airline logo on the chest.

When she bent, the front of the pajama top fell forward naturally.

Baek Si-on's gaze stopped on that swell for less than a second.

Then it turned back.

He turned the page.

Page eighteen.

He didn't read a single word.

Choi Jin-ri sat down, tugged the corner of the blanket over her knees, and glanced sideways at the seat next to her.

Baek Si-on was reading.

Expression earnest.

She glanced again at the partition controls.

The partition was lowered now.

Between the two seats, everything open to view.

She didn't raise it.

"Where's your manager?"

Baek Si-on's voice came from beside her.

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

The topic had come out of nowhere. She'd expected him to ask "why did you swap over" or something.

"Mm?"

"Your manager."

Baek Si-on turned a page, gaze still on the paper.

"Where is he?"

"Economy."

Choi Jin-ri's voice came soft.

Korean entertainment companies were rigidly strict on travel standards.

When a regular artist travelled abroad, the company would spring for a business or first class for the artist alone. The accompanying executive manager and assistants all went in economy.

"Go tell the flight attendant to upgrade him."

"Huh?"

"An upgrade."

"The company won't reimburse it… the difference between economy and first, the admin office won't approve it."

"I'll pay for it."

Choi Jin-ri looked at his profile, trying to read something off his expression.

But that face gave nothing.

"…Oh."

Choi Jin-ri stood up from her seat and padded a few steps forward in her slippers, finding the flight attendant straightening the trolley.

She said a few quiet words.

The attendant glanced at the empty seat in the cabin, nodded, and picked up the intercom to notify the economy crew.

Choi Jin-ri walked back and sat down again.

"Done. They're going to call him."

About five minutes later, the first-class curtain was pulled back.

The manager in the dark polo shirt walked over and stopped beside Baek Si-on's seat.

The partition was down, so he bent slightly and looked at Baek Si-on, then at Choi Jin-ri beside him.

No partition between the two of them.

The manager's gaze rested on that picture one second longer.

Before he could say anything, Baek Si-on spoke first.

"It's a long flight. Economy's too rough on you."

The manager leaned forward a touch — not a bow, but something with that intention.

"Mr. Baek, truly, thank you. You really didn't have to spend the money—"

"Have a good trip."

Baek Si-on finished, reached out, and pressed the partition control.

The partition on the outer side of seat D rose slowly.

The manager's face disappeared bit by bit behind the grey panel.

In the end only the tail of a "All right, thank you sir" floated over the top of the partition.

August 26 In flight First class · D & E, partition up The hum of the engines, and the quiet between two people

After the partition rose, the space was cut anew.

In first class, all that remained was the steady, continuous hum of the engines.

Choi Jin-ri held her phone, scrolled two screens of photos, exited, opened a downloaded movie, exited again.

Her finger slid around the screen, but her eyes weren't really watching.

She was hunting for a topic.

Instinct told her that two people sitting together not saying anything was a problem that needed solving.

She should find something to talk about.

The weather? Talking about weather on a plane was too stupid.

The Venice schedule? Too all-business.

What music have you been listening to lately? Wouldn't that question be too deliberate?

Her thumb stopped on the screen. The list of topics in her head had scrolled to the bottom and was still just blank.

It had been the same last time.

At his dining table in Yeonnam-dong, after she'd handed over the lunchbox, she'd sat there racking her brains for the next topic.

And Baek Si-on had demolished two lunchboxes in four minutes while she'd failed to come up with a single one.

She tilted her head and stole a look at the seat beside her.

Baek Si-on leaned back, turning pages at a slow pace, a page every two or three minutes. Occasionally he'd pause to look longer at some picture, finger tracing a light stroke along the page edge, then turn it.

Very quiet.

No intention of speaking.

And no sign of being embarrassed that she wasn't talking, either.

Choi Jin-ri flipped the phone over, face down on the armrest.

Stop. Don't chase it.

She curled herself down into the seat, pulled the thin blanket up to her chest, head tilted slightly toward Baek Si-on's side, resting against the headrest.

The hum of the engines. The faint rustle of a turning page. The even breathing of the person beside her.

These sounds mixed together, and it turned out they worked better than any sleep playlist.

She couldn't quite put a name to what it felt like.

Not excitement. Not nerves. Not the heart-thump that should come with being this close to a man.

Just… being there.

Being there, comfortably.

Nothing needed to be said. Nothing needed to be thought.

Choi Jin-ri's eyelids drifted down slowly.

The last thought before sleep: this must be what some people mean by "the vibes match."
