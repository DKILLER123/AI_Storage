October 8 A little past eleven Lee Ji-eun’s · a small lamp, a doorbell A kiss that would not stay on the set. A friend who did not need an invitation to press the bell.

A little past eleven.

Lee Ji-eun came home.

Shower. Hair. Skincare. Tomorrow’s clothes taken down from the hanger.

When every step of the list was done she got into the quilt, killed the main light, and left only the small lamp on the headboard.

The room went quiet.

The sounds from the set, in daylight, began to come back one by one.

She pulled the quilt to her chin and closed her eyes.

Worse, with the eyes closed.

The picture replayed itself.

The moment her arm went around Baek Si-on’s neck. The moment his hand landed at her waist and gathered in at the back. The second after they came off, when two foreheads were still almost touching. And the one she still could not quite name — the one where she went forward and kissed him again.

Could not name it?

Not entirely.

Choi Jin-ri’s appearing had a little to do with it. The main thing was how calm he had been.

The first time their mouths came apart she had already been taken in a little. Not because he was especially good at kissing.

Of course, he also… was.

Ji-eun, eyes closed, let her brow crease a very small amount.

Wrong. That was not the point. The point was after they came off.

Baek Si-on had not had the half-second of “sorry” a normal male actor showed after a kiss take. Even zero-point-five seconds. He had not had it.

In that second what rose in her was not shyness. It was fire.

She had suddenly felt herself very unattractive. Extremely. As if the nation’s little sister, the one with the music prizes, the Melon year-end regular, the woman every man in the country wanted to take home — as if Lee Ji-eun’s mouth were worth approximately zero.

Was that reasonable?

It was not.

So she had kissed him again. Not because she had lost control. At least she would not admit to losing control. It had been more like a counter: I will see how long you can keep pretending.

The result had been worse. He had, at the start, actually gone stiff. Afterward he had still been that flat calm.

Unspeakable.

At that thought she kicked once under the quilt. The quilt rose and fell.

She sat up. Got down. Bare feet on the floor, into the bathroom. Light on. The person in the glass squinted at the sudden brightness.

Ji-eun put both hands on the edge of the sink and stared at herself. Eyes large? Large. Skin good? Good. Mouth—

The look stopped. Then moved off fast. Do not think.

She put the fallen hair behind an ear. Turned the face. Another angle. Obviously pretty, thank you.

She confirmed the fact in the glass. Confirming it made her angrier.

“Honestly…”

“Best Actor, so what.”

“Next time I will make you panic once.”

“…”

She was still muttering, about to kill the light and go back to bed, when the doorbell rang.

Her motion stopped. A little past eleven. Who.

She went to the entry, put her eye to the peephole, looked out.

A woman in the corridor light. Yoo In-na. Ji-eun’s best friend. Also the kind of person who could, with a clean conscience, press a doorbell at this hour.

Ji-eun: “…”

She remembered. They saw each other three to five times a week. Sometimes late food. Sometimes a drink. Sometimes nothing — one person collapsed on one end of a sofa, each on a phone, and then, now and then, looking up to curse some lunatic the day had produced.

Tonight had been booked too. But because of the kiss on the set, because of Baek Si-on, because of that “or else,” because of — anyway. She had forgotten it clean.

Ji-eun closed her eyes. Done for.

She opened the door. The door had barely moved when Yoo In-na’s brows went up.

Yoo In-na

Actress · DJ · Ji-eun’s unnie who can ring a bell at eleven

Real · Raise the Volume, a laugh with no edge, a sofa that already knows the weather

They meet three to five times a week. Late food, a drink, or nothing at all except two phones and a curse for the day’s lunatic. Tonight she came because the date had been real, even if Ji-eun had forgotten it for a kiss.

“Yah. You stood me up?”

“I wrote you. You didn’t answer. I called. You didn’t pick up. I thought—”

Ji-eun looked down at her own pyjamas.

“Sorry, eonni. I forgot.”

“Forgot?”

“Mm.”

“You forgot me?”

“Not eonni. The meeting. Today’s meeting.”

“Is there a difference?”

“…There is.”

Yoo In-na looked at this honest confession and the fire she had prepared snagged.

“Fine. You’re already in bed. I’m going.”

She turned. Ji-eun’s hand went out at once and caught the sleeve.

“Don’t go.”

In-na looked back.

“What?”

“Come in.”

“Weren’t you going to sleep?”

“Not now.”

Ji-eun pulled her in. In-na was pulled half a step, still complaining.

“Yah. Slippers.”

“In the cabinet.”

In-na changed, walked into the living room, and slapped the main light on. The room went bright. Ji-eun squinted on instinct.

In-na folded her arms in the middle of the floor and looked at her.

“Talk.”

“About what?”

“Because of what?”

Ji-eun sat on the sofa with a cushion.

“Work.”

“No.”

“What’s no?”

“Forgetting a date can be work. That face is not work.”

Ji-eun buried her chin in the cushion.

“What face?”

In-na came and bent and stared.

“Guilty. Irritable. Can’t sleep. So there is something else.”

Ji-eun, under that look, was a little guilty. She moved her eyes and forced a change.

“Eonni.”

“Mm.”

“Am I pretty?”

In-na’s brows went up. She straightened slowly.

“Oh.”

Ji-eun looked up.

“What is oh?”

In-na looked at her.

“The man who put your weather in a mess. Who?”

“What?”

She sat up.

“Why did we jump to a man?”

“A woman asking her friend at midnight if she is pretty. Ninety percent, a man.”

“The other ten?”

“An ex.”

“…”

The sofa dipped when In-na sat. She did not push. She only reached and gathered Ji-eun, cushion and all, into her.

“Talk.”

Ji-eun’s head found the shoulder on its own. In-na knew her. Pulling a person in at midnight was not wanting the date to continue, and not insomnia that wanted chat. She needed a place for the weather to go. At such hours In-na did not have to give advice, and did not have to decide right and wrong. Sitting quietly was enough. Wait until she could not hold it.

Indeed. About a minute.

Ji-eun opened her mouth.

“He was really too much.”

In-na’s brow moved a little.

“How, too much?”

“Just…”

Ji-eun stopped. Daylight again. Carpet. Sofa. Grey knit. Her arm going up. Baek Si-on looking down. Then the kiss. She buried her face deeper in the cushion.

“Just… no reaction at all.”

“What reaction did you want?”

“At least… at least a look that ducked. Not meeting the eyes.”

In-na looked at her. Three sentences. Enough. Case reconstructed. Ji-eun had shot a kiss with a man today. And the kiss was probably not the work-flow kind, a mouth touching a mouth. After the take, the other party had been entirely normal. So Ji-eun had developed a very serious, entirely mismatched-to-her-station doubt about her own appeal.

In-na held the laugh. She could not laugh. Laugh now and Ji-eun would detonate.

“Maybe he’s the still-waters kind.”

Ji-eun blinked.

“Eh?”

In-na analysed it with a straight face.

“People who look that calm don’t let you see that, inside, they have already taken off.”

Ji-eun’s eyes blinked once, slowly. That angle… she had not thought of it. Followed down, it was not entirely without reason. Who was Baek Si-on. Venice Best Actor. Control of weather and micro-expression at a peak, of course. So. Maybe he had gone home and replayed it in bed too. Maybe he had stared at a ceiling.

Ji-eun’s face eased, bit by bit. Mm. Reasonable. Very reasonable. It had to be that. Still-waters bastard.

She lifted her face out of the cushion.

“Thank you, eonni.”

In-na patted her head.

“Don’t.”

Then, very naturally:

“So who is he?”

Ji-eun’s face stopped. Two seconds later, small:

“Baek Si-on.”

“Oh?”

The name In-na knew, of course. Not only because Korea was currently made of his news. Because in a few days Baek Si-on was coming on her show, Raise the Volume, to talk a film. The team had already given her a file. She had, for homework, read all of it two days ago. From the file, he was probably not the still-waters kind. At least not in the traditional sense. The larger probability was that he and Ji-eun had not sparked. Or at least had not sparked at once in the direction Ji-eun wanted.

In-na put a hand on Ji-eun’s head and rubbed once, lightly.

“Baek Si-on, ah.”

“Mm.”

Ji-eun buried her face in the cushion again.

In-na thought, and then very seriously gave an answer that would not injure a friend’s pride.

“Then you don’t have to mind it too much.”

Ji-eun looked up.

“Why?”

“Because that kind of man, the calmer he is, the more likely he is controlling it.”

“Really?”

“Really?”

In-na said it as if she had watched, with her own eyes, Baek Si-on go home and roll on a bed with a pillow.

Ji-eun slowly put her chin back in the cushion. In-na looked at that face and let her chest down. Good. Held, for now. Whether Baek Si-on was actually that animal. Not important. When a friend came apart at midnight, the truth was worth less than the comfort.
