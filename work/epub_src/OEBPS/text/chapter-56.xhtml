Chapter Fifty-Six

The Rise Starts With the Hair

July 26 Evening Baek Si-on's flat, Mapo A watermelon, a phone call, and a door that won't stay shut

Bieber's team had answered far faster than anyone had counted on.

Baek Si-on had barely made it home. The air-conditioner had just started to push cool air, the watermelon had just been cut, and the very first slice hadn't made it to his mouth yet when his phone rang.

Jung Jae-joon.

Jung Jae-joon

“Come back.”

“I just got home.”

“They emailed back.”

Baek Si-on looked down at the watermelon in his hand.

The toothpick was still stuck in it, and the juice from the cut face was running down the lid.

He put the melon back in its plastic box, sealed the lid, and shoved it into the fridge.

Then he walked out the door.

Baek Eun-ah had just walked in herself. She'd managed to get one shoe off when she heard Baek Si-on calling her out from the other end of a phone call.

“Where are we going now?”

“Jung Jae-joon's.”

“I just got home!”

“So did I.”

Baek Eun-ah looked down at her bare left foot and the shoe still on her right.

She put the sock and the shoe back on.

July 26 Evening Hapjeong-dong, studio 401 One email, two terms, and a price that comes with a name

401's door wasn't locked. Push and it opened.

Jung Jae-joon sat in front of the mixing board, an email open on the computer screen, and beside it the Korean document he'd run through Papago himself.

“Take a look.”

Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah leaned in on either side of him.

The email wasn't long.

It was written by Scooter Braun's assistant, the phrasing thoroughly corporate. Once you scraped off the polite prefixes and suffixes, the core came down to two clauses:

Scooter Braun · offer for “What Do You Mean?”

Instrumental buyoutUSD 30,000 · one-time

Songwriting splitBaek Si-on 60% · Justin Bieber side 40%

Baek Eun-ah's brow pinched at the second line.

“Hold on… forty percent?”

She turned to Jung Jae-joon.

“The songwriting is your cousin's, right? From the melody down to the whole lyric concept, it's all his. Bieber only sings it. Why does he get forty percent of the songwriting copyright?”

It was a direct question. And the same one Baek Si-on was waiting to have answered.

Jung Jae-joon leaned back in his chair, both hands crossed behind his head.

“Because he's Justin Bieber.”

The look on Baek Eun-ah's face made it clear that answer hadn't satisfied her.

The unwritten rule of Western pop

There's an unspoken rule in the Western music business. When a top-tier artist records a song, even if he hasn't changed a single punctuation mark from start to finish, his team will insist his name go on the writing credits.

“Credit?”

“Yes. Co-writer. Get the credit, and you get the right to carve off a piece of the songwriting copyright.”

“Isn't that robbery?”

“It's a traffic lever.”

“Your boss's song times his name, that's the song times a global amplifier. Forty points is the rental fee for that amplifier.”

Baek Si-on had kept quiet the whole time. Only once the explanation finished did he ask:

“What's the industry standard for that credit split?”

“It never usually goes past twenty points. Eighty-twenty, or eighty-five-fifteen, are the common range. Bieber's side opened at forty points straight away — doubled it.”

“A probing offer?”

“Very likely.”

Baek Si-on nodded, then pressed on.

“Is there anyone at roughly the same level? I mean, in the same lane as Bieber, and with a need for this kind of sound.”

Jung Jae-joon leaned back and thought for a moment.

“Shawn Mendes.”

Baek Eun-ah's head snapped up.

That name she knew.

A Canadian. Fifteen years old this year. He'd gone viral off Vine covers, and just a few months ago Island Records had signed him. Tall, good-looking, guitar in hand, love ballads — girls all across North America were screaming.

“Mendes?”

“Right. Mendes is the industry's consensus pick for ‘the next Bieber.’ Same Canadian, same teenage fame, same social-media launchpad. The difference is that Bieber's been drowning in scandals these past two years, his image largely broken down — and Mendes just happens to fill the ‘clean boy’ gap in the market.”

Jung stopped for a beat.

“More importantly, the two artists' labels have a direct competitive relationship. If Bieber won't take a fair price, handing this song to Mendes isn't impossible. A fifteen-year-old newbie needs one defining single to crack open a debut album — and tropical house probably suits him even better than it suits Bieber.”

Baek Si-on went quiet for a good ten seconds. Then he pointed to the first line of the email on the screen.

“This price. You accept it?”

Jung Jae-joon shook his head.

“No.”

No hesitation. He'd answered it cleanly.

“Last time I worked with you on Way Back Home, that was a work-for-hire deal. You paid, I did the job, the copyright was all yours, money and goods settled. I took that model because we'd just started working together.”

He nudged his chair forward a little.

“But this one's different.”

He lifted a hand and pointed at the letters “Justin Bieber” on the screen.

“That's Bieber. Even just his Spotify streaming numbers are astronomical. Thirty thousand dollars to buy out my production? I'd have to be a complete idiot to say yes.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

As expected.

No clear-headed independent producer would ever pick a one-and-done deal at a scale like this. Thirty grand was a fair pile of cash, but next to the long-tail revenue this song could throw off, it wasn't even the first digit of a rounding error.

“So how much do you want?”

“A point split.”

Jung rapped his finger on the tabletop.

“Four percent of the master.”

He paused, and the corner of his mouth moved.

“Three works too.”

The concession had come fast.

Not from doubt — from leaving Baek Si-on a little room to negotiate.

Baek Si-on didn't counter it.

He turned toward the folding chair in the corner.

“Agent Baek.”

That address made Baek Eun-ah's spine straighten a full two centimetres.

“Yes!”

“You've heard all the conditions.”

Baek Si-on held up two fingers.

“First — songwriting copyright. We want eighty. They get no more than twenty. Second — no buyout on the instrumental. Teacher Jung takes a three to four percent master point.”

Baek Eun-ah's thumb was flying across her memo pad, jotting and nodding.

“The negotiation is yours.”

Baek Eun-ah's hand froze for a second.

Last time, negotiating the distribution deal with LOEN, she'd been facing a Korean domestic business team — the language was common, the rules were common, and at least she could see the face of the person sitting across the table.

This time the person opposite her was Scooter Braun.

One of the sharpest music managers on the planet.

And the whole thing in English.

A little sweat had collected in her palm — but what her mouth said was:

“Understood.”

Baek Si-on gave no more instructions. He just waved a hand at Jung Jae-joon.

Jung was about to turn back to the mixing board when the gesture stopped him.

“What?”

Baek Si-on looked him up and down, sizing up that head of his.

The flaxen dye had faded until only the very tips still carried any colour. The black at the roots had crept over more than half the territory, so the whole thing looked like a tiramisu that had been sitting out for three days.

“Your hair's due for a re-dye.”

Jung Jae-joon blinked.

Then his gaze travelled from Baek Si-on's face to the top of Baek Si-on's head.

Buzz cut.

From the day they'd met until now, it had always been this buzz cut.

Jung reached out and pointed at Baek Si-on's head.

“You need to let yours grow out, too. Walk the Venice red carpet with that bald egg?”

Baek Si-on reached up and rubbed his own scalp.

Bristly under his fingertips.

Yeah. Time to grow it.
