Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Five

A Hundred Thousand Units Isn’t Even a Snack

Launch day · half an hour before doors

One pop-up counter · two shades · one queue already forming

Yeouido · Hyundai Department Store · the LAVUE pop-up

Yeouido.

Hyundai Department Store.

Half an hour before opening, Kim Ji-min, the staffer assigned to the LAVUE pop-up counter, came down to the first floor with her employee bag.

The mall lighting was already on.

The cleaners had just finished mopping; a faint scent of detergent still hung in the air, other brands’ counter staff were tidying their displays, and one by one the spotlights were waking up over the jewelry cases in the distance.

The LAVUE pop-up sat in the main first-floor artery.

A prime spot.

Pale pink and dark red split the counter down the middle — the left half belonged to No. 01, First-Love Peach; the right, to No. 02, Vampire Cherry.

Counter display · ad on loop

The central screen plays the commercial on repeat — Bae Joo-hyun lifting a cherry, then a peach, to her lips.

Below it, the display shelves: single tubes and the two-shade sets in clean ranks; the test-shade station stocked with mirrors, disposable applicators, remover cotton, and shade cards.

For today’s opening, Kim Ji-min had been through two full rounds of training.

The training, as drilled

How to read a customer’s skin tone at a glance.

How to control the inner-edge dosage of No. 02.

How No. 01 may ride just past the lip line for a fuller visual.

The complete two-shade layering demonstration — rehearsed until her hands could do it without her.

She had assumed today meant, at most, a few dozen Baek Si-on fans and Bae Joo-hyun fans lining up early — buy the product, take a photo, post one supportive note about an idol’s first business.

Then, passing the floor-to-ceiling glass facing the main entrance, she glanced outside.

Her steps stopped.

The queue ran from the mall’s front door all the way around the side of the building.

She followed its direction with her eyes.

More of it past the corner.

The mall’s security chief had already been called to the doors; the temporary barricade tape was going up.

❧ ❧ ❧

Ten o’clock sharp.

The front doors opened.

Shoppers headed for other floors funneled in through the right passage; LAVUE buyers were marshaled along the temporary railing to the counter.

Even with the split routing, the line at the counter stretched outward fast.

On the screen, Bae Joo-hyun lifted a cherry; dark-red juice touched her lips; the display cut to the full Vampire Cherry look. Plenty of people in the queue raised their phones to film it.

Kim Ji-min, following the training to the letter, prepared to demonstrate the two-shade layering for the first customer.

The customer arrived at the counter and opened with:

“Two-shade set. Two of them.”

Kim Ji-min’s demo script stalled in her throat.

“Would you like to try it fi—”

“No need. Watched the reviews.”

Scan. Pay. Bag. Next.

“Two No. 01, one set.”

“No shade test?”

“The reviews covered warm-toned skin. I can buy straight off that.”

Two full rounds of product training, compressed by real sales volume into training in the art of scanning a card.

Kim Ji-min had no complaints.

A demonstration cost several minutes.

A checkout cost the customer producing a bank card.

The second was far friendlier to the sales numbers.

❧ ❧ ❧

Ten forty-five.

Kim Ji-min noticed the stock boxes under the counter had gone conspicuously light.

She crouched and counted.

Of the inventory allotted to this store, exactly two boxes remained.

She ran to the stockroom to confirm. The number was unambiguous: the morning’s stock would not survive to noon.

She picked up her phone and dialed the Seongsu office.

Kim Ji-min → Choi Na-young · voice · the Yeouido counter, mid-morning

“Hello — this is Hyundai Department Store, Yeouido branch. Could central warehouse add five hundred more sets?”

Choi Na-young checked the system.

“Five hundred — no. One hundred and fifty is the most we can transfer.”

Kim Ji-min’s voice went urgent.

“A hundred and fifty won’t last an hour.”

“Every other store is requesting stock too.”

Another work phone was ringing on the Seongsu end. Choi Na-young said “hold please” and picked up the second line. The Pangyo store wanted stock. Pangyo put down; Sinchon called — No. 01 had sold out first, and No. 02 was down to a hundred-odd tubes. Before Sinchon was finished, Apgujeong was coming in too: the two-shade set’s number tickets had run out, and people were still joining the line.

Four work phones ringing at nearly the same moment — the Seongsu office had gone fully wartime. The duty-free channel wanted to pull the next production batch forward. CJ’s online mall sent a warning: without more stock, its homepage ad slot would become an empty link that only displayed “sold out” when clicked.

❧ ❧ ❧

Noon

One online restock · ten minutes · gone

The LAVUE site · the final wave

Noon.

The LAVUE site opened its remaining online stock.

A minute before launch, traffic had already slammed into the server’s warning line.

Choi Na-young stared at the dashboard.

The noon wave, as it fell

30,000 → 26,000 → 21,000 — at first she could still read the numbers.

Then the refresh rate lost the race with the orders; the figures changed faster than she could blink.

Singles zeroed out first. The two-shade sets, with the deeper allocation, lasted a stretch longer — and then they, too, went gray: SOLD OUT. Open to empty: under ten minutes.

❧ ❧ ❧

Afternoon.

The Hyundai branches reported sellouts one after another. When the last customers paid, the counter held nothing but display samples and tester tubes.

Kim Ji-min set the sign up beside the screen.

The counter, as signed

LAVUE No. 01, No. 02, and the two-shade set: today’s stock fully sold out.

Restock timing will follow the brand’s official announcements.

Even after the sign went up, people kept lining up at the counter.

“You really can’t sell the display units?”

“They’re tester samples multiple people have used. We can’t sell them.”

“I don’t mind.”

“Hygiene regulations don’t allow it.”

The customer stared at the empty shelves.

“What about the packaging, then?”

Kim Ji-min wondered whether she had misheard.

“Sorry — what?”

“Can I buy an empty box?”

“......”

LAVUE had not yet fallen far enough to sell empty boxes.

Though at today’s burn rate, that day might not be far either.

The customer sighed, and before leaving took a photo of the bare counter.

She posted it with a single line of caption.

Social feed · the empty counter

@took_a_day_off

Took a day off work specially — to visit a row of tester sticks.

floor 1

Adding my voice to the request: preorder option for the tester sticks, please.

floor 2

Seconded. Speaking for the entire queue behind her.

The post drew waves of resonance fast.

Nobody in the comments comforted her.

They all wanted to know whether the tester sticks could be preordered.

❧ ❧ ❧

Before the brand team could post its own battle report, the news had already traveled from the stores and the feeds to the media.

Naver’s entertainment and business desks hung the headlines almost simultaneously.

Naver · Entertainment

Naver · Business

Sold out online and off — long lines at the Hyundai Department Store LAVUE counter

Naver · Business

Naver · Entertainment

Naver · Entertainment

…

The media had outrun the brand.

Choi Na-young could only race the official report out first — the brand’s formal statement, before more inaccurate numbers got copied around.

The statement, as posted · LAVUE official

Thank you to every consumer for the support.

LAVUE’s first pre-sale: 40,000 units. Official launch: 100,000 units. Cumulative 140,000 units — all sold out.

The next batch is in accelerated production. Restock dates will be announced per actual capacity. No over-booking accepted.

…with regards, from all of us at LAVUE.

Consumer creativity, it turned out, was formidable: the restock demands had already evolved from ordinary complaints into literature. One person wrote a doggerel poem about waiting. One drew a comic of a consumer weeping before an empty counter. One left a comment: if you don’t restock, we delete every fancam recorded at Baek Si-on’s concerts.

❝

On the threat-scale of the fandom world, that one is rated nuclear.

— the fancam archive, held hostage for lip gloss

Choi Na-young was reaching to screenshot it for the archive when the office door swung open.

Baek Eun-ah walked in first, a cake box in her left hand, a bag on her right shoulder.

Behind her came a woman — baseball cap pulled low, mask over her face, carrying a bag of drinks and paper plates.

The four staffers took her for some brand-side person Eun-ah had brought along.

Until the mask came down.

Four people bounced out of their chairs at once.

“Irene!”

“Hello!”

Bae Joo-hyun stopped mid-step at the reception committee and waved both hands fast.

“Don’t get up — really, don’t. You’ve all worked more than enough today.”

They bowed, properly and in order, before sitting back down.

Baek Eun-ah set the cake on the central table.

“Cousin couldn’t make it today.”

Bae Joo-hyun added, very naturally:

“His wrap will run late.”

The sentence was out before she noticed the strange shape of it.

Eun-ah explaining the boss’s absence — perfectly reasonable. Her appending his schedule — that drifted past some line, as if the two of them stood on the same side of the desk, jointly informing the staff why only one of them had come.

She ducked fast and started aligning the drink bottles, labels all facing out.

With her hands occupied, the earlier sentence could pass for an ordinary supplement.

Choi Na-young’s thoughts went nowhere in that direction at all.

What surprised her was something else.

“You had no schedule today?”

“I did, actually.”

Bae Joo-hyun set the last bottle straight.

“The company planned to send me to the offline counter for the promotion. Then the product sold too fast — there wasn’t much work left for the endorser.”

The four girls laughed.

Hyundai’s stock had been critical from the moment the doors opened; by the time Bae Joo-hyun would have finished hair and makeup and arrived, the counter would likely have held one row of tester sticks.

Photographing the endorser before bare shelves could still make news.

The headline, frankly, wrote itself unluckily: ‘Irene visits the pop-up, warmly presents the sold-out sign.’

Baek Eun-ah untied the ribbon on the cake box.

“Clear the table first.”

Reports, empty cups, and charging cables were swept aside. The box opened — and the whole office’s colors brightened.

The cake, as opened

One round cake, split down the middle: pale-pink cream with peach flesh on the left, dense dark cherry on the right.

Where the colors meet, black chocolate script: “LAVUE — 140,000 sold out!”

Piped around it: four tiny sugar-cream figures — glasses girl, ponytail, short crop, hair bun.

Their outfits matched what the four staffers actually wore; the faces were four rice balls with features, but the identities were unmistakable.

The glasses girl leaned in close.

“There’s... us?”

Baek Eun-ah set her briefcase on a chair.

“Who else? It’s not as if we could put my cousin and Joohyun-unnie on a cake.”

“Although — the cake was chosen by Unnie personally,” she added.

Four heads turned in unison.

Bae Joo-hyun had been hoping to keep her distance from the credit. Now she owned it.

“I sent the shop screenshots from your startup diary.”

“The figures were done in a rush. They may not look that much like you.”

“They’re exact!”

The glasses girl pointed at her own sugar-cream glasses.

“This one you recognize at a glance.”

The product girl studied her piped double.

“Why am I holding a calculator?”

Bae Joo-hyun answered:

“The videos said that every time a restock comes up, you’re the one who runs the numbers first.”

The girl’s fingers stopped above the cake.

She had not expected Bae Joo-hyun to have actually watched the startup diary — and to remember what each of them was responsible for.

The meaning of the cake shifted on the spot. This was not a dessert grabbed on the way over. Bae Joo-hyun knew who they were.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Eun-ah did not let the room steep too long in warmth.

The day had produced enough emotional value.

Time to pay.

She opened the briefcase and set four envelopes down, one before each staffer.

Choi Na-young pressed the envelope’s thickness.

“What’s this?”

“Launch special bonus.”

Baek Eun-ah sat on the edge of the table.

“Two million won each.”

The four processed it slower than they had the cake.

The market-research girl recovered her voice first.

“Two million between the four of us?”

“Two million each.”

Baek Eun-ah picked up the cake knife and stripped its plastic wrap.

“Not salary. It doesn’t touch the account-growth bonus either. This is the launch reward my cousin approved personally.”

The office went quiet again.

Two million won approached a fresh graduate’s monthly wage at many small and midsize companies. These four had been hired less than two months ago — on salaries already well above their cohort, with a creativity award and an account-growth award already banked — and now each had one more envelope holding two million won sitting in front of her.

The market girl squeezed the envelope; it still didn’t feel real.

“Has the boss seen today’s numbers?”

“He has. He said — this is the kind of result that earns a bonus.”

Choi Na-young kept her head down.

The envelope’s edge had taken a small dent from her thumb.

When Professor Yoon Hye-ja had recommended her here, she’d thought only that her luck was absurd: a startup paying ridiculous wages, a brand project she couldn’t quite parse, and a boss the whole world recognized. She hadn’t imagined how much she herself could do.

Then the office had a name. Tube samples became real packaging. The startup account gathered followers. Product entered the warehouse. And 140,000 units emptied out inside a single selling cycle.

She could finally confirm it: the place Professor Yoon Hye-ja had sent her to paid benefits that mildly distorted fair competition in the Korean job market.

“What is there to cry about?”

Baek Eun-ah pushed the tissue box across.

Choi Na-young sniffed.

“I’m not crying.”

“Then what’s wrong with your eyes?”

“Staring at the back end too long today.”

“Mm. Occupational eye strain, consumer-payments division.”

Choi Na-young, embarrassed, pulled a tissue and pressed her eyes.

Bae Joo-hyun didn’t laugh at her.

She had once danced outside a supermarket in a too-thin stage costume; she had survived a rookie era of scarce resources and thick hate comments. When someone suddenly rules that your effort has value, the feelings are hard to hold entirely.

Especially when the ruling didn’t stop at “you worked hard.”

The envelope held an exact number.

❝

Baek Si-on’s way of expressing approval had always suited being deposited in a bank.

— the office consensus, sealed in four envelopes

❧ ❧ ❧

Bae Joo-hyun took over the cake knife herself.

“Which side do you want?”

“Peach.”

“Cherry for me.”

“Can I have both?”

“Of course.”

First she carved out the whole quadrant with the four sugar figures and set it, untouched, on its own plate. Nobody had the heart to eat that part.

The rest she divided by each person’s order.

Choi Na-young accepted her slice of cherry.

“Honestly, you didn’t have to come all this way.”

“The commercial did a lot of the work today too.”

Bae Joo-hyun set her fork on the plate.

“A commercial can only let people know the product exists.”

“Production, inspection, warehousing, distribution, customer service, stock transfers — people had to do every one of those.”

“One link missing, and today doesn’t sell a hundred thousand units.”

She said it with total seriousness. The face in the commercial belonged to her. The invisible work behind the commercial belonged to the four girls in front of her.

She cut another slice of peach.

“Thank you for these past weeks, for everything you did on his beh—”

The blade stopped in the pale-pink cream.

The word was already out. It could not be taken back.

She rode it downward and changed course:

“Thank you for everything you did for the brand.”

Choi Na-young, mid-bite, noticed nothing. The other two were photographing their sugar doubles.

Only Baek Eun-ah heard it clearly.

On his behalf.

The position of those two words was delicate. Delicate enough to put her back in that car ride to SM:

“Does Baek-sunbae pick at his food?”

“How does he eat that much and not gain weight?”

“Then he must have abs, right?”

And now the girl who once asked about abs was delivering her cousin’s gratitude to his staff — the relationship advancing with all the bearing of a Korean entrepreneur’s family member.

Bae Joo-hyun felt the attention and buried herself in cutting.

The knife met the paper board with a small sound.

“Eun-ah, which side do you want?”

“Cherry.”

Baek Eun-ah took her plate and asked nothing.

Bae Joo-hyun had strong pride, cared about face, and kept her small feelings pressed down where nobody could see them. Besides, her cousin’s love life was already complicated enough.

❝

As her agent, she could handle contracts. Handling a battlefield of the heart should be billed separately.

— Baek Eun-ah’s internal rate card

❧ ❧ ❧

Cake finished, the six of them cleared the table together.

The four sugar figures went into a transparent box, to be eaten — or not — when Baek Si-on next visited the office.

The glasses girl produced the camera from the cabinet.

“A photo?”

Nobody objected.

Six people lined up in front of the LAVUE logo wall. Choi Na-young tried to put Bae Joo-hyun in the center; Bae Joo-hyun stepped aside instead and pushed the four staffers into the middle.

“Today was your result.”

Baek Eun-ah took the other side. The four staffers each held their bonus envelope. Eun-ah raised No. 01, First-Love Peach; Bae Joo-hyun held No. 02, Vampire Cherry.

The timer light blinked. The shutter fell.

In the photo, the four staffers stand dead center — smiling tiredly, and smiling wide. Baek Eun-ah and Bae Joo-hyun flank them: the most recognizable face in Seoul, the face the whole city could name off a bus, and she had handed the real center seat away.

The photo never went to the brand account. It was too obviously an internal keepsake; posting it would only refill the comments with orders to go screw on bottle caps.

Later, Choi Na-young had it printed and pinned it beside the office whiteboard.

One line of small text underneath.

The whiteboard · under the photo

LAVUE’s first sellout: 140,000 units!
