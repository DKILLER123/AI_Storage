The Fight Starts

October 8 8:40 am Seoul · a serviced apartment rented as a set Nail holes. A worn counter. A room that had to look lived in before a camera asked it to.

Hwang Soo-ah had booked the apartment three days out. Scuffed wood. A kitchen edge that had been used. Nail holes in the living-room wall. She liked rooms like that. Love Yourself did not want the ad-shine of a pretty couple in a pretty house. It wanted the marks two people had actually left.

Baek Si-on arrived to Lee Ji-eun already there. Jeong Han-teuk beside her, a face more serious than yesterday. Not because an MV was a war. Because last night, late, he had understood: his artist and his friend were going to shoot close. Wrong, and he could not name the wrong. Hwang Soo-ah at the monitor with the DP, first-light. Door sound. She turned.

“You’re here.”

“Director Hwang.”

A small bow. Ji-eun looked up. One second.

“Morning.”

“Morning.”

Polite. Like two people meeting on a job for the first time. Hwang Soo-ah’s mouth moved. Fine. She had tools.

“Don’t sit. Clothes.”

The rack. His: a loose grey knit over a white T, dark house trousers. Hers: cream sweater, a long skirt with room. Ji-eun let a breath go. At least this morning was not club armour. Fifteen minutes. He first. Park Ji-hun only took the five-centimetre crop and pushed it a little back with a matte wax, a direction, not a sculpture. Clean. Loose. A man who stayed in and made coffee on a Sunday. She five minutes later. The cream sweater a size large, the neck finding a collarbone. She was already small; the cloth made the outline softer. Makeup so thin it could be denied, every millimetre still counted by a team. Hwang Soo-ah watched them in one frame and the eyes narrowed. Yes. That weather.

At the side, Baek Eun-ah and Han-teuk in folding chairs, looking at the same two people in the middle of a living room. Two seconds. Han-teuk first.

“Does it… look like?”

“Like what?”

“Like they actually did.”

Eun-ah turned. He added, fast:

“I mean the picture.”

She looked back. No talk between the two of them, and still the height, the colour, the stand, the Sunday cloth — a pairing the lens would invent if you let it. A few seconds. Then a judgement with no professional cover:

“I still think Jin-ri eonni sits better next to my cousin.”

Han-teuk lifted the iced Americano. He was not going into that water. That water drowned people.

“All right, don’t just stand.”

Hwang Soo-ah took a white Polaroid off the side table, put it in Ji-eun’s hands.

“You two. Take some of yourselves.”

“Selfies?”

“Mm. Later they live on the wall, the fridge, the nightstand, the shelf. Love that already happened cannot all be shot today. The room has to tell the viewer they lived here. And — warm-up.”

She did not want the first frame to be two people on a bed who had just changed clothes. Stiff made slow. Slow ate a day. Couple pictures broke the ice and put bodies at a distance the later takes could use.

Ji-eun lifted the camera at the window, tested light, turned to him, checked the stand, pointed.

“You in front.”

“Why?”

“Makes my face smaller.”

“Your face is already small.”

“It can be smaller.”

A reason with no hole. He took half a step. The height made the frame a problem. Arm at the end of itself, still wrong. She went up on the toes. Not enough. Again. He felt the person behind him rising and falling, looked.

“Want me to sit down a little?”

“No.”

Fast. Sit and it was oppa at an amusement park with a dongsaeng. She did not want that picture. She reset: him a little side, her face half out behind, chin on the line of his shoulder. Close enough for detergent in the knit. Faint. Good.

“Hold.”

Left hand around, hunting both faces. He watched her in the little glass, arm high, still protecting the small-face angle. The mouth bent without asking him. The shutter took that bend. White paper crawled out. She pinched it, shook twice from muscle.

“Don’t shake. Let it come.”

Hwang Soo-ah, from the side.

“Movies shake them.”

“Movies also let people walk out of bullets. You believe that too?”

The square went on the table. Milk to a picture. Him in front, a small smile. Her behind the shoulder, eyes bent, arm still in the work of holding a camera too high. Not a magazine. A fridge door. Hwang Soo-ah looked two seconds, nodded.

“Good. Again.”

October 8 Same morning A nanny van toward Gangnam · then a U-turn Work had come back. So had a quiet that a full diary could not quite kill.

Choi Jin-ri in the back, October streets running backward. After September’s half-stop, half-float, the calendar had remembered what a working idol’s squares looked like. SMTOWN. Fashion King heat. The Green Fly before November. Magazines. Brands. Radio. Interviews. One after another, no white left. That should have been a glad thing. It was. Busy was good. Busy meant not a room and a phone and a ceiling and a fridge compressor at three in the morning making the whole world too quiet. And she was not entirely glad. Because he was back in Korea and had not written.

She knew he was full. Fuller than she was. Knowing he was busy and he hasn’t written me were two facts that could both be true and still sit wrong in the chest. Red light. Phone. Thumb on the edge a long time. Then she didn’t not.

An MV. So he was actually working. Not not-writing. Working. The chest loosened a centimetre. Afternoon call still two hours out. Time. A word to the manager, a turn, a coffee shop on the way.

On set, Eun-ah had just sent the pin. Screen still awake. She looked up. Living room: Ji-eun in his arm, camera, angle.

“Come in a little more.”

“More and we’re stuck.”

“Stuck doesn’t kill anybody. Hurry.”

He moved. Click. Paper. Two heads over it. Eun-ah looked at that, looked at the pin she had just thrown, looked back at the next frame already starting.

A line went through her that had no business being there. Done. This coffee might come out hot.

Jin-ri at the curb. Eun-ah in the doorway, October taking her bangs sideways, a hand on the hair, a wave at the van. Makeup already on for the afternoon. The manager behind with two large bags.

“Eonni.”

Eun-ah came down the stair, looked at the bags.

“Why this many?”

“Crew drinks too.”

A smile. Eun-ah reached; the manager kept it.

“I’ve got it.”

“Thanks, oppa.”

Then Jin-ri.

“Eonni, you look good today.”

“You too. Did you lose? Face is smaller.”

“Is it? Maybe Los Angeles.”

“Mm. Darker, it shrinks.”

“…What theory is that?”

Standard girl-greeting. About forty percent true. The rest manners. Before the lift Eun-ah took Jin-ri’s arm. Jin-ri looked at the hand.

“What?”

“Eonni. I should say first.”

“I know.”

“It’s just — MVs have a story. The story has a past. The director will shoot some close things. It’s work. You’re an actor. You know, right?”

Jin-ri smiled. Mouth and eyes both.

“I’ve watched more close scenes than you’ve watched variety. Shooting is shooting. I can tell.”

“Oh… good.”

Half a breath out. Half kept. She knew Jin-ri. Professional actor was four words that might not be enough. The person was here. The coffee was bought. Stopping her now would look stranger. Into the lift, a prayer she did not say: please not a bed. Please.

Ding. Eun-ah first. A short hall, a turn, an open door. Jin-ri in behind. First: Hwang Soo-ah’s monitor. Second: stands and bounce making a half-room. Third: the middle of the half-room.

He on his back. One hand on a forehead. The other held. She kneeling, pulling that hand, muttering. He uses a little force. Her weight goes. She lands on his chest. Hwang Soo-ah’s eye lights.

Hold.

The camera keeps walking. Grey knit a soft edge. Cream cuff on his chest. Two colours deciding to be one.

Eun-ah went numb. Looked. Jin-ri’s smile from the door was still the same shape. The eyes had stopped doing the smile. Down a centimetre: the bag-strap white in the hand. Eun-ah’s heart did a stupid knock. She put herself behind Jin-ri and became a signal, waving at the bedroom like a fool. Oppa. Here. Jin-ri eonni. Pull it in. She almost used the tablet as a bounce. He had Ji-eun in his eye, which is to say the Ji-eun the shot had asked for. The door did not exist. Eun-ah had no language. Life was a wheel. Once Jin-ri had walked in to take a picture. Now she stood in a door and watched someone else laugh on his chest. Who held that.

“Cut.”

Hwang Soo-ah at last.

“Good. That one.”

Ji-eun pushed up. Ears a little red. Face still a professional’s. Heart could run. Face could not. He sat, smoothed the knit she had wrinkled. They started for the monitor. Two steps and Ji-eun’s look went over stands and bounce and found the door.

Choi Jin-ri. A hitch. Short. Short enough that if Eun-ah had not been watching she would have missed it. Next second the smile was a smile again.

“Peach!”

A small run. Jin-ri had not finished putting the last weather out of her eyes before she was in the hold.

“Set visit?”

“Yes.”

“To see me, or to see him?”

“Eonni, of course.”

Jin-ri could always talk hard. Ji-eun put a chin on the shoulder. Over it: Jin-ri’s manager handing cups down a line. They had known each other long enough. Ji-eun’s drinks were not a mystery. Coffee was an excuse. She said none of that. She held, and patted the back.

“Thanks, Peach.”

Soft. Soft enough Jin-ri almost asked her to say it again. Then the hold opened, an arm through hers, toward the monitor.

“You’re here. Look at the take.”

Jin-ri’s body did a small stiff thing.

“…The take?”

“Mm. It was good. Director Hwang liked it.”

The smile had no blade in it. No show. Jin-ri looked at that face and thought, suddenly, that she had not come to visit a set. She had come to sit an exam. Eun-ah’s eyes went between them twice. A bad weather stood up. Ji-eun was not not-knowing. She knew. She was walking Jin-ri to the glass anyway. What was that. A soft declaration. A pressure test between unnies. Eun-ah would not finish the thought. The more she finished it the more it looked like a preview of a war her cousin had not agreed to be in.
