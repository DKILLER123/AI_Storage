Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Five

Producer Lee Advances Step by Step

Past midnight · the booth, take after take

One trial vocal that quietly became the final

Nonhyeon-dong · the control room

“Stop.”

The instrumental cut out once more.

Son Seung-wan held her headphones, no longer daring to ask what had gone wrong, only waiting for the verdict through the booth glass.

From the first line’s tail note floating too high, to breathing straight into the mic, to the emotion being too tidy — she had been corrected too many times.

By now, even if Lee Ji-eun told her her left foot hadn’t stood invested enough while singing, she would adjust it on the spot.

“That’s enough. Come out.”

Son Seung-wan waited a beat, confirming the sentence wasn’t followed by “rest a while and again,” before taking off the headphones, pushing open the soundproof door, and standing by the console.

Having sung the same lyric too many times, her ears had lost the ability to judge objectively.

She could now only tell the difference between the first take and the last by the track names Lee Ji-eun had marked.

Failure seemed acceptable.

At least she now knew how strict a top producer could be about detail.

That experience alone was precious.

A moment later, she hesitated, then asked:

“Sunbae, when will I know the audition result?”

Baek Si-on was still organizing tracks.

“What result?”

“I mean……”

Son Seung-wan’s voice got smaller.

“Whether I passed.”

Baek Si-on turned to look at her:

“This is the final version.”

Son Seung-wan froze.

“But Eun-ah said tonight was only a trial.”

“You passed.”

“……”

Son Seung-wan glanced back at the microphone in the booth, then turned to Lee Ji-eun behind the console.

“Are you really using this take?”

“Mm.”

‘Everytime’ didn’t need a singer turning every high note into a professional qualification exam; what truly mattered was that feeling, between the male and female voices, of the heart skipping fresh each time they met.

Son Seung-wan’s current experience was enough for that.

Besides, Baek Si-on’s upcoming schedule left no room to hold a large open audition.

Son Seung-wan finally confirmed it had really happened.

She pressed both hands to her seams and bowed deeply.

“Thank you, sunbae!”

Straightening, she turned to Lee Ji-eun and gave a ninety-degree bow:

“Sunbae, thank you for your hard work just now.”

She had also realized: every time Lee Ji-eun called stop, she was polishing the final master to official-release standard.

Detailed demands were only natural.

As the recording light went out, Lee Ji-eun’s attack rating came down with it, and the sweet-little-sister business hours restarted.

“Don’t thank me. You sang it through yourself.”

She smiled.

“You did very well today.”

Son Seung-wan nodded quickly.

“Ne, I’ll keep working hard.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on handed the final project to Jung Jae-joon and turned to Alan.

“First time watching a K-pop recording — how does it feel?”

Alan thought for a while.

Baek Eun-ah had already shown him an English translation of ‘Everytime’s lyric gist.

He couldn’t parse the specific Korean pronunciation, yet from the two singers’ emotional shift after each re-take, he caught something beyond language.

“I used to care mostly about melody and the Drop.”

“Hearing you record tonight, I realized the same melody conveys a completely different emotion just by changing the breath and the voice.”

“Lyrics change the meaning of a melody, too.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

For a young producer who mainly released pure-electronic work on web platforms, this was a natural cognitive progression.

“So?”

Alan answered:

“When I’m back in Norway, I want to redo ‘Fade’. Give it a female-vocal version.”

Baek Si-on was a little surprised.

His own arrival had pulled Alan into the professional music industry early, but history’s original direction still stubbornly poked its head out.

Alan still walked from ‘Fade’ to ‘Faded’.

Only this time, the trigger that made him realize the value of a vocal was a late-night K-drama OST session in a Korean studio.

“Good idea.”

Baek Si-on beckoned to Son Seung-wan.

“Seung-wan, come here a sec.”

She came over at once.

“Ne, sunbae.”

Baek Si-on pointed at Alan.

“Exchange contacts with him.”

Son Seung-wan didn’t follow.

“Me?”

“If he ever needs to test a female-vocal part, you can help record a version.”

Son Seung-wan hadn’t expected an OST job to accidentally open her a channel to a European producer.

Alan was equally surprised.

He had only just formed the idea of a female-vocal version, and Baek Si-on had already supplied a candidate who could sing and whose English pronunciation was natural enough.

“If I ever need it, may I trouble you?” Alan asked.

“Of course.”

The two young people exchanged contacts.

Son Seung-wan didn’t know what ‘Fade’ was, nor that this young producer — who still felt shy about his game rank — would one day carry that melody to every corner of the world.

Baek Si-on didn’t say it aloud in advance.

The seed was already in the soil.

What it grew into was up to them.

❧ ❧ ❧

With all the work wrapped, the wall clock had passed one in the morning.

Baek Si-on glanced at the time.

Less than five hours until the six-a.m. wake-up for styling and the first ‘Sado’ roadshow.

“That’s it for today.”

“Eun-ah, arrange cars to take them back.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded and began confirming vehicles.

Choi Jin-ri stood up from the sofa.

She had originally planned to stay alone with Baek Si-on after work.

He was off to America again soon, after all; the time the two of them truly got alone was pitifully scarce.

But then she thought of the full day of film promotion in a few hours, and pressed that clingy thought down herself.

Then, very obediently, she went up and looped her arm through Lee Ji-eun’s:

“Ji-eun unnie, let’s go.”

Lee Ji-eun looked down at the arm that had been claimed.

“……”

This peach’s defense was getting more and more complete — she even personally escorted the personnel off the premises.

“Let’s go.”

Lee Ji-eun rather wanted to know how far Choi Jin-ri planned to walk her.

The elevator?

The parking lot?

Or straight into Jeong Han-teuk’s van, then personally confirming the door was locked?

❧ ❧ ❧

With Son Seung-wan’s recording done, Bae Joo-hyun naturally lost her legitimate reason to stay.

Before leaving, she looked at Baek Si-on and said:

“Sunbae, thank you for today.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“You too. Be safe on the road.”

Bae Joo-hyun had also wanted to remind him to rest early.

But that line had already been said by Son Seung-wan, and it had caused an atmosphere accident.

She decided not to repeat it.

The two walked out; before the door closed, Son Seung-wan was still chirping at Bae Joo-hyun:

“Unnie! I really got in! I still feel like it’s a dream!”

“Be happy back at the dorm.”

Bae Joo-hyun reminded her:

“The corridor has an echo.”

“……”

Alan shouldered his computer bag.

His interest in Korean PC bangs hadn’t vanished with the continuous work.

Quite the opposite.

Having just met Lee Sang-hyeok and finished the Worlds theme, he now urgently needed to log onto the Korean server and test how long European ranked experience survived here.

Baek Eun-ah arranged a driver to take him to a PC bang near the hotel.

As for whether he’d get beaten up by Korean-server players and greeted by their families — that, too, was part of the cultural experience……

Everyone left in turn.

The control room finally emptied.

Jung Jae-joon stood up from behind the console and stretched.

“You’re not leaving?”

“I’ll sleep right here.”

Baek Si-on pointed at the lounge sofa.

The studio was closer to the morning’s salon.

Going home now would burn nearly an hour round-trip; truly lying down in bed, the alarm would soon wake him again.

Jung Jae-joon nodded.

“Want me to keep you company?”

Baek Si-on: “?”

Jung Jae-joon froze, replayed his own tone from a moment ago, and only then realized what the sentence had become out loud.

“I meant, in case you sleep in,” he patched.

“It didn’t sound like that.”

“Then I’m going.”

Jung Jae-joon grabbed his coat and gave up explaining.

“Shut the door tight.”

“Got it.”

The door closed behind him, and the studio went utterly quiet.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on set his phone alarm for six and lay down on the lounge sofa.

The AC vent kept sending out a low hum of wind.

Downstairs a car passed now and then, tires rolling over the road, the sound coming through the wall, already very faint.

He lay for two minutes.

His hand reached for the tea table again.

The screen lit.

He opened the music player, found the ‘Fade’ Alan had uploaded to SoundCloud earlier, and pressed play.

♪

‘Fade’ · SoundCloud upload

Alan Walker · pure-electronic demo

Spacious electronic melody flowed out of the speakers.

Lonely.

And yet with an indefinable vastness.

Baek Si-on pillowed his arm, and his mind inevitably surfaced the future image of ‘Faded’ sweeping streets, video platforms, and every major festival.

Halfway through the second loop, Baek Si-on sat up.

The light came back on.

A blank sheet of score paper was brought in front of him.

He held the pen and, from memory, tried to fit lyrics to the melody.

The pen tip moved slowly over the paper.

The first line.

the lyric sheet · first line, from memory

You were the shadow to my light…

Write on.

The second line had only just begun.

The studio door was suddenly pushed open from outside.

Baek Si-on stopped his pen and looked back.

Lee Ji-eun, who was supposed to have been “escorted” home by Choi Jin-ri, now walked in swinging a black convenience-store plastic bag, utterly unabashed.

Baek Si-on paused the music.

“How are you back?”

Lee Ji-eun swept a glance around the studio first.

Confirming only the two of them remained, she stepped in and shut the door with her foot.

“Because I knew you wouldn’t sleep properly.”

She set the plastic bag on the tea table.

Glass bottles clinked, a crisp sound.

Then she sat beside him and pointed at the pen in his hand.

“Are you planning to write lyrics for that Norwegian’s song?”

“Trying.”

With that, Baek Si-on’s attention fell onto the black plastic bag.

Several bottles of soju lined up neatly.

The label on each bottle printed Lee Ji-eun’s own endorsement photo.

“What’s this?”

“Creative tools.”

“Alcohol?”

“Creation needs inspiration.”

Lee Ji-eun took one bottle out and stood it in the middle of the table.

“This can provide it.”

Baek Si-on refused flatly.

“I get up at six.”

“So drink less.”

“Not drinking.”

Realizing the reasoning route was a dead end, Lee Ji-eun naturally switched plans.

She shifted closer.

“I kept you company working so long today.”

Her voice softened too.

“Even without merit, there’s hardship, right?”

Baek Si-on corrected:

“Your credit, production fee, and royalty split will be settled as usual.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

This man appended commercial terms even while she was being cute.

She simply reached out and lightly tugged his sleeve.

“Have a drink with me, okay?”

The tail of the sentence was deliberately drawn out.

Sweet, very obviously.

The Producer Lee who had been calling stop mercilessly behind the console a few hours ago switched back into limited-time sweet-little-sister mode.

Baek Si-on looked down at the sleeve she gripped.

Tonight he had indeed dragged her into overtime on short notice; using her and then shooing her off was somewhat lacking in human warmth.

“One cup.”

Lee Ji-eun nodded at once.

“One cup.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Two paper cups were set on the table.

The soju cap was twisted open.

Lee Ji-eun filled both sides.

Baek Si-on picked up his cup and was about to drink straight, but she raised a hand to block him.

“Wait.”

“What now?”

“Don’t we say something before drinking?”

Baek Si-on thought for a moment.

“Here’s to your new album going smoothly.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

She had slipped back past Choi Jin-ri in the dead of night and hauled her own endorsed soju back into the studio — not to hear a partner’s blessing on a new project.

But since he had opened with it, she could only go along:

“Then here’s to your American shoot going smoothly too.”

The two paper cups touched lightly.

Baek Si-on tilted his head and finished it in one gulp.

Lee Ji-eun hadn’t expected him to be so decisive; she gritted her teeth and knocked hers back too.

The liquor had just hit her throat when her nose wrinkled.

Then she stuck out the tip of her tongue and fanned at her mouth.

Baek Si-on set his cup down.

“And that’s the soju endorser?”

Lee Ji-eun dropped her hand at once.

“Ya, don’t underestimate me!”

“I hold my liquor very well.”

“Yoo In-na unnie can’t outdrink me!”

Baek Si-on gave her a thumbs-up.

That expression was hard to read as praise, or as encouraging a professional endorser who needed to fan herself after the first cup.

❧ ❧ ❧

Lee Ji-eun put the empty cup back on the table.

Immediately she refilled both.

“Again.”

Baek Si-on didn’t touch his.

“I said one cup.”

“That cup celebrated my album and your American film.”

“So?”

“This cup celebrates ‘Everytime’ being recorded.”

Baek Si-on answered:

“One cup can celebrate many things.”

“No.”

Lee Ji-eun hugged the bottle and would not yield.

“The ritual has to be separate.”

Baek Si-on reminded her:

“You just said drinking was for inspiration.”

“It only kicks in from the second cup.”

“What does the first one do?”

“Activates the tool.”

She began wheedling again, voice soft.

“Just one cup~”

“You said that last cup too.”

“Last cup.”

Baek Si-on knew that if he didn’t drink, the grinding offensive would downgrade from “one cup” to “half a cup,” then to “just a sip is fine.”

He simply picked up the second cup.

The two paper cups touched again.

This time they skipped the blessing and just drank.

❧ ❧ ❧

The paper cups landed on the table a second time.

This time Lee Ji-eun didn’t rush to refill; she picked up the score sheet Baek Si-on had set aside.

On the paper was only one freshly written line, followed by half an image not yet organized.

“What are you planning to write for it?”

Baek Si-on pushed the soju bottle a little farther.

“Not decided yet.”

“Isn’t that Norwegian planning to do it himself?”

“I can give him a rough direction for the lyric first.”

Lee Ji-eun picked up his phone.

The player on the screen was still paused.

the phone player · paused

‘Fade’ — Alan Walker

━━━─── 01:43

She read it aloud, low.

“Fade?”

“It can also be read as fading away, vanishing.”

Lee Ji-eun pressed play.

The spacious electronic melody bounced around the quiet studio.

Listening, Lee Ji-eun suddenly noticed this song was the complete opposite of the ‘Legends Never Die’ Baek Si-on had just finished.

‘Legends Never Die’ told everyone: those who fell will come back.

‘Fade’ seemed to ask: the one who has already vanished from your life — where did they go?

As the melody climbed, the timbre turned more ethereal.

Like a hand reaching out into fog, grasping, and touching nothing.

Lee Ji-eun thought, then set the phone between the two of them, and said:

“The lyric should feel like it’s still left in memory, feeling very close.”

“But when you actually reach out, you touch nothing.”

Baek Si-on picked up the pen and broke that meaning into more lyric-suitable images.

Light.

Shadow.

A map that lost its direction.

A world gradually drowned by water.

Lee Ji-eun stared at the few lines on the paper and added:

“You could also write one person always searching for another. As for why they can’t find them? Maybe the other walked too fast.”

Baek Si-on, pen tip not stopping, said:

“Or maybe the one searching stood in the wrong place.”

Lee Ji-eun tilted her face:

“How do you know the other isn’t hiding on purpose?”

“Then ask directly.”

“Some questions, once asked, can’t go back to how things were.” Lee Ji-eun half-smiled.

Baek Si-on’s writing hand paused a beat.

Then kept writing.

He didn’t press the second meaning of the sentence; he simply folded that meaning into the lyric concept.

Lee Ji-eun leaned over and read the line he had just written.

the lyric sheet · as amended

Did I lose my mind…

“You use ‘lose.’”

“Mm.”

“You could use ‘fade.’”

“What’s the difference?”

“‘Lose’ is written from the seeker’s angle; ‘fade’ from the leaver’s.”

Baek Si-on revised the wording.

Lee Ji-eun looked at his handwriting.

“Your English handwriting is nicer than your Korean.”

“Off topic.”

“I’m just stating a fact.”

She reached for the bottle and topped up half a cup for each of them.

Baek Si-on bent over revising the lyric, not noticing she had poured again.

❧ ❧ ❧

At first the two sat a person’s width apart; later, to read the words on the paper, Lee Ji-eun shifted a little toward the middle.

Baek Si-on was weighing the lyric and didn’t mind.

Later, a few more lines in, she shifted again, humming along with the melody.

Finding one line’s stress landing awkwardly, she simply reached over and gripped Baek Si-on’s wrist, guiding his pen to change that word.

Alcohol breath mixed into each other’s breathing; the drunkenness accumulated bit by bit.

When the song’s whole narrative concept finally ran smooth, Baek Si-on noticed two things.

First, several empty soju bottles now lay on the tea table.

Second, Lee Ji-eun was practically draped over him.

Baek Si-on looked down.

Soju smell mixed with perfume, brushing past his neck with every breath.

Lee Ji-eun sensed his gaze shift and lifted her face.

“Finished writing?”

“Pretty much.”

“Then am I amazing?”

“Amazing.”

“That’s all?”

Baek Si-on glanced at the wall clock.

Close to three in the morning.

The alarm was set for six.

“The song’s written. You should go home.”

Lee Ji-eun still hugged his arm.

“The drink isn’t finished.”

“Save it for next time.”

“Once soju is opened, it tastes bad next time.”

“When has soju ever tasted good?”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

That sentence had touched professional dignity.

She was, after all, Jinro’s own endorser.

She could fan herself after drinking, but she could not allow this man to publicly slander the product she endorsed.

Lee Ji-eun pointed at the fifth bottle.

“I’ll leave when we finish it.”

“Sure?”

“Sure.”

She picked up the bottle and split the remainder into the two paper cups.

The last drop fell.

She turned the bottle upside down and shook it, proving no next round of negotiation was hidden inside.

“Last cup.”

Baek Si-on picked up his cup.

“You’ve said that many times tonight.”

“This time it’s true.”

Lee Ji-eun touched her paper cup against his.

“Say something?”

“We already toasted earlier.”

“Then here’s to this song going big.”

Baek Si-on raised his cup toward the score sheet.

“It will go big.”

“You’re always this confident.” Lee Ji-eun pursed her lips.

“Otherwise why spend the time writing it?”

The two paper cups met again.

The liquor went down.

Lee Ji-eun set the empty cup down and sat quietly for two seconds.

Then her body went soft and she collapsed onto Baek Si-on’s shoulder, motionless.

Baek Si-on looked down.

“Lee Ji-eun.”

No response.

“Up. Home.”

Still no movement.

She had even deliberately steadied her breathing, working hard to play a person utterly defeated by alcohol.

Baek Si-on listened for a while.

“Your breathing is too even when you fake sleep.”

The person on his shoulder immediately made her breathing irregular.

Baek Si-on: “……”

Corrected far too fast.

He didn’t keep exposing her; supporting Lee Ji-eun’s shoulder, he slowly laid her down on the sofa and draped his jacket over her.

“You sleep here.”

“I’m leaving.”

Lee Ji-eun’s eyes snapped open.

“Where are you going?”

Baek Si-on stood by the sofa.

“Aren’t you drunk?”

“Suddenly sober.”

“Fast recovery.”

“Young, good metabolism.”

Baek Si-on turned to go.

Lee Ji-eun sat up at once and grabbed his wrist.

“Don’t go.”

She put strength into her hand, pulling him toward her.

Given the two of their strength gap, that force couldn’t possibly move Baek Si-on.

But he didn’t shake her off.

His center of gravity followed her pull, forward.

Lee Ji-eun hugged his waist and, borrowing the momentum, fell backward.

The sofa gave a soft sound.

Baek Si-on braced both hands on the backrest, looking down at Lee Ji-eun trapped between his arms:

“Is this also drunk?”

Lee Ji-eun met his gaze and said:

“You know perfectly well I’m faking.”

“So I was going to leave you some face.”

“I don’t need it.”

“Then what do you need?”

Lee Ji-eun proved with action what she needed.

After their lips touched, she drew back a little, arms still looped around his neck.

Baek Si-on didn’t chase the kiss.

Nor did he get up.

Lee Ji-eun stared at him and asked:

“Are you going to pretend nothing happened again?”

“Haven’t you been pretending all along too?”

Her fingers tightened at the nape of his neck.

“Then don’t pretend this time.”

The soju taste mixed again.

Baek Si-on drew back one bracing arm, setting it behind her waist, pulling her close.

Sensing the response, Lee Ji-eun’s movements grew fiercer.

❧ ❧ ❧

In the end, Baek Si-on stopped first.

Their foreheads rested together.

Lee Ji-eun’s breathing hadn’t steadied; her arms still hadn’t let go.

“Why stop?”

Baek Si-on glanced at the wall clock.

“Up at six.”

“There’s still over two hours.”

“So we sleep.”

Lee Ji-eun stared at him.

“Can you even sleep now?”

“If you let go of me first.”

She only hugged tighter.

“Then don’t sleep.”

“Lee Ji-eun.”

“Mm?”

“If we continue, it won’t be explainable as fake-drunk.”

She didn’t dodge.

“I never planned to explain anyway.”

That sentence tore away the last buffer in the air.

Going further, the two of them would undergo a change no amount of stubborn talk could erase.

But they had been drinking.

Tonight had already crossed a lot.

Stopping here at least left a tomorrow they could face each other sober.

Baek Si-on pushed himself up.

Lee Ji-eun still wouldn’t let go.

“Don’t go.”

“I’m only going to the work desk.”

“The work desk is also a no.”

“What exactly do you want?”

Lee Ji-eun buried her face beside his shoulder and closed her eyes.

“Sleep here with me.”

“This sofa can’t fit two people.”

“Didn’t it just now, when I fell?”

Baek Si-on had no reply.

Falling over and sleeping a night were clearly not the same spatial test.

Lee Ji-eun added:

“I won’t do anything else.”

Baek Si-on glanced down at the hands still looped around his waist.

“Your behavior lacks persuasiveness.”

“Then you can supervise me.”

She didn’t let go.

Baek Si-on ultimately didn’t leave.

He shifted Lee Ji-eun toward the inside of the sofa and propped himself on the outer edge.

The narrow space pressed their shoulders together; a slight turn might shove the other off.

Lee Ji-eun, quite satisfied, pulled his jacket over the two of them.

“Wake me when the alarm goes.”

“Are you planning to send me to the salon?”

“I can supervise you getting up.”

“Guarantee you can wake first.”

Lee Ji-eun had already closed her eyes.

“Young, good metabolism.”

The same sentence, used by her a second time.

Its credibility remained low.
