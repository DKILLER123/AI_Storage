Chapter Thirty-Nine

A Two-Hour All-Kill and Six Boxes of Meat

July 21 Evening Across every chart — the All-Kill The day SISTAR owned the air

July twenty-first, and SISTAR released Touch My Body.

Si-on saw it happen from the bottom of his phone feed, at first a single song drifting up a chart, then two, then — within two hours — the whole surface of the music landscape tilting toward one group of four women who had somehow, in a single afternoon, become the loudest thing in the country.

Melon · August week one · all-chart sweep

02 — SISTAR · Touch My Body (café) HOT

03 — f(x) · Red Light

04 — SISTAR · Touch My Body

A two-hour All-Kill, then the hold.

The term All-Kill got thrown around a lot. But Si-on had never actually seen one from the outside before, and the way it worked was both breathtaking and a little absurd: one song, and then the whole machine of the chart — the daily, the real-time, the weekly, the download — folding itself over that single track until the only question left was how long it would stay.

It stayed two hours on the full sweep. Then it settled into the thing that lasts: the top of the chart, with the rest of the summer crowding up behind it.

And f(x), four strong without Jin-ri, held at number three.

“They're still there,” Eun-ah said, reading over his shoulder. She pointed at Red Light, third from the top, sitting steady.

“They wrote a song with a hole in it and they're still charting.” Si-on said it quietly, almost to himself. “That band is going to be fine.”

“You think?”

“I think the song's the point. The hole's just part of the arrangement.”

Eun-ah looked at him for a long moment the way she'd been doing all summer, that sharp, sideways measuring glance she'd inherited from her mother.

“You keep talking about her like you're the only one who's allowed to worry about her.”

“I'm the only one who's met her.”

“I've known her longer than—”

“Then act like it,” Si-on said, and it came out flatter than he'd meant. “You've been giving everything to a song for a month. You've got a house full of boxes and a cousin who's finally stopped talking about his movie. Tell me what you've done for her besides feel guilty.”

Eun-ah closed her mouth. Her jaw worked once. Then she said, very quietly:

“We could go visit her.”

Si-on set the phone down.

“Alright. We go visit her.”

July Late afternoon A market, then the street, then an apartment building Six boxes of meat, a stove, and a decision

The plan, so far as Eun-ah had one, was simple: do not arrive empty-handed. The plan, so far as it had developed in the course of half an hour in a market, had grown considerably heavier.

The visit · provisions · two people, one bag each

Six boxes of meat — beef palisade, pork belly, chicken thigh, in thin slabs and thick ×6

One portable butane stove, still in its box, one-year warranty no one would need ×1

A plastic bag of snacks — the kind a hospital would not recommend and a guest would anyway ×1

Eun-ah had also packed a small tin of matches, because she'd watched her mother cook on gas for twenty years and did not trust a butane stove she couldn't light herself.

Si-on paid for the meat, shouldered two of the boxes, and let Eun-ah take the rest. They rode the bus for a while in silence, and then walked the last two streets to the building Eun-ah already had the address for, the one on the edge of a quiet block where the trees had grown tall enough to keep the windows shaded.

The lobby had a single elderly guard who looked up from a newspaper, looked at the meat, looked back at the newspaper, and waved them through without a word.

The elevator climbed slowly, floor marker ticking over, and at the top it stopped.

Eun-ah pressed the hall button and the doors slid open on a floor where the light was already on.

“She's awake,” she said, and didn't move.

The light, spilling out from under a door at the end of the corridor, was warm and lit in the way a person who has been alone for a long time learns to keep a room — not bright, not welcoming, but on. As if the act of keeping it on were a way of talking to yourself.

Si-on set the boxes down.

“Then let's not make her wait.”
