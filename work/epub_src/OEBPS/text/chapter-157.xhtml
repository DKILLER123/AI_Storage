Chapter One Hundred Fifty Seven

Before the Peach Got Pickled

December 3 · 10:38 p.m. Song of the Year, just decided · then the long tail of the night Hong Kong · AsiaWorld-Expo · artist seats, center block — and one sofa in Seongbuk-dong There are words for moments like this. There is also the correct unit of exchange, which — as two different people would each demonstrate before midnight — is not words.

When the Song of the Year trophy landed in ’s hands, most people’s first reaction was surprise.

Not because ‘Friday’ didn’t deserve it.

Quite the opposite: the song fit Korea’s digital market the way a key fits the door it was cut for.

But MAMA had never been a place that looked only at digital numbers. Votes, sales, the committee, attendance, company relationships — and, as ever, which slice of the meat the organizers felt like handing to whom.

Lee Ji-eun knew those rules cold.

Which is exactly why she understood, better than anyone, that this trophy should not have arrived this smoothly.

What she had actually wanted to fight for was Artist of the Year.

At least by her own assessment, that was the grand prize with the best odds of being negotiated loose tonight.

And now.

She was holding Song of the Year.

Lee Ji-eun was not a fool.

She knew precisely why this had happened.

It was not that the organizers had been so moved by her sarcastic little speech that they reformed on the spot.

It was the sentences Baek Si-on had said to Baek Eun-ah an hour ago.

She felt she ought to say something.

But language, at this particular moment, felt pale and structurally unsound.

Then she suddenly remembered the AMAs.

Taylor had kissed Selena. Kissed Sam Smith. Then turned and given Baek Si-on a kiss on the cheek.

The GIF of Baek Si-on’s half-step backward had looped on the Korean forums for two full days.

Lee Ji-eun was of course not going to do that.

This was not Los Angeles.

But it occurred to her that she could at least do one thing Cindy would not be able to mock.

“Stand up for a second.”

“?”

Baek Si-on tilted his head, with zero intention of moving.

By industry convention, the people who stood up to applaud a winner were the juniors.

He considered himself the senior, where Lee Ji-eun was concerned.

Though this was a classification Lee Ji-eun had spent years refusing to sign.

So he had absolutely no plans to stand.

Lee Ji-eun looked at his air of pure entitlement and lowered her voice.

“Hurry.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

In the end, he stood up.

The next second, Lee Ji-eun opened her arms and hugged him.

Not the dragonfly-touch, ceremonial kind.

Both arms around his back, cheek against his shoulder — a hug with honest, load-bearing force.

Kissing was out of the question. However far Cindy’s spirit carried her, Lee Ji-eun was not about to deliver a Taylor-spec cheek kiss to Baek Si-on in front of a live national broadcast. That wouldn’t be a trending topic tomorrow. That would be an earthquake.

But the hug alone was enough to freeze Baek Si-on solid for one instant.

The hall went quiet for half a beat.

Then the screaming arrived like a kettle with its lid lifted off, and boiled over.

The director reacted fast — the cut landed on them immediately.

On the big screen, Lee Ji-eun hugging Baek Si-on expanded to a few dozen square meters.

His arm hovered in mid-air for something under a second. Then it came down lightly on her shoulder.

And patted. Twice.

the six-chair peanut gallery · evidence secured

Two seats back, Soyeon watched the whole thing for exactly two seconds. Then, wordlessly, she turned her head toward the camera position, one specific question on her face.

Jiyeon supplied the answer in a whisper.

“We’re on the big screen, aren’t we.”

They were. Caught clean: all six sisters’ stunned faces, archived into MAMA’s all-Asia broadcast, indefinitely.

…

Choi Jin-ri had no schedule tonight.

She was curled up on her apartment sofa in Seongbuk-dong, a thin blanket over her knees, that cup of saffron water on the coffee table. The water was no longer hot. It tasted as bad as ever. She had, at this point, adjusted.

A phone stand stood at the table’s edge, streaming MAMA.

During the ‘Human’ opening she watched in perfect silence. It was a handsome scene. So handsome she briefly forgot she was drinking water that tasted like that.

Later, when the director’s sweep crossed the edge of the artist seats, she saw the six of T-ara standing there, looking awkward. She didn’t see the specifics. But the next time the broadcast cut back, they were seated in the row behind Baek Si-on — and Choi Jin-ri understood.

She suddenly remembered an awards show from two years ago, when she had given up her own seat for T-ara to use.

Choi Jin-ri had thought that was kindness.

Tonight, Baek Si-on showed her a different kind.

Kindness that doesn’t have to step backward.

Kindness that can sit down where it is, look up, and say:

“Have them sit next to me.”

…

Later still, Lee Ji-eun went up three times in a row.

Choi Jin-ri was happy for her. Genuinely.

Right up until she saw Lee Ji-eun stand up and hug the also-standing Baek Si-on.

The screaming from the phone turned piercing all at once.

Choi Jin-ri’s finger moved slowly toward the side of the phone. She didn’t turn off the screen. She only lowered the volume a little — because she could not bear to miss Baek Si-on’s own award later.

But by the time her finger came back from the side button, the barrage had already gone feral.

the live barrage · Choi Jin-ri’s phone · volume: one notch down

They hugged!! They hugged they hugged they hugged!!

IU hugged Baek Si-on AAAAAAH!

These two are SO well matched!

Taylor gives kisses, IU gives hugs — what IS Baek Si-on, international public property?

The volume had been lowered exactly one notch. The barrage declined to lower itself at all.

Choi Jin-ri bit her lip. She endured, and endured, and finally exited the live — out of sight, out of mind.

The living room went quiet. The saffron water on the table had gone properly cold. She hugged a cushion and sat there for several minutes while her thoughts started to misbehave.

Had Lee Ji-eun been wanting to hug him all along?

Did he — not mind?

They were still singing together at the concert.

Would the internet keep egging them on?

The more she thought the messier it got; the messier it got the angrier she grew; and by the end of the anger she honestly could not tell whether she was angry at Lee Ji-eun, at Baek Si-on, or at herself.

She picked up the phone and placed a video call to Goo Hara.

It rang a few times and connected. Goo Hara’s face appeared — freshly showered, hair half-dry, loose pajamas, a bottle of water in one hand.

video call · Goo Hara · 11:12 p.m. · from a Seongbuk-dong sofa to some half-dry hair

What’s wrong, Peach?

Choi Jin-ri reeled her expression in about forty percent.

Nothing. Just wanted to ask if you watched MAMA.

Some of it.

Did you see T-ara?

Saw them.

A respectable topic, located at last. She deployed it at once.

Baek Si-on had them sit next to him.

Mm.

I think he did really well.

So you called to compliment him?

A silence, one second long.

Not exactly.

Goo Hara watched her through the front camera, water bottle paused mid-air.

Then is this about the trophies, or about the hug?

……

Silence on both ends of the screen, perfectly synchronized.

Looks like the hug.

……I was just asking.

‘Just asking’ doesn’t sound that sour.

I’m not sour.

Mm. That’s what the honey peach said too — right before it got pickled into kimchi.

Choi Jin-ri pressed the cushion into her face. Held it there. Let it back down after a few seconds.

I really don’t blame Ji-eun unnie.

Goo Hara listened, and said nothing at all.

Her award — I should be happy for her. T-ara getting to sit down — I’m happy about that too. He did really well.

But when she hugged him, I wasn’t happy.

Does that make me a bad person?

She had always assumed she wasn’t like this. She could be happy for her friends, ache on her sunbaes’ behalf, feel proud when someone did the correct thing. But the moment any of those feelings touched Baek Si-on, they stopped coming out clean. She noticed things. She compared. She soured. She knew — knew — that Lee Ji-eun had earned that hug. It hurt anyway.

Goo Hara smiled, suddenly.

Jin-ri-yah.

Being kind doesn’t mean slicing your heart open to go with somebody’s drink.

You can be happy for her and sad for yourself. The two things don’t cancel.

Choi Jin-ri’s nose stung. She sniffed.

But I don’t have any standing.

When you like someone, the first thing that grows is usually jealousy with no standing.

Goo Hara took an unhurried sip of water.

What you envy isn’t Ji-eun hugging him. It’s that she had a reason to.

And you have your reasons too. Yours just don’t come with an awards-show camera.

The line landed, and an uninvited reel started playing: Venice, the closing banquet, him standing up on the banqueting table pointing at her while he sang. The Green Bottle Fly speech — no your performance, no my trophy, he had said. The set visits. The support trucks. The saffron water. The wet-wipe photo.

None of it had ever been magnified onto a MAMA screen. No all-Asia live feed. No director’s close-up. But it was real — and between the two of them, the things that counted had never been only the things other people could see.

Goo Hara reeled the topic back in.

Also — forget the hug for a second. Wasn’t the thing you most wanted to tell him tonight the T-ara thing?

Choi Jin-ri stalled, cushion in her lap, head going down slowly. It was. The moment she saw T-ara seated behind him, her very first thought had been to tell him exactly that — you did well. Really well. The hug had scrambled it.

Then say that one first. Don’t let the vinegar drown what you actually wanted to say.

The call ended, and the living room went quiet again.

Choi Jin-ri picked the phone back up and opened his chat thread.

She stared at the input box for a long time. Then, slowly, she typed.

Choi Jin-ri looked down at that line of text for a long moment.

And the hug that had soured her for half the evening went quietly out of focus.

What came back instead was two years ago: an awards ceremony where the entire hall was isolating those six girls, and the moment she pushed her own chair aside for them — and how afraid she had actually been, right then. Afraid of being painted with them. Afraid of the pile-on finding her name too. Afraid of simply having done it wrong.

But someone had remembered.

Someone had reached across two years of malicious comments and hot searches, and remembered that under that kind of pressure she had once done a very small, very small good thing.

Her thumbs moved. Then his reply. Then, before she could stop them, her eyes.

This time the tears came down for real. She wiped once. Wiped again. Then couldn’t help laughing, and huffed softly at the ceiling.

Typical.

She knew he would say exactly that.

…

The ceremony finally closed in a very MAMA way.

Song of the Year to Lee Ji-eun. Album of the Year to EXO. Artist of the Year to Baek Si-on. The pork carved, publicly, with perfect clarity.

Whether the cuts looked good — that depended entirely on the long-form essays every fandom would be writing on the way home.

After the ceremony, the corridor between backstage and the artist seats warmed up again. Staff guided artists out. Managers hunted trophies. Stylists hunted coats. Some people talked. Some people sprinted off to be photographed with sponsors. And some people already had their phones out, reading the first wave of hot-post titles off the forums.

Baek Si-on came down the side channel with the Artist of the Year trophy, rounded a camera rig, and found Lee Ji-eun standing there.

Beside her, Jung Han-teul was holding two trophies, wearing the expression of a temp worker who has just signed for high-value fragile goods at a warehouse.

Lee Ji-eun still had her Song of the Year trophy in her own hands. She looked at his first.

“Artist of the Year.”

“Mm.”

“Congratulations.”

“You too.”

They walked toward the greenroom area side by side. A few steps in, Baek Si-on spoke up.

“No more ambushes like tonight.”

Lee Ji-eun paused.

“What ambush?”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“The hug.”

She blinked — innocence, retail-grade.

“Didn’t I give you advance notice?”

“When?”

“I told you to stand up.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

He paused. It was, technically, unarguable. Procedurally, notice had been served. Between the notice and the event, roughly forty steps of information had simply been omitted.

“That is not a reason to suddenly hug me.”

Lee Ji-eun, perfectly calm:

“Cindy doesn’t need a reason.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Excellent. The boomerang had completed its arc and found his own back.

Behind them, Jung Han-teul walked with the two trophies, temple twitching, and developed a sudden worry: once Producer started shooting, would every act of whim now be filed as role immersion? Chocolate runs at three in the morning. Reason on the form: Cindy wants it.

Baek Si-on closed his eyes for a beat.

“What I mean is — next time, specify the content of the event in advance.”

Lee Ji-eun tipped her head.

“I did specify.”

“You said ‘stand up.’”

“Cindy doesn’t do details.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Two seconds of silence.

“Never again.”

Lee Ji-eun mouthed something small that was almost certainly stingy, but let it rest there. At the corridor fork they nodded to each other and split off toward their separate greenrooms.

Baek Si-on didn’t take that one. He sat down on the sofa, took out his phone. Many messages. He swept through them and stopped on one.

The one from Choi Jin-ri.
