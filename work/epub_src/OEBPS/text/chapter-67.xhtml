Chapter Sixty-Seven

The 6.8% Forged Out of Hunger

August 1 Live broadcast KBS · the main stage A song, a number, and a room full of strangers on the sofa

SISTAR's stage was second to last.

The moment "Touch My Body"'s intro sounded, the temperature in the hall rose a good two degrees.

The minute the chorus hit, the signature hip-swivel dance was thrown open without a shred of reserve.

The screams from the live audience slammed into the ceiling and bounced back, mixing with the low frequencies from the speakers, and the entire air of the hall was vibrating.

Four minutes later.

The source queen's stage came to an end, and the audience's emotions were sitting at the absolute peak of the whole show.

"Next! The final stage of today's Music Bank!"

Park Seo-joon and Bora stood at the MC position at the side, facing the lens.

"Sweeping domestic and international music charts with the digital single 'Way Back Home,' and taking the Music Bank stage for the very first time today—"

Bora picked up the line: "Baek Si-on!"

The light at the MC position went out.

The light on the main stage went out too.

The whole hall went dark.

Dark for about five seconds.

A single beam of light fell straight down from directly above the stage, landing on the one black high stool at its centre.

Baek Si-on sat there.

Deep-grey draped blazer hanging open, black slim-fit turtleneck knit hugging his upper body, both legs crossed and resting on the stool's footrest.

His right hand loosely held the mic, his left rested on his knee.

Camera three pushed in for an impossibly brutal straight-on facial close-up.

The payoff for going all day without a drop of food came due at this exact moment.

Under the spell of high-definition lenses that add a full size to any ordinary person, Baek Si-on's cold, sharp face withstood every bit of scrutiny.

The crisp, bouncing synth timbre poured out of the top-tier speakers, a rhythm with an almost granular texture, shattering the trailing note of the previous revelry.

Baek Si-on raised the mic.

Way Back Home · first live performance · Music Bank

Chorus

No matter how far I walk, Step, Step

This city's neon, Blink, Blink

They're all smiling at me asking: hey, where are you going?

I've turned out every pocket

And can't find the key called "home"

It's fine — I'll keep walking

Before the world turns upside down, before the street lamps go out

Take the wandering as my Way Back Home

Bars — four of them, with nothing underneath

From morning to now, he hadn't eaten a single thing.

Yet the body's depletion hadn't made his voice worse. Instead it had laid a faint frosted edge along the vocal cords, one that arrives on its own without effort — so as the breath pushed out, the tail of the sound naturally hung a touch lower.

It wasn't put-on weariness.

It was hunger.

In the second half of the first verse, the accompaniment quietly slid in a very thin string pad.

It rose slowly out of the low range, like the water level climbing unawares in the night.

Baek Si-on's voice climbed a little with the strings.

The rhythm of the accompaniment changed abruptly.

The drums just stopped.

Every percussive instrument vanished on the first beat of the pre-chorus.

The whole hall for that one instant held only one thing.

Baek Si-on's voice.

With no drum groove underneath, the vocal was stripped bare. Every breath, every tiny vibrato, even the faintest scrape of the vocal folds closing, stood naked in the air.

And Baek Si-on's voice held steady through four whole bars in that vacuum.

No shake.

No drift.

Then the kick drum slammed back in on the final beat.

"—making my wandering, my Way Back Home—"

The last note dragged on a long time.

Long enough that the accompaniment had already run through its final chord, the string pad's ring dying away in the speakers, and only that last note still hanging in the air.

August 1 Live · the last note still hanging KBS · control room Eight monitors, and a curve that's about to take off

Eight monitors arrayed in two rows, live feeds flickering across the screens.

Baek Si-on's last note was still lingering in the air of the hall, and the main stage's light banks had already started switching into full-hall mode.

The ratings monitor in the seat at the far right, staring at the curve of AGB Nielsen real-time data jumping on his screen, suddenly popped out of his chair.

"PD-nim!"

He turned his head and called out.

"Live ratings — 6.8 percent!"

In the control room, the several people who'd been bent over their own screens all snapped their heads around in unison.

The head PD leaned out from behind the monitors, glanced at the curve on the ratings screen.

6.8% — a figure that would be a flop for an evening drama, but for a music-show chart was a veritable myth.

A tripling of the whole hour. In 2014, when young fans no longer watched television at all.

Then Baek Si-on came on.

The curve started climbing from the very first line, took off outright in the four bars where the drums vanished, and by the time the last note ended, the real-time figure had frozen at 6.8.

Tripled.

The head PD leaned back in his chair and stared at the curve for several seconds.

It was 2014.

Smartphones and YouTube were fully ubiquitous. Young fans had stopped watching TV entirely.

Comeback stages were watched on fancams online, chart results were refreshed on a phone in real time — who on earth would sit in front of a TV set at five on a Friday afternoon waiting for Music Bank?

The music-show ratings at every network had long since fallen into the mud.

Music Bank was scraping by at a weekly average of 2.0 to 2.5, Mnet's M Countdown was regularly dipping below 1.0, and its worst episode had once recorded 0.6.

Even as the biggest draws — acts like EXO and SISTAR — came to promote, the rate never moved.

The reason was simple.

Idols attract fans.

Fans don't watch TV.

So no matter who stood on stage, no matter how many followers they had, the people holding the remote control simply weren't in that ecosystem.

The head PD finally understood why Entertainment Bureau CP Kim Ho-sang had pushed so hard to get Baek Si-on on the show.

The social labels on this man were too heavy.

Lead actor of a Venice-shortlisted film.

A debt-collecting thug clawing his way up.

A Dispatch-exclusive reversal.

In this week, those labels had battered their way, one after another, through the social-affairs sections of all three major networks.

The middle-aged women and men who never watched a music show at all had all been hooked by a real-life plot more fantastical than any morning drama.

They would turn on KBS on the dot.

Just to see what this grassroots nobody who'd done his country's cinema proud actually looked like, and what tune he sang.

In a steel box where ratings were king, national recognition always trumped fandom.

August 1 Live · result KBS · main stage Warm white, all twenty-odd acts, and a trophy

Meanwhile, the lights in the hall shifted back to a warm white.

The more than twenty acts that had performed that day — over a hundred young people in garish stage outfits — all poured onto the stage.

At the dead centre of that sea of colour.

Baek Si-on and SISTAR, as today's number-one contenders, stood shoulder to shoulder at the very front.

Park Seo-joon and Bora stood on either side of the two groups, each holding a script.

The live audience went quiet.

Park Seo-joon faced the lens.

"Now, the moment to announce this week's Music Bank number one!"

Bora looked down at the card.

"Digital source score…"

The number was read out.

Baek Si-on was ahead.

"Viewer vote score…"

Baek Si-on was ahead.

"Album sales score…"

SISTAR was ahead.

Baek Si-on had released a digital single, no physical album, so this category was a straight zero.

Park Seo-joon flipped the card to the back.

"This week's Music Bank K-Chart number one!"

Park Seo-joon and Bora spoke at the same time.

"Baek Si-on! 'Way Back Home'!"

"Boom—"

Two high-pressure air pumps kicked in simultaneously above the stage.

Countless gold-glittering streamers burst from the ceiling, scattering down in a shower, covering the whole recording hall.

The hall's cheers and the applause of the idols at the back boiled over at the same instant.

The speakers automatically cut into the chorus melody of "Way Back Home."

Baek Si-on stood in the rain of streamers.

No excitement at all.

No red-rimmed eyes.

No hand covering a mouth in a broken sob.

Only calmly turning to the side, reaching out, and taking the trophy from Park Seo-joon's hands.
