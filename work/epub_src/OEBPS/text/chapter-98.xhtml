Chapter Ninety-Eight

An Ad Man’s Ordinary Days

September 12 Morning into 15:00 Samseong-dong · KB brand studio Four to five centimetres. A short side part. One-eighty stills. Soft light that was not a filter.

September twelfth. KB Kookmin Bank brand studio. Gangnam, Samseong-dong. Baek Si-on in the makeup chair. Park Ji-hun behind him, looking in the glass at that head in a transition period.

The buzz from two months ago had grown out, overall about four to five centimetres. Past the scalp-stuck plate cut, still at least a month of growth from “can style freely.” At this length, styling was not taste. It was craft. Ji-hun dug a small ball of matte wax on a fingertip, from the front, along the hair’s natural direction, lightly back and to the side. When the effect came it was an extremely short side part. Forehead cleanly exposed under the lights, the short hair on both sides given a direction. Dry. Sharp. What KB wanted. With a deep navy three-piece, white shirt, one button open at the collar.

The shoot went extremely fast. KB’s brand side had almost nothing to adjust on Si-on’s lens sense. Photographer said look at the lens, he looked. Said fifteen degrees left, he went. Said the eye a little softer, he softened. Every take, one pass. Whole run about two and a half hours. One hundred eighty stills. Six fifteen-second TVC pieces. The photographer, watching playback on the monitor, let a line out of his mouth: does this person come with a soft-light filter. Ji-hun beside him heard it, face unchanged.

“Not a filter. Base formula.”

Three p.m., wrap. Baek Eun-ah already off with a USB to copy the day’s dailies.

“Oppa. KB said the dailies will lock before next Monday. After lock, straight into post.”

“Mm.”

“Tomorrow morning, SK Telecom.”

“What time?”

“Ten. Internal studio at SK HQ. Style brief they sent — ‘young, tech, future.’”

“Those words wouldn’t be wrong in any telecom’s brief either.”

Eun-ah looked up at him.

“Oppa you said something like that at the KB shoot too.”

“Because that’s the fact. A brand brief is always those words in combination. The difference is only what thousand-K the gaffer sets the colour temp to.”

“…Just don’t say that in front of a brand.”

“I can tell.”

✿ ✿ ✿

September 13 10:00 SK Telecom HQ · internal studio No product in the hair. A slogan. Two hours.

September thirteenth. SK Telecom headquarters. This time Ji-hun put nothing on Si-on’s head, only blow-dried the washed short hair to seventy percent and raked it a few times with fingers. SK wanted “young.” Four to five centimetres of short hair had a natural advantage in “not styling” — short enough not to mess, enough to show a looseness. Si-on looked at Ji-hun once.

“You’ve improved lately.”

“It’s your hair that grew. That July tea-egg, I didn’t even have a place to put a hand.”

“…”

The shoot faster than KB. Reason: SK’s TVC idea was very simple. Si-on standing in front of a huge LED, SK Telecom speed-test numbers rolling on it. What he had to do was look at the lens, then say one slogan.

“Connect everything. Beyond imagination.”

Two hours. Wrap.

✿ ✿ ✿

September 15 Morning into 18:20 Yongin · Namyang R&D · test track Chassis 0001. Real car, real road, real wind. A temporary plate. Sixteen parks, not seventeen.

September fifteenth. Gyeonggi, Yongin. Hyundai Motor Namyang R&D, attached test track. Unlike the closed studios of the last two days, Hyundai had prepared a real-location shoot. Real car. Real road. Real wind. The whole track temporarily cleared a large block, tape on the outside, Hyundai staff, crew, security, brand reps adding up to nearly fifty. Two camera cars, one Russian arm, one high-speed follow. Three drones. Track, jib, in-car suction, under-car wheel — all of it.

Weather good. High autumn, dry. Sky a very standard Korean September blue, sun from high and slanted, landing on the silver-grey guardrail by the lane, throwing a cold metal light. When Si-on got down from the nanny van, the first thing he saw was the car. Hyundai Aslan. Black. Chassis 0001. The first production Aslan in the world. Also the one the contract wrote as a gift to him.

Body lines not showy. Not a sports car’s deliberate outward aggression, not a European luxury’s over-fine showing-off. The weather was unified. Steady. Heavy. Able to hold a room. Like an adult male lion lying on the ground at rest, not moving, and nobody would doubt whether it could spring.

The marketing director came up fast.

“Mr. Baek. You worked.”

“Everyone worked more.”

Si-on shook once, eyes on the Aslan again.

“Today we shoot the full four-minute first, then split fifteen, thirty, sixty shorts, right?”

“Right.”

The director nodded.

“The idea you put last time, we passed it all inside. The full ad version is being made to brand-short plus MV spec.”

Meaning of that sentence, simple. Money given enough. The floor pulled full. Today was not an ordinary ad. It was a front campaign Hyundai meant to take out and hit a round of brand volume with.

Hair and makeup fast. Two looks. First, black turtleneck knit and a long charcoal coat. Second, dark suit. The director — early forties, khaki work jacket, having shot countless high-end car ads — talking the scene to Si-on behind the monitor, words very held.

“Not a young person’s first-car excitement.”

“Not a newly-rich sitting into a luxury and showing.”

“This car’s weather is you don’t need to prove you already succeeded, because everyone already knows you succeeded.”

Si-on nodded once.

“Understood.”

First setup. Exterior start in morning fog. Aslan at the end of an empty lane, lens low, on the ground, far on it. Si-on walking in from off. The long coat’s hem moved lightly in the morning wind. Voice in the walkie:

“Music—”

Next second. Way Down We Go’s intro pressed heavy out of the large field monitors. Low end landing first. Like an unseen giant stepping a beat up from under the earth. Si-on opened the door, sat in, shut, ignition. Engine not loud, even held. Stacked with that intro, the whole picture’s weather stood at once. The Russian-arm car began to move. Aslan starting slowly. Behind the monitor, quiet to a degree. Nobody speaking. Only the director on the screen, right index tapping the beat lightly on his leg. The lens walked a whole line. The car did not stop. Straight into second setup.

Second was a tunnel. Headlamps cutting the dark inside. Low end enlarged in the concrete, echo bouncing back a little. Lens from outside to in. Si-on one hand on the wheel, the other hanging naturally, eyes forward, no performed “harsh” or “cold,” only focus. The instant the chorus began. The car out of the tunnel. Morning light smashing into the windshield. The director’s hand up at once.

“Don’t stop. Another lap. This time drone takes the exterior long.”

By the third round the whole team was fully in. Hyundai’s side had originally thought, however good this song, in the end it was only ad music serving a car. Once lens and music actually bit together they realised it was not that. This song was not backgrounding the car. It was writing Aslan’s register.

Noon, a short rest. Si-on under a shade tent, water in his hand, not saying much. Eun-ah with a tablet beside him sorting the shoot list, now and then looking up at monitor playback, more awake the more she looked.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“This isn’t an ad anymore.”

“Then what?”

“Like one of those high-end brand shorts they put in front of a feature.”

Si-on smiled once.

“That’s what Hyundai wants.”

Eun-ah looked down at playback again. Indeed. If it were only selling a car, shooting to here was already enough. What Hyundai wanted was to borrow Si-on, borrow a new song, borrow a whole image weather, and lift Aslan from “new model launch” into “brand-image upgrade.” This set going out, selling not only a car. Also face.

Afternoon, second look. Suit. In-car close-ups mostly. Where fingers sat on the wheel, how much watch showed, how many seconds the eye held in the mirror, even the rhythm of a hand going up to press a centre-console button — the director picked it to the extremely fine. Commercial ads at the highest rank were not actually a fight of story. They were control. Si-on was not the kind of artist who needed a director to feed motion frame by frame. You gave him one abstract instruction — like a man who just finished a merger meeting, about to go sign a second company — and he could himself put the angle of a loose shoulder, the lift of a chin, the place the look landed, all onto the most right line.

Evening, 18:20. Last take done. The director stood from behind the monitor, walkie in hand, looked around the floor.

“Everyone.”

He drew a breath.

“That’s a wrap.”

Applause at the edge of the track. The marketing director came over first, the smile on his face not pressable.

“Mr. Baek.”

“Mm.”

“I have a very strong feeling now.”

“What?”

“Aslan this time. People will actually remember it.”

“They will.”

Done, Si-on lifted his chin a little, toward that black Aslan.

“Then can I drive it now?”

The director froze a second. Then laughed.

“Of course. It was yours to begin with.”

He turned and waved at the staff behind.

“Put the temp plates on.”

Two young men in Hyundai kit jogged over, two yellow-and-black temps and fixing screws in their hands. One front, one back. Electric driver buzzing a few times, under two minutes, on. Plates on. A staffer stood, keys in both hands to Si-on. The director made a “please” at him.

“Mr. Baek. Handover complete.”

“Thank you.”

Si-on nodded, took the keys. Passenger side. Eun-ah had already, extremely on her own, gone around, pulled the door, got in, belted. Once seated she could not help looking down and touching the stitch at the seat edge.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“I’m sitting in the passenger seat of the first production Aslan in the world?”

“Accurately, the first one with a temp plate.”

“…You have to do a technical correction at a time like this.”

Si-on did not take that. Head down, ignition. Outside. Ji-hun standing by that black nanny van, looking this way. He had come with the nanny van today; makeup kit, spare clothes, the pile of floor-tidy things, all in the boot. Naturally he could not leave with Si-on. Eun-ah put the window down a little, waved at him.

“Ji-hun oppa! You take the nanny van back, okay!”

Ji-hun rolled a very professional eye.

“Thank you, Manager Baek, for the reminder. I can tell which car is mine.”

“Then be careful on the road!”

Ji-hun looked toward the driver’s seat once.

“Boss Baek, you too.”

“I drive steadier than Baek Eun-ah.”

Eun-ah in the passenger seat turned at once.

“That’s a personal attack.”

“That’s a statement of fact.”

“I’ve improved a lot already!”

“Mm. Reverse-park from seventeen turns down to sixteen.”

“…”
