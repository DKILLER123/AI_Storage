Chapter Two Hundred Thirty-Eight

The Confession Hidden in the OST

The small hours · after the scratch on the case

One piano · one ballpoint · two songs owed

Lee Ji-eun’s apartment · the studio corner

With the scratch on the case as evidence, Lee Ji-eun extended only limited trust to what Baek Si-on called a scene demonstration.

Still — the accident of the phone hitting the floor had let her understand, bodily, how the leads’ first meeting was supposed to feel. A soldier barging into a doctor’s life by way of the totally unexpected. The other party’s rhythm knocked loose. Wanting to be angry, yet finding the man mildly interesting. Trying to ignore him, yet he keeps surfacing in exactly the places he shouldn’t.

That question, landing in Lee Ji-eun’s own chest, had quietly picked up a private weight.

She had never meant to step between Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri.

At least — she had once believed that very firmly.

Collaboration was collaboration. Roles were roles. A kiss scene was also work. Every boundary crossed could find its explanation.

Tracing it backward now, she couldn’t say herself where the change had begun.

Maybe it was ‘Way Back Home’. Maybe it was the night she listened to ‘Love Yourself’ as a love song. Maybe it was that arranging lesson in Milan.

By the time Lee Ji-eun truly registered how heavy this person had grown inside her heart, everything was already too far along to pretend otherwise.

She knew Choi Jin-ri had arrived first. She knew she looked like the one who came after.

And she had never once planned to forfeit because of it.

❝

That cognition did not suit discussion. It suited a song — perfectly safe, so long as the subject was renamed Kang Mo-yeon.

— the confession, filed under a drama title

Baek Si-on tried a few chord sets on the keyboard first. Light ones. Bright ones. The tone began in the warm grain of the piano and gradually took on a very thin layer of guitar.

Lee Ji-eun pulled a blank sheet from the desk, took up her pen, and let the melody into her ears. The pen touched paper. Her angle was Kang Mo-yeon: a surgeon ambushed again and again by an uninvited guest; a woman who says stop showing up while her whole body tracks where he is.

The first line came quickly.

the page, as written · first sheet

Quietly, without a sound, you’ve been becoming important in my heart.

This can’t go on. How did I get so captivated by you?

I don’t know the reason either — why do you keep lingering at my side?

Whatever I do, I notice you.

Whatever I do, I think of you.

That’s what I’m like — kissing you without ever meaning to.

The chords shifted under her as she wrote. At the line about noticing him she glanced sideways without meaning to — Baek Si-on was holding the chords with his left hand and tuning the rhythm track with his right, focused to the point of coldness, not watching her at all. She looked back down fast. That glance never happened. At the kiss line, the black ink spread slightly where the last word landed.

Inside a television drama, that line was entirely unremarkable. How could a Kim Eun-sook script possibly lack a kiss scene?

Lee Ji-eun stared at it for a long moment. In the end, she did not strike it out.

She could go on lying to herself. It was Kang Mo-yeon’s psychology. It had nothing to do with her.

❧ ❧ ❧

The chorus melody crawled out of the keyboard. Baek Si-on tried humming two lines, then stopped.

“Where are the lyrics?”

“Almost.”

Lee Ji-eun flattened the sheet and wrote the final chorus section.

the chorus, as finished

Say it — don’t hesitate.

Stop looking around like a fool.

You’re the only one for me.

Clumsy at everything else, I still only want to stay by your side.

Is this love?

This is love.

Pen down. She handed the page over. “Look.”

Baek Si-on took it and read from the first line down. The handwriting carried a rushed, slightly untidy slant; whole phrases had been crossed out and rewritten. On its surface the lyric was about a drama’s feelings — one person whose life another person keeps disrupting, guessing over and over why the other is always nearby, finally wishing the other would stop hesitating and just say it. It could pass, entirely, for character work.

Reading the line about kissing without meaning to, Baek Si-on’s thumb pressed lightly against the edge of the page.

Lee Ji-eun caught it at once and fired before he could open his mouth.

“Is there a problem?”

Baek Si-on raised his face.

“You’ve read the script?”

“When Kim Eun-sook writes a man and a woman in love, do you honestly think she ends them with a handshake and a goodbye?”

He looked back down and finished the chorus.

the chorus, as reread

Say it — don’t hesitate.

You’re the only one for me.

This is love.

He set the page on top of the keyboard.

“It doesn’t read like a doctor writing to a soldier.”

“Then what does it read like?”

Baek Si-on’s gaze stopped on her face. Lee Ji-eun sat very still; her fingers had already clamped the pen cap tight.

The moment before the answer landed, he put his attention back on the page.

“Like one stubborn person who can’t help hurrying another, even more stubborn person into speaking first.”

The pen cap gave a small click between her fingers. The sentence wore the coat of character analysis. Its content, though, was precise enough that she couldn’t determine whether he was describing Yoo Si-jin and Kang Mo-yeon — or the two of them.

She reached for the lyric.

“If it’s not right, I’ll change it.”

Baek Si-on’s fingers didn’t release the page.

“I didn’t say it wasn’t right.”

They held opposite ends of the sheet. She stared at him. He stared at her. The tension between fingers and paper hung, delicately, for a while — and in the end Lee Ji-eun let go first. Any longer and the page would tear in half.

❝

The stubbornness in the lyrics hadn’t earned its love yet; the stubbornness in the room was already owing damages to stationery.

— the tug-of-war, called on account of the paper

“Then let’s keep building it.”

Baek Si-on set the lyric on the music stand.

“Mm.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The song took shape along the existing frame. Baek Si-on adjusted the verse melody; Lee Ji-eun adjusted syllable counts beside him. One sung line landed stiffly on a downbeat — she rewrote the words, he swapped the tail note to match. The most contested spot remained the kiss line: Baek Si-on proposed keeping it, and Lee Ji-eun turned around and asked whether he was sure. They went one full round on what the character needed, and neither of them mentioned a single thing that had happened in reality.

The project played to its end. Lee Ji-eun sank into the chairback.

“What’s the song called?”

Baek Si-on circled the core line of the chorus.

“‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’”

She turned her face toward him.

“I’m asking you the name.”

“That is the name.”

“......”

Inside the song, the name urges the other person to stop hesitating. Out of Baek Si-on’s mouth, on its own, it sounds far more like an interrogation of what exactly she is sitting here doing. Lee Ji-eun decided to refuse the shoe-in.

“It’s weird.”

“It’s memorable.”

“It sounds like two people fighting.”

“They do spend the whole drama fighting.”

Unable to produce a better title, she let the name pass — for now.

❧ ❧ ❧

Once the song was done, the two of them discovered the practical problem almost together. ‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’ suits the ambiguous stage of Yoo Si-jin and Kang Mo-yeon perfectly as an insert song — but it is a solo.

And Kim Eun-sook’s original commission had been explicit: not one-sided heartache from the male lead, but two feelings answering each other.

From first bar to last, ‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’ holds only one voice doing the urging. The other person never speaks.

Sung by Baek Si-on, it works fine. It just cannot complete the assignment.

“So what do we do with this one?” Lee Ji-eun asked.

“We keep it.”

“For ‘Descendants of the Sun’?”

Lee Ji-eun reminded him:

“Kim Eun-sook is paying for exactly one song.”

“It goes in as a freebie.”

“An hour ago you educated me on market prices with three million dollars, and now you’re running a buy-one-get-one-free?”

“International clients and domestic acquaintances run on different rate cards.”

“Does Kim Eun-sook know she’s classified as a subsidized cause?”

“I sent her the quote screenshot.”

Lee Ji-eun: “......”

She could already picture the screenshot landing in Kim Eun-sook’s phone — and the reaction being precisely the one she herself had produced.

“Fine.”

She saved the ‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’ project into a new folder.

“Then keep going.”

The second song’s direction had to flip completely. ‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’ was written about: at some point I stopped being able to ignore you. The second song had to be written about: every time I meet you, I confirm my heart all over again.

Baek Si-on restated Kim Eun-sook’s original visual brief. This time Lee Ji-eun didn’t rush full lyrics — she went to the keyboard first, tried several brighter chord sets on the white keys, C major to open, then a little ninth-chord color on top.

The writing, as split

She plays the ascending line; he hums the descending answer.

She changes an inversion; he adjusts the rhythm of the chorus entrance to match.

One starts a phrase; the other completes it. Nobody keeps score of which half belonged to whom.

Her bridge — the passage from verse into chorus — drew his verdict: “like two people finding each other again across a crowd.” Her fingers paused on the keys; she rounded the last two notes’ rising arc until it felt warmer. His nod: that’s the feeling.

The concept, as it crystallized

Every time I see you.

Every time I get close to you.

Every re-meeting after another parting.

The feelings that identity, distance, and danger keep interrupting — all of them return to their original position the next time the two people meet. Outside the window, the sky went from deep blue to gray-white, and the session folder held a second complete project: ‘Everytime’.

The project saved. Baek Si-on was about to create a vocal track inside it, finger stopped on the touchpad, when a small sound came from beside him — Lee Ji-eun pulled off her headphones and set them on the console.

“Not recording anymore.”

Baek Si-on stopped.

“Why?”

The chair spun around. Lee Ji-eun crossed her arms, her entire face inscribed with the grievance of a laborer squeezed by an unscrupulous employer since dawn.

“Do you think I’m Jung Jae-joon?”

“He doesn’t strike this early.”

“So you admit this is exploitation?”

She stretched out her hand and began settling today’s hours.

“‘Legends Never Die’, ‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’, ‘Everytime’ — add it up, that’s almost EIGHT hours!”

She pressed down the last finger.

“Debt labor is protected by labor law too!”

Baek Si-on: “......”

The Lennon-guitar production debt was less than a day old, and the debtor was already researching labor rights. At this rate of development, the next step was a union.

Lee Ji-eun clutched her stomach.

“Also, I’m hungry.”

Baek Si-on deleted the freshly created vocal track.

“Fine. Tomorrow, then.”

Half the striker’s fatigue receded on the spot.

“Eat what?”

“At ‘Good Days?’”

Lee Ji-eun picked up her phone to check the time.

“If we go now, my aunt’s probably already closing up.”

She added:

“And you’re paying.”

“It’s your family’s restaurant, and I pay?”

“You called the overtime.”

Baek Si-on accepted the logic.

“Call and ask.”

Lee Ji-eun unlocked the phone, restating the terms:

“Okay — you’re paying.”

“Fine.”

She found her aunt’s number. Before the call could go out, the screen lit first with Jeong Han-teuk calling.

Lee Ji-eun answered.

“What?”

Jeong Han-teuk → Lee Ji-eun · voice · the studio, past midnight

Han-teuk opened:

“Are you still at the studio?”

“Yes.”

“Is Baek Si-on there too?”

Lee Ji-eun glanced at the man beside the console.

“What’s that to you?”

“Confirming the work location.”

“Then say your business.”

Han-teuk laid it out fast. Park Myung-soo had finished his schedule and finally freed up his evening; he wanted to meet tonight and keep hammering at the ‘Leon’ arrangement. The ‘Infinite Challenge’ crew considered it excellent footage — the festival’s point had never been only the final stage, but the whole arc of the host and the singer from disagreement to finished work. The crew was already nearby; if she agreed, rigging the cameras would take very little time.

Lee Ji-eun closed her eyes.

“They’ve already arrived?”

“They haven’t come upstairs yet.”

“Got it. Send them up.”

The call ended. Nominally it had asked her consent; in practice, the production had advanced to the waiting-at-the-door stage, missing only her own voice announcing the city gate had fallen.

The phone landed on the desk. The mood that two finished songs had restored got pressed flat again by the word ‘Leon’.

Baek Si-on read the outcome from the sound of the landing.

“Dinner’s off?”

“The crew is already nearby,” Lee Ji-eun said, flat.

“Then film first, eat after.”

“You’ll wait?”

“Mm.”

The gloom on her face cleared by half.

“Really?”

“Call me when it’s done.”

“You’re not allowed to eat first.”

Baek Si-on countered:

“Until what time are you planning to record?”

“No idea.”

“And if it runs till dawn?”

“Then we’ll have breakfast together.”

As clauses went, this one offered the waiting party flagrantly unfair terms. Baek Si-on had no intention of signing.

“Don’t sulk at Park Myung-soo-sunbae later.”

Lee Ji-eun sat up straight.

“Please. How many years has it been since my debut? I have at least that much emotional intelligence.”

“Couldn’t detect it when you argue with me.”

“You’re different.”

The answer was out before she could weigh it.

Lee Ji-eun herself froze for a beat. Baek Si-on’s hand stopped on the touchpad. The sentence could mean: the two of them were close enough that seniority etiquette could be dropped. It could also carry the other reading. She did not patch it. He did not press. He looked down and went back to the session files.

The projects, as locked

‘Legends Never Die’ — Riot, an unannounced event. ‘Everytime’ and ‘Say It! What Are You Doing?’ — Kim Eun-sook’s new OST. None of them may exist inside ‘Infinite Challenge’ footage.

Sessions encrypted. Temporary exports swept from the desktop. The last window closed.

The doorbell rang.

“That fast?”

“They’ve been downstairs waiting for your surrender all along.”

Baek Si-on packed the laptop. His original plan had been to leave before the crew came up — but hiding in another room now would lend a perfectly normal music collaboration the air of a crime scene. Lee Ji-eun went and opened the door.

❧ ❧ ❧

The shoot · lens caps still on

One crew · one sunbae’s deduction · one elevator going down

Lee Ji-eun’s studio · turned set

Park Myung-soo walked in front, the ‘Infinite Challenge’ director, writers, camera, sound, and several equipment staff behind him. The cameras still wore their lens caps; before any proper filming, the crew needed the location release, the shootable zones, and the equipment positions confirmed.

Park Myung-soo looked up and recognized the man standing by the console immediately.

“Ai-goo, Si-on-ah!”

The warmth could easily have convinced a bystander that the two of them shared a hot-spring subscription.

Baek Si-on bowed, properly.

“Hello, sunbae.”

Park Myung-soo stepped in, grabbed his hand, and shook it hard twice.

“Good, good. ‘The Three Evils’ wrapped?”

“Mm. Wrapped today, actually.”

“Wrapped and straight into work?”

“I’m free these two days — talking some music with Ji-eun.”

Park Myung-soo’s attention drifted to the console. Two half-finished drinks. One opened box of chocolates. Two headsets sitting side by side. Two chairs carrying the disorder of hours of use.

“Understood, understood.”

The nodding was vigorous. Late night. One man, one woman. A private studio. Discussing music. Set into an entertainment headline, that combination reads ambiguous all the way down to its punctuation. Park Myung-soo had been in this business long enough to know which questions could be asked as jokes, and which ones, asked aloud, would hang trouble directly on a young artist.

“Understanding, understanding — Si-on’s been so busy. ‘Sado’ promotion has started too, right?”

“First promotional event the day after tomorrow.”

Park Myung-soo seized the opening at once.

“Then come on my radio show once? Perfect for the film.”

The invitation was direct. He hosted ‘Park Myung-soo’s Radio Show’ on KBS Cool FM. For the singer Baek Si-on, radio was one of the most effective channels there was. For the actor Baek Si-on, it was close to useless — radio has no picture, cannot play a trailer, and cannot show off Song Kang-ho and his own character looks; a lead monologuing at listeners about Yeongjo, the Crown Prince, royal rites, and the rice chest risked converting film promotion into a Joseon-history night lecture. Beyond that, the ‘Sado’ campaign was scheduled centrally by the distributor, Showbox; Baek Si-on could not add engagements to the calendar for private ties.

“Thank you, sunbae.”

He lifted the laptop bag.

“But film promotion has to follow the distributor’s overall plan — that part isn’t mine to decide. If the promo team thinks radio fits later on, I’ll have Eun-ah contact your show.”

Park Myung-soo heard it for exactly what it was: a polite, complete, gapless refusal.

A trace of reluctance stayed.

“Then when you release a new song, you HAVE to come.”

“Okay.”

“Agreed.”

“Mm.”

“No hiding behind an America schedule.”

Baek Si-on promised generously:

“As long as you reach out ahead of time.”

With that secured, Park Myung-soo stopped pushing. At minimum, his future show now held a global-superstar-candidate guest slot. Tonight’s overtime session hadn’t even started, and the upside was already good.

The crew hauled the equipment in, and the studio’s open floor vanished under tripods, portable lights, and sound rigs. Baek Si-on cleared the walkway on his own initiative.

“I’ll leave you to your filming, then.”

He turned to Lee Ji-eun.

“Call me when it’s done.”

She nodded. He said his goodbyes to the crew, carried the laptop into the elevator, and the doors closed at the end of the corridor. The floor numbers began to fall.

Park Myung-soo waited until the elevator had touched the first floor before turning to Lee Ji-eun. The crew was still rigging; the sound engineer was busy measuring room tone; the proper shoot had not begun. He leaned toward her and dropped his voice low.

“Are you two dating?”

“Sunbae, don’t go spreading things.”

Read literally, she was warning him not to manufacture a scandal. But to a man who had already completed his on-site deduction, the sentence was easy to hear as the other thing entirely. Sudden enlightenment bloomed across Park Myung-soo’s face. No wonder. Tonight’s pop-up recording had clearly interrupted the youngsters’ private arrangements — and a rare pang of guilt welled up from the bottom of Park Myung-soo’s heart. He turned to the crew and clapped his hands sharply.

“Faster.”

“Rig those cameras NOW.”

“Stop fiddling with the lights — no dawdling today.”

The PD was taken aback. On any normal shoot, Park Myung-soo’s first order of business was locating food, and his second was complaining the lighting made him look old. Rushing the schedule sat in the back half of his priority list. Today, he had reversed entirely.

Lee Ji-eun was equally baffled.

“Why are you suddenly so motivated?”

“A musician’s time is precious.”

With a solemn face, Park Myung-soo pulled up a chair and sat down.

“Let’s begin.”

It was the first time in the history of his shoots that he had ever hurried one along. After all — downstairs, an international superstar was waiting for his girlfriend to get off work.

— the relationship, unilaterally confirmed by Park Myung-soo

Lee Ji-eun kept feeling that he had misunderstood something.

She thought about it for another second — and did not correct him.
