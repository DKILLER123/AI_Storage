Chapter Fifty-Nine

The Sounds of Life

July 28 1:00 a.m. Hapjeong-dong, the charcoal grill at the end of the alley A song in the air, a message on the phone, and a debt of face

The three of them sat in the studio staring at the just-signed DocuSign page for about thirty seconds.

Baek Si-on stood up first.

“Let's eat.”

Jung Jae-joon glanced at his phone.

“It's one in the morning.”

“Are you hungry?”

“……I am.”

Baek Eun-ah was already gathering up the empty water bottles and the printed pages off the table.

“Where are we eating?”

“The charcoal grill at the end of the alley.”

At the far end of the Hapjeong-dong alley stood a charcoal grill that stayed open even at this hour.

The exhaust pipe at the entrance spat white, oily smoke, mixed with the salty char of pork-belly edges and the rising steam. The whole alley was wrapped in that smell.

They pushed the glass door.

Heat, voices, and the background music on the ceiling speakers all rushed in at once.

Baek Eun-ah's steps faltered.

Then she whipped her head around, eyes going wide.

“Oppa! Your song!”

That little speaker in the ceiling was playing Way Back Home.

The pluck's grain jumped in and out of the crowd's din, the chorus mostly buried under a table of drunk older men playing their drinking game, but the melody still leaked out cleanly through the gaps.

Baek Si-on swept a glance around the room.

A one-a.m. grill, about seventy percent full.

Office workers, designated drivers, a few young people out to close out their second round.

Not one person looked up at him because of the song.

But Baek Eun-ah's face had already broken into sunlight.

“Oppa! They're playing your song! The convenience store played it too! I heard it in a taxi!”

Baek Si-on pulled up a chair and sat down, expressionless, picking up the menu.

“Three orders of pork belly. Four orders of short rib. Two orders of chicken thigh.”

Baek Eun-ah was still looking around, thrilled, as though any moment another customer might stand up and announce “the original singer is among us.”

Of course nobody did.

Baek Si-on turned the menu over.

“And two orders of cold noodles.”

His face didn't show it.

But honestly — it felt good.

This song, from its release at the start of July, had gone through an initial silence, a mid-period controversy, D's reversal report, a second detonation overseas — and now, here it was, playing naturally from a ceiling speaker in a one-a.m. grill.

Not because of promotion. Not because of fans voting it up a chart. Because it had genuinely seeped into the capillaries of this city.

Convenience stores. Taxis. Grills. Hair salons.

The shop owners didn't care who the singer was, didn't care whether he'd made it into Venice, didn't care if D had cleared his name.

They simply thought the song was good, and good for background — so they played it.

That was the true mark of a song making it.

◆ ◆ ◆ Not the numbers on a chart. The sound in life.

The grill plate came to the table.

Pork belly laid over the charcoal, fat beginning to hiss and bubble, white smoke rising off the edge of each slice.

Baek Si-on lifted a slice onto the hottest spot in the center of the plate, flipped it, waited for the edge to crisp, then rolled it into a lettuce leaf with garlic, red bean paste, and a sliver of green chili, and put the whole thing in his mouth.

Jung Jae-joon sat across from him, picking at a piece of short rib with his chopsticks, eating quietly.

Baek Eun-ah glanced at him.

“Teacher Jung, that re-dye came out okay. Way more natural than last time.”

Jung chewed the short rib and mumbled an answer around it.

“Last time the base didn't take the colour right, it went yellow. This time I switched to a—”

Halfway through, his chopsticks stopped.

“Oh, right.”

He looked up at Baek Eun-ah.

“That salon guy this afternoon... did you get back to him?”

Baek Eun-ah's mouth was stuffed with lettuce-wrapped meat.

Her chewing slowed.

“……Which guy?”

“The front-desk one at the salon in the Hapjeong alley. The one Boss Baek told to contact you. Said he'd done three years at a Cheongdam-dong salon for artist styling.”

Baek Eun-ah chewed the food down quickly, swallowed, wiped her fingers with a tissue, and dug into her bag for her phone.

She opened the call log.

The screen's glow lit her face.

Two missed calls. Same number.

4:17 p.m. 5:03 p.m.

And a text message.

Messages

Park Ji-hun · JB Hair · Hapjeong-dong · 5:17 PM

“Hello. I am Park Ji-hun from JB Hair in Hapjeong-dong. Mr. Baek Si-on told me to get in touch with you. I have three years of artist styling experience working at a Cheongdam-dong salon. I have attached a link to my portfolio. I look forward to your reply.”

Now · 1:26 AM — 8 hrs later

Sent at 5:17 the afternoon before. It was now 1:26 in the morning. Over eight hours gone by.

Baek Eun-ah stared at the text for a few seconds, then guiltily swiped at the screen, muttering to herself.

“Next time tell me about important things directly, don't let people call me on their own — how am I supposed to know you're not selling insurance...”

She mutter-swiped.

Her finger stopped.

Another unread message was sitting in the inbox.

The sender wasn't Park Ji-hun.

Baek Eun-ah opened it.

Messages

KBS 2 · Music Bank program team · 6:11 PM

“Hello. We are the KBS Channel 2 Music Bank program team. On behalf of Arts Division CP Kim Ho-sang, we cordially invite Mr. Baek Si-on to appear on this week's Music Bank stage. CP Kim places great importance on this collaboration and hopes to confirm the schedule with Mr. Baek Si-on's team as soon as possible. Should you have any needs, the program team will fully cooperate. We look forward to your reply.”

Sent at 6:11 in the evening.

Baek Eun-ah's mouth slowly fell open.

“Oppa.”

Baek Si-on had the tenth piece of short rib in his mouth.

“KBS.”

He didn't stop chewing.

“Music Bank.”

He finished, and swallowed.

“A performance invitation — issued in CP Kim Ho-sang's name.”

Baek Eun-ah held her phone out.

Baek Si-on took it and read.

Kim Ho-sang.

The head of music programming in KBS's Arts Division.

Not a junior writer testing the waters, not an executive PD going through routine. This was the CP himself, personally authorized.

Baek Si-on handed the phone back.

“Go.”

Baek Eun-ah blinked.

“Really? A music show has to be self-funded, doesn't it? The stage build, the backup dancers, the styling — the agency pays for all that. I remember from SM: one performance — rehearsal plus outfit, makeup, transport — at a minimum several million won...”

“Go.”

Baek Si-on repeated it.

Baek Eun-ah looked at his expression, thought about it.

“Then... why? You've always said you're an actor. What does a music show do for you?”

Baek Si-on picked up a slice of pork belly and set it on the grill, watching its edge curl slowly as the fat seeped out and crackled fine and clear against the charcoal.

Going on Music Bank to sing once did almost nothing for the song's chart numbers.

This record held its position on the strength of the overseas backflow and the social-news heat after D's report. One more performance, one fewer — neither would shift the tide.

And for his “actor” positioning, it contributed precisely nothing.

But Baek Si-on understood one thing better than anyone.

Kim Ho-sang was the CP with real power in KBS's Arts Division.

Arts Division and Drama Division were two departments, yes — but they worked in the same building and shared one personnel system. The mid-to-upper management network crossing between them was a knot of yarn no one could untangle.

If he snubbed Kim Ho-sang's face today.

By tomorrow it would be all over the KBS break room.

And the day after, when a casting director in the Drama Division slid a name up onto the table — Baek Si-on — what would the people around them say?

“Oh, that's the one who turned down CP Kim's music show after he asked in person.”

One sentence.

Nothing more was needed.

In the entertainment world of Korea, woven into an enormous spider's web of personal relationships, a twenty-two-year-old independent artist with no agency had no right to refuse a goodwill invitation at CP level.

Unless he had already grown strong enough that everyone came begging to get him on their own shows.

At that point he could posture. He could pick his dates. He could run a cost-benefit analysis.

But that time was not now.

He was still climbing.

“The rules of the trade.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded, half understanding, and lowered her head to draft the reply.

She typed two lines, stopped, and looked up at him.

“How do I pitch the tone?”

“Courteous, but not servile. Thank them for the invitation, confirm the attendance, then ask about the time and the person we coordinate the process with.”

Baek Eun-ah's thumb tapped fast across the screen.

Done, she held it up for Baek Si-on to check.

He scanned it for two seconds and nodded.

She pressed send.

Then locked the screen.

She'd barely let out the breath of relief when something lethal surfaced in her head.

“Wait! Oppa! If we're going on a music show this week, we've got no makeup artist, no stylist, no costume designer.”

On the industrial assembly line of a music-show stage, having no styling team was basically stepping up there naked.

Jung Jae-joon, sitting across from her, delivered the knife-blow with perfect precision.

“Scroll your message screen up a little. That Hapjeong salon guy — isn't he still waiting on your reply?”

Baek Eun-ah: “……….”
