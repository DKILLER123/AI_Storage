Chapter Two Hundred Eleven

When I See You Again

April 17

Evening

CGV Yongsan, Seoul · the ‘Furious 7’ Korea premiere

April 17. ‘Furious 7’ opened officially in Korea.

The premiere was held at CGV Yongsan in Seoul — red carpet, press pit, fan zone and the theater lobby all packed to the railings. Per the arrangement worked out with Universal in advance, Baek Si-on attended: walked the carpet, took the press photos, did the brief pre-screening greeting, and then performed ‘See You Again’ live.

From Universal’s perspective, this was the standard, by-the-book sincerity of a Hollywood blockbuster entering the Korean market.

From the Korean media’s perspective, the math was cruder and far more fun.

❝

The theme song of this picture is sung by a Korean. And that Korean is Baek Si-on. That alone is enough to send their headlines into orbit.

— the domestic press, doing the only math it knows

After the stage greeting, the staff would happily have kept him longer. There was a short media scrum pending. Several investor representatives wanted to say hello. Even Universal was sending glances meaning: a little more showmanship would be acceptable. And so Baek Si-on was detained for an additional hour.

When the premiere finally released him, he got into his van.

“Where to?”

“Busan.”

The driver and the bodyguard had long since stopped asking questions. The engine started, and the car merged onto the highway. Baek Si-on leaned against the back seat and watched Seoul’s night skyline recede.

He and Choi Jin-ri hadn’t seen each other in half a month. Since she’d left for the Busan shoot, the relationship had run on messages. The set was brutal, the director was strict, the zombie makeup was terrifying — but, she reported, the acting was deeply fun. His side hadn’t slowed either: recordings, songwriting, a sprawl of tedious business. He’d never managed to carve out the time to go.

Today was supposed to be an ordinary workday too.

Until a message from Choi Jin-ri arrived.

Message · Choi Jin-ri

Jin-ri

Missed you.

And so Baek Si-on went.

April 17

Night

The ‘Train to Busan’ set, near Haeundae · a rest chair behind the monitors

By the time his car reached the set near Haeundae, it was fully dark. He had the driver park on the perimeter and walked in alone.

The ‘Train to Busan’ shoot was tighter than the rumors. PA’s and assistants crossed back and forth; farther off, extras waited for the next block of blocking. He searched the crowd once and finally spotted Choi Jin-ri on a rest chair just behind and to the side of the monitors — in a standard Korean high-school uniform, head down over her script.

Or performing “reading the script.”

As he approached, her head was dipping, degree by degree, unmistakably fighting sleep. He stood behind her and looked down. Her thighs were bare in the April sea wind; the skirt’s fabric was in no position to fight that breeze. Worse, her chair sat right beside a walkway — every few minutes somebody hurried past, dragging a stripe of cold air behind them.

Baek Si-on didn’t say a word to wake her.

He simply took off his jacket, bent down, and laid it across her legs, naturally, like something he’d done a hundred times.

The instant the jacket landed, her shoulders twitched. She jerked upright and turned — sleep still clinging to her eyes, and then, in the next second, washed completely away.

“You came!”

Several waiting extras turned to look. Choi Jin-ri caught herself mid-lunge, aborting the whole trajectory of pouncing on him — but the laughter in her eyes could not be pressed down at any price.

Baek Si-on reached out and ruffled her hair, lightly.

“What time do you wrap?”

Right — the actual question. She checked her phone:

“No idea. A scene up ahead got stuck today, so it might be a long wait…”

“Do you have any more scenes today?”

She shook her head.

“My parts finished shooting today. But rules are rules — until the director calls the wrap, nobody leaves first.”

Baek Si-on considered for a moment.

“Wait here.”

He turned and walked toward Yeon Sang-ho.

The walk-over, as watched from her chair

Her private forecast, in order of dread: the director concluding she was pulling a diva move. Worse — Baek Si-on being so overbearing that the crew would file her under trouble by association. She was, after all, a supporting player with a modest page count here, nowhere near the tier where one leaves early as a privilege.

What she actually saw, from a distance: Yeon Sang-ho, bent over a monitor replay, straightening up the moment he recognized the visitor — the expression switching from “who is interrupting my work” to “sir, you should have said you were coming,” at switch-speed. A handshake. A few words. Then the director smiling her way and making an OK sign.

She couldn’t hear a word of it. She didn’t need to.

When Baek Si-on walked back, she couldn’t help asking, quietly:

“Are you close with Director Yeon?”

“Reasonably.”

“How did he just let me leave, like that?”

“Told him I had business.”

“What business?”

“Private business.”

She studied the face that offered absolutely nothing further, and decided against pressing. The result was good; that was the part that mattered.

“Oh.”

She stood, still clutching his jacket, and handed it back with a touch of embarrassment:

“Then I’ll go change — wait for me a bit!”

And she was off at a half-run toward the vans. Baek Si-on took the jacket, flicked it straight, and put it back on.

“I’ll wait.”

❧ ❧ ❧

By “change,” he had assumed roughly ten minutes. Uniform off, casual clothes on, done.

The wait turned out to be nearly an hour.

The hour, as logged in ten-minute increments

Minutes one through ten: standing outside the van, admiring the Haeundae night, letting the sea wind blow. Genuinely pleasant, as it happens.

Minutes eleven through twenty: hunger detected; a crew boxed meal procured from the PA table. Minutes twenty-one through thirty: said boxed meal, completed.

Minutes thirty-one through forty: phone triage — several messages handled, several pointless emails deleted. Minutes forty-one through fifty: leaning against the van, beginning to suspect a black hole operating inside it.

Minutes fifty-one through sixty: Director Yeon passing by, greeting him amicably, and observing, “Still here?” — a remark Baek Si-on chose to interpret as containing a hidden blade. Minute seventy: the van door opened.

Choi Jin-ri emerged.

The styling switch, as executed

Out: standby-mode actress Sulli, high-school uniform, script, zombie-adjacent exhaustion.

In: Choi Jin-ri, going to see her boyfriend tonight — pale-pink knit sweater making her skin look fairer than fair, the blush on her cheeks reading as sea-wind-made rather than makeup-made, hair loose enough to drift.

She jogged over, the steps carrying a small embarrassed hurry, and stopped only when close, tilting her face up at him: “Long wait?”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

The complaint he had fully composed in his head, one second ago, became unsayable.

“No.”

He reached out and tucked the wind-messed hair back behind her ear.

“Let’s go.”

She tipped her head at him:

“Aren’t you going to say something?”

“Such as?”

“Such as… do I look pretty?”

Baek Si-on pulled the car door open.

“Get in first.”

She huffed and climbed in. He got in after; she turned to look at him again.

“So — do I look pretty?”

His hands paused on the seatbelt.

“Mm.”

“Just ‘mm’?”

“Worth it.”

She didn’t follow.

“What was worth it?”

“The one hour.”

She turned to the window. Busan’s night view slid slowly past the glass, and in its reflection her mouth-corner curved up, blatantly, beyond any hope of pressing it flat.

She had planned to perform a little restraint.

The restraint lasted about three seconds — at which point she remembered that the car was, in fact, empty of audience. The driver drove. The bodyguard had roughly the presence of a car air freshener.

So she turned back, leaned a little toward Baek Si-on, and kissed him lightly on the cheek.

“A reward.”

Baek Si-on gave her a look.

“One hour buys this little?”

She didn’t take the bait — instead she tilted her head and gave it serious thought.

“Then next time, you could try waiting two hours.”

“Not possible. In two hours I’d starve to death.”

“You could snack on something first to hold yourself over.”

“Did.”

“Eaten? What did you eat?”

“Crew boxed meal. Minute twenty.”

She glared at him — and then lost the fight and laughed outright.

“You are unbelievable.”

Baek Si-on settled his arm across her shoulder and eased her over against him. She didn’t resist; her head found his shoulder and stayed.

“What are we doing tonight?”

“‘Furious 7’ first.”

“And then?”

“Seafood.”

She nuzzled into his shoulder. The answer read like an itinerary, and somehow that was exactly what made it feel safe. He had arranged the whole evening. All she had to do was follow.

“Oh — right.”

Choi Jin-ri suddenly remembered something.

“Have you been torturing Hara unnie horribly lately?”

Baek Si-on looked down at her.

“Did she file a complaint with you?”

“She says you and Jung Jae-joon are like two interrogators, grinding her down word by word.”

“If she’d coasted on her face two years less, she’d have earned ten fewer notes today.”

Choi Jin-ri burst out laughing.

“How can you admit to being that harsh with a face that proud?”

“I’m saving her from future criticism.”

She poked him in the ribs.

“Are you that harsh with juniors too?”

“I’ve been extremely gentle.”

“Then possibly your definition of ‘gentle’ is broken.”

Baek Si-on didn’t argue the point.

She nuzzled his shoulder again and closed her eyes. The streetlights withdrew one by one behind the car, light and shadow alternating across her face.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“I’ve really missed you lately.”

Baek Si-on said nothing — but the hand on her shoulder tightened, slightly.

Choi Jin-ri decided that was enough.

April 17

Late night

A Haeundae complex, top floor · Screening Hall — population: two

The cinema occupied the top floor of a commercial complex near Haeundae. He brought her in through the side door; staff met them at the entrance. A long corridor, then the screening-room door pushed open — and Choi Jin-ri stopped dead on the threshold.

The hall was empty.

The big screen glowed, still running ads, and not one single seat was occupied.

She turned to Baek Si-on.

“Just us?”

He nodded.

“You booked the whole theater?”

“Mm.”

She stood there for two seconds, then reached out and took his hand.

“You’re going to spoil me like this.”

Baek Si-on pulled her toward the center.

“You were spoiled already.”

“Yah.”

They sat in the exact middle row — not too close, not too far: the distance you’d choose if you genuinely intended to watch a film properly.

Whether tonight permitted genuine film-watching was, of course, another question entirely.

The lights dimmed. The screen came up. The opening chase moved fast, and Choi Jin-ri really did watch, at first — with occasional muttered commentary to the effect that no car should still be driving after that, and that this man surely could not die.

Baek Si-on’s reactions ran far calmer than hers. He had, after all, just completed the premiere circuit.

❝

His “professional viewing obligations” for this picture had been fulfilled to a satisfactory level. The identity he sat under tonight was simpler, and unpaid: boyfriend.

— the screening, reframed

Then the Lykan entered.

On screen, the red supercar burst out of a skyscraper’s window, tore through the glass curtain wall, drew its arc, and dropped into the floor below. Choi Jin-ri’s expression went from stunned — to a very particular sense of displaced reality. She turned to Baek Si-on.

“That car… is it the same as the one at my house?”

Baek Si-on watched the screen.

“Mm.”

She turned back. The car was still flying. She thought of the Lykan HyperSport sitting quiet and patient in her own garage — one of seven on the entire planet — and concluded, again, that her life’s script had probably been written by someone drunk.

“Sometimes I don’t even dare look at it when I walk past. Feels like looking too long shaves years off my life.”

Baek Si-on laughed despite himself.

“You could drive it more.”

“I don’t dare. One scratch might equal three years of my fee.”

“Scratches are on me.”

She gave him a sideways look.

“You said it.”

He gathered her in a little closer. Screen-light flickered across the two faces, bright, then dark, then bright.

Somewhere past the middle of the film, Choi Jin-ri lost the plot’s thread. Not because the film was bad — because her attention had split: perhaps half a tenth still on the screen, and the remaining ninety-five percent entirely on the man beside her. There was a very good smell about Baek Si-on. She buried her face in the hollow of his shoulder, closed her eyes, and breathed in.

“You’re not watching the movie.” Baek Si-on’s voice came down from above her head.

“I’m experiencing.”

“Experiencing what?”

“You.”

Baek Si-on looked down. Choi Jin-ri looked up. The distance between them collapsed to centimeters. On screen, the film had obligingly cut to a quiet dialogue scene; the hall went dim except for the faint reflections on two faces.

“You have popcorn crumbs at the corner of your mouth,” she whispered.

“No, I don’t.”

“You do.”

She lifted her face slightly and kissed him. He bowed into it and returned something considerably more substantial. By the time their lips parted, a plane was roaring across the screen; neither of them saw it.

She whispered again:

“The movie is still playing.”

“You started it.”

“I did not.”

“Then who invented the popcorn crumbs.”

“……”

Choi Jin-ri elected not to litigate the matter. She buried her face back into his shoulder. Two minutes passed. Her head came up again.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“Were you actually watching the movie?”

“No.”

“Aren’t we being terrible to Universal right now?”

“Why?”

“You sang the theme song so earnestly, and I’m in here busy kissing you.”

Baek Si-on looked down at her.

“I’m only responsible for the credits.”

“Oh.”

She nodded, seriously.

“Then I’ll continue.”

And she reached up and kissed him again.

❧ ❧ ❧

Where the film eventually got to, neither of them could have said. It wasn’t that they didn’t watch — they watched in fragments. Mostly, the film played itself up front while the two of them conducted their romance in the back.

When the end credits began, Choi Jin-ri was curled into Baek Si-on’s arms. Charlie Puth’s piano intro walked four bars first — and then Baek Si-on’s voice rose out of the surround channels. She went still.

On screen, the Paul Walker footage moved frame by frame. The white car and the silver car parted at an intersection, each driving toward its own direction. The sunlight was warm. The road was long.

‘See You Again’ · Wiz Khalifa feat. Charlie Puth · the credits, live

“It’s been a long day without you, my friend.”

“And I’ll tell you all about it when I see you again.”

She had heard this song many, many times.

It had never felt like this: farewell on the screen, company in the seat beside her.

“……”

The credits rolled. The lights didn’t come up. Neither of them moved. She leaned on him; he held her; the afterglow of the credits song dispersed, bit by bit, into the empty hall — until the last line of text faded and the screen went black, and only then did the house lights rise, slowly.

She looked up at Baek Si-on. His shirt had wrinkled a little; his hair was slightly wrecked; the color of his lips was noticeably deeper than at entry.

Baek Si-on stood first and reached down for her. She took his hand and came up with him. Out in the corridor the light was harsh and bright, a different universe from the screen-lit one they’d just left. She let go of his hand on reflex — and Baek Si-on caught it and held on, firmly.

“There’s no one.”

He said.

“Even if there were, it’d be fine.”

She looked up at him, eyes bent into crescents.

“Mm~”

Baek Si-on walked her toward the elevator. Nobody was in any hurry to be anywhere next.

❝

Because the best part of tonight was not the film, not the seafood, and not the Lykan, one of seven on Earth. It was this. Walking together. Hands warm. Wind just right.

— the evening’s final accounting, closed in profit
