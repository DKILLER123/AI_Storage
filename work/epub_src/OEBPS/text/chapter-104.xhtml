Chapter One Hundred Four

Vogue’s October Issue Burns Through Seoul

October 1 9:25 am Gwanghwamun · Kyobo Book Centre · imported periodicals Milan, Paris, New York, London, Tokyo on a kraft box. For the man on the floor, the cities meant price tags.

Kim Min-soo was on his heels in the imported-magazine bay, opening the overseas cartons that had come in that morning. The waybills were in English. Milan. Paris. New York. London. Tokyo. On brown boxes the names sounded like a fashion-industry map of the world. For him they had one practical meaning: a lot of stickers today.

Kim Min-soo

Part-timer · Kyobo Book Centre · Kyung Hee, on leave

OC · twenty-four, two jobs, not a fan, not not a fan

Official reason for the leave: personal. Actual reason: tuition. Seven-thirty at the shop, three at the chicken place in Hongdae. He knows what Vogue is. He also knows he is not the person it is for. He is the person who opens the box.

Twenty-four. Kyung Hee, paused. He was at the shop at seven-thirty, off at three, then Hongdae for a second shift over fryers. He knew Vogue. He knew GQ, Esquire, W, Interview, Dazed, the family of things that were expensive, thin, and did not look as if they had been made for ordinary people. The people who bought them were easy to spot. Dressed like models, not always models. Photographing a cover for Instagram. Standing in front of the imported bay able to finish, in three minutes, a conversation about whether this season’s Prada volume was a nineties return. He was not any of those people. He opened, stickered, shelved.

Today was October. Vogue. GQ. Esquire. Interview. W. Dazed. A few Japanese and British fashion books. He picked up Vogue Italia first. An Italian actress on the cover, black satin, an old Milan theatre behind her, makeup like an oil painting. Very Vogue. Very Italy. He did not think about it. Sticker. Shelf.

His thumb stopped. He went in. First post: Vogue Korea’s official, a picture. Black turtleneck. A Venice hotel window. Grey-blue lagoon. Baek Si-on on a wooden chair, body slightly turned, one hand on the arm, eyes not at the lens, past it, further.

Vogue Italia · October

The Man Who Refuses To Be Defined

Twelve pages. A Venice window. A lagoon that does not look at the camera either.

Condé Nast global pool · Vogue Korea full translation + editor’s note · Vogue Japan 4pp · Vogue US / Paris / UK online galleries

Not a cover. A feature. The difference, in this building, is a kind of money.

Min-soo stared at the caption for five seconds. Then he looked at the shrink-wrapped row he had just slotted: France, Korea, Japan, America. He understood, a little late, that Baek Si-on was inside them.

Eleven-oh-five. First customer. A woman around thirty, phone in hand, Vogue Korea’s post on the glass. She came straight to imported.

“Do you still have Vogue Italia?”

He stood.

“We do.”

“The one with Baek Si-on’s twelve-page original interview?”

“Should be.”

She picked up an Italia, looked.

“Korea?”

“Here.”

“Korea’s the full translation?”

“That’s what they said.”

She did not hesitate. Both.

Eleven-seventeen. Second wave. Two university girls. One reading off a phone: Italia twelve pages, Korea full translation, Japan four with a different layout, US no print, only the gallery. The other: so which do we buy. Italia, obviously, original. Korea too, we can read it. America? The menswear pictures are different. Three seconds of silence. A shared sigh. All of them, then.

Min-soo’s eyelid twitched. Italia ₩38,000. US ₩36,000. Korea ₩22,000. Three copies were several days of his food. They tapped a card with less friction than coffee.

Eleven-forty-five. A man in a suit, staff badge, the look of someone who had run out of an agency or a brand office on lunch, holding Vogue Italia, photographing the wrap and the issue data in the corner. Then, into a phone:

“Bujangnim. Not the cover. Inside feature.”

“Right, but it doesn’t feel like bought space. Vogue Italia main book, editorial, into the global pool.”

“That meaning is different.”

“He’s not just a Venice Best Actor now. He’s a male face you can bind to an international fashion narrative.”

Min-soo pretended to tidy bookmarks. His ears did not pretend. He could not hear the other end. He could hear the man go lower:

“Hyundai already signed. If we talk now, it won’t be September’s number.”

“If we want an Asian face for next spring menswear, we have to touch him now.”

“Later, watches, jewellery, tailoring will move first.”

He hung up, took Italia and Korea, one each. At the door he looked back at the shelf the way you look at an asset that has decided to appreciate.

Twelve-twenty. The manager came down, saw the cluster at imported, frowned, came closer, saw Vogue Italia in several hands, and changed expression.

“Baek Si-on?”

Min-soo nodded.

“Online says Italia has twelve pages, Korea the full translation —”

“Stock?”

“Italia seventeen. Korea twenty-three.”

“Pull half the entrance table. Italia and Korea.”

“Entrance is the Nobel literature longlist.”

“Nobel will still be there tomorrow. Baek Si-on will sell out today.”

“…Oh.”

Min-soo carried a stack of Korea and Italia toward the door. Shrink-wrap whispered on his arm. He thought, uninvited, that the whisper sounded a little like money.

One o’clock. Naver, second.

The Nobel longlist was moved. Nobel would still be there tomorrow.

Two o’clock. France gone. Korea gone. Japan a little under half. The manager was on the phone to the supplier, getting sharper.

“Not a girl group. Baek Si-on.”

“Right. Male actor.”

“I know he isn’t the cover.”

“The problem is the customers want the editions that have him inside.”

“Italia, how many more can you move? Ten? What’s ten?”

Min-soo stood there and watched a manager, for the first time, lose his calm over a male actor’s inside pages.

Three. He handed over the shift. Apron off, bag on, Hongdae waiting. At the staff-door he stopped. Looked back. The entrance table had two Japanese Vogues left. Two girls stood over them, discussing whether to buy one more to cut up. He watched a few seconds. Then he went to the register.

“Hyung. Staff price on Japan?”

The cashier looked up.

“You’re buying?”

“Mm.”

“You don’t even follow stars.”

“It’s not following.” He thought. “I just want to keep one.”

The cashier smiled.

“Fine. Twenty thousand.”

He tapped. ₩20,000. Much cheaper than France. Not cheap for him. He slid the intact Vogue Japan into the bag and walked out. Seoul autumn had started to have a shape. He waited for the bus, head down in the phone. Still second. The headlines had already rotated.

Korean press · afternoon of 1 October

Baek Si-on, twelve pages in Vogue Italia’s October issue; multiple Vogues pick it up

Not a cover, and the import shelf still sells out: Baek Si-on enters the global fashion content system. From a Venice cup to a Vogue world feature, an international path is no longer a wish.

Under the last headline, a comment with too many likes: We used to say cultural export and it sounded like a line in a government reel. Today there’s a Korean male actor in twelve pages of Italian Vogue, and suddenly the thing is specific. Specific as a shrink-wrapped magazine. Specific as a queue in a bookshop. Specific as noticing the world is actually looking at us.

Min-soo read it. The bus came. Doors. Card. A window seat. Gwanghwamun sliding backward in frames: buildings, trees, people, lights. Even now he did not think he was a fan. Not entirely. He only thought that years from now, if someone asked — the first time a Korean man made you feel that standing inside a world magazine could look like this, who was it — he could pull this October off a shelf, turn to the inside, and point.

This. Baek Si-on.

October 1 Afternoon SBS Deungchon · the studio of To Me, the One Who Is Cute No call time. A teammate who would not say she was not all right. A magazine that had to be hidden behind a back.

Choi Jin-ri sat in a folding chair behind the monitors with an Americano, watching a set she did not have to be on. Today had no booking. The diary was so empty she sometimes forgot she was still an idol in motion. She had come to visit Jung Soo-jung’s set. Two months ago, in the Red Light practice, Soo-jung had asked in front of everyone why Jin-ri had stopped trying while everyone else was killing themselves, and the sentence had kept Jin-ri from speaking to her for a long time. A fight was a fight. A freeze was a freeze. Soo-jung was still her teammate, and still the most awkward, hardest, least-bowing little sister in this strange family.

Especially yesterday. SM had formally announced Jung Soo-yeon’s leaving. Outside, a show. Fans tearing each other in the cafés. Piece after piece on the Jung sisters, Girls’ Generation, an SM civil war. Jin-ri knew that for Soo-jung none of that was news. That was her unnie. At an hour like that, Jin-ri had felt she should come. So she had come.

“Cut.”

The director stood up from the monitor. He did not say the take was good or not. He said rest a while. Soo-jung came out of the set, saw Jin-ri waving, and stopped.

“…Why are you here?”

“To see you.”

“You don’t have to see. I’m fine.”

“Mm. I know you’re fine.”

Jin-ri put the coffee on the stool, stood, and put her arms around her. Soo-jung went stiff for a second. Then the forehead came down onto the shoulder. Jin-ri patted the back, the rhythm you use on a child. Once. Twice. The director waited seven or eight minutes behind the monitor, confirmed the two of them had finished being private, and lifted a walkie.

“Ready?”

Soo-jung stepped back, dragged the back of a hand across her nose, and went back into the frame. Jin-ri sat. She had just sat when two staff beside her dropped their voices.

“Wow… this picture, honestly —”

“Is Baek Si-on’s proportion even legal?”

“Sexier than Rain.”

“Yah. Quieter.”

“Oh —”

The volume went down. Jin-ri had already heard. Not because she was trying. Because before the volume went down they had said a name. She thought, pulled the cap lower, put on a mask, and left the studio.

Ten minutes. A bookshop. A few people already at magazines. Only a few Vogue Japan left on the temporary entrance table. She pulled one. Counter.

“This.”

“Twenty-two thousand.”

Card. Out. Back to the set. The whole thing under twenty-five minutes. Soo-jung’s scene had just been passed. The director said good, print; a few shoulders dropped. Soo-jung turned, found Jin-ri returned, and her eye found the magazine.

“What did you buy?”

Jin-ri hid it behind her. Too obvious. Soo-jung’s eyes, which had not had much focus all day, found a point.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Let me see.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because it’s mine.”

Soo-jung looked at her for two seconds.

“Baek Si-on?”

Jin-ri had no answer that would survive the air. Soo-jung’s mouth moved. Shallow. The first thing all day that had been adjacent to a smile. Jin-ri thought, suddenly, that the magazine had been worth it. Even if all it had bought was half a second of Soo-jung’s face doing that.

She sat with it beside the monitor, waited until the staff had scattered into their jobs, then looked down and opened the wrap as if the wrap might bruise. First page, a title. Then body. She did not read the words. She could not. First picture: him on a Venice hotel balcony, jacket over the shoulder, white shirt two buttons open, wind off the water lifting a corner. Second: a dark sofa, an unlit cigarette, eyes down, as if he had just come back from a dream that did not belong to anyone. Third: black and white, a close face, bridge, brow, jaw cut cold by light. She turned. Beside her the staff were still going, quiet: this one is a poster, Vogue’s eye is vicious, is he about to do luxury, of course he is. The voices arrived in her ear through water.

What they were seeing was the man in Vogue. A Korean face Italian fashion had spent twelve pages telling. Fresh talk: sexier than Rain. That Baek Si-on was far. Far enough to be forwarded by every Vogue, priced by brands, analysed by press, an international asset.

Jin-ri had seen the near one. Near enough that when he ate spicy chicken he picked only the meat and would not touch the dried chilli. Near enough that he had threatened to tell Choi Min-sik sunbae on her. Near enough that she had heard him say, in a car, Choi Jin-ri is still an idol. Near enough to know he was not unkind. He was unkind in the way of a person who did not like kindness being found.

She put a fingertip on the face in the picture. A strange small satisfaction came up. Not he is mine. She did not have that much face. It was this: the magazine was for the world. What the world did not know was that he ate like an industrial sorting machine. And that he could take a cinema’s worth of almost-romance and say, instead, your hair is poking my neck. It itches.

Her mouth bent. She turned to the interview, ran it through the phone.

Vogue Italia · October · the last question

How do you define yourself? Actor, singer, or Korea’s next cultural idol?

Definition is a spectator’s job. Mine is to keep walking.

She looked at the exchange twice. The third time, she closed the book. Three seconds. Opened it. Turned back to the picture. Under her breath:

“Don’t act cool.”
