Chapter Sixty-Four

Waiting-Room Social

August 1 Afternoon KBS new building · waiting room Four girls with dyed hair ends, and a name on a sheet of A4

Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah stepped into the lobby of the new building's first floor.

The electronic screen above the front desk scrolled today's recording schedule. Music Bank sat in the afternoon slot.

The moment the two of them stopped, a young man in a work badge with a walkie-talkie gripped in one hand hurried straight over.

Two steps to a stride, and from a metre away, he dropped into a perfect ninety-degree bow.

“Baek sunbae! I'm the assistant PD for Music Bank. The station director specifically told me to wait for you here.”

The assistant PD straightened, the smile on his face so warm it was impossible to fault.

Baek Si-on glanced at the badge on his chest: Lee Sang-woo.

“Waiting for me specifically?”

That was overkill, surely.

He wasn't the cash cow of one of the big three. He wasn't a certified top-tier star. He was just a "wildcard" singer who'd fought his way back up the domestic charts on the strength of overseas sources.

An assistant PD waiting down in the lobby in person — that level of ceremony was a little excessive.

“I don't really know the exact reason myself. The director gave the order, so just follow me, please. This way.”

Baek Si-on turned to look at Baek Eun-ah.

Baek Eun-ah gave her shoulders a little lift, meaning don't look at me, I don't know either.

He didn't press. He followed.

Assistant PD Lee Sang-woo led the two of them through the ground-floor connecting corridor and swiped a card to get into the recording-district passage.

The corridor was busy with people coming and going.

Two stagehands carrying a camera track lifted an aluminium rail through a side door, and Baek Si-on sidestepped to let them through.

Behind him, a female assistant carrying four coffees trotted down toward the far end of the hall.

As he passed the side door to the recording hall, Baek Si-on's step slowed by a beat.

The door was half open.

Behind it, every stage light was on. Dozens of PAR lamps burned down from the truss overhead, white and blue beams crossing at the centre of the stage, heat rising up from the light banks in visible, floating halos in the air.

Four girls stood dead centre on the stage.

The ends of their hair were dyed in four eye-searing colours: red, yellow, blue, green.

They weren't in real stage outfits. They wore the tracksuits they practised in, and pinned to their chests was a white cloth about the size of a sheet of printer paper, each one written on in thick black marker with a name.

This was the rule for every newcomer idol.

In case the director and the camera operators couldn't tell them apart, during rehearsal they had to hang their names on their chests like a labelled parcel.

“Baek sunbae?”

Assistant PD Lee Sang-woo, spotting him stopped by the door, followed his gaze up to the stage and explained in a low voice:

“That's the new girl group SM just debuted today, running their morning rehearsal. Newbies, lots of rules — they have to walk the stage. Your rehearsal is in the afternoon, so you don't have to push yourself like that.”

Baek Si-on turned his head.

His gaze settled on Baek Eun-ah, half a step behind him.

Her eyes were fixed, unmoving, on the stage through the half-open door.

Baek Si-on knew exactly what she was looking at.

Those four girls with the white cloths pinned to their chests — maybe one of them had once squatted in the corner of the same practice room as her, sharing a package of convenience-store gimbap.

The night before a monthly evaluation, maybe they'd helped each other stretch, checked each other's mirror lip-sync, murmured the same song's lyrics together in the corridor.

And now. They had stepped onto a fully-lit stage, ready to meet the gaze of the whole country.

And she stood here, holding a black makeup case weighing twenty or thirty jin.

“Had your fill?”

Baek Eun-ah's shoulders jumped.

She turned her head; there was something pooling in her eyes, but it hadn't fallen.

“If you think standing here holding a case and watching them is some great grievance — do you want me to remind you of their actual situation?”

“They signed a seven-year exclusive indentureship at minimum. They sleep less than four hours a night. And every won they earn for the next three years goes straight to paying off the company's initial investment costs.”

Baek Eun-ah's lips parted slightly.

The little brew of self-pity she'd been simmering was shattered into powder by those hard, flat words.

“And you, my cousin.”

“You may not have had the fate to debut. But yesterday I spent twenty-six million won buying you a car.”

“And I am using what's left of my life to tolerate you turning reverse parking into a bumper car.”

“…”

Baek Eun-ah's mouth shut.

The dampness that had been gathering behind her lashes, not yet set, was instantly stirred into one indistinguishable lump of moved, wronged, and utterly speechless.

All that was left was a silent glare that couldn't produce a single word.

Baek Si-on tucked his hands back into the pockets of his suit trousers, tilted his head toward the assistant PD, and gave a little lift of his chin.

“Let's go.”

Assistant PD Lee Sang-woo, standing nearby, had heard the whole thing.

He was used to seeing seniors lay into juniors at the station, and managers brainwash their artists.

But this particular brand of hard-core therapy — curing internal friction with a used twenty-six-million-won car and a bumper car — was a first.

“Right, sunbae. Turn left up ahead.”

✿ ✿ ✿

The waiting room was on the first floor, near the stage entrance.

A sheet of A4 paper on the door, printed in bold type, no decoration at all.

“Baek Si-on.”

Assistant PD Lee Sang-woo swiped the card, pushed the door open, and stood aside to let them pass.

“Sunbae, this is your waiting room for today.”

Baek Si-on looked around.

The room wasn't big, but everything that needed to be there was.

A makeup mirror took up one whole wall, a row of warm bulbs in front of it, bottles of water and wet wipes laid out on the counter.

Off to the right, a dark-grey double sofa, a snack basket prepared by the show team and two cans of drink on the coffee table.

Baek Si-on gave a small nod.

“Thanks.”

Lee Sang-woo bowed and stepped out.

The door closed.

Baek Eun-ah set the black makeup case down with a heavy thunk beside the counter, shook out the fingers that had gone creased from the straps, and planted both hands on her hips.

“Cousin!”

“Mm?”

“From now on, don't say anything bad about my driving in front of other people.”

“Then don't cry little pearls in front of other people.”

Baek Eun-ah's hands dropped from her hips.

“I don't!”

“You do.”

“I really don't! It was just too bright in the lights, my eyes were uncomfortable—”

“I don't believe you.”

“Yah!”

“…”

The two of them were still bickering when the door was knocked three times.

“Knock, knock, knock.”

“Coming, coming.”

Baek Eun-ah assumed it was the stylist Park Ji-hun, who'd gone down to the car park, walking up to rejoin them. She turned, strode over, and yanked the door open.

Only, it wasn't him.

Standing outside were the four girls with the red, yellow, blue, and green hair ends.

Irene, at the front, still had her hand in mid-air, frozen in the act of knocking.

Irene

Leader of Red Velvet

Real-name counterpart · Bae Joo-hyun

The calm at the centre of a debuting group, the one who calls "one, two, three" and makes four voices land on the same syllable. On the day Red Velvet debuted she sized up Baek Si-on's name on the sheet of A4, shut the nostalgia gate in her own head, and led the four of them through the bow.

The moment their eyes met.

“Eun-ah?”

Irene's eyes went wide.

“Huh?”

Kang Seul-gi leaned half her body out from behind the leader's shoulder.

Kang Seul-gi

Red Velvet

Real-name counterpart · Kang Seul-gi

The member who leans half her body out from behind the leader's shoulder to ask the question nobody else will — bright, unguarded, and the first to let her curiosity slip out before the politeness catches up.

“Why are you here?”

All four gazes landed on Baek Eun-ah at once.

If this had been ten minutes earlier, down at the side door, Baek Eun-ah would have shrunk away. Or genuinely let a pearl drop.

But now, that scolding about "capital," about the "indentureship," about the "twenty-six-million-won bumper car" had closed into a perfect circle inside that eighteen-year-old head.

The startlement on Baek Eun-ah's face melted in an instant into a perfectly natural smile.

“Wow — eonnis! Congratulations on your debut!”

She stepped aside, opening up the space inside the room.

“I'm a manager now.”

A manager?

Irene blinked.

Without thinking, she turned her head to look at the sheet of A4 on the door.

Baek Si-on.

What that name had meant in Korea's music world over the past week, she knew very well.

The song that had pinned SISTAR firmly to second. The man about to carry Korean cinema into Venice.

In this staggeringly rank- and hierarchy-obsessed real world they had just stepped into, he was one senior absolutely not to be crossed.

In Irene's head, the nostalgia gate slammed shut.

She grabbed at Seulgi, who still had her mouth open to ask questions, and stepped briskly into the room.

The four of them lined up in the open strip of floor between the makeup counter and the sofa.

“One, two, three!”

Irene led the count.

“Happiness!”

Four voices rang out together.

“Baek sunbae, we're Red Velvet, debuting today! Please look after us!”
