Chapter Nineteen

Old Shoes for New, Stepping Out of SM

June Yeonnam-dong The sports lottery, and the fine print

Si-on didn't get to cash the tickets. Korea's sports lottery works like this: small prizes can be redeemed at a convenience store, but big prizes must go to a designated branch of the Industrial Bank of Korea. And you had to wait for the bank to open at nine in the morning. It was 5:40 a.m.

Eun-ah yawned, eyes watering.

“So… we wait over three hours?”

“No.”

“Then where?”

Si-on looked across the street. A 24-hour jjimjilbang, the neon sign lit, taxis parked out front.

“Bath.”

✿ ✿ ✿
June The bathhouse A soak with Han-teuk

The bathhouse was nearly empty. At this hour, the respectable people were at home asleep, and the not-so-respectable were about to be. The three of them each took a wristband locker and wandered off. Si-on showered, changed into the fresh shorts and T-shirt, found a lounge chair in the rest area, and closed his eyes for a bit. When he opened them, it was 7:30.

Han-teuk was already sitting beside him, in the same bathhouse clothes, hair still damp, clutching the waterproof pouch of tickets.

“You didn't sleep?”

“Couldn't.”

Han-teuk hugged the pouch to his chest like a family heirloom.

“I'm afraid if I fall asleep I'll wake up and find out it was all a dream.”

Si-on looked at him, said nothing, stood, and went to get three boiled eggs and a cup of sweet rice punch. Eun-ah drifted out from the women's side, hair wrapped in a towel, face flushed, looking a lot more alive than she had at four in the morning.

“Cousin, what time is it?”

“8:20.”

“Then let's go?”

“Sit a while.”

Si-on handed over an egg. “Eat something.” Eun-ah took it, cracked it on her head, and muttered while she peeled.

“How am I supposed to eat now…?”

She peeled it, put it in her mouth, chewed twice.

“…That's really good.”

✿ ✿ ✿
June 9:00 a.m. Hongdae, Industrial Bank of Korea Cashing out a small fortune

Nine exactly. The Hongdae branch of the Industrial Bank of Korea.

Cashing them was a bit of a production, mostly because Si-on was holding well over a hundred winning tickets, which sent the young teller into a panic and called the branch manager. Verification, registration, paperwork — nearly an hour of it.

“Mr. Baek, all your tickets are genuine and valid. Per the Korean Lottery Act, the portion under 300 million won is taxed at 22%, the portion over at 33%. Your total winnings—”

The manager held out a black bank card and an ID, both hands, to Si-on.

“361.8 million won.”

“After tax, the amount actually credited is 271.2 million won. It's already on this card. You can check it at any ATM. This is our bank's VIP card — annual fee waived, dedicated account manager.”

Si-on took the card, glanced at it, tucked it in his pocket.

“Thanks.”

The manager smiled, then added.

“Mr. Baek, this sum sitting in a current account earns almost nothing. Might I suggest some wealth-management products? We've just launched a—”

“No need.”

Si-on stood.

“Not needed right now.”

✿ ✿ ✿
June SM Entertainment A card, a number, and a plan

When Si-on walked into the lobby with the after-tax card holding 271.2 million won, Han-teuk had already redeemed his two tickets at a regular counter. After tax he'd gotten five and a half million won. Si-on ignored him and turned to the counter.

“Forty million won in cash, please.”

The teller looked up, glanced at him, then at the card in his hand, and without a word began counting. Han-teuk leaned in and lowered his voice.

“Why withdraw so much cash?”

“To pay a debt.”

“But you don't need cash for that, right? Why not transfer?”

“Cash has ceremony. And impact.”

The money counted, three bundles, in a kraft paper envelope, heavy. He tucked it under his arm and looked at Han-teuk.

“What time is it?”

Han-teuk checked his phone. “9:50.”

“Don't you have work?”

Han-teuk froze.

“Aish… I'm late.”

“Then run.”

Han-teuk bolted.

✿ ✿ ✿
June Outside the bank No rush straight to SM

Coming out of the bank, Si-on didn't rush straight to SM. First he steered Eun-ah into a department store. Ten minutes later, her frayed, blistering sandals were in the trash, replaced by a pair of limited-edition sneakers so cushioned they felt like walking on cotton.

On the way to SM, he called the legal man.

“Hello, I'm Baek Eun-ah's cousin. Yes, settling today. Right.”

✿ ✿ ✿
June SM Entertainment, lobby The receptionist picks up

SM Entertainment headquarters.

“Hello, do you have an appointment?”

“Yes. Legal department, surname Baek.”

The receptionist checked the computer, picked up the phone and dialled an internal line.

“Hello, legal? A Mr. Baek says he has an appointment with you… mm, all right, I see.”

She hung up, took two visitor passes from the drawer, and held them out. The two turned and walked to the elevator. The instant the doors closed, Eun-ah caught the receptionist's muttering.

—“Wasn't that the one who ran off a few days ago? Why's she back…”

—“Back for what? To beg forgiveness?”

—“Who knows. Probably to ask the company not to pursue the penalty fee…”

She kept her head down, staring at the new shoes on her feet. The shoes were wonderful, soft as treading clouds. Si-on suddenly reached over and patted her on the back.

“Stand straight.”

Eun-ah bit her lip, looked at him, and then slowly let her shoulders open, her back rise.

✿ ✿ ✿
June SM Entertainment, meeting room The room with the long table

The elevator doors opened. At the end of the corridor, the legal man was already waiting at the door of the meeting room. Seeing Si-on, he gave a by-the-book nod.

“Mr. Baek, Ms. Baek Eun-ah. This way.”

The meeting room wasn't large. A long table, a few chairs, the SM logo on the wall. The legal man moved to the head of the table and gestured.

“Please sit.”

Si-on didn't sit. He took the kraft envelope from under his arm and dropped it on the table.

Thump.

A dull sound. The kraft envelope was fat, the seal unglued, the mouth open — inside, bundle after bundle of won.

“Thirty-two million.”

“Bring the contract.”

The legal man looked at the envelope, then at Si-on, expression shifting.

“Mr. Baek, we're a legitimate enterprise. For a penalty this size, we normally only accept bank-to-bank transfer. We don't take cash.”

“Which Korean law says the won can't be used to transact?”

The legal man was caught.

“I don't mean that. It's just that the finance side has trouble booking it—”

“That's your problem, not mine.”

With that, he pulled out a chair and sat. Crossed his legs, hands folded on the chair back, looked at the legal man.

“I only take cash. You can verify it bill by bill. I've got all day.”

The legal man stared at this hard-to-budge, gangster-flavoured man, his face going through colour after colour, and finally, grinding his teeth, picked up the desk phone and dialled finance.

Finance came fast. A woman in her forties, glasses on, a portable bill counter in hand, still breathing hard when she came through the door — she'd probably jogged over.

“Where's the money?”

Si-on lifted his chin. The woman walked to the table, looked at the gaping kraft envelope, and blinked. Thirty-two million in cash just sitting there, exposed — it did have a certain impact. She didn't ask questions, just pulled open one bundle, fed the bills into the counter.

Whirr… whirr…

One bill after another flipped over, the digits climbing. When the last bill slid out, she glanced at the number on the screen and nodded.

“Thirty-two million. Correct.”

She unplugged the counter, picked it up, and left. The meeting room was quiet a few seconds. The legal man stood, walked to the wall cabinet, opened it, and pulled out a file. Came back, set it on the table.

“This is the termination agreement. Ms. Baek Eun-ah on signing ends it. And this is the debt-settlement confirmation. Once signed, the matter is closed.”

Si-on reached out and took it, flipped through. Termination agreement, three pages, dense clauses. Debt-settlement confirmation, one page, two signature lines at the bottom. He read it over, found nothing off, and handed it to Eun-ah.

Her hand shook a little taking it. Not out of fear. Because this stack of paper was so light. Four years. Fourteen hundred-odd days. And this was all they came out to.

She opened the first page, the dense clauses blurring, nothing in. What surfaced wasn't the clauses — it was the bag of oranges Sulli unnie had handed her, bent low, back in 2010, when she'd still been new and walking hugging the wall so as not to be in anyone's way. She'd eaten those oranges alone over three days.

“Ms. Baek Eun-ah?”

The legal man's voice yanked her back. She blinked and looked down at the termination agreement still in her hands, the first page not even fully turned.

“Don't rush her.”

Si-on's lazy voice came from beside her. The legal man opened his mouth, closed it. Eun-ah took a breath and turned past the first page. Second. Third. Nothing worth looking at — she had to sign anyway. She turned to the last page and picked up the pen.

The tip hovered over the signature line. Two seconds.

Were these four years worth it? They were worth a heap of nothing. But not entirely nothing, either. She wrote the first character. The stroke a little crooked, but recognisable.

Baek Eun-ah.

Three characters, done. She set the pen down and pushed the agreement back.

“Done.”

The legal man took it, checked the signature, and passed over the other one. The debt-settlement confirmation. She took it, didn't look, flipped to the last page, and signed. Two documents, two minutes. Four years, finished.

The legal man took the documents, re-checked both signatures, then brought out the company seal and pressed it hard onto the debt-settlement confirmation.

“Then, this debt is settled.”

He stood, slid one copy across to Eun-ah, and offered his right hand.

“It's a pity we couldn't see it through to the end with you. Still, I wish you every success going forward, Ms. Baek Eun-ah — may you shine in whatever field you choose.”

Eun-ah's body reacted faster than her head — on reflex she stood, bent forward, and reached out.

But the instant her rear left the chair, a broad palm landed on her shoulder and pressed her, firmly, back into the seat. Si-on stood, stepped past her, took the two documents from the table, held them up to the light, looked them over, then folded them into a neat block and tucked them into the breast pocket of his flower-print shirt.

“Let's go.”

He didn't look at the legal man, nor at the hand still hanging in the air. He turned and walked toward the door. Eun-ah scrambled up and followed. At the threshold she glanced back, unable to help it. The hand had already been withdrawn. The man stood there, looking at the bag of money, then at Si-on already at the door, his expression neither good nor bad.

The door closed.

Eun-ah jogged to catch up to Si-on.

“Cousin.”

“Mm?”

“Why didn't you let me shake his hand just now?”

“Did you want to?”

“Not… wanted to. I just… it's habit.”

“Then break the habit.”
