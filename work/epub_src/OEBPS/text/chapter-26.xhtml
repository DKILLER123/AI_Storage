Chapter Twenty-Six

The Layman’s Absolute Ear

The afternoon after the number Studio hours Hapjeong-dong · 401 Twenty pyeong. A pad like cotton. The looseness of a night walk.

The studio was not large. A little over twenty pyeong.

401 · the room

Cablesoff the desk, a few loops on the floor
Boothhalf-height · foam on three sides · acrylic on the fourth
Insideone mic · one pair of cans · one folding chair

Five SM booths away from what a trainee calls a studio.

Baek Eun-ah looked the place up and down from the door.

The distance between this and the “music studio” in her head was about five SM recording booths.

She didn’t speak. Found a folding chair by the door, sat, took out her phone, opened Notes.

First day on the job.

Professionalism.

Jung Jae-joon sat back into the swivel, half a turn to face Baek Si-on.

Jung Jae-joon

Independent producer · studio 401

The ear that finds the note

An underground producer working out of a cluttered fourth-floor studio. To Baek Si-on he is less a business partner than a translator, reading a picture in someone's head and writing it in sound.

“Hara mentioned you. Said a DSP sunbae, looking for a producer to make a song.”

He leaned into the chair-back, both hands crossed behind his head.

“Talk first. What do you want to do?”

Si-on didn’t sit.

Stood at the desk, both hands in his pockets.

“I have a song. The melody’s in my head, maybe sixty, seventy percent done. I need you to make it. Arrangement, recording, mix, the whole set.”

“Let me hear it first.”

Si-on nodded, walked to the door of that half-height booth.

“Use this?”

“Whatever. Hum standing there if you want.”

Si-on thought, then still walked in, adjusted the mic height. Didn’t put the cans on.

Closed his eyes.

Quiet a few seconds.

Then started to hum.

● RECno cans · verse first

No intro. Straight from the verse.

Not loud. The skeleton of the melody clear as a line drawn on paper.

To the chorus.

When that rising melody came out of his mouth, Jae-joon’s hand on the cup stopped.

The place of the key change, outside expectation.

Not a hard cut. A slide, like water from one step to the next, natural enough you almost didn’t notice the key had already changed.

Si-on finished, opened his eyes.

Outside the booth, quiet about ten seconds.

Jae-joon set the mug on the desk, turned the chair to face him.

“Hum it again.”

Si-on hummed it again.

This pass, Jae-joon’s hand on the MIDI.

When Si-on hummed the third line, the keys started to sound.

Very light, one note following one note, two chords tried, the first wrong, the second locked.

By the chorus Jae-joon’s fingers had already found the main chord walk.

Right hand with the melody, left a root in the low, thin, just enough to hold the line up.

Si-on stopped.

Jae-joon stopped too.

He leaned back, looking at Si-on.

“You wrote this?”

“Yes.”

“That chorus change is pretty. You thought of it yourself?”

“Mm.”

Jae-joon sat forward, both hands on his knees.

“Fine. Then how do you want to make this song? Style, mood, reference tracks — any thoughts?”

Si-on opened his mouth.

Style.

Mood.

Reference tracks.

He knew all three words. Together they pointed at a professional field he had never set foot in.

Last life he was an actor. Breaking a role, reading a script, analysing motive — he could do that with his eyes closed.

Music production, he truly did not know a thing.

So he used the only way he had — an actor’s way.

“Have you heard a convenience store late at night?”

Jae-joon blinked once.

“…What?”

“Two, three in the morning. Nobody on the street. You push into a convenience store. The fluorescents a bit too white. The cold case humming. The clerk almost asleep on the counter. You take a can of beer, pay, walk out.”

Si-on gave him a shot composition.

“The air outside warmer than inside. You stand in the doorway, take a sip, don’t go anywhere, just stand. The streetlight yellow. The wind muggy. Somewhere far, music, but you can’t hear what song. Only the kick. Dull. One. One.”

He stopped a beat.

“This song should be that kick. Not exploding next to your ear. Drifting over from two streets away. You can’t hear it true, but you’ll stand there, willing to listen a little longer.”

Finished.

Jae-joon leaning in the chair. The look at Si-on changed.

Not the blank of “what did you just say.” A very particular face.

Like a translator hearing a strange language, not catching every word, but guessing the meaning and holding it.

“What you just said.”

Jae-joon reached up, touched his chin.

“Dropped onto the technical floor.”

He turned the chair to face the keys.

401 · into technique

Probably a Tropical House road. BPM around a hundred. Synth laid under. Vocal sample for the hook. Kit electronic but the reverb pulled long, and it will sound like that—

the looseness of a night walk.

Every term coming out of Jae-joon’s mouth sounded to Si-on like a foreign language.

BPM, synth, hook, reverb.

He knew each character.

Strung together, he didn’t.

The last word he heard.

Looseness.

Yes.

That was looseness.

He nodded once.

“Right. That’s it.”

Jae-joon didn’t ask more.

He turned, right hand on the keys, left a few clicks on the computer.

A very light kick sample came from the speakers. Dong, dong, dong, dong. The tempo not fast. Like a heartbeat.

Then the right hand went down.

A synth timbre spread. On that ground he played a chord walk, four bars, looped once.

Looked back at Si-on.

“This direction?”

Si-on listened a few seconds.

The direction was right.

But one place was not.

“Too clean.”

Jae-joon lifted an eyebrow.

“Where?”

“The layer underneath.”

Si-on didn’t know how to say it in the terms. Thought.

“The sound laid at the very bottom. The one like cotton.”

“Synth pad.”

“Right. That. Too clean.”

Jae-joon stared at him two seconds, turned back, moved a parameter on the computer.

Timbre not swapped. A very fine grain added.

“Like this?”

Si-on’s brow loosened.

“Like this.”

Jae-joon mm’d, marked on the keys, saved.

Leaned back, turned to look at Si-on. The face complicated.

“That pad texture just now — nine singers out of ten wouldn’t hear it.”

Jae-joon tapped the desk with a forefinger.

“You’re not listening with your ears. You’re listening with the picture in your head.”

Si-on didn’t pick that up.

Not humility. He truly didn’t know how to pick it up.

He had only felt that sound was wrong, couldn’t name the technical reason, but looking at it it sat wrong.

Eun-ah on the folding chair by the door, fingers flying in Notes.

She had not understood a single word.

But a manager recording a meeting — that was basics.

Whether she could read what she had written later was later’s business.
