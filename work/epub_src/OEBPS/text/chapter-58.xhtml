Chapter Fifty-Eight

The Definition of a Bet

July 27 → July 28 Late night Hapjeong-dong, studio 401 All of it, or none of it

Hapjeong-dong.

Studio 401.

Baek Eun-ah sat in the folding chair that squeaked whenever she shifted, phone screen lit up. A new email had just flashed into the inbox of her mail app.

The sender was Scooter Braun's team.

“They replied?” Baek Si-on stood beside the mixing board holding an iced americano.

“Replied.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded, her gaze lingering a second on Jung Jae-joon's freshly flaxened hair before moving to Baek Si-on.

“He says, sign a bet agreement.”

“Specifically?”

“If the song takes Billboard Hot 100 number one, the producer takes four points on the master. If not, it's a thirty-thousand-dollar buyout and everything else stays the same.”

The bet · Billboard Hot 100

What Do You Mean?

Justin Bieber

1

Number one → producer four points on the master. Else a thirty-thousand-dollar buyout.

Baek Eun-ah read it out, then flipped the phone around to show Baek Si-on the original email.

Baek Si-on glanced at it.

A bet.

A clean counter-punch.

Scooter Braun really was one of the sharpest managers on the planet.

Jung Jae-joon's expression hadn't shifted much when he heard the terms.

Four points against thirty thousand dollars.

Win, and you get the long-term split. Lose, and you've got a thirty-grand floor. However you looked at it, it didn't read like a loss.

Baek Si-on thought for a moment.

“The terms need to change.”

“Change them how?” Baek Eun-ah asked.

“Since it's a bet, let's bet it clean. Take Billboard number one, and the producer's master split isn't four points — it's five. And the producer credit line is allowed exactly one name. Jung Jae-joon.”

In the Western music industry, the producer credit on a big hit usually carried three to five names: co-producer, executive producer, assistant producer. Each one carved off a slice, each one helped themselves to a portion.

Sole credit meant something else entirely.

Every person in the world who heard this song, who opened its information page, every single one would see the name behind “Produced by” — and there would be only one.

Jung Jae-joon's expression stayed light.

Five points, sole credit.

It sounded better than the last version. So much better it was almost unreal.

“And the cost?” he asked.

“If it doesn't reach number one, the demo is a gift.”

The ease on Jung Jae-joon's face cracked.

Five points plus sole credit. Or nothing.

“Boss Baek.”

“Mm.”

“Are you serious?”

Baek Si-on looked at those wide, stunned eyes.

“The meaning of a bet is: take it all, or take nothing. Anything in between isn't a bet — it's haggling.”

Jung Jae-joon stared at him for a long moment, then slowly leaned back in his chair.

“Genuinely insane.”

He paused a beat.

“Bet it.”

Baek Si-on turned to Baek Eun-ah.

“Reply exactly that.”

Baek Eun-ah lowered her head and started typing, thumb flying across the screen.

✿ ✿ ✿

It wasn't even an hour later.

The second email came in.

No Hollywood long-windedness. No probing at the extreme terms, no feelers sent out to test the ground.

Baek Eun-ah looked at the single short line of English on the screen.

“Crazy. But deal.”

Insane. But I'm in.

Then, right behind it, a seventeen-page English PDF electronic contract came through over the system. She had the translation app and the PDF open side by side, line by line, cross-checking each paragraph.

About three minutes in, she looked up and rubbed her aching eyes.

“Oppa. I can't read this.”

Baek Si-on walked over, bent down, and skimmed from page one to page three.

Songwriting allocation, master revenue split, the bet trigger conditions, cross-border tax clauses, jurisdiction —

Every sentence was legal English. Every clause was wrapped in three layers of qualifier. Every comma seemed placed precisely where it would make a layman's temples throb.

About two minutes in.

He gave up too.

A professional matter needed a professional. He needed a lawyer who understood overseas contracts.

He pulled out his phone, opened the contacts, and ran his thumb down the list.

Goo Hara.

He paused.

She was close with the Japanese legal side. The American direction wasn't quite her match.

He kept scrolling.

Choi Jin-ri.

SM did have a North American legal team, but at a juncture like this, having her help route a cross-border contract would only make her position inside the company that much more fragile.

He scrolled past her.

Lee Ji-eun.

LOEN Entertainment.

Korea's largest music distributor, its business covering Japan, Southeast Asia, and North America. Their legal department processed hundreds of cross-border copyright contracts a year.

He pressed.

Three rings.

Answer.

Baek Si-on → Lee Ji-eun · on the phone

“Hello?”

“Borrowing a lawyer who knows American contract law.”

“You forgot again?”

Baek Si-on's temple jumped.

Honorifics.

“I can pay double the appearance fee,”

he insisted, clinging to his supremely practical money-first principle.

“It's not about the money.”

Lee Ji-eun's tone stretched out a little.

This was her, openly, waiting for him to lower his head and use the honorific.

Baek Si-on's back molars clenched quietly.

Even facing down that iron-willed professor at home, he'd never been this lowered.

“If we count by group debut date, A'ST1's April is earlier than your September.”

He tried one last fight.

“But your status now is an independent artist. You're not bound by a group contract.”

Her logic was as tight as a judge's.

“………”

A long silence.

Then Baek Si-on, with no warning, swung a hard, empty punch at the air in front of him.

Baek Eun-ah's eyes went wide in the folding chair.

She'd never seen this reaction from him. Even those first two days when Way Back Home had been running cold, he'd just stood on the balcony at night feeding the mosquitoes, not a flicker on his face.

And now, because of one phone call, he was punching the air like a child.

Who on earth was on the other end?

Baek Si-on closed his eyes.

Gave in.

“……Sunbae.”

The delighted laugh came back through the receiver at once.

“I'll find someone for you. Send me your address.”

The call ended.

Step by step, that was the whole matter settled.

Baek Eun-ah leaned forward at once.

“Oppa. Who was that?”

Baek Si-on slid the phone into his pocket and looked down at her, utterly expressionless.

“Don't go snooping around in social affairs.”

“……Oh.”

Baek Eun-ah retracted her neck and went back to the PDF she couldn't read.

✿ ✿ ✿

LOEN's legal counsel, Attorney Choi, arrived at nine.

Forties, grey suit, a briefcase holding a laptop and a pen.

A short greeting, a business card handed over. No tea — mineral water, in its place.

To work.

Attorney Choi spread the PDF out on the screen. To his left sat the Korean translation Baek Si-on had printed out; in his right hand a pen. He went through it clause by clause.

Three core provisions.

Scooter Braun / Bieber — bet agreement · core terms

Clause One · Songwriting

Creator 80% · artist 20% — accepted as-is.

Clause Two · The Bet

Number one on the Billboard Hot 100 within the first eight weeks → producer master split 5% · sole credit.

Not achieved → producer receives a one-time $1 · songwriting copyright to artist side.

Clause Three · Surrounding

Jurisdiction, audit rights, indemnity — standard, no ambiguity.

Terms extreme, drafting clean, trigger condition explicit. No grey space anywhere.

Baek Si-on pointed to the $1 on page five.

“What does this mean?”

“Consideration, in American contract law.”

Attorney Choi pushed his glasses up.

“Give a song away for nothing, and legally it's a gratuitous gift — and the giver can revoke it. Pay one dollar and the nature of it shifts from gift to sale. Once the transaction completes, you can't take it back. It's a protective clause for them. But it does you no real harm either.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

The rest — jurisdiction, audit rights, indemnities and damages — went by as Attorney Choi worked through them in about two hours.

Baek Eun-ah sat to one side, three pages of notes filled, most of which she couldn't understand herself, but the habit of taking notes had long since been carved into her muscle.

Jung Jae-joon stayed put in front of the mixing board the entire time, not one word interjected.

He didn't understand the law, and he didn't need to. Baek Si-on had brought in a professional to do the professional thing. All he had to do was wait for the result.

Twelve-twenty in the morning.

The last clause was confirmed.

“No problems. It's signable.”

Baek Si-on pulled a kraft-paper envelope out of the bag at his feet.

“Attorney Choi, sorry for the late hour. A small token.”

Attorney Choi glanced at the thickness of the envelope, didn't refuse, and tucked it into the side pocket of his briefcase.

“Contact me anytime.”

Baek Si-on saw him to the door. The footsteps faded away down the corridor in the small hours.

✿ ✿ ✿

The studio was left with just the three of them.

On the screen sat the final confirmation page of the DocuSign e-signature system.

Baek Si-on took the mouse and drew his name on the e-signature box.

The strokes weren't smooth. A mouse feels nothing like a pen, and what came out was a squiggle, like the homework of a first-grader.

But the legal effect was the same.

Signed, he pushed it in front of Jung Jae-joon.

“Yours.”

Jung Jae-joon swivelled away from the mixing chair and looked at the empty signature line waiting on the screen.

“Boss Baek.”

“Mm.”

“If the bet lands—”

Jung's gaze left the screen and settled on him.

“—I'll give up my arrangement fee.”

That was a considerable number. And he was giving it up of his own accord.

Being able to take five points on the master plus sole credit already moved him into the rarefied air of global producers.

But this was the empire Baek Si-on had built for him. Handing over the arrangement fee now was a very standard gesture of homage.

Baek Si-on made no grand speech, and didn't try to talk him out of it.

“Do you know The Neptunes?”

Jung Jae-joon blinked.

Of course he did.

Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo.

One blazing in the spotlight, one steering the finest pop sound of the early two-thousands from behind the scenes. Bound together, they'd ruled over an enormously long American golden age.

“I know them.”

Jung nodded.

“From now on, that's our model.”

At that wildly ambitious line, Jung Jae-joon said nothing at all. He signed his name in the counterpart slot, quick and neat.

A little tidier than Baek Si-on's. But not by much.

Signed.

He let go of the mouse, turned, and stuck out his right fist, clean and deliberate.

Baek Si-on raised his hand.

Two future absolute rulers of Korean pop, in a room rented for remarkably little money, completed a gesture that carried real weight.

Bam.
