Chapter Eighty-Six

Jung Jae-joon: Those Lyrics Are Clean as an Elementary Summer Diary

The day of the return Late afternoon into 19:45 Insight · KWHL · Hapjeong 401 Two million in an envelope. Seventy million on a receipt. Bang Bang. We, not I.

Insight. When Park Jae-won pushed the door in, the shirt under his arm was already soaked through. He put the lav mic on the desk and reached for a paper cup at the cooler.

“Jae-won. KWHL again.”

Son Nam-won from behind the desk. Jae-won’s hand stopped on the cup rack. KWHL. Korea Women’s Hot Line. Domestic violence, sexual violence, a national rights body for women, children, single mothers.

“Now?”

“Now. When Baek Si-on took first on Music Bank he made a pact. July’s digital settlement already went out. Knowing him, the money’s either already in or going in.”

Son Nam-won came up off the chair.

“If you can shoot him donating in person, best. If you can’t get him, shoot the remittance receipt. The second you have it, send it to me.”

Jae-won took the hand back from the cups.

“Oh.”

From a pocket, a memory card, onto Son Nam-won’s desk.

“Airport footage.”

Son Nam-won picked it up, turned it, into the reader. Jae-won was turning to go.

“Wait.”

Son Nam-won opened the right-hand drawer, fished a white envelope, unsealed, tossed it Jae-won’s way. An arc. Into the chest. Jae-won caught it on instinct, pinched the thickness. Opened. Cash. Yellow fifty-thousand-won notes, a stack. Rough count. About forty. Two million. He looked up. Son Nam-won was already back at the screen, clicking the mouse, importing. No explanation. No “you worked.” Just an envelope thrown. Jae-won stuffed it in a back pocket, bent a ninety-degree bow.

“Thank you, sajangnim!”

Louder than he had yelled Si-on’s name at the airport. Then the lav off the desk, door, out. The door slammed. A sticky note on the frame blew to the floor. Son Nam-won did not care. Watched the copy bar. Ten-odd seconds. File in. Fast-forward. At Jae-won’s question he cut back to normal, watched once more. Then the backend editor.

Insight · draft

Son Nam-won

SHOCKING! The first woman the new Venice Best Actor went to see after coming home — is actually her?!

Title done. Son Nam-won looked once. Mm. I really am a genius.

Even with an extra twenty thousand won of tip for a taxi driver standing on the accelerator, Jae-won still lost to Seoul’s evening crush. Si-on had left about fifteen minutes before he arrived. After Jae-won showed an Insight badge the KWHL staffer hesitated half a minute. He added, “Mr. Baek authorised us to report.” Half true. She had no way to check. From a drawer, a photocopy of the official donation receipt. Jae-won lifted the camera. Focus. Shutter. The numbers on the glass, clear.

KWHL · official receipt

copy

Donor: Baek Si-on. Amount: 70,000,000 KRW.

Jae-won looked at the slip. For the first time, toward an entertainment person, something close to looking up. In this circle of tax dodge and tax theft, in this industry that used charity as PR and donation figures as topic marketing — someone had actually given. Given and left. Not even a lens held. If the man had a hundred billion and ten million dollars a year, fine. What rank was Baek Si-on? Volpi Cup yesterday. Commercial value not yet cashed. That Way Back Home digital money just starting, not even warm, already smashed into a foundation’s corporate account.

Same evening Before 19:45 Hapjeong · 401 Garlic fried chicken. Bang Bang. A red marker. Stadium clap.

Hapjeong. 401. Si-on rang. Three seconds. Door from inside. Jung Jae-joon in the doorway. First reaction not surprise. A scan. Top to bottom.

“Venice Best Actor honours this shack. Need me to lay a red carpet?”

“No. Instant-noodle boxes will do.”

Si-on went in. Same as before: forty pyeong stuffed with kit. Monitors, synths, MIDI, desk, the tower in the corner always humming low. Only change: a takeout box on the table. Fried chicken. Half eaten.

“This is dinner?”

“Correction. Afternoon tea.”

“Afternoon tea is garlic fried chicken?”

“Mozart’s afternoon tea is Viennese coffee and mousse. Mine is iced Americano and takeout chicken. The dopamine mechanism is, in essence, identical.”

“…In essence it isn’t.”

Fist bump. Jae-joon moved the chicken, made a scrap of desk.

“Say it. What’s so urgent?”

Si-on did not waste words. The notebook written in Korean Air first class, that page, held out.

“Look at this. Any juice in it?”

Jae-joon took it. Scanned those extremely short lines.

“You wrote this?”

Si-on’s face did not move.

“A friend.”

“Oh.”

Jae-joon looked again. Lips moving with the characters, hunting a basic 4/4 rest.

“How do you want to do it?”

“You don’t think the lyrics are a problem?”

Jae-joon looked up, puzzled.

“What problem?”

“You don’t feel…”

Si-on weighed the word.

“A bit chuunibyou?”

Jae-joon laughed out. Turned, Spotify on the side screen. A few letters in search. Play.

Bang Bang into the room—

Intro exploded out of the monitors. Kick hard, rhythm dense, synth hook earworming from second three. Production solid, low end full, the whole forty pyeong vibrating. Si-on nodded twice with the beat on instinct. Catchy, yes. Then the lyric. Jessie J’s line out, recognition high, every bite harsh. His English was enough to follow the gist live. First lines still normal. Then one. Gist: she can only hold your hand at school. I can take you all the way to base. Si-on’s head stopped. No more nodding. Nicki Minaj’s rap in. Speed extreme, words extremely plain, almost every line the same act in metaphor and pun. Same category as “base.” About seventeen ranks higher. Chorus. Bang Bang into the room—. He now fully understood Bang Bang was not a gunshot. Spotify numbers. Global plays: forty-five million. Debut Billboard Hot 100 at 6. Now 3.

Debut 6. Now 3. Global plays: forty-five million.

Jae-joon watched Si-on’s face slide from stiff into a kind of philosophical confusion, and hit pause. Speakers quiet. The silence rushing into the ears made Si-on freeze a second. Jae-joon turned the chair to him.

“See. Charting singers over there can sing, extremely plainly, how many positions people used in bed, what powder they snorted, that kind of garbage.”

He picked up Si-on’s notebook, shook it in the air.

“You… your friend’s stuff. ‘Become legend.’ ‘A name that lasts a thousand years.’ At most extreme-confidence self-pep. Next to them it’s clean as an elementary summer diary.”

Notebook back on the desk.

“But I still feel…”

“A bit ashamed?”

“Mm.”

“That’s simple.”

Jae-joon pulled a red marker from the cup. After every “I,” a “we.”

“Look. I becomes we. The song’s definition jumps straight into heat and fight in sport.”

Si-on leaned in.

we, not I

We will become legend.

We will write our own history.

Our names will last a thousand years.

Subject singular to plural. Meaning unchanged. The hear of it flipped entirely. “I will become legend” — a narcissist’s midnight mutter. “We will become legend” — a locker room, hands stacked, last roar before a match. Listeners worldwide would take it as a war-song for anyone chasing a dream. Only he knew. First-draft lyric: a twenty-two-year-old man under a first-class reading lamp, a love letter to himself.

“Style. Any suggestion?”

Jae-joon back on the chair, hands stacked behind his head, eyes on the ceiling.

“This kind of lyric should go off in a stadium of fifty thousand. So there’s only one answer for style — rock.”

He did not waste spit on which school of rock, what arrangement logic, which references. He turned to that ring of complicated synths, master on, tracks lighting. Then both hands down on the MIDI. Left laying a synth ground, right in, kick up from the bottom, downbeat extremely steady. Over the kick, hi-hat frequency in, metal shatter in the gaps, the skeleton of the rhythm standing. Jae-joon’s body starting to sway a little with the beat. Left off the pad, another tone. A heavily distorted synth riff splitting out of the speakers. Chorus, eight bars. Pause.

“How is it? This weather right?”

“Kind of a villain-boss-entrance déjà vu.”

“Villain?”

Jae-joon’s brow up. Thought. Back to the desk. A few taps on the drum machine, a new loop. A standard stadium clap. Clap. Then another track. Stomp. Stomp and clap taking turns. Then the whole arrangement demo, front to back, once. Si-on listened. Looked at the Jaeger-LeCoultre on his wrist. 19:45.

“I’m going.”

“Mm? Already? It was just getting good. Not grind a little more?”

“I have to go home. After landing I donated, then ran here to hear fried-chicken rock. If I don’t go back, eomma will be angry.”

Jae-joon laughed once.

“Fine. Go. I’ll work the demo. Come hear it tomorrow.”

“OK.”
