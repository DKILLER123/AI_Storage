On the way off, the small procession passed the captains of the S1, S2 and S3 champions. Fnatic. TPA. SKT T1. Lee Sang-hyeok at this point in time was not yet the symbol the whole world would one day call the Demon King. He was only a young mid laner who had been ground into the dirt by a Samsung team. Legends have valleys too. Most legends, in fact, grow up out of valleys.

Baek Si-on looked away and walked on toward the backstage.

Because of its hard-edged sound, Legend had been sitting steadily between third and sixth place on domestic charts like Melon and Genie. Then, just now, carried on the live broadcast reaching every corner of the globe, that irresistible live energy punched straight through every listener’s eardrum. The curve shot vertically upward — and shoved down the song that had been riding high, the collaboration between IU and Seo Taiji, “Sogok-dong.”

✿ ✿ ✿

October 19 3:00 p.m. Sangam World Cup Stadium · the finals A massacre, a half-time, and four girls who danced into a gap that was never going to be warm.

The match began for real. As the predictions had said, it was almost a one-sided slaughter. Samsung White played with a suffocating, terrifying dominance, taking two games cleanly and pushing Royal Club into a 2–0 corner. On the stream, the live audience that had swelled past twelve million began to drain away in plain sight. Ten million. Nine million. It settled around eight-and-a-half. The barrage changed from “Go Royal!” to “can’t beat them,” “it’s over,” “going to sleep,” “try again next year.”

And it was into that slightly awkward half-time that Red Velvet walked, to perform “Be Natural.”

★ Red Velvet · “Be Natural” · half-time set · 3:07

The stage drops to smoke and a single cool key of light. Four figures in velvet-dark, moving as if the song were being poured rather than played.

R&B. Chair work. Lines. Sultry. Everything the rehearsal room had drilled since debut week — delivered with no freeze, no scatter, no formation broken.

“Be natural —”

On a music-show stage, with the right camera and the right light, a room would sit and savour this slowly. This is a Worlds half-time, thirty minutes after a man set the whole building on fire with drums.

They danced with everything they had. They did not freeze, not once. But “Be Natural” was not the kind of song that could reignite a room full of esports fans at first contact. R&B. Chair work. Lines. Sultry. On a music-show stage, with the right camerawork and the right light, an audience would sit and savour it slowly. This, however, was the League of Legends World Championship. Just beforehand, Baek Si-on had detonated the entire Sangam World Cup Stadium with Korean drums, rock and a Bangtan backing. Just afterwards, the games themselves had become a Samsung White masterclass. The esports crowd was getting restless. They were either cursing Royal Club for losing, waiting for game three, or spamming “when does Bones come on.”

Under those conditions, Red Velvet’s set could only land as middling. There was applause. There were cheers. But it was not hot. It was not the image SM had imagined of “the world gasping over a rookie girl group.” Coming off stage, the four members’ first instinct was to look back at the main stage, as if unsure whether the three minutes had actually ended.

Their manager was there first.

“You did well.”

The four girls turned to look at him at the same time.

“Really well. You’ve been debuted for less than three months. To stand on a stage like this, on a broadcast like this, not rattled, not scattered, no formation breaking, no one dropping a step — that alone is already hard.”

He looked at Bae Joo-hyun.

“Especially you, Joo-hyun. Expression management was excellent.”

Bae Joo-hyun gave a small bow.

“Thank you.”

Kang Seul-gi lowered her head and said in a small voice:

“But the crowd out there was kind of…”

“Not warm.”

The manager finished the sentence for her and shook his head.

“That’s not your fault. The pace of the matches, the mood of the crowd, the kind of stage — none of that is something you control.”

He clapped his hands.

“Don’t overthink it. Go tidy up, then take your leave of the director and the sunbaes.”

“Yes.”

The four answered together.

October 19 3:30 p.m. Red Velvet’s holding room · then a corridor, a door Three centimetres of stubbornness was not enough. Seven would do.

In the holding room, the stylist unnie was packing up the garment bags. The four members of Red Velvet touched up their makeup and changed out of the shoes they had worn on stage — heels never meant for walking long corridors backstage. Bae Joo-hyun sat in her chair, looking down at the flats she had just put on. Then she lifted her head.

“Unnie.”

The stylist turned.

“Mm?”

“Do you have any more heels?”

“Heels?”

“Mm. These rub my feet a little.”

The stylist did not think much of it, and fished a pair of black stilettos out of the shoe case she carried.

“These are seven centimetres. Will they do?”

Bae Joo-hyun looked at them. Seven centimetres. Good. Three centimetres of stubbornness had not been enough — so seven would take the revenge.

“They’ll do.”

She put them on and stood, taking a couple of steps. They were tiring to walk in. But her presence, and her height, were immediately and physically raised.

Park Soo-young’s gaze drifted naturally down to her feet. Paused for a second. Then came back up to her face. Bae Joo-hyun did not even blink.

“Let’s go.”

Park Soo-young: “…”.

Son Seung-wan had seen it too. She said nothing. Only Kang Seul-gi asked, with perfect innocence:

“Unnie, did your foot really get rubbed?”

Bae Joo-hyun looked at her.

“Mm.”

Kang Seul-gi nodded.

“Then we’ll put a band-aid on when we get back to the van.”

Bae Joo-hyun: “…”.

Sometimes that gentle bear’s sincerity was a danger in itself.

✿ ✿ ✿

Baek Si-on’s holding room was at the end of the corridor. Bae Joo-hyun walked in front. The seven-centimetre heels clicked crisply against the floor. The other three followed.

They reached the door and stopped. Knocked.

The door opened from inside. Baek Eun-ah’s face appeared in the gap, and the moment she saw who it was, her eyes lit up.

“Eonnies!”

She pulled the door wide and hugged Bae Joo-hyun first. Then Kang Seul-gi. Then Son Seung-wan. Then Park Soo-young. Each and every one of them.

“You were amazing up there!”

Baek Eun-ah understood exactly how they were feeling, so after the hugs she added one more thing:

“My cousin watched your whole set.”

All four gazes landed, quite naturally, on Baek Si-on — sitting in the styling chair, scrolling through the internet’s praise on his phone. He heard the commotion and turned. The second look was already finished.

If the Legend opening look had been the rock hero — front-facing, hot-blooded, carrying the battlefield — then the Baek Si-on in front of them now was a villain who had just stepped off the set of some crime film. The sort who is the most handsome person in the whole movie, the one the audience knows stands on the wrong side of justice and still cannot help hoping he wins. Dangerous. Mesmerising. And frankly unreasonable.

Park Soo-young swallowed. Son Seung-wan’s fingers curled into the sleeve of Kang Seul-gi beside her. Kang Seul-gi did not shake her off, because she needed an anchor too. And inside the seven-centimetre heels, Bae Joo-hyun’s toes curled very slightly.

Baek Si-on set down his phone and stood up from the chair. That broke the spell. All four came to their senses at once and bowed in perfect, uniform ninety degrees.

“Sunbae-nim!”

He had not intended to say anything. But Baek Eun-ah was signalling him furiously from the side, her eyes extremely clear: praise them. Praise them now. If you don’t, they’ll be hurt. He looked at her. Then he looked at the four girls.

“Not every stage can be judged by how the crowd reacts. From where I was standing, your set was good.”

“Thank you, sunbae-nim!”

The four bowed again, in unison. Baek Eun-ah watched from the side, deeply satisfied. Oppa’s emotional intelligence was online today. Excellent.

The next second, her gaze swept downward and stopped on Bae Joo-hyun’s feet. Seven centimetres. Thin heels. Black. Eun-ah’s eyes paused, then lifted — and met Bae Joo-hyun’s unruffled gaze head-on. The two of them held it for one second.

A light went on inside Baek Eun-ah’s head. She understood in that instant the true value of those seven centimetres, and the unyielding spirit standing in those shoes.

She spun to Baek Si-on, her voice bright.

“Oppa, everyone worked so hard today. Let’s take a photo to remember it?”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on did not refuse. This time no direction from Eun-ah was needed. Bae Joo-hyun stepped to Baek Si-on’s side with total naturalness. Smooth. Serene. As if that spot had been assigned to her by fate since the moment of the Big Bang. Eun-ah raised her phone; the picture was auto-focusing. Bae Joo-hyun stood beside Baek Si-on, her head inclined a fraction toward him, her right hand raising a small peace sign by her cheek. Her mouth was curved. Her eyes were curved. She was glowing inside the frame.

Watching that pose, Eun-ah felt a little light-headed. She remembered, very clearly, that this beautiful unnie had been famous during her SM trainee days as an iron wall — a girl who kept everyone at a perfect arm’s length. And yet now — Bae Joo-hyun was behaving as though the very concept of “personal space” had never existed. Surely she didn’t have feelings for Oppa? Then, on second thought… no. Surely not. Oppa and she had met, all told, only a handful of times. Love at first sight already? At that rate, was Oppa’s rate of radiating charm simply illegal?

“Done yet?”

Baek Si-on cut ruthlessly across the eighty-episode chaebol makjang drama that was already assembling itself inside Eun-ah’s head.

“Ah. Almost.”

Eun-ah reeled her imagination back in and looked at her cousin’s utterly expressionless face on the screen. She could not help herself.

“Oppa, smile!”

Baek Si-on lifted one corner of his mouth, perfunctorily. Click. The photo froze. In the middle stood Baek Si-on, long black coat holding his silhouette in line, wearing the most cursory excuse for a smile — which, on this look, read not as disinterest but as the villain’s brand of “I refuse to perform.” On either side of him, four girls, each beautiful in a different direction. Eun-ah checked the composition, confirmed it, and forwarded it to Red Velvet’s manager at once.

“Sent.”

The manager took the phone and looked at it. This kind of group photo could not be posted — it could only be a private keepsake for now. Baek Si-on’s name was far too hot, and Red Velvet was still SM’s brand-new girl group. Posted, it would only be read as riding his coattails. The manager pocketed his phone and led the four in another bow.

“Baek sunbae-nim. Sorry to intrude today. Good luck with your stage later.”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Take care.”

The four girls answered together:

“Sunbae-nim, fighting!”

They turned and left. The door closed. Baek Eun-ah stood where she was and stared at the closed door for two seconds. Park Ji-hun followed her gaze.

“What is it?”

“Nothing.”

Eun-ah looked away.

“Just thinking my cousin’s been a little dangerous lately.”

Park Ji-hun glanced at Baek Si-on, who had sat back down in the styling chair and was listening to music through earphones.

“Not lately.”

Eun-ah turned her head.

“Hm?”

“He’s always been.”

Eun-ah: “…”.

She could not argue with that.

In the backstage corridor, the Samsung White champions were lined up waiting for the final round of press photos. Baek Si-on walked past. A member of the organising staff hurried up.
