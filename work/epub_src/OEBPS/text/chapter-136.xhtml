Chapter One Hundred Thirty Six

Choi Jin-ri’s Retaliatory Wish

November 2 Just past noon · lunch break Jeonju studio lot · the middle of the set A cup of coffee, a rubber band, one very loud stomach, and a wish that is only a little bit evil.

At the centre of the set, Baek Si-on watched the two directors walk off. Then he turned, and took the coffee from Choi Jin-ri and the ticket stubs from Lee Ji-eun — one in each hand, at the same time, without a single beat of hesitation.

Then, drinking the coffee, he ran his thumb along the edge of the stub stack.

“You really booked out a hall?”

“Mm.”

Lee Ji-eun’s chin lifted a fraction.

Candid. And a little proud.

“Thank you for supporting Korean independent cinema.”

“……”

Lee Ji-eun’s chin came slowly back down.

That sentence was somehow just as infuriating the second time she heard it.

She had come to visit the set. She had bought the tickets. She had even brought the stubs. And with a single “thank you for supporting Korean independent cinema,” she had been reclassified by Baek Si-on from “a woman with a heart” into “a member of the public doing her civic duty at the cinema.”

Beside her, Choi Jin-ri raised a hand to cover her mouth and let out a very small laugh.

Lee Ji-eun turned to look at her.

Choi Jin-ri’s eyes immediately drifted elsewhere.

She was pretending, with great concentration, to be a completely uninvolved Choi Jin-ri who had merely happened to pass by and had heard nothing at all.

Baek Si-on looked out at the two support trucks parked side by side in the distance.

One announced, on its hanging banner, that the crew should please take good care of its Crown Prince, Actor Baek Si-on.

The other congratulated Actor Baek Si-on on the Billboard Hot 100 number one.

Festive. And extremely visible.

“You two both sent support trucks?”

Choi Jin-ri nodded.

“Mm.”

Lee Ji-eun nodded as well.

“Mm.”

“Then I’ll go eat first.”

He had taken two steps, holding up the hem of his gonryongpo, when Baek Eun-ah’s voice came floating in from outside.

“Oppa — I brought you food!”

Baek Si-on stopped mid-step.

A few seconds later, Baek Eun-ah came trotting in, carrying a large tray.

The tray was richly loaded. Sandwiches. Kimbap. Rolled omelette. A platter of fruit. And, tucked at the side, a small box of nuts.

She set the whole thing down in front of Baek Si-on.

“Don’t go queue. If you walk over there now, the staff will all try to let you go first and nobody will be comfortable.”

“……Fine.”

“Mm-mm.”

Baek Eun-ah had answered, but her eyes were not behaving at all.

They rolled first to Choi Jin-ri. Then to Lee Ji-eun. Then back to Baek Si-on — who was already eating.

Dressed in a crown prince’s court robes. Holding a sandwich.

The picture did not quite cohere.

It looked like Crown Prince Sado, having failed at presiding over the court, had decided to suppress his cortisol with carbohydrates.

An odd quiet settled over the scene.

Baek Eun-ah, standing in the middle, could feel a pressure that did not belong to her age bracket pressing down on both shoulders.

With Choi Jin-ri she had long been comfortable. And Lee Ji-eun was not a stranger either — not when she had spent so many late phone calls with Ji-eun’s manager, Jeong Han-teuk, synchronising their artists’ diaries.

She cast about, and finally found a safe topic.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“KB Bank sent an email this morning.”

“And?”

“They say the person who sent you that risk-assessment email was an intern.”

Baek Si-on looked up.

“An intern?”

“Mm.”

Baek Eun-ah’s expression was very serious.

“And he’s been fired now.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

The explanation sounded very KB.

And very large-Korean-corporation.

Something goes wrong — find an intern first.

Whether it was really that intern’s doing or not, an intern stays forever young, forever useful, forever ready to absorb, on behalf of the organisation, a portion of history that was never his to carry.

“And the contract?”

“Unchanged.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded.

“The cooperation stays as it was, and the review of the next ad materials will go ahead normally. They also said KB has always deeply respected the achievements Actor Baek Si-on has made on the global stage, and that they believe he carries the kind of public trust that fits a financial brand.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

He made no comment on the matter at all.

He went on eating his sandwich.

Choi Jin-ri, on the other hand, was listening with growing confusion.

“What intern?”

Baek Eun-ah looked at her.

“After the Lakers season opener the other day — you remember, Oppa nearly charged onto the court over the thing between Kobe and Howard.”

Choi Jin-ri nodded.

She knew that incident, of course.

She had even, at the Green Bottle Fly media screening, been uncharacteristically fierce on Baek Si-on’s behalf.

Baek Eun-ah went on:

“KB being a financial brand, they care a lot about a sense of security and trust. So someone inside wrote an email proposing a re-evaluation of the endorsement. But now they’ve changed their story.”

Lee Ji-eun said, in a light tone:

“Because of the Billboard number one?”

“Right.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded.

“Because of the Billboard number one. And because public opinion on the American side has flipped completely. The North American sports and hip-hop world is all praising Oppa for being loyal to his people, and the phrase Tom Ford Gangster even went global.”

Choi Jin-ri finished listening, and her brow drew down a little.

“So an intern takes the fall?”

“More or less.”

Baek Eun-ah shrugged.

“Big companies are all like this. When something goes wrong, an intern is more reliable than a fire hydrant.”

Lee Ji-eun gave a small laugh at that.

Choi Jin-ri, though, looked at Baek Si-on.

“So you’re all right?”

Baek Si-on had not even opened his mouth before Baek Eun-ah answered for him.

“Of course he’s fine. The night it happened, he asked me, ‘Is Korea scolding me or praising me?’ I said scolding. He said, then he won’t look.”

“……”

Choi Jin-ri could not help laughing.

This man really was—

Seeing Choi Jin-ri laugh, Baek Eun-ah seized the moment to add a compliment:

“But Jin-ri eonni was really cool at the Green Bottle Fly media screening. The reporter asked so sharply and eonni spoke up for Oppa on the spot. When I saw the clip online I nearly cried.”

Choi Jin-ri was a little embarrassed by the praise.

“It wasn’t that much…”

No sooner had the words left her mouth than she felt a quiet gaze settle on her from the side.

Wrong.

Very wrong.

Baek Eun-ah switched on her repair protocol on the spot.

“……Ah, right! Ji-eun eonni!”

Lee Ji-eun looked at her.

“Your support-truck setup today is too thoughtful — hand warmers! I saw them while I was handing out food! It gets cold at the set at dawn and at night, and the staff really need those!”

Lee Ji-eun’s eyes curved a little.

“Eun-ah-ya.”

“Mm?”

“Are you balancing your remarks right now?”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

Choi Jin-ri also raised her eyes to look at her.

All of a sudden, Baek Eun-ah felt that life was too hard.

She was eighteen.

An eighteen-year-old’s rightful share of drama was a cover video that would not come together before the upload deadline — not the delicate diplomatic crisis of two support trucks unfolding on the Sado set.

She gave a dry laugh.

“No, no — I genuinely think both eonnis are wonderful.”

Lee Ji-eun nodded, smiling.

“Mm. I believe you.”

Choi Jin-ri smiled too.

“I believe you as well.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

That “I believe you” of theirs did not sound even slightly like belief.

Baek Si-on finally finished the sandwich. He looked at Baek Eun-ah.

“Go eat.”

Baek Eun-ah felt like a prisoner granted amnesty.

“Okay!”

She spun around and ran, her whole back radiating the relief of a person who had just survived.

✿ ✿ ✿

After Baek Eun-ah left, the quiet settled back in.

Both women plainly had things they wanted to say to Baek Si-on.

And yet, with the other one present, none of those questions were easy to raise.

Baek Si-on, for his part, suffered from no such difficulty at all.

He sat by the monitor and, head down, ate with the momentum of a storm.

Sandwich.

Kimbap.

Rolled omelette.

Fruit.

Nuts.

Bite after bite. He ate with total seriousness.

So serious that Choi Jin-ri and Lee Ji-eun, standing nearby with their own separate thoughts, watching him, both arrived, by some eerie convergence, at the very same question.

Were these ordinary foods really that good?

They were the most standard support-truck fare in the world.

And yet the picture of Baek Si-on, in a crown prince’s robes, seated in a period film set, head bent over a sandwich, carried a strange persuasion that made a person want to have a bite too.

That, presumably, was the infectiousness of a top actor.

Even an eating broadcast came with acting.

Baek Si-on finished the sandwich at last, picked up the coffee, drank, and raised his head to meet the gazes of the two women.

“Are you two free this afternoon?”

Choi Jin-ri was the first to react.

“Ah… I have things.”

She looked down at the time on her phone.

“I still have a roadshow stop, and the Fashion King press event.”

Fashion King was the film she had signed before The Green Bottle Fly — the film meant to be her first real headlining role on the big screen.

If The Green Bottle Fly was the film that let audiences see her ache, see her break, see her step out of the shell of “the peach of the human world,” then Fashion King was the other lane entirely.

Youth. A comic-book energy. Fashion as spectacle. And a more traditional idol-actor promotion cycle.

Releasing at exactly this moment, it was plainly meant to drink up every drop of attention she had accumulated through The Green Bottle Fly and the Venice red carpet.

Lee Ji-eun listened, then asked, with perfect timing:

“Which of your two films does Peach hope does better at the box office?”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

That question was mean.

She thought for a moment.

“The Green Bottle Fly, I suppose.”

“Oh? Why?”

Choi Jin-ri curved her eyes and added:

“After all, we should support the growth of Korean independent cinema, shouldn’t we?”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

Brat of a Peach.

From Baek Si-on’s mouth that sentence was infuriating.

Learned and repeated by Choi Jin-ri, it was twice as infuriating.

Lee Ji-eun decided not to keep sparring with her on that front.

She shifted the line of attack.

“Your afternoon is that packed, so when are you leaving?”

Choi Jin-ri looked down at her phone.

On the screen, a message from her manager had just arrived.

“I’m almost at the set entrance.”

She looked up.

“Manager oppa is nearly here.”

Then, as if the thought had just occurred to her, she added in a very natural tone:

“Eonni’s heading back to Seoul too, right? Want me to drop you?”

The offer sounded perfectly considerate.

Lee Ji-eun looked at her, eyes curved.

“I’m not going back to Seoul.”

“Eh?”

“I happen to have a schedule here in Jeonju.”

Choi Jin-ri’s brows very nearly got away from her.

Jeonju?

What schedule could Lee Ji-eun have in Jeonju?

LOEN’s activities were always clustered around Seoul and Busan, with Jeju as the furthest outpost. Jeonju, apart from bibimbap and the hanok village, was practically insulated from the entertainment world.

Not one hundred percent believable.

But she could hardly say so out loud. She smiled instead.

“Is that so.”

Lee Ji-eun smiled as well.

“Mm.”

The two women met each other’s eyes.

The air was extremely harmonious.

So harmonious that Baek Si-on lowered his head and quietly ate another piece of fruit.

✿ ✿ ✿

Outside the set, a car door closed.

Choi Jin-ri’s manager had arrived.

She glanced at her phone, then at Baek Si-on.

“Then I really should go.”

Baek Si-on set down the fruit plate and stood up.

“I’ll walk you out.”

The light in Choi Jin-ri’s eyes — the light that had sunk a little at “I’m not going back to Seoul” — floated gently back to the surface.

“I’ll walk Peach out too.”

“……”

There it was.

The familiar feeling.

Choi Jin-ri suddenly remembered that last time, when she had visited the Love Yourself music-video shoot, it had been exactly like this.

Even that mood of hers — wanting so badly to stay, yet having to be the one to leave — felt as though it had been cut from an old reel and pasted, unaltered, on top of today.

She felt that fate could be very unreasonable.

Why did it always arrange for her to be the one who went first?

At the entrance to the film base, the van was already waiting by the roadside.

Her manager opened the door for her.

Before she got in, Choi Jin-ri could not help leaning half out and asking one more time:

“Eonni, you’re really not going back?”

Lee Ji-eun, standing beside Baek Si-on, waved at her with a bright smile.

“Mm. Not going back.”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

Bad eonni.

She looked at Baek Si-on, working hard to keep her voice as light as before.

“I’m off, then.”

“Take care.”

“……See you tomorrow?”

“Mm?”

“I mean — tomorrow I’ll be in Korea and you’ll be in Korea too, so from the earth-is-this-small angle—”

Even as she said it, Choi Jin-ri realised how hopeless the sentence was becoming.

She gave up on it.

“……Anyway. Goodbye.”

“Goodbye.”

Choi Jin-ri got into the van.

The door shut.

The engine started.

The van eased out of the film base, slowly.

In the back seat, Choi Jin-ri turned her head and looked back through the window.

Baek Si-on and Lee Ji-eun stood side by side at the entrance to the film base.

Sigh.

She turned her head back to the road ahead.

Her manager glanced at her in the rear-view mirror.

“Sulli-ah.”

“Mm?”

“Why is your face so sour?”

“……”

She rested her head against the window and made herself a small wish. A retaliatory wish. A wish that was not quite evil.

She hoped that the schedule Lee Ji-eun had invented for her afternoon was an endorsement shoot for a kimchi brand.

✿ ✿ ✿

At the entrance to the film base, the van’s tail lights vanished around the corner.

Only then did Lee Ji-eun turn to look at Baek Si-on.

“Baek Si-on-ssi.”

“Mm.”

“Are you pursuing Peach?”

“What makes you say that?”

“She said you gave her flowers.”

“What flowers?”

“Saffron.”

“……”

Baek Si-on was silent for two seconds.

Then he turned and walked toward his own van.

“What is it?”

Lee Ji-eun followed, watching the man in Joseon court robes stride at a brisk clip across the parking lot of a twenty-first-century film base. The absurdity of his silhouette was difficult to put into words.

Baek Si-on did not answer.

He climbed into his van, rummaged in the paper bag of odds and ends in the back seat, and came out with a small glass jar.

He got out and handed the jar to Lee Ji-eun.

Lee Ji-eun took it with both hands and looked at it curiously.

“What is this?”

“Saffron.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

She looked down at the jar.

Then up at Baek Si-on.

“Are you sure?”

“Dubai probably doesn’t sell dyed radish shavings.”

“……”

Lee Ji-eun sighed.

So “gave her flowers” really was giving her something with the character for flower in its name.

Brat of a Peach.

Not even slightly cute anymore.

She had made it sound as though Baek Si-on had gone down on one knee with ninety-nine roses.

“Here — give it back.”

Baek Si-on did not take it.

“It’s yours.”

Lee Ji-eun paused.

“Mine?”

“Mm.”

“Why?”

Baek Si-on said it quite naturally.

“The last time I was at the radio station, senior Yoo In-na happened to mention that you don’t sleep well at night, that your head runs. Steep this in water and drink it. It settles the mind.”

Lee Ji-eun went very quiet.

Yoo In-na had said she doesn’t sleep well at night.

Her head runs?

Runs about what?

In that instant, a stream of images extremely unsuited to public broadcast flashed through her mind.

It was over.

Completely over.

Her little reservoir of maiden feelings — had her best friend leaked it like a sieve, and had the leak run precisely to the ears of the man himself?

“In-na eonni was talking about… work stress.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Mm.”

Lee Ji-eun stared at him.

“What does that ‘mm’ mean?”

“Understood.”

“You had better have truly understood.”

“Mm.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

She felt she could not stay a moment longer.

Another moment and she might begin explaining that she had not, in fact, been letting her head run about any particular someone.

She tucked the little glass jar into her canvas tote, and cleared her throat.

“Then I’ll take it.”

“Mm.”

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome.”

“I’ll go first, then.”

“Take care.”

“……Mm.”

Lee Ji-eun turned and walked away, growing smaller and smaller under Baek Si-on’s gaze.

Saffron.

Steeped in water.

Settles the mind.

She closed her eyes.

Yoo In-na.

You had better have only mentioned work stress in passing.

If you said anything else — then when you come back, you will not get a single piece of the chocolate I keep for you.

Not one.

And I won’t even share the saffron water.
