Chapter Seventy-Seven

How to Elegantly Hint That an Opponent's Body Odour Is Over the Limit

September 2 Late evening Excelsior Hotel · private beach, the corner table A chair scraped onto the boardwalk, and the origin of perfume

Baek Si-on was leaning back in his beach chair, chatting with Daddario about the film she'd brought to Venice.

"Dead Before My Eyes? No — The Buried Ex. Horror-comedy. Sounds silly, but the script's actually quite good. A man finds out his ex-girlfriend has turned into a zombie — and she's hotter than she was alive…"

"Sounds like the kind of project a Korean variety show would pitch."

"Really? Then the two of us must have a lot in common."

Daddario laughed and was about to go on.

Footsteps on the sand.

Then the metallic scratch of a beach chair's legs against the wooden boardwalk.

Scrape.

Baek Si-on and Daddario turned at the same time.

Choi Jin-ri was standing beside the low table.

One hand carrying a white folding chair she'd hauled from somewhere, the other lifting the hem of her gown.

"Hope I'm not interrupting."

The standard Korean-social opening line.

The unspoken subtext of that sentence was "you'd better say no."

Setting the folding chair down, she opened it and placed it beside Baek Si-on's beach chair.

Sat down without waiting to be asked.

Didn't look at Baek Si-on.

Looked straight at Daddario.

Daddario tilted her head.

Her blue eyes ran down Choi Jin-ri once from head to toe — the exposed collarbones, the navy satin gown, the crossed ankles, the silver heeled sandals.

And back up.

Stopping at Choi Jin-ri's dark-brown eyes.

Smiled.

"If you've come to chat, you're not interrupting."

She said.

"But if you've come to fight over a man — that's very rude."

Choi Jin-ri froze.

In Korea, even when two women had their eye on the same man, the social sparring between them stayed wrapped in three layers of politeness and seven layers of implication.

Nobody said it out loud.

Three or four replies popped into her head, and every single one was stopped at the gate by her self-censoring mechanism.

Too soft — no good.

Too hard — not appropriate.

Too polite — that meant conceding.

The result was that nothing came out.

"She's the lead actress of my film."

Baek Si-on's voice cut in from the side.

Daddario's gaze moved from Choi Jin-ri to Baek Si-on's face, then back to Choi Jin-ri.

"The lead actress?"

She raised an eyebrow.

"Nice."

Daddario sat up straight in her beach chair and extended her right hand.

"Hello. Daddario."

As she extended her hand, her upper body deliberately leaned forward a few degrees.

The neckline of the black slip dress sagged a little at that angle.

Everything below the collarbone was laid out in full plain view, directly in front of Choi Jin-ri's line of sight.

Not accidental.

Choi Jin-ri's gaze collided with it involuntarily, then snapped away again in a hurry.

"…Choi Jin-ri."

She held out her hand and shook.

Quickly.

So quickly that her fingers touched Daddario's palm and withdrew in under a second.

Expression management failed completely.

Daddario withdrew her hand as if nothing had happened, turned back, and went on talking to Baek Si-on.

"So you were saying, the story of The Green Fly is set in Seoul? I've always wanted to visit Seoul. I hear the barbecue there is delicious…"

Daddario gave her full attention back to Baek Si-on.

Choi Jin-ri had been written into the category "present but not participating."

Baek Si-on was answering Daddario's question about Korean barbecue.

He said there was a whole street of barbecue places in Hongdae, but the best one was in the alley under his own building.

Daddario laughed and said she absolutely had to try it.

The two of them talked for a while.

Choi Jin-ri's eyes went left to right, then right to left, like she was watching a tennis match in which she held no racket.

A voice in her head was yelling.

Say something.

Say something, quick.

Take the conversation. You speak English — enough for daily conversation, at least. Open your mouth!

But every time she found a moment to jump in, Daddario had already pulled the conversation away with the next question.

Every time she'd assembled an English sentence, ready to speak, Baek Si-on had already answered, and the topic turned another page.

Choi Jin-ri lowered her head.

She was furious at herself, at her own mouth, at the fact that she'd walked over here and couldn't say a single thing.

Furious at that woman opposite, at her figure, at the way she talked.

And furious at Baek Si-on.

He was sitting right beside her.

One step away.

But from the moment she sat down until now, he hadn't said a single word to her.

As if she'd carried a chair all this way just to change seats and listen to other people talk.

✿ ✿ ✿

Daddario's topic drifted from Korean barbecue to American BBQ, and from American BBQ to Texas smoked brisket.

Then she stopped.

Leaned back into the beach chair, and switched which leg was crossed.

"Speaking of which, Bai."

She looked at Baek Si-on, a smile with an unreadable angle at the corner of her mouth.

"I'm staying at the Excelsior. Room 312."

"There's a bottle of bourbon in my room that I brought all the way from Los Angeles. If you're interested, you're welcome to come by tonight. We can keep exploring the difference between American barbecue and Korean barbecue."

When she said this, her gaze was direct.

Direct enough that there was no room left for it to be misread as "mere politeness."

Then she turned and looked at Choi Jin-ri.

Winked at her.

"Goodbye, Miss Choi."

Choi Jin-ri's mouth moved faster than her brain.

"Goodbye."

She regretted it the moment she said it.

What was there to look forward to with her?

Daddario stood up from her beach chair and winked at Baek Si-on once more:

"I do hope I'll see you tonight, Mr. Bai."

Then she turned and walked on the sand toward the bright banquet area.

The silhouette of the black slip dress swayed twice in the torchlight, and was swallowed by the crowd.

The beach corner fell quiet again.

Only the sound of waves on the shore.

One.

Then another.

Choi Jin-ri stared at the direction where Daddario had disappeared for two seconds.

Then turned her head.

"You… going to go?"

Baek Si-on turned to look at her.

"The question you're asking — is it 'I want you to go' or 'I don't want you to go'?"

Choi Jin-ri's hands rested on her knees, the navy skirt lifting slightly in the sea breeze, then settling back.

"If I say I don't want you to — would you not go?"

"Maybe."

Choi Jin-ri looked at his face for two seconds.

Moved her gaze away, out to the Adriatic Sea.

"Do you know how perfume was invented?"

"What?"

"The origin of perfume."

Baek Si-on clearly hadn't expected the topic to jump from "will you go to room 312" to "the origin of perfume."

The leap was a little wide.

Choi Jin-ri went on, unprompted.

A Detour Through Medieval Europe · as delivered at the beach

In the Middle Ages, European aristocrats didn't bathe. They believed water spread plague; going months without touching water was the norm, and some nobles bathed only twice a year.

But body odour still had to be covered. So they invented perfume — flowers, plants, resin, mixed and put on the body to cover the smell. Later it got finer and finer, but the essence never changed.

It's all for covering.

Baek Si-on gave up trying to understand the logical link between this and the current situation, and decided to listen to the end first.

Choi Jin-ri tilted her head.

The fringe of her hair blew across her eye in the sea wind, and she didn't brush it away.

A wave broke on the shore.

Baek Si-on sorted the sentence through his head.

Middle Ages.

No bathing.

Body odour.

Perfume.

Covering.

He caught on.

Choi Jin-ri had gone around an eight-hundred-year piece of historical trivia.

At its core was one sentence.

Daddario smells.

Baek Si-on looked at the woman who was solemnly giving him a lesson in medieval European hygiene.

"So — those old European men left you feeling quite gassed?"

Choi Jin-ri blinked twice.

He'd read her little scheme as "discomfort with the social environment of the banquet."

Wrong.

She wasn't talking about those old men.

She'd been talking about…

Well.

Never mind.

The steps were all laid out now, so no point not taking them.

Choi Jin-ri scrunched up her face.

"That Italian critic's perfume is still stuck in my nasal passages."

She fanned her hand in front of her nose twice:

"I don't know how a single person can give off sandalwood, musk and garlic all at once."

"If you don't like it, don't go."

Baek Si-on leaned back into his beach chair.

Choi Jin-ri brought her hand down from in front of her nose.

"But the company arranged this… and Mr. Lee said, in a social situation, whoever you can meet, meet…"

"Choi Jin-ri."

Baek Si-on said her full name.

"You're here. So the world's here. If you leave, the world leaves with you."

A wave broke.

"So do whatever you want to do."

Choi Jin-ri looked into his eyes.

The torchlight danced in his pupils, bright one moment, dim the next.

That sentence was too big.

From her debut at SM until now, everyone around her had told her only "what the company needs you to do," "what the fans want you to be," "how you should look."

The world existed before her.

Her job was to adapt to it, go along with it, and fit herself into that mould.

Nobody had ever told her the world existed because of her.

Nobody.

But right now, all of it felt very far away.

What was near was only the sentence he'd just said, and the way he was looking at her now.

If she could really do whatever she wanted to do.

Then what she wanted to say was:

I don't want you to go.

She opened her mouth.

"I don't—"

"I ate too much."

Baek Si-on's voice stepped in first.

The second half of Choi Jin-ri's sentence got caught in her throat.

Baek Si-on patted his own stomach.

"A little sleepy."

He turned his head toward her.

"Do you want to head back?"

Choi Jin-ri's "I don't want you to go" rested on the tip of her tongue, pushed back by his "won't you go back."

Baek Si-on's eyes were calm.

Calm enough that she suddenly understood one thing.

He didn't need her to say it.

He already knew.

"Ate too much" was the answer he gave.

"A little sleepy" was the reason he gave.

He'd answered that question for her.

Choi Jin-ri slowly closed the mouth she'd opened.

Nodded once.

"Okay."

The two of them walked along the edge of the beach toward the pier.

The waves lapped on the left; the lights and voices of the banquet faded into the distance on the right.

After about twenty steps.

"Eun-ah wants to go to the beach tomorrow. Are you coming?"

Choi Jin-ri didn't hesitate.

"Yes."

She answered fast — so fast the tail of Baek Si-on's sentence had barely finished before she'd replied.

Then, realising she'd answered too fast, she lowered her head and pretended to tidy her skirt.
