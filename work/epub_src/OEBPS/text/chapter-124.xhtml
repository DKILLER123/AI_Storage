Chapter One Hundred Twenty Four

Only Taylor Swift Stands in the Way

October 26 Morning Korea · the business pages and the trading floor The economy section catches fire before the entertainment section does — and the man at the middle of it is asleep in a van.

October 26, morning. The first Korean news to detonate was not on the entertainment pages.

It was on the economy pages —

The morning’s business section · headline wall

Hyundai Aslan’s first-day orders top 4,000 units — three times internal expectations

The morning’s business section · headline wall

Analysts: the Aslan’s day-one figures could rewrite Hyundai’s entire mid-to-high-end strategy

The morning’s business section · headline wall

Hyundai Motor Group shares open strong, climbing as much as 3.6% intraday

Three point six percentage points. Against the Korean market as a whole, that was hardly a number to shake the heavens.

But set against a single conglomerate with a total market capitalisation of ₩36 trillion, 3.6% meant that, in one trading day, the company had added more than a trillion won in value.

And what had driven that number was not a breakthrough in engine technology.

Not a new-energy strategy.

Not an overseas expansion.

A single person.

A man in a dark suit, walking through morning mist in a television commercial.

For the first time, financial analysts were obliged to discuss, inside their own research notes, the direct influence of an entertainer on a listed company’s share price.

Someone coined a term for it.

The “Baek Si-on effect.”

A brokerage’s morning meeting minutes · internal circulation → leaked screenshot

The Aslan’s first-day order anomaly

The term first appears in the minutes of a certain securities firm’s morning meeting. The relevant line, verbatim:

The note

The abnormal deviation in the Aslan’s first-day order data cannot be explained by conventional product analysis, pricing strategy, or marketing-spend efficiency. The driving factor comes chiefly from the personal-IP premium of the endorser, Baek Si-on. Recommendation: monitor the potential pull of the “Baek Si-on effect” on Hyundai Motor’s subsequent product lines.

The minutes had been meant to circulate only within the institution. Someone screenshotted them and put them online.

Very quickly, the term “Baek Si-on effect” began crawling from the finance pages up to the entertainment pages.

From the entertainment pages onto the forums.

And from the forums onto Naver’s real-time search.

At first, netizens were just playing with it.

The forums that morning · first reactions

Baek Si-on effect — what the hell is that? An actor can move a share price?

Two more endorsement deals and he’ll take the entire KOSPI to a new record high.

……

But nobody inside the industry was laughing. At least nobody on the brand side was.

They had all, at the same instant, registered a very practical fact — Baek Si-on was no longer a “celebrity endorser” in the ordinary sense.

He had begun to carry the ability to re-price a product.

October 26 Morning On the road · a second-hand Kia Carnival nanny van, heading for Gimpo The man the finance channels, the hot searches and tens of thousands of comments are all discussing sits in the back, eyes closed.

And at that very moment, the man being discussed at once by the finance channels, the real-time search boards and tens of thousands of comments was sitting in the back seat of a second-hand Kia Carnival nanny van, eyes closed, resting.

Baek Eun-ah sat up front, tablet in hand, scrolling Naver’s real-time search.

She read for a while. Then looked back over her shoulder into the rear seat.

Whether he was asleep, or simply choosing not to talk, she could not tell.

Baek Eun-ah held out for two seconds.

She could not hold out any longer.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

So he was not asleep after all.

“Do you know you’ve taken Hyundai Motor’s share price up 3.6 points?”

“Just learned that.”

“Do you know what they’re calling it now?”

“What?”

“The Baek Si-on effect.”

Baek Si-on fished his phone out of his pocket, intending to look up whether this so-called “Baek Si-on effect” was meant as praise or as ridicule.

The moment he unlocked it, a KakaoTalk message popped up.

Choi Jin-ri.

KakaoTalk · Choi Jin-ri

Choi Jin-ri

Heading to Dubai today? Safe travels~

Behind it came a sticker of a little person waving. Round face, big eyes — drawn with a cuteness that carried an indescribable resemblance to the sender herself.

Baek Si-on looked at the sticker.

He typed.

KakaoTalk · Choi Jin-ri

Baek Si-on

Mm.

Baek Si-on

Anything you want as a souvenir?

The reply came back at once — she clearly had her phone in her hand.

KakaoTalk · Choi Jin-ri

Choi Jin-ri

What kind of specialties does Dubai even have?

Baek Si-on

Oil.

Choi Jin-ri

……

Choi Jin-ri

What would I do with oil?

Baek Si-on

You can sell it once the price goes up.

Choi Jin-ri

Then you might as well just bring me back two jars of sand.

Baek Si-on

Okay.

Choi Jin-ri

I was joking! Don’t you dare actually scoop up sand! Just come back safe, that’s enough!

Baek Si-on

Got it.

Choi Jin-ri

Then… safe trip.

October 26 Later that morning Gimpo International · VIP channel → the apron No queues, no security lines, no fans. Just two men in dark suits and a plane with no airline on its tail.

Gimpo International Airport. The VIP channel.

At the VIP entrance, two staff members in dark suits were already waiting.

One of them stepped forward, gave a slight bow, and spoke in English.

“Mr. Baek, welcome. We are the reception staff arranged by His Highness Sheikh Mohammed Al Maktoum’s private events team.”

Baek Si-on shook his hand.

“Thanks for the trouble.”

The other man smiled, impeccably.

“The private jet is ready. We will handle your luggage. Before boarding we will need to do a simple check of passports and the passenger manifest.”

The process moved fast. So fast that even Baek Eun-ah felt a little out of her depth.

No queuing. No ordinary security lanes. No fans pressing in. No long telephoto lenses from the press.

They were taken straight into the VIP lounge, drank half a cup of coffee, signed two confirmation documents, and were then driven by shuttle to the apron.

On the apron, a Gulfstream G650 sat waiting.

Pure white fuselage. No airline logo on the tail — only a single line of small Arabic script and a gold family crest.

Baek Eun-ah paused at the foot of the boarding stairs.

She had flown first class before.

But a private jet was another matter entirely.

However good first class was, you still shared the plane with other people.

This one —

the whole aircraft belonged to one person.

Or rather, one family.

The cabin did not feel like an aircraft at all. It felt like the living room of some Middle Eastern magnate, compressed and tucked into a long cylindrical space.

Beige leather seats. Real wood panelling. Carpet underfoot. A reading light and a charging point beside every seat. And at the rear, a bedroom with an actual door.

Baek Eun-ah pushed the look of a bumpkin gaping at a palace off her face, raised the tablet to hide behind it, and typed a single line into her memo app.

Eun-ah · memo

Goal: within five years, get Oppa one of these too.

She looked at it, thought for a moment, and deleted it.

Too insane.

Buy a house first.

October 26 Seven-plus hours later · afternoon into night Dubai · a private estate A soundcheck shorter than the flight’s taxi, and two songs for fewer than three hundred guests.

Seven and a half hours later — Dubai.

As the plane descended, the window showed a city bleached white by sunlight. Sand tones. Gold tones. The glare of glass curtain walls. In the distance, towers rose out of the desert like metal sculptures pulled straight up from the ground.

Private channel. Fast-track immigration.

A black motorcade was already waiting outside the apron.

The host for this leg had changed to a Middle Eastern man with a short beard, fluent English, and a courtesy so soft it almost sounded gentle.

“Mr. Baek, welcome to Dubai. His Highness is very much looking forward to tonight’s performance.”

“Thank you.”

The motorcade left the airport and drove toward the private estate.

Afternoon. The estate.

The soundcheck was so short that Baek Eun-ah felt the money being earned was almost unreal.

The stage stood in the middle of an open courtyard. Behind it, fountains and lights. The sound system was top of the range. No band was needed — the backing tracks had been delivered in advance.

Baek Si-on stepped up, sang half the chorus of Legend, and ran the hook of Bones once more.

Ay-ay-ay-ay-ay-ay——!

Somewhere at the side of the courtyard, a member of the lighting crew adjusting the rig unconsciously hummed along.

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

Good.

“Ayi-ya-yi-ya” had already reached the Middle East.

The soundcheck ended. The events coordinator came over.

“Excellent. Tonight at nine twenty we open with Legend. Between the songs the host will speak. At nine thirty-five, Bones. After the two pieces, His Highness may wish to meet with you briefly.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Understood.”

“In addition, His Highness would like to know whether you would be comfortable with a private photograph.”

“I don’t mind at all.”

“Thank you.”

Dubai’s night fell quickly. The estate’s lights came up one by one.

Fewer than three hundred people had gathered in the courtyard.

The composition of the guests matched almost exactly what Scooter had described — Middle Eastern royals, European aristocrats, Hollywood stars, and a scattering of people whose names could not be placed but who looked, unmistakably, very rich.

Nine twenty. The host finished introducing Baek Si-on. Applause rose.

He walked onto the stage.

The opening of Legend began, and the conversation in the courtyard slowly died.

……

The song finished, and the applause ran hotter than Baek Si-on had expected.

Then came Bones.

The moment that song started, the atmosphere changed visibly. It suited this kind of private party better than Legend did. Arrogant. Dangerous. A rhythm that sank its hooks in immediately.

Ay-ay-ay-ay-ay-ay——!

By the second chorus, people in the courtyard were already nodding along to the beat. A few Western guests were even calling the hook out with their glasses raised, laughing.

Baek Eun-ah stood at the side of the stage and was struck by a deeply absurd feeling.

The song’s official audio had not even been released yet.

And yet it seemed the whole world already knew how to sing it.

The two songs ended. Baek Si-on bowed slightly. The applause rose again.

After the performance, Baek Si-on was taken to meet His Highness Sheikh Mohammed Al Maktoum.

The meeting was short. A handshake. Pleasantries. A photograph.

His Highness praised Legend as powerful, and called Bones “interesting.”

The word was spoken very lightly.

But Baek Eun-ah suspected that, translated into plain language, it meant something closer to:

This song is insane.

I like it.

The photograph done, the coordinator escorted them back to the lounge. On the table sat fruit, bottled water, dates — and a wooden box with exquisite packaging.

“A small gift from His Highness to Mr. Baek.”

Baek Si-on opened it and glanced inside.

A wristwatch.

A Patek Philippe.

He closed the box again without a word.

“Please thank His Highness for me.”

“Of course.”

Before boarding the return flight, Baek Si-on still took ten minutes to buy souvenirs.

Two boxes of camel-milk chocolate. Two boxes of dates. And several small, exquisitely packaged tins of saffron.

No sand was scooped up.

Choi Jin-ri might have joked on KakaoTalk about bringing her sand, but he was not so cluelessly literal as to actually pack two jars of dirt from the desert of the United Arab Emirates.

October 27 1:00 a.m. · Los Angeles local time Private channel at the airport → the car Scooter’s arms are open before the man is even off the plane. The folders in his assistants’ hands say the rest.

Los Angeles local time. One in the morning of the twenty-seventh.

The plane touched down.

Outside the private channel, Scooter Braun was already waiting, two assistants behind him.

Seeing Baek Si-on come out, he spread his arms.

“Welcome back, Legend.”

Baek Si-on looked at him.

“Don’t call me that.”

Scooter laughed and pulled him into a hug.

“You’ve lost the right to refuse that title.”

Baek Eun-ah followed behind, tablet hugged to her chest.

She noticed that both of the assistants beside Scooter were holding folders, and a bad feeling settled in her stomach at once.

Because it meant there was a whole pile of work waiting behind this.

They got into the car. Scooter sat next to Baek Si-on. Baek Eun-ah took the front passenger seat. The bodyguard and the assistants followed in a second car.

“I have good news for you.”

Baek Si-on was still fastening his seat belt.

“What?”

Scooter’s eyes glittered like a vulture that had just spotted a rabbit.

“You know the song at number one on the charts right now — ‘All About That Bass’?”

“I know it.”

“A couple of days ago it got exposed. Suspicion of plagiarism.”

Baek Si-on’s hand paused on the seat-belt buckle.

“Plagiarised from whom?”

“A singer from your country. Someone called…”

Scooter glanced down at the memo on his phone.

“Koyote? Yes, that’s the one. It’s their song ‘Happiness Mode.’”

Baek Si-on: “……”

“The fight online is vicious right now. And for us, this is the chance of a lifetime!”

Baek Eun-ah listened quietly from the front.

Her English had not yet reached the level where she could toss it around freely, but there were a few words she could now pick out on reflex: “plagiarism.” “chart.” “number one.”

She ran the translation through her head twice.

And then —

her eyes flew wide.

Number one was accused of plagiarism.

Which meant the backlash would drag down the reputation and the streaming trajectory of “All About That Bass.” If the radio stations, the media and the chart discourse kept this up, Meghan Trainor’s hold on number one would start to loosen.

And right now, Legend sat at number three.

The only opponent still standing in front of it was Taylor Swift’s transformation single —

“Shake It Off.”
