Chapter One Hundred Twenty Eight

An Ordinary Man’s Nine God-Tier Songs

Late October Later that morning Los Angeles · SB Projects · the conference room Two coffees, three songs, and the first full album of a life that began with none.

SB Projects. The conference room.

When Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah arrived, two coffees were already waiting on the table.

One americano.

And one of those caramel things loaded with foam — put out, obviously, for Baek Eun-ah.

From the hotel to the office, all the way over, she had gone through every screen at once: the Twitter trends, the Instagram hot posts, the rate the YouTube comments were piling up, the real-time Spotify play curve, and the screaming from Korea.

The conclusion split clean down the middle.

Korea’s gossip accounts would gladly have him escorted home for a period of national reflection.

American hip-hop had already given him a new nickname.

Twitter · worldwide · trending

#TomFordGangster

a man in haute couture, mid-storm

Baek Eun-ah had stared at the two halves of her screen the whole ride, eyes going wide.

Every scrap of crisis-PR knowledge she had scraped together over the past few months had just been taken back to school and re-educated by the North American internet.

So this was how it worked. The same person. The same move. The same frame. Change the cultural context, and a “career-ending scandal risk” became a “masculinity point.”

This was harder than tax law.

Before she could finish being traumatised, the door opened.

Scooter Braun took one look at them, spread his arms wide, and came in beaming.

“Welcome, Tom Ford Gangster.”

Baek Si-on let himself be clapped on the back for exactly the polite duration, then stepped back.

“The songs?”

“You have the emotional range of a fire extinguisher, you know that?”

Scooter Braun shrugged, walked behind the enormous desk, and pointed at the leather sofa across from him.

“Sit. Prepare to have your ears knocked up.”

He took the mouse, clicked once.

Play.

The intro of the first song filled the whole office in an instant.

Heavy drum.

Sharp handclaps.

A stomping beat with real weight behind it.

The arrangement logic was very close to the opening of Legend — except the kick drum sat even heavier and meaner in the mix, as if it had come to pick a fight.

Baek Si-on nodded along.

Then the chorus cut in.

SB Projects · reference vocal · the song stops being polite

Ruling from the throne—

Looking down below—

What a sight to see—

The crown is getting heavy—

But my name will live forever—

He stopped nodding for a second.

Up to that point he had privately felt that the lyrics of his own Legend were on the cautious side. At least Legend only promised “until we become legends.”

This song was already ruling from the throne and looking down on the world below.

Beside him, Baek Eun-ah’s mouth had fallen slightly open.

One thought was cycling through her head.

Arrogant.

Incredibly arrogant.

The kind of arrogant that felt like the songwriter had written the hook with someone leaning into his ear the whole time, shouting:

“More confident!”

“More king!”

“Make them kneel a little harder!”

The song finished.

Baek Si-on looked at Scooter Braun.

“What’s this one for? Celebrating me taking number one?”

Scooter Braun’s face hung there for a moment.

“?”

He looked at Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on looked back.

Two seconds passed.

Scooter Braun spread his hands.

“That one’s for LeBron.”

“????”

Baek Si-on turned to the monitor.

The track title on the player said, in plain letters:

playback window · SB Projects workstation

01 · King.mp3 — 3:44 · reference vocal

The cursor sits on the file name. Nobody has renamed it.

It was never about the man listening. It was about the man it was written for.

Ah.

King.

Not a legend.

A king.

For LeBron James. The man the world had spent years calling King James.

Baek Si-on let his expression return to perfectly level, as if the misunderstanding had never happened at all.

Scooter Braun watched that unshakeable face and the corner of his mouth twitched.

“So. What did you think?”

Baek Si-on considered it.

“Very LeBron.”

“……”

Was that a compliment or a dig?

Scooter Braun decided, wisely, not to ask.

“Hear this one.”

Scooter Braun clicked a couple of times. Another audio file opened.

Play.

The intro was a piano. Slow. Heavy. The kind of cadence that belonged in a chapel, or a funeral.

Baek Si-on closed his eyes, slowly.

The singer on the demo opened his mouth.

The first line —

Goosebumps climbed Baek Si-on’s arms.

Not because the melody was beautiful. The melody was beautiful, sure. But that wasn’t what did it.

It was the picture the arrangement and the lyric put inside his head, all at once.

Every downward step of the piano was water rising, inch by inch.

Just stop your crying, it’s a sign of the times—

…

April.

Cold water.

Children who had been told to stay where they were — and then the ship tilted, and the water came in, and they stayed where they were.

If someone had shouted that line out loud, back then. If someone had ignored the voice over the loudspeaker saying stay put, stood up, grabbed the people next to them and run —

Maybe…

The song ended.

Baek Si-on opened his eyes.

“Play it again.”

Scooter Braun looked at him for a moment, did not ask why, and dragged the progress bar back to the start.

He played it again.

Baek Si-on closed his eyes once more.

When the second listen was over, he opened them and looked at the screen.

The title:

playback window · SB Projects workstation

02 · Sign of the Times.mp3 — 5:12 · reference vocal

The file name is underlined by the cursor, like a sentence waiting to be signed.

Some songs you collect. Some songs collect you.

“I can see this one got to you. So let me offer a suggestion.”

Baek Si-on looked over.

“All the money from this song goes to the relevant foundations. And we hold the music video until the one-year anniversary.”

Scooter Braun kept going.

“The date will make its own memorial. The song’s art stands on its own — it doesn’t need to borrow heat from any one event.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

That plan was right.

No cashing in on tragedy.

No milking emotion.

Just take a good song, let it go at the right time, and let it speak for itself.

The money goes out. And you don’t need a press release about “Baek Si-on donates.” Do it quietly. The people who need to know will know.

“Who wrote it?”

“A British kid named Harry Styles. You wouldn’t know him.”

Baek Si-on’s face did not move.

But behind it, a few facts flicked past very fast.

Harry Styles. A member of One Direction.

One day, one of the biggest male solo artists on the planet.

Right now he was still in the boy band — but he had already started writing for the outside.

Interesting.

“Anything else?”

“One more. This one you’ll like better.”

Scooter Braun clicked a few more times. A third audio file opened.

Play.

…

The instant the intro hit, Baek Si-on’s body answered on its own.

Not goosebumps this time. His toes started keeping the beat before his brain had signed off on it.

Underneath it was the root of the blues. On top of it was the shell of rock. In the middle, a stomping beat.

It sounded like a man standing in the middle of an empty plain, saying to every court that had ever judged him:

I am not a saint.

I am not the monster you projected onto me.

I am only a man.

✦ ✦ ✦ Human · the demo playback

The song ended.

Baek Si-on looked at the screen.

The title:

playback window · SB Projects workstation

03 · Human.mp3 — 4:09 · reference vocal

The three files sit in a row now. Nothing else in the folder.

King. A sign of the times. A human.

“Eun-ah told me you’ve had some friction with the left-leaning film crowd back home.”

Baek Eun-ah snapped her head up.

“I never said that!”

Scooter Braun spread his hands.

“You phrased it more politely.”

“……”

She turned to look at her cousin.

Baek Si-on turned to look at her.

Baek Eun-ah sat up straight at once.

“Oppa. I only said that Director Bong Joon-ho has very strong self-expression.”

“Mm.”

“I didn’t tell on you.”

“Mm.”

“What is that ‘mm’ supposed to mean?”

“Noted.”

“……”

Why did that sound even more like there was a case file?

Scooter Braun rapped the desk and pulled the meeting back on track.

“This Human can be your position statement. ‘I’m only human — stop pinning your political metaphors and your moral straightjackets on me.’ What do you think? Direct enough?”

Baek Si-on looked at him. Thought about it.

“So — apart from the first one, you had the other two custom-made for me?”

Scooter Braun leaned back in his chair and spread both arms, face written with the words “yes, that was all me.”

“I have prepared myself to receive your praise.”

“……”

Baek Eun-ah looked down, fighting not to laugh.

Scooter Braun waited two seconds.

Three seconds.

Five seconds.

Finally, Baek Si-on opened his mouth.

“Thank you for your hard work.”

“……”

That again.

That line again!

He pointed at Baek Si-on, laughing despite himself.

“Baek. Do you know how many connections I burned to get these demos made?”

“No.”

“……”

Scooter Braun sighed. He surrendered, completely.

Extracting a single emotionally-full compliment out of this Korean man was roughly as hard as getting Nick Young to pass the ball to Kobe in a live game.

“Forget it.”

He spun his chair back to the monitor.

“Expecting emotional value out of a person like you — that’s on me.”

Baek Si-on didn’t take the bait. He was looking at the list of three audio files on the screen.

“Who wrote Human?”

“A guy called Rag’n’Bone Man. British blues singer. Built like a refrigerator, voice lower than one.”

“……”

Baek Eun-ah had no words.

The comparison was bizarre.

And somehow, weirdly vivid.

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Cost?”

“For Human I bought it out, fifty thousand dollars.”

“The Sign of the Times one was trickier — messier writer team — but I got you fifteen percent of the songwriting split.”

At the words “songwriting split,” Baek Si-on was suddenly taken back to the day he had written “What Do You Mean?” for Justin Bieber.

Back then, Baek Si-on had been the creator. The one on the losing end of the split.

Who would have thought the wheel would turn.

Now he was the one who had crossed over to the capital side — calmly exercising the kind of “superstar privilege” that used to belong only to Bieber, slicing off a share of other creators’ cake and feeling perfectly entitled to it.

Truly…

The dragon-slayer becomes the dragon.

It felt pretty good, though.

“Those three make eight. Add the live version from last night.”

Scooter Braun turned the screen around.

On it, the tracklist:

the first full album · draft tracklist

1 · Legend

2 · Way Back Home

3 · Way Down We Go

4 · Love Yourself

6 · King

9 · Legend — Lakers Opening Night · Live Version

Nine songs. One complete record.

“Nine tracks — one full-length album.”

Baek Si-on looked at the list.

Nine.

At the start of the year he had arrived here without a single song to his name. Now there were nine.

Some he had written.

Some he had “borrowed.”

Some had been given to him.

Some Scooter Braun had bought outright.

Behind every single one of them sat a story that was at least a little bit abnormal.

“The album’s called Human.”

“Legends, kings, street brotherhood, sports-hero narratives — and underneath all of it, you’re just an ordinary man.”

“What do you think?”

“Layered enough for you?”

Baek Si-on had to admit it: Scooter Braun really knew what he was doing.

This wasn’t just a title.

It was a funnel — collecting every messy, explosive, absurd, self-contradictory image the public had built of Baek Si-on over the past weeks and running it all down to one central idea.

You call me a legend.

You call me a madman.

You call me violence-prone.

You call me loyal to my brothers.

You call me a global superstar.

But I am only a man.

An ordinary man.

“Fine.”

“Good.”

Scooter Braun smiled, satisfied.

“Then tomorrow we announce the album’s pre-order.”

“Tomorrow?”

“Tomorrow. If Legend really takes number one when Billboard refreshes, that’s the single best moment to open the pre-order window.”

He went on.

“The pre-order unlocks four songs.”

“Legend.”

“Way Back Home.”

“Way Down We Go.”

“Bones.”

Baek Eun-ah was taking notes at speed.

Scooter Braun kept talking.

“November twenty-third.”

“The AMAs.”

“You premiere King on that stage.”

Baek Si-on looked up at him.

“King?”

“King.”

“So it’s the second single?”

Scooter Braun smiled a little.

“More precisely — the second era label after Legend.”

“The NBA doesn’t need only one legend of the past. They need a face of the present. This isn’t us forcing the trend, Baek. This is a narrative the league was going to push anyway.”

“If we don’t do it, someone else will.”

On the level of pure business logic, there was nothing wrong with that read.

On the level of temperament, Baek Si-on kept his opinion reserved.

It wasn’t that he disliked LeBron James. It wasn’t that he doubted LeBron’s commercial value.

It was that he knew exactly why he hadn’t gone to LeBron first when casting the Legend music video.

Because the man would most likely treat the collaboration as an act of charity — the superior bestowing something on the inferior.

He would scrutinise you. Evaluate you. Run you past his team and measure how much traffic you could add to his kingdom. Then decide whether he would “permit” you to borrow his name.

Seeing Baek Si-on make no reply, Scooter Braun pressed on.

“Early on the twenty-fourth of November — a few hours after the AMAs end — the full album goes up worldwide. Nine songs, all at once.”

“Fine.”

Scooter Braun saved the file and looked over at him.

“Next you learn the songs. Recording back in Korea works too.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Jung Jae-joon?”

“Fine by me.”

Scooter Braun said,

“But this time I’ll have the production team over here package the demos, the stems, and the reference vocals properly. You record in Korea, send the dry vocals and the sessions back, and we do the final mix on this side.”

Baek Si-on gave an “mm.”

Scooter Braun added one more thing.

“And don’t drink too much before you record.”

Baek Si-on looked at him.

Scooter Braun spread his hands.

“I know you used alcohol to get into the zone when you recorded Legend.”

“Depends on the song.”

“Human can be rough around the edges. Sign of the Times can’t. That one has to stay restrained — grief held at the back of the throat, not cried out loud.”

“And King?”

“Confident. Very confident.”

“Like LeBron?”

“Like LeBron.”

“Then I might have to learn how to form a superteam.”

“……”

Baek Eun-ah lowered her head, shoulders shaking with suppressed laughter.

Scooter Braun waved a hand.

“All right. That’s it for today.”

He stood up.

“You two head back to the hotel. Baek — learn the songs. Eun-ah — confirm the Korean pre-order announcement translation, the copyright info, the track order, the S.W Studio receiving entity, and the distribution route with Universal.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded at once.

“Understood.”

Scooter Braun looked back at Baek Si-on.

“Billboard updates tomorrow morning.”

“If it’s number one?”

“We go to Under Armour.”

“If it’s number two?”

“Also Under Armour.”

“If it’s number three?”

Scooter Braun gave him a look.

“Then we eat first.”

“……”

Scooter Braun laughed.

“Relax, Baek.”

“Whatever number it is — you’re already in the game.”

He paused.

“But if it’s number one, we’re not going to talk partnership.”

“We’re going to harvest.”

Baek Si-on stood.

“See you tomorrow, then.”

Scooter Braun nodded.

“See you tomorrow.”
