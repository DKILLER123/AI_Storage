Chapter One Hundred Twenty Three

DNA Swapped, Personality Synced

Late October Dinner Cheongdam-dong · a sushi-ya’s private room The apology is poured in sake. The interrogation, in soy.

Cheongdam-dong. A sushi-ya. A private room at the back.

Lee Ji-eun was pouring sake for Yoo In-na. Tonight’s meal was a formal apology. A few nights before, a certain someone had come off a kiss scene looking as if nothing whatsoever had happened, Ji-eun’s heart had gone into a complete spin, and in the dead of the night she had left her dearest friend standing outside the door. A dereliction of best-friend duty that serious could only be atoned for with very expensive Japanese food.

Yoo In-na picked up a slice of sashimi and dipped it in the soy, unhurriedly.

“So.”

The instant Lee Ji-eun heard that tone, her alarms went off.

“Unnie.”

“Mm?”

“Don’t use that tone.”

“What tone?”

“The tone you use right before you interrogate me.”

Yoo In-na laughed once.

“I haven’t even asked anything yet, and you’re already feeling guilty?”

“I’m not.”

Yoo In-na was just about to keep teasing her when the phone Ji-eun had left on the table suddenly buzzed.

The caller ID.

Jeong Han-teuk.

Lee Ji-eun glanced at it.

“Unnie, let me take this.”

Yoo In-na made a go-ahead gesture.

Jeong Han-teuk → Lee Ji-eun · the offer that outruns the introduction

“Ji-eun-ah!”

Jeong Han-teuk’s voice detonated out of the earpiece — at least three decibels above the volume of any normal social conversation. Ji-eun instinctively moved the phone a little farther from her ear.

“Oppa.”

“Writer Park Ji-eun just called!”

Lee Ji-eun froze for a second. Writer Park Ji-eun. Baek Si-on had only floated that name past her over KakaoTalk in the small hours of last night. And by today the call had already reached Han-teuk’s phone? Wasn’t the turnaround a little too fantastical?

Before she could think it through, Han-teuk was still talking.

“Writer Park says she’s finished a new script, and there’s a second female lead in it — a character called Cindy!”

“And she said it especially —”

“That the part was made for you! She wants to formally invite you to take it!”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

When Baek Si-on had said “introduction,” she had pictured him helping her get a foot in the door for an audition, and whatever came after that would be up to her own efforts.

And then?

This was an introduction? He had tied the role up in a pink ribbon, fitted it into a gift box, wrapped the whole parcel, and set it down in front of her. He had even topped it off, perfectly arranged, with the most premium of show-business phrases — “made for you.”

“Ji-eun?”

Han-teuk waited a few seconds at the other end.

“Are you listening?”

“Ah. I’m listening.”

“Then why don’t you sound the least bit excited?”

Han-teuk delivered the question that cuts straight to the soul.

“You don’t actually not know who writer Park Ji-eun is, do you?”

“I know…”

But she genuinely could not work up any excitement. Because she had a very good guess at the real situation. Writer Park Ji-eun could not have dreamed of her, Lee Ji-eun, out of nowhere last night, then slammed the table this morning declaring that Cindy had to be her and no one else.

Far more likely — Baek Si-on, through some channel or other, had pushed the whole affair straight to this point himself.

“Their side wants to set up a meeting as soon as possible. To talk over the script and the part.”

“I know. Oppa, first thank writer Park for me. Tell her I’m taking the part. We’ll go over the details at the company tomorrow.”

“Oh… all right. I’ll go reply to them, then. You really aren’t excited?”

“I am. That’s exactly why I need to go and process it.”

Lee Ji-eun hung up with an entirely expressionless face.

She set the phone down.

Yoo In-na was watching her, curious.

“What is it?”

Lee Ji-eun thought for a moment about how to phrase it, then explained the matter in brief.

“Writer Park Ji-eun came looking for you?”

This time Yoo In-na was genuinely stunned.

No one understood better than she did what a second female lead in writer Park Ji-eun’s new drama was worth. Because in My Love from the Star, that had been exactly her own position. In that drama, true, Jun Ji-hyun’s light had been so strong it pressed almost everyone else into shadow. But there was no denying the show had carried Yoo In-na to a towering level of recognition across Asia.

“Why do you look like you’re not excited at all?”

Lee Ji-eun picked up her cup and took a small sip of the sake.

“Excited.”

Yoo In-na: “……”

She studied that face, calm past all reason. That was excited?

She kept looking, and looking, until a highly unscientific thought surfaced in her mind. Her expression slowly turned subtle.

Could it be that one truly takes the colour of the company one keeps? Because Ji-eun had kissed a certain someone while shooting the music video, exchanged DNA with him, and now even their personalities had started to sync?

Of course, Yoo In-na did not voice the absurd idea. She simply put down her chopsticks unhurriedly, took a tissue, and dabbed the corner of her mouth.

“Let’s head back.”

Lee Ji-eun looked up.

“Huh?”

“Aren’t you meeting writer Park tomorrow?” Yoo In-na looked at her. “Go to bed early. Let the face de-puff, get your condition back up to par. Don’t go in wearing dark circles and make writer Park Ji-eun start wondering whether she picked the wrong person.”

“I don’t have dark circles.”

“Not yet. You will have them by three in the morning if you lie there spinning your thoughts tonight.”

“……”

✿ ✿ ✿

Late October Same night The building both of them live in · an elevator ride, then a quiet flat Two floors apart, one piece of advice, and a chat window left open on a coffee table.

The two of them lived in the same complex. Same building, different floors.

The elevator rose. The floor numbers ticked up one by one.

It reached Yoo In-na’s floor.

Ding.

The doors slid open. Before she stepped out, Yoo In-na looked back once.

“Ji-eun-ah.”

“Mm?”

“Don’t overthink it. A good part won’t get any simpler because you’re afraid of it, and it won’t get any safer because you’re not.”

“……”

The doors slid shut again, slowly. Lee Ji-eun watched the last thread of light between them narrow and close.

After two seconds, she let out a small breath.

Home. She turned on the living-room light. The flat was quiet to the point of being too quiet.

Lee Ji-eun took off her coat, changed into slippers, and stood still for two seconds.

She took out her phone. Opened KakaoTalk. The thread with Baek Si-on.

She typed.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Writer Park contacted me.

Sent.

She put the phone down on the shelf by the front door. Walked into the bathroom.

Shower. Brushing. Pyjamas. Face mask.

Only after the whole routine was done did she come back to the living room and pick the phone up.

The screen lit. One new message.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Si-on

Mm.

Lee Ji-eun rolled her eyes at that single syllable.

Even if he refused to take the credit for himself, the least he could manage was a “congratulations” or a “prepare well.” A reaction like that really did drain a person of every urge to share good news.

She jabbed furiously at the screen.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

What did you do?

Baek Si-on

Had someone pass along a word.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

One word, and writer Park is calling the role tailor-made for me?

Baek Si-on

Maybe she already thought so.

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

Lying this perfunctorily, even for him.

Writer Park Ji-eun might well genuinely think Ji-eun suited Cindy. But between “thinks she suits it” and “personally rings up the manager to say the role was made for her” there sat at least three layers of production meetings, two rounds of casting discussions, a period of the agency feeling out the offer — and a fair number of adults trading favours behind the scenes.

Lee Ji-eun stared at the phone screen for a few seconds, then typed, slowly.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Thank you, either way. When do you have time? I’ll buy you dinner.

After sending it, Lee Ji-eun looked at the “I’ll buy you dinner” sitting on the screen and felt a small twinge of regret.

Too forward.

Given how many times she had done things for Baek Si-on before, by the sunbae–junior terms of their relationship, buying him a meal counted for nothing at all.

But the problem was —

the context was no longer the same. They had filmed a music video. They had kissed. And she had just received a role of considerable value, handed over gift-wrapped.

At a moment like this, “let me buy you dinner” sounded, however you listened to it, like the opening move of a date.

Lee Ji-eun bit her lower lip.

Should she recall it?

She checked the message’s sent time. Over half a minute had already passed.

Technically, it was still possible.

Psychologically — impossible.

Because Baek Si-on had already read it.

Fine.

Lee Ji-eun turned the phone face-down on the coffee table. Went to the fridge, opened it, took out a bottle of water. Drank. Drank again.

Think of it as an ordinary thank-you. Buying a meal for a sunbae was the most ordinary thing in the world. Nothing different about it.

Mm.

Nothing different.

She had to repeat it to herself twice before she believed it.

The phone buzzed.

Lee Ji-eun set the bottle down, walked back to the coffee table, and picked the phone up.

A photo.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Si-on

[photo]

She opened it.

It was the release-date poster for Green Bottle Fly, opening in Korean theatres on November 1.

In the image, Baek Si-on squatted at the foot of a wall in an old quarter of the city. The plaster was mottled, the ground damp. In the distance, grey tower blocks and a mess of sagging utility wires.

He wore a dark-green shirt, his shoulders slightly slumped, a cigarette held between his lips. The smoke drifted upward and blurred half of his face.

Lee Ji-eun blinked.

Wait.

She remembered that the poster from the Venice Film Festival had not been this one.

She had seen that poster. In the stretch after Venice, the news had come so thick and fast — photographs tied to Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri had swept the Korean entertainment pages almost without pause.

Lee Ji-eun switched out of KakaoTalk and opened a search.

She typed, into the search bar: Green Bottle Fly — Venice poster.

The search results surfaced quickly.

Indeed — not the same image.

The Venice version had shown Baek Si-on underwater, face turned upward, one hand reaching desperately for the shore. Choi Jin-ri stood at the water’s edge, bent forward, reaching out to him in turn — but behind her, another hand had a firm grip on her arm, holding her back.

The composition had been heavy with visual impact and a sense of fate.

And yet the domestic release had swapped it for the single-figure, smoking version.

Lee Ji-eun did not understand politics especially well.

But she was not entirely without awareness, either.

What had happened in April of this year — of course she knew.

Put those images together, in the Korea of 2014, and they could too easily be read as a certain kind of thing.

The poster had probably been changed for exactly that reason.

Lee Ji-eun did not linger on the subject. She was not part of the film world. Nor was she a political commentator.

Her attention quickly swung back to the act itself — to Baek Si-on sending her this poster.

She typed.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Is that your way of saying I should block-book a screening?

In fact, when Lee Ji-eun had imagined facing this kind of gentle tug-of-war invitation, she had expected Baek Si-on to fob her off with some blunt man’s line — “no need to be polite” — or else answer coolly that he had no time.

Instead he had gone straight ahead and sent over the release poster of a film he starred in himself.

The subtext could not have been clearer:

Skip the meal. If you really want to thank me, come support my box office.

How could a person even be like this — converting a favour owed directly into film promotion and ticket sales?

Looking at the poster, Lee Ji-eun suddenly laughed. A little exasperated. And, she had to admit, a little endeared.

Ding.

Baek Si-on’s reply.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Si-on

Sure.

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

He really meant it.

She leaned back against the sofa and the corner of her mouth lifted.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

And no social-media promo needed from me?

Baek Si-on

Okay.

Lee Ji-eun looked at that “Okay.”

Her smile grew more pronounced.

Honestly. He had not even offered one token refusal. Not one.

She had half wanted to see him look a little embarrassed for once. Instead the man had accepted her promotional resources as his perfectly rightful due, without a flicker of self-consciousness.

And yet, for some reason, she could not bring herself to get angry. Because he had been exactly like this from the very beginning.

Lee Ji-eun bent over the phone and typed again.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Fine. I’ll go see it.

Baek Si-on

Remember to buy a ticket.

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

The little pocket of soft warmth that had just risen in her chest was stamped flat by that one line.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

I’m not so broke that I need a complimentary ticket from you!

Baek Si-on

Thank you for supporting Korean independent film.

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

She closed her eyes, and flipped the phone face-down on the table in a single motion.

“Endearing,” her foot. This man really was, when all was said and done, the most infuriating person she knew.
