Chapter Two Hundred Two

Early Romance Comes With Many Demands

March 31 · 7:40 a.m. · a bathroom, a phone, and a breakfast table An arm extracted like a band-aid · forty million dollars and one sprinting stick figure · two briefs and a trash can that needs sorting · a demo that sounds like a deposition · lyrics too honest for an afternoon slot · a state secret, amended in dark blue the apartment → Seoul → tomorrow, a set full of zombies

When Baek Si-on woke, his first thought was whether his right hand still belonged to him.

He tilted his head and studied Jin-ri’s sleeping face.

If he woke her now, the sequence would run as follows.

If she woke now: the projected sequence

Step one — dazed.

Step two — discover she is pillowing on his arm.

Step three — pretend she is not.

Step four — anger, on the assumption that he is laughing at her.

Projected duration: ten minutes, minimum. Executive decision: self-rescue.

He extracted his arm the way one removes a band-aid that has been on the skin too long — a millimeter at a time, from beneath her head.

Jin-ri frowned in her sleep. Baek Si-on stopped. She nuzzled the pillow, shifted into a new position. He resumed. At the final half-inch, her lips moved and mumbled something inaudible. He didn’t catch it — but judging by the shape of it, either don’t go or stop making noise.

He elected to believe the former.

The arm came free. Baek Si-on raised it and opened and closed his fist, sensation crawling back through a dense field of pins and needles.

Painful. But considering last night’s service assignment for this arm, he declined to file a complaint.

He took the phone from the nightstand and walked barefoot into the bathroom.

❧ ❧ ❧

The bathroom light came on; the screen was stacked with unread messages. He checked the time first. 7:40 a.m., Korea time. Then scrolled.

The inbox, as found at 7:40 a.m.

FROM BAEK EUN-AH — one sticker: a stick figure sprinting flat-out with a money bag clutched to its chest; overhead, in letters: “IT ARRIVED!!!”

Cross-checked against the amount-confirmation SMS and approved with a nod: the tour’s first-round settlement, the North America digital-rights stage split, and the overseas flows of several earlier Korean endorsements — the deployable funds in S.W Studio’s US account had finally landed. Forty million dollars.

FROM CHARLIE PUTH — one audio file: OOPS_demo_CP_MT_v1.mp3. CP, Charlie Puth. MT, Meghan Trainor.

The demo’s origin story began two nights earlier.

On Jin-ri’s birthday, after the dinner-table conversation with Hara about her solo direction, Baek Si-on had messaged two people that same night.

The first was Charlie Puth. The message was simple.

The second was Ed Sheeran. The message was simpler.

The two briefs, as sent on the birthday night

TO CHARLIE PUTH — “Write a male-female duet. Light, built for a stage, the hook must catch, and it must not lean on vocal firepower. For the Asian market. Demo within three days.” — reply received: one salute.

TO ED SHEERAN — “Does your trash can hold any dead drafts that suit a female voice or a male-female duet? Melody on the light side. Nothing too English-bitter.” — reply received, the next morning: “My trash needs sorting. Give me a few days.”

Baek Si-on wasn’t in a hurry. He had the patience to wait for Ed’s dead drafts. Charlie, meanwhile, had delivered in two days flat.

Commissioning both Charlie and Ed at once wasn’t only about helping Hara.

He wanted, for himself, to use the opportunity to open Japan formally. Japan was the world’s second-largest music market: the physical record still alive, fan spending power deep, and the record shops, television stages, magazines, and advertising systems all capable of growing a very long tail.

He could, of course, force the door. A Billboard double-crown Korean singer descending on Japan would guarantee coverage. But that was the hard way. The Japanese market had its own exclusionary inertia, and its own modes of acceptance. Rather than pressing in under the banner of GLOBAL SUPERSTAR OFFICIALLY ENTERS JAPAN, it was cleaner to borrow Hara and do something that looked natural.

❝

Do not kick the door in under a banner. Borrow a friend, bring a song, and let the door decide it was always open.

— the Japan doctrine, drafted between toothbrush and telephone

The demo played out of the phone’s speaker.

♪

OOPS_demo_CP_MT_v1

Charlie Puth × Meghan Trainor · demo v1 · doo-wop finger-snaps · unmastered

Retro. Light. Doo-wop finger-snaps and claps underneath. Meghan’s voice came first — playful, clear — and then Charlie’s cut in, and the two of them began trading lines. The melody hooked; the chorus hooked almost past the point of manners.

Baek Si-on’s brushing slowed.

The duet’s staging space was unmistakable. Reworked as a Japanese-Korean bilingual number, built as an interactive stage song — there was real potential. The problem was the lyrics. The theme, generously summarized: two people did a few things unsuitable for broadcast in a television station’s afternoon slot, then pretended nothing had happened, saying “oops” to each other in voices of extremely insincere innocence.

The melody was innocent. The rhythm had a spotless record. But every line of the lyrics read like a crime-scene confession delivered in the cutest possible register.

Baek Si-on spat out the toothpaste, rinsed — and dialed Charlie Puth.

Charlie Puth ↔ Baek Si-on · voice · the demo deposition · Seoul, 7:47 a.m.

“Boss?”

“Did something happen between you and Meghan at that party in Dubai?”

The line went quiet for four seconds. Four seconds on an international call amounts to one complete out-of-body experience.

“…Why would you ask that?”

Charlie’s voice had turned extremely careful.

“Because your demo doesn’t sound like creation. It sounds like a deposition.”

“That’s inspiration!”

“Why did inspiration wake up in someone else’s bed?”

“That’s metaphor! Imagery! Artistic expression!”

“You’re rattled.”

Charlie’s voice got very small.

“Boss, I swear this song was for work…”

“Then why does the first line of the chorus go Oops, my baby?”

“Because… it’s catchy?”

“Charlie.”

“…Okay.”

A long breath down the line.

“There may have been some… non-musical inspiration involved.”

“Involved to what degree?”

“To the degree that… nothing I say now will ever be believed.”

“The melody works. The hook is good.”

Charlie came alive instantly.

“Really?”

“But the lyrics are being rewritten.”

“Rewritten? How clean?”

“Clean enough that your mother can listen to the whole thing and never ask who Meghan is.”

Charlie: “……”

“That might mean deleting half the song.”

“Then delete it.”

“But the best parts ARE the unclean ones—”

“Charlie.”

“I’ll rewrite it.”

The call ended on a mutter about art being murdered by the Asian market.

❧ ❧ ❧

When Baek Si-on came out of the bathroom, Jin-ri was awake — sitting up in bed, hair a bird’s nest, eyes not fully open, a pillow hugged to her chest.

“Who were you on the phone with just now?”

Her voice still carried the rasp of waking.

“Charlie.”

“The one who played backup for you at the concert?”

“Mm.”

She rubbed her eyes.

“Were you interrogating him?”

“Why would you say that?”

“Because through a closed door I could hear that he was about to cry.”

Baek Si-on sat on the edge of the bed.

“I offered some notes on a demo he wrote.”

“What kind of notes?”

“Lyrics.”

She tilted her head at him. He handed her the phone.

“Listen.”

She took it and pressed play. At first her face was very serious — Baek Si-on had asked her to listen, so a professional assessment was owed.

The intro came up: retro, light, genuinely cute. The corner of her mouth lifted, slightly. The chorus arrived.

OOPS_demo_CP_MT_v1.mp3 · the chorus, through a bathroom door

“Oops, my baby...”

“You woke up in my bed...”

“Oops, we broke up, we’re better off as friends...”

The mouth-corner held through the first line. On the second, it began to hesitate. On its face, the third could still pass for an ordinary breakup song — but the line before it had already confessed the entire scene in writing. The mouth-corner withdrew from service.

When it finished, Jin-ri looked at Baek Si-on:

“This is for Hara unnie?”

“It may have been, originally.”

“And now?”

“Now it reads more like Charlie’s own accident report.”

Jin-ri laughed before she could stop it — then composed herself and said, seriously:

“The melody really is lovely.”

A pause.

“If the lyrics weren’t so… honest.”

“Hence the rewrite.”

Baek Si-on took the phone back, and considered.

“This song’s temperament doesn’t suit Hara. Her Japan solo needs something more grown.”

“Then who is it for?”

He looked at the filename on the phone.

“Still thinking.”

Jin-ri made an acknowledging noise, not much interested, and buried her face in the pillow. It came out muffled:

“I join the set tomorrow.”

Baek Si-on said nothing. Half a face rose out of the pillow.

“Aren’t you going to say something?”

“Such as?”

“Such as… good luck?”

“Good luck.”

She wanted to say thank you, but thank you felt too much like distance. So she asked a very small question instead.

“Will you come visit the set?”

“If the schedule allows.”

Instant dissatisfaction.

“This is the moment where you say you’ll definitely come.”

“I will definitely check the schedule.”

She thumped him. Not much force; the point traveled intact. Baek Si-on caught the fist and used it to pull her closer.

“If there’s time, I’ll come.”

Still not satisfied. He added:

“And if there’s no time, I’ll find some anyway.”

She pouted, visibly adjudicating whether to forgive him — and then he lowered his head and kissed her forehead, and the adjudication concluded early.

“One more before you go, then.”

“You ask for a lot.”

“Early romance asks for a lot.”

“And later?”

“Later, more.”

He looked at her. She looked at him. The two of them laughed together.

❧ ❧ ❧

At breakfast, Jin-ri asked a question out of nowhere.

“What color toothbrush do you like?”

Baek Si-on’s chopsticks paused.

“Toothbrush?”

Jin-ri studied her rice with great attention, voice elaborately casual:

“Mm. The house is missing a spare.”

“Blue.”

Under the table she slid her phone out and tapped at the memo.

“What are you writing?”

She locked the screen at once.

“Nothing.”

“I saw it.”

“You saw wrong.”

“Toothbrush. Slippers. Towel. Home clothes. Socks.”

Jin-ri’s face ignited from the ears down.

“How is your memory this good?”

“Your font size is set enormous.”

“That’s because I’m nearsighted!”

“Then buy glasses first.”

She shoved the phone under her thigh.

“Is this a shopping list?” Baek Si-on asked.

“No.”

“Then what is it?”

“A state secret.”

“Your house is building a military base?”

She glared. He looked back with complete sincerity.

“Buy the slippers dark blue.”

She went still.

“Light colors show dirt. Impractical.”

She watched him. He kept eating, as if the two of them had been discussing something perfectly ordinary. But by the time Jin-ri looked down, the corner of her mouth was beyond all suppression.

The state secret, as amended in real time

Toothbrush — blue. His answer, delivered over rice.

Slippers — dark blue. Amendment entered: ——dark blue. Proposed by the other stakeholder, on grounds of practicality.

Towel — dark.

Home clothes, socks — pending.

File classification: state secret. The base under construction: domestic.

She hid the phone away again and looked up at Baek Si-on eating.

She had never once imagined that a life of one could gain a toothbrush, a pair of slippers, and one dark towel — and that it would make a person this happy.

❝

But it really was this happy. Happy enough that tomorrow, even chased three kilometers by zombies on set, everything would be completely fine.

— the arithmetic of ordinary; the zombie estimate, revised downward
