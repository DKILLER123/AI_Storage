Chapter Sixty-Two

The Chequebook and the Bumper Car

July 29 Evening Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong A dining table turned signing desk

That night, the dining table in the living room was commandeered into a signing desk.

The contract had been drafted by Lawyer Choi and brought over already printed.

When Baek Si-on had called LOEN's legal affairs office that afternoon, he'd laid out the core terms over the phone — the investment amount, the equity split, the division of management authority.

Lawyer Choi's side had framed it up, run three paper copies out of LOEN's print room, then simply carried his briefcase over after work.

He set them out on the table.

The contract wasn't complicated.

Baek Si-on / Son Nam-won — venture agreement · core terms

Clause One · The Investment

Baek Si-on, in his capacity as investor, puts in ₩150,000,000, paid in three tranches: ₩50,000,000 on signing; ₩50,000,000 once the core editorial staff are hired in full; the final ₩50,000,000 when the site goes live and the first batch of news is published.

Clause Two · Equity

Baek Si-on holds 51%; Son Nam-won holds 49%.

Clause Three · Management

Day-to-day editorial decisions, personnel management and content direction are wholly Son Nam-won's. Baek Si-on is not involved in day-to-day operations and reserves only a single veto on major matters — financing, merger, dissolution.

Arbitration and confidentiality clauses appended. Handwritten addendum, re-confirmed by both parties. In three identical copies.

The contract was signed on the spot.

Baek Si-on and Son Nam-won each wrote their names at the end of the document.

“Both of you, the contract takes effect today. Send over the incorporation materials when they're ready; it'll be about one to two weeks.”

“Good. Thank you for your trouble.”

Baek Si-on slid a paper envelope he'd prepared earlier across the table to Lawyer Choi's hand.

Lawyer Choi swept it into the briefcase with a practiced motion, closed his laptop and stood.

“My duty. Come to me at any time for legal matters.”

Baek Si-on saw him to the door, watched him go, and closed it. He walked back into the living room.

Son Nam-won was still at the table, holding the contract, thumb rubbing slowly back and forth along the edge of the paper.

Baek Si-on sat down across from him and held out his hand.

“A pleasure working together, Editor Nam.”

Son Nam-won blinked, then registered it, set the contract aside, and reached out to take the hand.

“A pleasure working together, Boss Baek.”

✿ ✿ ✿

Not long after Son Nam-won left, the sound of a key turning came from the front door.

“Cousin.”

Baek Eun-ah came in carrying a bag, kicking off her canvas shoes at the doorway and swapping them for slippers.

She'd gone off running back and forth like that.

Every day from her own place to Yeonnam-dong, work finished, back again — forty minutes each way, over an hour of her day eaten up in the subway.

So she'd simply moved in with Baek Si-on.

Professor Yoon Hye-ja's response had been to cook a little more rice, set out one more pair of chopsticks, and put a note up on the fridge:

Waste-sorting chart is on the back of the kitchen door.

Baek Si-on turned and stopped.

Baek Eun-ah's hair — that straight black hair that had once been straight enough to use as a ruler — had been blown out into full, airy waves, loose but sharply layered, falling over her shoulders.

Her base makeup was so clear there wasn't a trace of powder, but a single fine, upward-tilted line at the outer corner of each eye pulled her naturally round eyes two millimetres longer.

Her lips were the pink of someone who'd just eaten strawberries — not red, not flashy.

The whole effect was of an active girl-group member about to stand in for a brand.

Sparkle, top to bottom.

“Who did that?”

“Your stylist.”

Baek Eun-ah couldn't hold back the corner of her mouth any longer.

“After the Music Bank schedule at KBS this morning, I came back and interviewed him. He said he wanted to look at my face shape and facial proportions first — and then he just went to work. Cousin, I’m telling you, this man has the real thing.”

She turned in a circle, letting him see the back of her hair.

“When he was done he took a picture with his phone, and I looked at it and didn't even recognize myself.”

Baek Si-on leaned against the edge of the table, arms folded.

“Fine. And the Music Bank side?”

“Confirmed. You don't need to be at the 5 a.m. commute, and you don't need the 6 a.m. group rehearsal. You just go straight to the afternoon full-makeup rehearsal.”

She pulled out her phone and scrolled to the memo.

“Stage lighting and sound are handled by the show team. Styling is on us. The stylist says no problem — he'll have the full plan ready by tomorrow, a complete proposal for hair, makeup and outfit.”

Baek Eun-ah swiped the screen twice.

“Oh right — he also asked if there's any colour you don't like.”

“No.”

“Any style you don't like?”

“Let him decide.”

Baek Eun-ah typed a line into the memo: "Cousin: no requirements."

She read it back.

Something about that line felt like describing a piece of salted fish.

She deleted it and typed again: "Artist side: styling fully delegated to the stylist, no special preferences."

Much better.

Professional.

She tucked the phone back into her pocket and looked at Baek Si-on.

He was already on his way to the bedroom.

“Cousin!”

“Mm.”

“You haven't told me if it looks good!”

Baek Si-on did not stop walking.

“I did.”

“You just said 'fine'! Is 'fine' the same as good?!”

The bedroom door shut.

Baek Eun-ah stood in the living room, glaring at the closed door for a full three seconds, then stomped back to her own room in a huff.

July 29 Morning Seoul Immigration Office · Mapo branch A buzz cut grown out, and an ID photo that beat the man

Baek Eun-ah had booked a number online in advance, so the two of them were there by 8:30, with only three people ahead of them.

When Baek Si-on had his ID photo taken, the photographer asked him to take off his cap.

The buzz cut had grown out a little — no longer pasted flat against the scalp, but not yet long enough to style.

The photo that came out was strong anyway.

The photographer looked at the preview on the screen, then back up at the man in person, and muttered, "I've seen plenty where the ID photo is better than the person. I've rarely seen the person better than the ID photo."

Baek Eun-ah, standing beside them, nearly laughed out loud.

Baek Si-on stayed deadpan the entire time.

Rush passport. Five working days. It would be in hand by August fourth.

✿ ✿ ✿

That afternoon: buy a car.

Baek Si-on handed the job to Baek Eun-ah, with exactly two requirements.

First, black. Second, not too new.

Baek Eun-ah spent the afternoon digging through forums.

She finally found one at a used-car lot in Guro: a second-generation Kia Carnival, black, forty thousand kilometres on the clock, interior at about eighty percent new, rear seats that folded flat.

The dealer named his price at thirty million won.

Baek Eun-ah haggled it down to twenty-six million.

By the time she'd paid and driven it back to Yeonnam-dong, she was sitting bolt upright behind the wheel, both hands at ten-and-two, grinning brighter than the makeup she'd done the day before.

Baek Si-on stood downstairs and watched her park it into the roadside spot.

It took seventeen back-and-forths.

“Do you have a licence?”

“Got it six months ago.”

“How many times have you driven since?”

“…This is the second.”

Baek Si-on looked at the gap between the rear bumper and the car behind it, said nothing, and turned to go upstairs.

July 31 Afternoon Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong A makeup case, a bag of clothes, and three looks

The stylist, Park Ji-hun, came to Yeonnam-dong with a black makeup case and a bag of clothes.

Park Ji-hun

Stylist · JB Hair, Hapjeong-dong

Three years of artist styling at a Cheongdam-dong salon

The front-desk stylist who chased Baek Si-on out of the shop to offer himself. Three years in artist styling, a portfolio ready, and the good sense to read a million-won membership card for the door it was.

He started by measuring Baek Si-on's head circumference and face proportions, then pulled three full outfit options out of the bag and hung them in a row on the rack.

Three looks stylist's proposal

1 · Formal Deep, dark tones. Baek Si-on glanced at it — shook his head.
2 · Street Oversized jacket with wide trousers. He shook his head again.
3 · The choice A black slim-fitting turtleneck knit, a deep-grey draped blazer over it, black slim trousers. Not a single piece of ornament. Clean. Sharp. Cutting.

The look that would survive a high-definition camera: nothing extra, nothing to catch the light.

Baek Si-on picked up the blazer and looked at it.

“This one.”

Park Ji-hun let out a breath of relief.

“Your skin's in excellent condition. The base can be just a half-layer—”

“No.”

“…Then a thin coat of foundation for oil control? High-definition lenses will catch the shine.”

“That's fine.”

Park Ji-hun noted it down.

Baek Eun-ah stood off to the side, privately congratulating herself on her decision to interview this man at all.

Park Ji-hun explained his reasoning before every proposal, didn't push when he was refused, and immediately offered a substitute.

That kind of measure wasn't something a small shop could teach.

She scored him in her head.

Nine out of ten.

The one point off so he wouldn't get too proud.
