Chapter Ninety-Seven

Three Days, You Said. You Made It in Five Hours?

That night into the next morning 23:40 · 00:17 · 05:27 Mapo · a taxi · Hapjeong 401 · SK · Hyundai 17F No second round. A cover for Hwang. A demo named after a car. Zero notes.

When the table broke it was already 23:40. Park Chan-wook stood.

“Second round.”

Two words. Not a question. A statement. Song Kang-ho stood too.

“Where?”

“A new whisky bar toward Hongdae. Scottish owner. Single malt as far as Lagavulin 16.”

The speed at which Bong Joon-ho came off his seat said his feeling for single malt far outranked respect for jet lag. Baek Jeong-hun was about to stand with them. Baek Si-on put a hand on his uncle’s shoulder.

“Uncle. I’m not going.”

Jeong-hun turned.

“Why?”

“Contracts tomorrow.”

If he went second-round with these old men to three or four in the morning, then went to see a brand reeking of drink, the contracts might not need signing.

“You go, uncle. Don’t drink too much.”

Park Chan-wook and Bong were already at the door. Song Kang-ho outside called, “Jeong-hun, faster.” Jeong-hun stood. The second round’s pull was too large.

They split at the shop door. Park Chan-wook, Bong, Song Kang-ho and Jeong-hun into a black van. Jeong-hun, before getting in, turned and waved at Baek Eun-ah.

“Go back early.”

Eun-ah, face empty.

“Dad. You’d better go back early too.”

Door shut. The van into Mapo’s late traffic. Eun-ah at the kerb, watching the tail lights go around a corner, let a breath out.

“Umma may be a widow tonight.”

Si-on looked at her once.

“Watch the wording.”

“Oh. A living widow.”

“…”

They had not driven. So a taxi toward Hapjeong. A thin car-perfume in the cabin. The driver had the radio very low. Midnight news, some assemblyman’s bribery investigation. Back seat. Eun-ah down in her phone. Scrolling, then suddenly remembering.

“Right. Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“What about Director Hwang?”

She held the phone over. On the glass, Director Hwang Soo-ah’s reply. Si-on read it. Thought. Hwang Soo-ah’s ability did not need doubting. But Legend’s MV had already been taken by Bong. Could not let her run empty.

“Ask her to shoot Can’t Take My Eyes Off You.”

Eun-ah froze a second.

“The cover?”

“Mm.”

He gave the phone back.

“Director Hwang is good at people, at weather, at a little almost that isn’t cheap.”

Eun-ah nodded. Indeed. Legend, that stadium rock bound to Kobe and Bong — the picture logic was already not what an ordinary MV director could take. Can’t Take My Eyes Off You was different. A cover. The core sell was not “epic.” It was “weather.” A man at a Venice dinner jumping on a table, pointing at a woman, singing Oh pretty baby. That itself had a picture. A director who had long shot visual concepts for IU — a fit.

Eun-ah typed, head down.

KakaoTalk · Hwang Soo-ah

Baek Eun-ah

Director Hwang, hello! Thank you so much for the reply. The project has a small adjustment — we’d like to invite you to direct the official cover MV for Can’t Take My Eyes Off You. Style more vintage-romantic. Would you have interest? We can set a time to talk in detail~

Send. She had barely put the phone down when she remembered another thing.

“A cover drop needs a mechanical licence. If we shoot an MV we also need sync. Those rights papers…”

Si-on against the back seat, watching night go past the window. This kid’s growth speed was actually fast. Three months ago she had not even heard the words “mechanical licence.” Now she could say them offhand in the back of a taxi.

“Next week in L.A. we talk the rights on the way.”

“All right.”

She made a new line in Notes.

L.A. run: cover rights / mechanical / sync / publisher.

Done, she looked at Si-on beside her. The man had already closed his eyes. She looked at that side face cut light-dark by the street lamps, and suddenly realised something. She had thought she was busy enough today. Hyundai twelve billion. KB ten billion. Director Hwang. L.A. run. But in Si-on’s head there were probably a dozen lines running at once. Endorsements. New song. MV. Kobe. Bong. Rights. And that demo due to Hyundai in three days. She quietly locked the phone. Forget it. She would run one line clear first. A five- or six-floor gap was not something you climbed in one night.

Same night 00:17 Hapjeong · 401 A second song. Low like a tyre in a tunnel. Eun-ah asleep on a folding stool.

Hapjeong. Studio 401. Si-on rang. Three seconds. Door. Jung Jae-joon in the doorway, seeing Si-on and Eun-ah outside, clearly froze.

“No.”

He looked back at the clock on the wall. 00:17. Then back at Si-on.

“You two at this hour — finally decided to ice me?”

Si-on fist-bumped him, went straight in.

“Work.”

Jae-joon looked at him.

“Didn’t you just record one this morning?”

“Another one.”

Jae-joon: “…”

He looked at Eun-ah. Eun-ah spread her hands. Meaning: don’t look at me, I just found out too.

“What’s the situation?”

Si-on sat straight down by the desk.

“Hyundai signed a year. Aslan new car end of October. They asked if I could do an ad song.”

“A Hyundai ad song?”

“Mm.”

“When do they want it?”

“Demo in three days.”

Jae-joon leaned on the frame a while, then turned to the fridge, pulled the door, took a pouch of ready Americano, slapped it on the desk.

“Fine.”

He sat. Opened the DAW.

“Say it. What weather?”

Si-on closed his eyes. In his head that bus crawled with zombies, the cigarette at Carlos’s mouth, the calm one second before the blast, rose again. Then the melody rolling out on a low end.

“Low.”

Si-on said.

“The first hit has to be low.”

Jae-joon’s fingers stopped over the keys.

“How low?”

“Like a tyre over tunnel ground.”

Jae-joon’s brow went up. Si-on went on.

“Not bright speed. Not a sports car’s sharp engine. Aslan is a business sedan. The tone should be steady, heavy, able to hold a room.”

He knocked the desk once with a finger.

“But not old. A force that presses forward.”

Jae-joon heard it.

“Low-end lead. Kick heavy, not dirty. Synth dark. Riff with a memory point.”

“Yes.”

“Vocal.”

Si-on thought.

“First half pressed. Chorus opens.”

“Words.”

“Leave the words. Melody and the arrangement skeleton first.”

Jae-joon nodded.

“Hum.”

Si-on came a little closer. Eyes shut. Dragged that melody bit by bit out of a seam in memory. First time incomplete. Second a little smoother. Third, Jae-joon was already finding pitch on the MIDI. Low register pressed down first. A heavy root. Then a layer of synth with a distortion edge. Then drums. Dong. Dong. Dong. Each one like a tyre over ground.

Eun-ah on a folding stool in the corner, watching two men, one humming, one tapping, one frowning, one nodding. She suddenly had a very absurd feeling. An hour ago they had still been drinking with Park Chan-wook, Bong, Song Kang-ho at a pork house. Half an hour ago they had been talking U.S. copyright in a taxi. Now they were sitting in a forty-pyeong studio in Hapjeong, about to make an ad song for Hyundai. Did this industry have a normal rhythm. No. Si-on did not. Since Venice his life had been as if someone had hit three-times speed.

Jae-joon tapped about two minutes, stopped.

“Like this?”

He hit play. A rough low-end riff with a contour already pressed out of the monitors. Not a whole song. Only a skeleton. The weather was already there. Heavy. Cold. Rolling forward. Si-on listened, nodded.

“This direction.”

Jae-joon turned to him.

“Title thought of?”

“Way Down We Go.”

Jae-joon nodded once, did not comment on whether the name was rustic, did not ask if it had been made up on the spot.

“Continue.”

He said. Then the two of them kept grinding. Si-on in charge of hum, change, cut, hum again. Jae-joon in charge of translating those skeleton lines and weather keywords into an arrangement structure that could actually stand. Si-on was not the kind of client who said “I can’t say it but you should know.” On the contrary, his asks on sound were often precise past the point. Don’t be bright here. Press a little more. Don’t let the kick blow open. When the vocal comes in, it isn’t charging out. It’s coming up slowly in the rear-view. That kind of description, to an ordinary producer, might be a heaven-book. Jae-joon just happened to get it.

In the corner. Eun-ah on that folding stool, originally still holding her spirit, wanting to pretend she was also part of this making meeting. Result, holding to 00:50, her head started dropping bit by bit. One o’clock. Two o’clock. Three o’clock. Finally fully asleep. Phone still in the hand. Screen dark. Person tipped against the wall, sleep-pose very un-manager. About twenty minutes more. Si-on turned from the desk, looked at her once.

“Eun-ah.”

No reaction.

“Baek Eun-ah.”

Still no. Jae-joon beside him laughed once.

“Sleeping pretty dead.”

Si-on stood, went over, tapped her shoulder. Eun-ah startled, eyes open hard.

“Sign! Where do I sign?!”

“…”

Si-on looked at her.

“Go home. Sleep.”

Eun-ah froze two seconds before pulling her soul out of Hyundai and KB contracts.

“Ah?”

“SK Telecom HQ tomorrow.”

He took the phone out of her hand, put it back in her coat pocket.

“Now. Go.”

She looked at the wall clock. 01:14. Then at the pile of coloured tracks on the desk screen.

“You’re still going?”

“Mm.”

“Till when?”

“Depends.”

Eun-ah yawned, picked up her bag, waved at Jae-joon, went downstairs swaying. Door shut. They continued. 02:00, verse melody all set. 03:00, chorus skeleton up. 04:00, bridge treated. Si-on into the booth for a full demo vocal. Front to back, three minutes forty-two. Jae-joon behind the desk listened, hit rewind, listened again. Second time done. Thumb up. Same as Legend that morning. 05:00, rough mix. Jae-joon balanced all the tracks once, a coarse EQ pass, pressed the vocal’s high-end burr, cleared a layer of low-end mud. Not a final mix. Demo-grade rough, enough to play for a client. Enough. 05:27. Export. WAV. Filename: ASLAN_WayDownWeGo_demo_v1_0911. Then the whole person like bones pulled out, went sideways into the old sofa beside.

“…I’ll die ten minutes first.”

Said it, no sound. Five seconds later, snore. Si-on looked at him once, did not call. Stood, to the bath. Cold water on the face, rinse, hair pushed back. The person in the glass had a little overnight red in the whites, spirit still stable. Coming out, Jae-joon still on the sofa sleeping like a squeezed-dry corpse. Si-on put the demo on the phone. Out.

SK Telecom’s contract signed even faster. Quarterly short, three hundred million won, brand tone and exposure both good. Seo Eun-ju last-checking competitor exclusivity, Eun-ah the business line, Si-on in, sign, shake, out, as usual. He came out of the signing without even a breath, straight into a car. Destination. Hyundai Group.

When Hyundai got the call they had thought Si-on was coming to talk shoot details. Result, once he was there, one sentence:

“Demo’s done.”

The brand marketing director in the meeting room, the one who had yesterday suggested an “ad song,” clearly froze.

“Done?”

“Mm.”

“…This fast.”

“Early delivery shouldn’t dock points, should it.”

The director smiled, did not say more. Si-on hit play. Intro out of the iPhone speaker. Lights not off. Curtains not drawn. Standard business-meeting air. Precisely because of that, when that heavy, low-pressure intro with a dark metal weather rolled out of the speaker, the whole room’s air seemed pressed down an inch. Like a large beast walking unhurried through dark, toward you. Then drums in. Then vocal in. Chorus opened, and the whole picture stood up at once. Not a young person showing off speed in a fast car. Mature, cold, expensive, and sure it could hold a room. Song done. The director opposite first rubbed an arm.

“Goosebumps.”

Not courtesy. Real ones.

“Sounds like… an elegant lion, watching prey in the dark. You don’t move, it doesn’t. You move, it’s faster.”

“This weather and Aslan’s brand position, it’s simply…”

He did not say “seamless,” “complementary,” those cheap words. The meaning arrived. Si-on looked at him.

“Anywhere you think needs a change?”

The director thought.

“Personally, no. But I can’t make this call alone.”

He stood.

“Please come with me.”

Same day After SK Hyundai Motor HQ · 17F Danish ceiling speakers. Nine people. Zero notes. Four minutes.

Hyundai Motor headquarters, seventeenth floor. Brand strategy’s large meeting room. A six-metre oak table, glass walls two sides, four sets of Danish ceiling speakers in the ceiling. The marketing director had pulled core people from brand strategy, creative, and market on short notice. Nine in all. Plus Si-on and Eun-ah, eleven in the room.

“Everyone. Mr. Baek’s ad-song demo for Aslan. Listen once first.”

USB into a computer, onto the room system. Play. Danish ceiling speakers versus an iPhone speaker, heaven and earth. Low-end detail all released. Each kick like vibration coming up from under the floor, the distorted-guitar riff cutting a physical grain in the air; when Si-on’s vocal came out of the speakers, three people’s backs in the room went straight at once. Chorus.

Way down we go—go—go—

The descending melody under full-range ceiling playback showed a dimension an iPhone speaker could not present. Sound pressing down from above the ceiling, onto the table, onto the floor, into everyone’s chest. Three minutes forty-two. Done. Of nine people, eight silent. The ninth opened his mouth. A man at the far end of the table. Brown hair, deep sockets, silver frames on the bridge.

Ryan Mitchell

Brand advisor · Hyundai Motor global · Detroit, then Seoul

OC · a Texas cowboy with a Korean folk song, in reverse

American. Ten years in GM North America brand. Hyundai hired him to hold overseas tone. He takes his glasses off, wipes them on a shirt-tail, puts them back, and asks if a Korean made this.

Ryan Mitchell. Hyundai Motor global brand strategy’s Korea-based advisor. American. Detroit. Ten years in GM’s North America brand, hired over by Hyundai to hold overseas-market tone. He said one sentence in English.

“A Korean made this?”

The marketing director turned and looked at him. Mitchell took the glasses off, wiped the lenses on a shirt-tail, put them back.

“I mean—”

His look from the speakers to Si-on at the other end of the table.

“This song’s ground is pure American southern blues-rock. Distorted-guitar tone, kick logic, the space between vocal and arrangement… all that Nashville–Memphis line of DNA.”

“A Korean musician making this… it feels no less than a Texas cowboy suddenly producing an extremely authentic Korean folk song, and singing it with every syllable in place.”

A few people in the room laughed once. Mitchell went on.

“And the most extreme thing isn’t the style. It’s the speed. Under twenty-four hours — no, maybe actually shorter — finished to this degree. Too much. Really. Too much.”

Si-on did not take that. He was afraid that if he let “the real effective work time was about five hours” slip, the other side would next produce some associations hard to explain. For instance: this good, how were you that unknown before. That kind of question could not be answered. Could not say, because I was reborn. So he could only pull the subject back on track.

“Does anyone feel there’s a place that needs a change?”

Nine people looked at one another. Marketing director first.

“Creative.”

Creative head shook.

“Market.”

Also no. Mitchell spread a hand. Nobody put up a change. Nine people. Zero notes. Opinions collected, the marketing director asked back.

“Then Mr. Baek. Any ask on your side.”

Si-on thought, and put his core ask out straight.

“Yes.”

“I hope this ad can be made as a complete version around four minutes.”

“Four minutes?”

Creative head first.

“You mean… not the usual TVC cuts of thirty, fifteen, six, but an extra full narrative?”

“Yes.”

“You want to use it directly as an MV?”

Si-on did not go around.

“Yes.”

This was actually double-win. For Hyundai they already had to drop a whole set of launch materials. If the ad itself could have MV-level finish and spread, the content value would only be higher. For Si-on, the same. Hyundai borrowing his heat and song to push a new car. He borrowing Hyundai’s shoot budget, drop resource, and brand volume to push this song. Borrowing each other’s weather. Nobody short.

The marketing director had clearly also seen that logic in an instant.

“From brand spread, this proposal is very attractive.”

He looked at the people beside him, then back at Si-on.

“If we do a four-minute complete, we need to run budget and drop inside again, but I personally lean yes.”

Done, he put a hand out to Si-on.

“Mr. Baek. This collaboration may be more interesting than we first imagined.”

Si-on took it.

“I think so too.”
