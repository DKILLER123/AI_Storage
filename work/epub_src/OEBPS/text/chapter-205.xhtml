April 1

8:10 a.m.

Seoul — a phone, a bathroom mirror, and a file named 2002_demo_Ed_v1

When Baek Si-on woke up, he picked up his phone and checked the time first.

Eight-ten in the morning.

Then the messages.

Ed Sheeran.

Sent from London in the small hours, which put it at early morning in Seoul.

Message · Ed Sheeran

Ed Sheeran

Dug through half a trash can and found a song I wrote last summer. Melody’s decent. The words probably need work. See if you can bring it back to life.

Attachment: 2002_demo_Ed_v1.mp3.

He carried the phone into the bathroom.

Toothpaste, toothbrush, play.

♪

2002_demo_Ed_v1

Ed Sheeran · demo v1 · unmastered · rescued from a London trash can · streams over a running faucet

The intro streamed out of the speaker.

Strummed acoustic guitar, a brisk rhythm, a bit of folk-style bounce.

Very Ed.

His sound, identifiable from the first bar.

The lyrics were in English.

Baek Si-on listened while brushing his teeth.

The demo, as heard over the toothbrush

The chorus strings together a handful of turn-of-the-millennium Western pop hits: Britney’s ‘Oops!... I Did It Again’, *NSYNC’s ‘Bye Bye Bye’, Jay-Z’s ‘99 Problems’…

A nostalgia run built out of song titles from the turn of the century — the tenderness of “we grew up in that era.”

Melody: no problem. Structure: no problem.

❝

The quality of the man’s discarded drafts remains such that his trash can is worth more than most people’s studios.

— Ed Sheeran’s discard pile, appraised mid-toothbrush

The problem: if Goo Hara sang it as written, she would die.

Not a metaphor.

A vocal-technique death.

The vocal verdict, rendered clinically

Ed’s original arrangement leans British folk-rock — heavy acoustic strum, full drums, a track that needs a voice with some thickness to carry it.

Hara’s voice is sweet, light, built to drift on a summer sea breeze. Putting her under an arrangement this heavy is like sending a small sailboat across the Atlantic shipping lanes.

Conclusion: the arrangement must be rebuilt. Cut the heavy drums and the strumming; replace with the Japanese-Korean retro-synth aesthetic, a light drum machine, dream-palette tones. Move the whole song from “an English country-pub boy with a guitar” to “the kind of song that plays outside a convenience store in Shibuya at night.”

Baek Si-on spat out the toothpaste foam and rinsed.

He looked up at the mirror.

His gaze dropped, entirely without permission, to his neck.

Above the collarbone: a small red mark, clearly visible.

He stared at it for two seconds.

Clicked his tongue.

The turtleneck decision

Option one — gray crewneck sweatshirt. Collar clearance: insufficient. Returned to the closet.

Option two — black fine-gauge turtleneck. Pulled on, collar adjusted, mark confirmed: fully covered.

Conditions on the ground: Seoul, April, daytime temperatures of seventeen to twenty degrees. A turtleneck runs warm. Warmer than being asked “what is that on your neck.”

He picked the phone back up and called Goo Hara.

Baek Si-on ↔ Goo Hara · voice · the listening-session summons

“Hara, free?”

“Yep — what’s up?”

“Come to Jung Jae-joon’s new studio. Listening session.”

“New studio? He moved?”

“Moved.”

“Wow. Jae-joon oppa finally escaped poverty?”

“Mm. Sending you the location.”

“Okay~”

He hung up.

One last check in the mirror.

The turtleneck covered every trace perfectly.

Good.

Today was April Fool’s Day.

But the thing on his neck was no joke.

Late morning

Jung Jae-joon’s new studio occupied the seventh floor of an office building in Nonhyeon-dong.

When Baek Si-on pushed the door open, his first reaction was: clean.

The new studio, as smelled

Freshly painted walls, newly laid floors, console gear in perfect order — the monitor speakers set left and right like two soldiers standing guard. The booth glass polished to a shine; mic stands and pop filter, all new.

Even the air was different. Gone was the Hapjeong-dong signature blend — smoke, leftover takeout, sweat, and the warm-electronics exhaust of a synthesizer nobody turned off last night.

The present atmosphere: a serious, functioning music studio. One that pays its building management fees on time.

Jung Jae-joon swiveled around behind the console.

He had changed more than the studio. Hair trimmed, a decently cut black shirt, and a man who looked visibly more alive.

Money really does different things to different people.

‘Way Back Home’ — he had given up the rights split, back then.

April 1

Early afternoon

Seongsu-dong · a street south of Seoul Forest

The afternoon sun in Seongsu-dong was lovely.

So lovely that nothing in sight betrayed the district’s future as Seoul’s most expensive retail strip.

Baek Si-on’s car parked on a street along the southern edge of Seoul Forest park.

Goo Hara got out and looked around.

On the left, an auto-body shop, shutter half open, welding sounds from inside. On the right, a row of old red-brick factory buildings, the faded signage of a shoe brand from decades ago still on the wall. Across the street, a small café that hadn’t been open long — two iron chairs out front, one young person typing on a laptop.

Other than that, very few pedestrians.

After one full circuit, Goo Hara’s expression wavered.

“You’re sure this kind of place appreciates?”

Baek Si-on started walking.

“Look over there.”

He pointed to the end of the street.

Through the gap between two old buildings: the tree crowns of Seoul Forest.

Early-April tender green, pretty in the sunlight.

The block, as inspected

Position — five minutes on foot to Seoul Forest; across the bridge, Gangnam; under ten minutes’ walk to Seongsu Station on Line 2.

The asset — three connected red-brick factory buildings. Ceiling heights 4.5 to 5 meters; interiors opened up, they become natural studio floors and rehearsal halls. The brick façade stays: restore lightly and the industrial look is ready-made. The empty lot beside them: parking now, expansion later.

Goo Hara didn’t understand buildings. She understood the tone of his voice when he talked about them: “So S.W Studio’s headquarters moves here?” — “Mm.” One more glance at the auto-body shop. A welding spark flashed in the shutter gap.

The agent’s file, as rehearsed since yesterday

The agent — one Park Sang-min, fourteen years selling real estate in Seongsu-dong, arrived on a silver scooter. Fourteen years of inventory: aging shoe factories, dead print shops, warehouses nobody wanted. The occasional young walk-in with a café or studio dream, budget three to five hundred million won.

Yesterday — a woman in a business suit, a lawyer, viewed the three connected factories plus the lot, asked the total, left a card: “confirming tomorrow.” He hadn’t thought much of it. Viewers were many; buyers, few — and at the forty-two-billion-won level, even inquiries were rare.

Today — the lawyer called again: two o’clock, the buyer himself would come confirm in person. Park Sang-min arrived half an hour early, in his best jacket, shoes polished.

Two o’clock sharp: a black Hyundai Aslan stopped at the curb.

Park Sang-min decided this must be the client and went to meet him.

“Hello — the viewing appointment?”

The turtleneck man nodded.

Baek Si-on’s hands rose on instinct, to push her away.

But when his fingers reached her shoulders, they paused for a beat.

That one instant of pause — Lee Ji-eun seized it.

She gave him no chance at a second reaction; biting down on the chocolate, she passed the melting sweetness across, a little at a time.

❝

— Lee Ji-eun, offhand, chocolate at the corner of her mouth

Baek Si-on looked into her eyes.

She said it so lightly — as if the outcome didn’t worry her at all, as if she needed no promise from him, as if leaving a mark on him already counted as winning.

And in fact that was exactly how it was.

Lee Ji-eun had never once seen herself as the party waiting to be chosen.

She was the party doing the choosing.

If one day Baek Si-on chose her — she won.

If he didn’t — she lost nothing.

Every advantage worth taking had already been taken.

Lee Ji-eun swung off his lap, pushed the door open, and looked back once before stepping down:

“Right — the lyrics will be delivered on schedule.”

“Come often.”

She didn’t wait for a reply. The door shut, and she clicked away toward the set on her high heels.

The sound of heels on concrete echoed through the underground garage, fading farther and farther.

A woman had just fed him a piece of chocolate mouth-to-mouth, said “no responsibility required,” and left.

❝

If all of this was an April Fool’s joke, then he was probably the most thoroughly fooled man of the year.

— Baek Si-on, eyes closed, chocolate he never asked for still dissolving
