Chapter One Hundred Sixty Three

Beautiful Enough to Freeze: Blue Dragon Night

December 17 · evening, into the night Red carpet → a dignified ceremony → an unscheduled delivery to Jamsil Seoul · the Blue Dragon Film Awards — then the rehearsal stadium A dress that costs more than some cars, an uncle who weaponizes brand exposure, an acceptance speech with exactly four lines on it, and one trophy carried across the city by the least official courier in Korea.

December 17. Blue Dragon Film Awards day.

The makeup artist finished the last touch of color on Choi Jin-ri’s lips.

In the mirror: a girl with clean skin and clear eyes, the season’s couture lending her that expensive-but-not-trying kind of polish — and somehow, instead of burying her, bringing her natural brightness up underneath it.

Her manager watched for two seconds and couldn’t help himself.

“You look really beautiful today.”

Choi Jin-ri pressed her lips into a small smile.

“Thank you.”

She looked down to check her phone.

No message from Baek Si-on.

She looked at the time. Put the phone back in her bag.

Outside, the Hyundai-sponsored ceremony car was already waiting at the salon door. Baek Jeong-hun sat inside, looking down at his phone. At the sound of the door he looked up. Choi Jin-ri bent in and sat.

“Good evening, director.”

Baek Jeong-hun studied her for two seconds.

“You look beautiful today.”

She smiled.

“Thank you, director.”

Seatbelt on. The car pulled out. She watched the street go by, hesitated, then asked:

“Where’s Si-on-oppa?”

“Rehearsal. Too busy today. He can’t come.”

Choi Jin-ri made a small acknowledging sound and said nothing else. She knew he was busy. Not coming was normal.

Normal was normal.

There was still a very small drop in the chest about it.

The drop did not survive the red carpet.

the statement gown · Blue Dragon red carpet December 17 · 6:40 p.m. · flashbulb-tested

The dress Current-season couture in black and white — a corseted bodice with sculpted contrast panels falling into a full skirt, the kind of dress that costs more than some cars and refuses to whisper about it. On a carpet notorious as a “fashion wasteland” of safe black gowns and boneless beige numbers, this one walked in and said the quiet part at volume: I am not the same as any of you, and I didn’t come to apologize for it.
The styling Clean skin, clear eyes, soft lip, small clutch, no weaponized jewelry. The makeup brief was, in effect, “expensive but harmless” — which, as the evening would prove, fooled absolutely no one.
The weather exposure Bare arms, December, Seoul. The carpet is short and the photographers are many; the bill for both arrives later, outside the venue, at a stadium staff entrance, with interest.

Expensive but harmless — a brief that fooled no one.

The screams outside the car door settled the fashion debate before the carpet even started: the crowd had picked its side.

At the Blue Dragon venue, the media pit had been packed for an hour. Fans were held along both sides with banners raised, and the flashes began the moment a car door opened.

Baek Jeong-hun got out first. Then he paused, and waited, angled.

Choi Jin-ri stepped out of the car.

The venue went briefly silent. Then the screaming stepped up a whole register.

the fan line · outside the barriers

“Sulli!”

“Choi Jin-ri!”

“Here! Over here!”

“Jin-ri-yah!”

The shutter barrage from the press zone came down like rain on a tin roof. Baek Jeong-hun stood beside her, listened to that wall of sound, and found the whole thing genuinely interesting. When ‘Green Bottle Fly’ started shooting, the industry’s entire vocabulary for her had been “an idol trying film.” Now she stood on the Blue Dragon carpet as a Best Actress nominee. The nod was encouragement-weighted — but a nomination is a nomination. Some doors, once you have been through them once, are much harder to throw you back out of.

They stopped at the signing wall. Choi Jin-ri gave the cameras their turns, profiles, over-shoulder, hand raised. Baek Jeong-hun kept his interview answers at his usual export volume.

Asked whether he had expectations for tonight’s prizes:

“A nomination is already good.”

Asked how it felt, first Blue Dragon Best Actress nomination:

Choi Jin-ri held the microphone with a steady voice.

“Very nervous, and very thankful.”

“Being nominated alongside so many incredible sunbaes is already a huge encouragement to me.”

Standard answer. Delivered sincerely. She bowed slightly to the cameras and went in.

The heating hit the moment they crossed into the hall — and the cold wind and the screaming were shut outside with it.

Choi Jin-ri had assumed that if the room didn’t exactly roll out warmth for her, it would at least manage basic politeness.

The first thing she actually felt in there was cold — a different kind.

A few actresses watched her pass, let their eyes slide across the dress, and turned away a few degrees too deliberately. A seated veteran actress looked her outfit over, then leaned and murmured something to her neighbor. The neighbor smiled.

Choi Jin-ri couldn’t hear the words.

She didn’t need to. The temperature was aimed at her.

Baek Jeong-hun glanced at her and kept his voice low.

“Don’t look.”

“Director — did I do something wrong?”

“No.”

A pause. Then she looked down at her own dress again.

“Is it too… much?”

Baek Jeong-hun nearly laughed. This girl could be razor-sharp about a scene and then walk face-first into the obvious.

“What do you think?”

The red carpet had always been a beauty contest with a dress code. For film awards, doubly so — the actresses might not all win trophies, but nobody was permitted to lose the carpet. Korean ceremonies had spent years getting roasted as a fashion wasteland: gowns in black, in white, or in whatever shade of safe you couldn’t identify a designer on. And today’s outfit had walked straight into the spotlight and announced to the entire room: I am not the same as you.

The jealousy was guaranteed. It came with the tailoring.

Walking her toward the seating, Baek Jeong-hun kept it quiet.

“Don’t take it to heart.”

“They don’t hate you.”

“They hate that they worked at this for years, and tonight half the cameras are going to a pretty kid in her early twenties.”

Choi Jin-ri pressed her lips together.

“But I might not even win.”

“The award is the award. The cameras are the cameras.” Baek Jeong-hun said. “Some of them will win, and get fewer next-day reposts than your carpet photos. Tell me that doesn’t sting.”

Choi Jin-ri thought it through.

It did, honestly, sound stinging.

She suddenly wanted to laugh — and caught it, because laughing at a funeral-quiet hall felt rude.

Baek Jeong-hun saw the expression and shook his head.

“Laugh if you want to laugh.”

“This isn’t the ‘Green Bottle Fly’ set.”

“I’m scared of being hated more,” she said, small.

“They hate you anyway. You may as well be beautiful while it happens.”

Choi Jin-ri blinked. Then she actually laughed.

So the director — who sounded like he sworn at the world for a living — turned out to be, occasionally, good at comfort too.

They sat in the Green Bottle Fly block. He added one more line, quiet:

“Tonight, remember one thing.”

“What thing?”

“You are not here to be liked by them.”

She looked at him. He settled back against the chair, eyes running across the industry seated ahead of them.

“You are here to eat at the table.”

Choi Jin-ri looked down at the couture on her own body — and the dress abruptly stopped feeling conspicuous. At minimum, she was wearing it with a clear conscience.

…

The ceremony began.

Unlike the Baeksang — pop idols performing, comedians working the room — the Blue Dragons ran on pure cinema atmosphere. Which is a polite way of saying: slower.

The host was dignified. The presenters were dignified. The winners were dignified. When the cameras cut to the audience, even the applause was dignified.

Nothing wrong with any of it — unless you were a first-time Best Actress nominee seated in that audience, in which case time moved like syrup.

On stage, one winner’s eyes went red mid-sentence. Another choked up completely and couldn’t finish. Choi Jin-ri watched them — actresses she knew, actresses she didn’t — dissolving one by one in the spotlight.

Her own thoughts, meanwhile, had drifted clean off. To Jamsil Olympic Main Stadium. And to the oversized down coat hanging in her closet, still officially unreturned.

The awards marched on without her soul in the room: New Actor. Supporting Actor. Popularity Award. Cinematography. Music. Art Direction. Editing.

Every award mattered.

Every award was also fully capable of carrying off some section of the audience’s soul while it did.

Then, near the end —

“The winner of Best Director at the 35th Blue Dragon Film Awards is…”

The presenter looked into the envelope.

“‘Green Bottle Fly’ — director Baek Jeong-hun. Congratulations.”

Choi Jin-ri snapped back to the planet. She shot up, applauding hard with both hands — while Baek Jeong-hun, looking like a man who had expected this since breakfast, rose slowly and straightened his suit jacket.

“Director — congratulations.”

He looked at her.

“Back with us now?”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

She clapped even harder.

the Best Director speech · verbatim, all four sentences of it

Baek Jeong-hun took the trophy to the microphone and declined, as ever, to decorate.

“Thank you, Blue Dragon.”

“Thank you to every actor and every crew member of ‘Green Bottle Fly.’”

“This was a very small film. Being allowed to stand here tonight means small films can also be seen.”

“Thank you to Baek Si-on, and to Choi Jin-ri. The two of them gave this film a bigger life than the one I had imagined for it.”

Off stage, he patted the shoulder of the girl who had stood up to receive him, and sat back down.

Choi Jin-ri sat back down too. Her heart was going faster than the applause warranted.

Because she knew what was next: Best Actress, minutes away.

She knew she was running a victory lap for other people tonight. She knew the nomination was mostly encouragement. But when your own name comes up on a big screen, in a list next to genuinely legendary women — nobody on this earth is entirely without hope. That is simply how hearts are built.

the big screen · Best Actress · the nominee package

KIM HEE-AE

JEON DO-YEON

SON YE-JIN

CHUN WOO-HEE

CHOI JIN-RI

Applause rolled in for each name in turn, as protocol demanded and the room genuinely meant.

The presenter opened the envelope. A beat. Then:

“The Best Actress of the 35th Blue Dragon Film Awards is…”

“‘Han Gong-ju’ — Chun Woo-hee. Congratulations.”

One instant of total silence.

Because this was an upset in the truest sense of the word. Who would have bet that Jeon Do-yeon — Cannes’ Best Actress — and Son Ye-jin — a grand-slam winner of Korea — would both lose to an 85-line actress carrying a lead film role for the first time?

Whatever the arithmetic — and there was encouragement for indie cinema in it, and Blue Dragon’s ambition to show off its eye — the moment itself was beautiful.

Choi Jin-ri didn’t hesitate. She stood and applauded the winner, properly, with weight.

Because she now knew she was not the only young actor at this table tonight — and Chun Woo-hee’s win proved the table was not governed by seniority alone. That made her a little envious, and genuinely glad, in about equal measure.

Chun Woo-hee reached the microphone already crying, voice shaking.

“Thank you…”

“Jin-ri-yah.”

Baek Jeong-hun, suddenly, beside her.

Choi Jin-ri turned, instantly defensive:

“Director — I’m not disappointed. Really, I’m not.”

“I didn’t ask that.”

“…Huh?”

“I mean — in a moment, if Si-on takes Best Actor. Do you want to go up and accept it for him?”

Choi Jin-ri froze solid.

“Me?”

“You.”

He set his own Best Director trophy aside.

“I’ve already been up once tonight.”

He looked her outfit over, and added:

“Besides — your couture hasn’t given the brand its full camera time yet.”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

She looked down at her own dress. When a director stoops to product placement as persuasion, the argument is effectively over.

And Si-on wasn’t here. In the Green Bottle Fly delegation, after Baek Jeong-hun, the correct person to receive for him was obviously her.

Only —

she realized she was nervous. More nervous than she had been for her own category, minutes ago.

Baek Jeong-hun, prepared since God knew when, produced a folded piece of paper from his inside pocket and handed it over.

“What’s this?”

She took it.

“The acceptance speech.”

She unfolded it slowly. The page was, frankly, insultingly short.

the acceptance speech · as authored by Baek Si-on · four lines, total

Thank you to the jury.

Thank you to the ‘Green Bottle Fly’ crew.

Thank you to the audience.

I will keep working hard.

Length: writable on a sticky note, with room left over for a grocery list.

“…”

Choi Jin-ri looked up at Baek Jeong-hun.

“This is it?”

He nodded.

“What were you expecting him to write?”

She thought of the man himself — how saying two consecutive sentences visibly drew down his battery.

Fair. Expectations adjusted to factory settings.

“If you think it’s too short,” Baek Jeong-hun said, “you may add to it.”

Choi Jin-ri held the paper and smiled despite herself.

“Meaning I can say a couple of lines on his behalf?”

“Just don’t confess to anything.”

Her ears went hot.

“Director!”

He leaned back and became selectively deaf.

…

Chun Woo-hee’s speech ran long, breaking and restarting, and the breaks made it more moving than anything rehearsed could have been. The applause held and held.

Then the stage lighting reconfigured, and a new set of presenters walked out.

“Next, we present the Best Actor of the 35th Blue Dragon Film Awards.”

The big screen lit — a montage of the five nominees’ finest screen moments. When it ended, the screen split into five tiles showing each nominee live, wherever they were.

Baek Si-on’s tile: a photograph. Song Kang-ho’s tile: also a photograph.

The Blue Dragon Best Actor field had absented itself in the proudest tradition of the Korean film industry. Not arrogance.

Everyone was just at work.

The envelope opened. The pause. Then the whole hall heard it:

“The Best Actor of the 35th Blue Dragon Film Awards is…”

“‘Green Bottle Fly’ — Baek Si-on. Congratulations!”

Applause arrived instantly, and this time there was no upset in it, and very little argument. The Volpi Cup stood first in evidence, Green Bottle Fly’s indie box-office record second; the year’s film ledger had been pressed flat on the table for everyone to read. Blue Dragon could not route around it.

Baek Jeong-hun tipped his chin at Choi Jin-ri. Meaning: go.

She breathed in deep, stood, and walked toward the stage holding four sentences on a piece of paper, cameras tracking her the whole way — the couture immaculate under the lights, the soft makeup taking every edge off her.

the stage · one trophy, four borrowed lines, and then some

On stage she bowed with one hand over her chest, and received the trophy on Baek Si-on’s behalf from the presenter.

“Hello, everyone. Actor Baek Si-on is preparing for his concert tomorrow and couldn’t attend tonight — we ask for your understanding.”

She looked down at the paper. The four lines were still four lines. She read them out, faithfully.

“Thank you to the Blue Dragon jury for this recognition.”

“Thank you to the entire crew of ‘Green Bottle Fly.’”

“And thank you to every audience member who supports independent film.”

There she stopped.

By the original text, the next line was I will keep working hard — a speech you could transfer onto a sticky note in one motion. And Choi Jin-ri suddenly felt that with Baek Si-on absent, some things ought to be said on his behalf.

And, it turned out, on her own.

“Special thanks to director Baek Jeong-hun.”

“Though when he yells at people on set, it’s genuinely hard to believe he’s my real uncle.”

The hall went still for a beat — then the laughter broke and rolled.

“But without him, this film never starts, and never gets here.”

“‘Green Bottle Fly’ is a very small film. When we shot it, we never imagined it would go to Venice — never imagined this many people would see it.”

Her grip on the trophy tightened a little.

“And finally…”

The laughter died down. Most of the room braced for the arts-and-crafts portion of the program — cinema, performance, the meaning of it all.

Choi Jin-ri looked out at all those waiting eyes, and jumped off the cliff.

“Thank you to actress Choi Jin-ri.”

Plenty of people in the audience visibly stalled. Baek Jeong-hun: “……”

Her ears were burning. She kept going anyway.

“Thank her for finishing this film with me.”

“Without her, I couldn’t have taken this award alone.”

“So half of this award is hers.”

She bowed, deep, and held it.

The applause came down warmer than she had budgeted for.

Back at the table, trophy in her arms, Baek Jeong-hun leaned over, voice low.

“That second half was your own acceptance speech, wasn’t it.”

“…Yes.”

He looked at her for a moment, thinking something through — and let it go without another word.

…

The grand prize, Best Film, went to The Attorney. Song Kang-ho wasn’t in the hall, and still managed maximum presence by absence.

The ceremony ended. After it: the industry party — press, producers, actors, directors, money, all funneled to one room where nobody eats enough but everybody trades cards, glances, and the kind of pleasantries that curdle into projects by spring.

Choi Jin-ri had been on the list.

Baek Jeong-hun struck her from it personally.

“She’s tired. She’s going home.”

The manager looked at Choi Jin-ri; Choi Jin-ri nodded fast.

“Thank you, director.”

“Don’t thank me. Just don’t go get yourself cried at by somebody’s little dig at a party.”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

Outside the hall, the December wind hit her bare arms like a slap — and she looked down and discovered she was still hugging Baek Si-on’s Best Actor trophy.

She stood on the steps and thought for a moment. Then she turned to her manager.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“Let’s go by Jamsil Stadium.”

“Now?”

“Yes — now.”

The manager looked at the time, then at the trophy in her arms.

“He’ll be in rehearsal.”

“That’s exactly why now.”

He opened his mouth. Closed it. Nodded.

“…Fine.”

The car left the venue and ran toward Jamsil, Seoul’s night sliding past the windows — neon, convenience stores, people heading home from overtime. Choi Jin-ri set the trophy on her knees and watched the city. What would she even say when she saw him? Just hand over the trophy? Report that his uncle took Best Director, that she lost but gave a speech anyway? The cold eyes at the ceremony? The —

The stadium’s outline rose up ahead of the windshield, brighter than every building near it. Singing drifted from somewhere inside, faint but unmistakable. His voice.

“Here,” the manager said.

Choi Jin-ri looked at the floodlit stadium, then at the trophy on her knees. She breathed in, pushed the door open —

and the wind arrived at once, with feeling.

She had no coat.

The gown was beautiful. That was real. The cold was also real. And December-night wind off Jamsil Stadium went, with great precision, for the two arms a couture gown leaves bare.

She had actually turned back toward the car for her coat when her fingers touched the door handle — and stopped.

She thought of the down coat currently living in her closet, property of Baek Si-on.

She put her hand down.

No. She had come this far.

Hugging the trophy, she set off for the staff entrance, arms bare. The manager followed, watched her for a moment, and sighed at the state of youth.

The romance hadn’t even started yet, and the cold-resistance module was already fully deployed.

the statement gown, second life · Jamsil staff entrance December 17 · near 11 p.m. · couture × down × gold

The gown, hour nine Still immaculate. The corseted bodice that out-shouted a fashion wasteland now doing quiet duty at a stadium rehearsal, hems gathering December off the pavement. Couture, it turns out, is location-agnostic.
The coat His long black down coat, draped cape-style over bare arms, sleeves hanging empty past her hands — worn the way you wear something that is about warmth, but not only about warmth. On the hanger it is outerwear. Over that gown it is an announcement.
The hardware One Blue Dragon Best Actor trophy, hugged in both arms against the black-and-white bodice. Handoff pending. The gold goes with everything; the gown knew what it was doing.

Couture did its job. The coat did the rest.

The night’s real red-carpet photograph happened where no carpet was — staff entrance, work lights, her breath in the air, and the most photographed silhouette of the awards season wearing somebody else’s coat on purpose.

…

Inside, rehearsal was still running. Stage lights at half, the huge screens lit, empty stands climbing tier on tier, sixty thousand seats already zoned and marked with no one sitting in them.

At center stage, Baek Si-on stood in the lights, singing ‘Way Down We Go’.

Baek Eun-ah spotted the beautifully-frozen Choi Jin-ri first.

“Eonni?”

Choi Jin-ri saw her coming over from the work area and waved, smiling.

“Eun-ah.”

Baek Eun-ah took in the gown and her eyebrows collapsed.

“Aren’t you cold?”

“Not cold.”

Baek Eun-ah did not believe a syllable. This was Jamsil Stadium, not a spring day on Jeju Island. She was already shrugging out of her own jacket to drape it over her — and Choi Jin-ri caught her hands and stopped her.

“I’m fine, really.”

She smiled at Baek Eun-ah, very gently.

“You take care of yourself instead. You’ve got a full day tomorrow.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

The sensation was less “touched” than “deeply puzzled.”

Right then, the singing on stage stopped. Baek Si-on pulled out an earpiece monitor and came over from the thrust stage — and the first thing he saw was Choi Jin-ri, two bare arms, one trophy.

“Aren’t you cold?”

“Freezing.”

Baek Eun-ah: “?”

No. Eonni. That is not what you said. That is not what you said at all.

“Cold, and dressed like that?”

Choi Jin-ri held the trophy out to him — righteous, with a small injustice folded in.

“I was in a hurry to bring you your trophy.”

Baek Si-on didn’t even look at it; he dumped the trophy straight into Baek Eun-ah’s arms. She grabbed it in a scramble. And the next second he had pulled off his own down coat and settled it over Choi Jin-ri’s shoulders.

She took hold of the lapels, looked down at it, then up at him.

“Aren’t you cold?”

“No.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

She had come to feel extremely surplus. Also extremely visible. Visible like a stadium floodlight.

She looked down at the thing in her arms.

Fine. At least she had a Blue Dragon Best Actor trophy for company.

“Come on. I’ll walk you to your car.”

Choi Jin-ri nodded. The two of them headed from the infield toward the exit — past lighting techs adjusting rigs, an audio engineer cycling the sub-bass over and over, a stage manager jogging through with a walkie. Somehow the stretch of corridor they walked bent open around them, the chaos politely making room.

She walked slowly in his coat. He didn’t rush her.

“I accepted for you today.”

“I know.”

“Do you know what I said?”

“No.”

She looked up at him.

“Aren’t you afraid I said something wild?”

“No.”

Choi Jin-ri paused a step.

“I thanked actress Choi Jin-ri.”

Baek Si-on’s stride stopped too.

“On my behalf?”

“Mm.”

Her voice got smaller.

“I said — thank her for finishing this film with me. Without her I couldn’t have taken this award alone. So half of it is hers.”

Finishing, she felt a bit exposed.

“Was that too much bragging?”

“No.”

“Really?”

“Really.”

Choi Jin-ri stood there, stopped. The cold on the drive over, the cold eyes on the carpet, the veteran actresses’ slow once-overs inside the hall, the composure she had held together with both hands in that dignified auditorium — all of it got pressed gently down by one flat word, and stayed down.

She looked down and pulled his coat tighter by the lapels.

“Then you have to thank me.”

“Thank you.”

“Too fast. No sincerity.”

“Thank——you.”

“……”

The two dragged-out syllables wedged in her throat. She wanted to laugh and swallowed it.

“Fine. This round barely counts as sincere.”

…

They came out of the staff entrance. The night wind met them head-on. The car waited a little way off, rear door already held open, the manager standing by it, eyes elsewhere on principle.

“I should go.”

“Wait.”

Choi Jin-ri stopped — one foot already into the car — and pulled it back out. He had extended a hand toward her. Her heartbeat, uninvited, picked up. She pressed her lips together and slowly offered her own hand.

Their fingertips were almost touching —

and Baek Si-on withdrew his hand, and pointed at the down coat she was wearing.

“The coat.”

“…Huh?”

“You’ll be warm in the car. I still have rehearsal.”

“……”

The small shy thing in her chest got run through, clean, no survivors.

“Is it appropriate to demand a girl’s clothes right now?”

“They are my clothes.”

Choi Jin-ri gathered the coat around herself and took half a step back.

“I’m not returning it.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“You’re collecting down coats now?”

“Yes.”

“……”

In the car, the manager stared conscientiously forward, a man impersonating furniture.

When he still hadn’t spoken, Choi Jin-ri finally relaxed. Before sitting down she looked back once.

“See you tomorrow.”

“See you tomorrow.”

The door shut. In the back seat she pulled the coat close around herself, lowered her head, smelled the collar — then immediately straightened up and gazed out the window like a person who had done nothing at all. The stadium lights fell away behind them.

Baek Si-on stood where the car had left him, watching the taillights turn the corner. A few seconds later he turned back toward the building.

Baek Eun-ah was waiting at the entrance, hugging a Best Actor trophy.

“Cousin.”

“Mm?”

“Your coat is gone again.”

He glanced at her.

“Have Under Armour send two extra tomorrow.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

Wonderful.

The correct way to use a sponsorship, apparently.
