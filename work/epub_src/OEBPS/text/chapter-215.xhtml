Chapter Two Hundred Fifteen

The Yellow-Hair Archaeological Site

Early May

Morning

A photography studio, Seoul · a beverage shoot with one member short

Irene had worn high heels today.

The decision had been made before leaving the house, after roughly a minute and a half of deep deliberation.

The heel decision, as defended

The advantages were numerous: added height, longer lines, an overall air of composure and poise.

But the greatest advantage of all: heels gave no one, under any circumstances, the opportunity to grab anything that should not be grabbed.

Every hazard, eliminated at the physical layer.

❧ ❧ ❧

The van parked outside the studio.

The five of them climbed out one after another. Irene led in her heels, stride steady, posture perfect. Joy trailed behind, glanced down at her leader’s shoes, and very wisely said nothing.

Inside, the studio was already working. Light rigs were being re-angled, the backdrop not yet fully fixed, several set hands crouched on the floor taping down markers.

Irene’s gaze swept the room on reflex.

No Baek Si-on.

He could be in the greenroom, though. Or not here yet. A senior sunbae, after all — arriving a little late was perfectly normal.

She was heading for the makeup room when two camera assistants clustered nearby fell into chat.

“Have you seen Naver? The hot search has exploded.”

“The Baek Si-on one?”

“Yeah, yeah — the TIME 100! The hundred most influential people in the world!”

“Aish — for real?”

“For real. Officially announced minutes ago!”

Irene’s steps slowed.

She knew what the TIME 100 meant.

It meant that today, in all probability, he would not be appearing in a photography studio to shoot a beverage commercial with five girl-group members.

Irene bit her lip.

Then she did something very unlike a leader. She walked over to the brand’s floor coordinator, and asked in a tone of magnificent casualness:

“Unnie — is Baek sunbae not coming today?”

The coordinator looked at her and shook her head.

“He flew to New York.”

“New York?”

“Is your phone not connected?”

“…It’s connected.”

“The Met Gala! He’s the only invitee from Korea this year.”

“…Oh.”

“His parts get shot separately when he’s back in the country. They’ll composite them in later.”

And then the coordinator pointed at the center of the studio.

“Today you’ll be shooting with that.”

Irene followed the pointing finger.

Dead center of the studio, in the brightest pool of light, stood a life-size cardboard standee of Baek Si-on.

Joy came up behind her, saw the standee, went briefly blank — and then delighted.

“So today we shoot with cardboard?”

“Mm.”

“And I actually did my hair for this.”

“…You did your hair for the commercial.”

“Of course I did.”

Joy said, with complete sincerity:

“The same way unnie wore high heels here, and it definitely wasn’t for anything else.”

Irene decided that today, she would no longer speak to Park Soo-young.

❧ ❧ ❧

In the makeup room, the shoot outfits were already hanging — every one of them paired with a fruit-shaped headpiece.

Joy picked up her kiwi, clapped it onto her head, and gave the mirror two spins.

“I was born to be a kiwi.”

“Yesterday you said you were a strawberry.” Seung-wan said from beside her.

“Did I?”

Irene fitted the purple-grape headpiece onto Yeri, and Joy’s eyes lit up as she pounced to squish her cheeks.

“So cute, Yeri!”

“Unnie, stop — my makeup’s going to ruin!”

Irene fixed her own watermelon in place and gave herself one look in the mirror.

Quite cute.

If you ignored her mood entirely.

❧ ❧ ❧

By the time the van was rolling toward the dorm, the cabin had gone quiet.

Irene sat in the back row, a U-neck pillow around her neck, the heels already kicked under the seat. The phone in her hand was dialed down to minimum brightness — maybe to avoid disturbing the person beside her, or maybe so that nobody could see what she was about to do.

She opened Naver.

The search bar.

Her thumb tapped a single character onto the virtual keyboard.

Baek.

Before she could type the next two, the autocomplete had finished her entire thought for her.

Naver · search suggestions · one letter in

1Baek Si-on named to the TIME 100

2Baek Si-on Met Gala, Korea’s only invitee

3Baek Si-on ‘See You Again’ takes Billboard Hot 100 No. 1 again

She had typed one letter. The world had typed the rest.

Irene looked at the column of blue suggestions, and her finger stopped for a second.

Then she opened the one at the top.

The page loaded. At the top sat a photograph: Baek Si-on in a black turtleneck, half his face lit, the other half sunk in shadow.

She scrolled down. Korean media had translated excerpts of TIME’s official citation, boxed in bold quotation marks in the middle of the article.

❝

His influence is not confined to music. From Best Actor at the Venice Film Festival to the commercial miracle of a world tour, from fashion covers to crossovers into sport — Baek Si-on is redefining the ceiling of the words “Asian superstar.” If it even has one.

— TIME, the official citation, excerpted and translated

Irene read the citation one character at a time.

Then she kept scrolling. The comments.

The Korean internet had gone completely feral.

netizen_01

A TIME 100 honoree! What kind of monster has our country gone and produced?!

netizen_02

Just seeing “Billboard triple crown” and “Venice Best Actor” sitting on one Korean’s résumé makes me feel like living in this era was worth it.

netizen_03

HE is the Republic of Korea’s real cultural president!!!

netizen_04

netizen_05

netizen_06

Irene stared at the comments for a while.

DSP.

A’ST1.

Flop.

Bottom of the bill.

The Baek Si-on she knew was the producer who stood in the recording studio and ground through their songs one line at a time. The man who had caught her when she almost went down — by the ankle, of all places.

The wrong place. But that wasn’t the point.

The point was that she genuinely could not picture what this man looked like while “flopping.”

Curiosity, once it surfaces, is very hard to push back under.

Irene left the article and typed fresh words into the search bar.

Naver · search

Baek Si-on A’ST1|

No suggestions this time. She typed every word herself.

A pile of related searches sprang up instantly. She opened the video with the plainest title.

the danmaku, before the picture even loaded

Archaeology team, assemble!!

the danmaku, row two

Welcome to the Billboard-triple-crown Dark History Museum.

the danmaku, row three

High-energy alert! Superstar dark history ahead!

the danmaku, row four

2009 DSP: this will never catch on. 2015, the entire world: ???

the danmaku, row five

Mr. Baek, if you were being held hostage at DSP back then, please blink twice.

Reading the flood of comments, Irene’s mouth-corner had already started climbing.

Music leaked from the phone speaker; she hurried the volume down to near zero. Then she switched the danmaku off.

The frame resolved into a boy group.

The stage lights were the cheap blue-violet only that era could produce.

Irene’s gaze swept the five of them, hunting for Baek Si-on.

And then she found him.

One position left of center.

Beneath hair bleached a deeply period-typical, deeply nonconformist yellow.

Thinner than now. Eyeliner laid on thick. A last layer of baby fat still clinging to his face.

Irene’s first reaction was verification. She paused the video, zoomed in, and studied the face.

It was him.

Definitely him.

Her second reaction was —

“Pfft.”

Laughter leaked out between her fingers.

She didn’t know why she found it this funny. He wasn’t ugly. Looked at carefully, he was even cute.

But the man who had made the TIME 100, who was at the Met Gala right now, who could stand under the brightest spotlight on Earth — that man had once worn this yellow hair and seriously performed idol choreography like this on the SBS promo stage…

It was, genuinely, hilarious.

Irene even felt that every scrap of today’s disappointment had, in this one moment, been cured by that yellow-haired head.

She let the video play on. The music started up. Watching the yellow-haired Baek Si-on on the screen, tucked into her U-pillow, she secretly mimed two counts of the choreography.

And mid-mime, an idea surfaced in her head.

The scenario: next time they met, she would perform this 2009 yellow-hair-era promo dance, note for note, in front of Baek Si-on himself.

“What would his face do?”

“Silence?”

“A frown?”

“Or would he be so furious he’d throw her out on the spot?”

The more she imagined it, the more magnificent the scene became.

Magnificent to the point that the bra-strap-flick debt from the recording studio was suddenly — suddenly — negotiable.

“Ahaha——”

Joy shoved her eye mask up onto her forehead and scanned left and right in alarm.

“What happened, what happened?”

Seung-wan’s eyes opened too; she looked back over her shoulder.

“Unnie?”

Irene’s laughter completed an emergency braking maneuver in three-tenths of a second, her facial muscles rebuilding to standard at the fastest speed of her life.

“Nothing.”

Her voice was so level the laugh might have come from Taeyeon.

“I watched a funny video.”

“Sleep.”

The four of them stared at her for a few seconds as if at an alien, then one by one subsided.

Only when the cabin was quiet again did Irene ease her gaze back to the phone.

She was about to save the video for keeps when a push notification slid down from the top of the screen —

Irene’s finger froze.

In the bottom half of the screen, paused: 2009 — the yellow hair, the thick eyeliner, the boy-group idol Baek Si-on.

In the banner across the top: 2015 — the global superstar Baek Si-on, in the flashbulbs, on the steps of the Metropolitan Museum of Art.

The screen, as it held both 2009 and 2015

The push banner, in full: LIVE · Met Gala red carpet direct — Baek Si-on and Justin Bieber make their entrance.

Same person. Same screen. Six years between the two halves of it.

She tapped.
