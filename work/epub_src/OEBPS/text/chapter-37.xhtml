Chapter Thirty-Seven

The Song That Fell Off, and the Wind It Decided to Wait For

July Morning Hapjeong-dong, Jae-joon's studio A chart, a long fall, and a decision

By the second week of July, Way Back Home had run out of the small push the Uaena thread had given it.

Si-on knew the shape of it before he opened the app. He'd been doing the maths in his head since the day after the release, watching the line rise for one night, hold for another, and then — gently, the way a thread lets go of a bead — slide.

The song dropped out of the Melon top one hundred on a Tuesday.

✿ ✿ ✿

July Afternoon Hapjeong-dong, Jae-joon's studio Jae-joon, spinning a pen, refusing to panic

Jae-joon looked at the chart, then at Si-on, then back at the chart, and turned his chair around with the unhurried air of a man who has seen every song he'd ever made fall off something.

“It's a fine song.”

“It's not charting.”

“It fell off the chart. That's not the same thing. Charting is a weather report; the song is the climate. It's still good, it just —” Jae-joon waved a hand at the window, at the whole torpid afternoon beyond it “— isn't getting any rain.”

“So we wait for rain.”

“No. Rain comes to you if you stand somewhere it's raining.” He set the pen down. “You've been releasing this to one tiny corner of the internet, and you've been hoping the corner notices. The corner's asleep. So either you wake it, or you walk the song somewhere it's already awake.”

“Where's that?”

“Abroad.”

Jae-joon said the word without any of the dread the independent scene usually put into it. Not because he was optimistic — he'd spent too long in a room with speakers you can't turn off to be optimistic — but because he knew how the machine worked, and he knew the one lever an unknown song with an unknown singer still had.

July Night Hapjeong-dong, the laptop The route nobody had a name for yet

That night, from the sofa with a laptop balanced on his knees, Si-on started walking the song to a place it could be heard — not through a gatekeeper, not through a label, but straight into the ears of the people who decided what got loved first.

The route was a ladder. It went up like this:

Overseas route · no budget, no PR fee

01 — SoundCloud. The raw drop, where a song can live without an audience or a push. WIDE

02 — YouTube curators — MrSuicideSheep, Majestic Casual, The Vibe Guide; channels a stranger can submit to. WIDE

03 — Hype Machine. The aggregator that turns a handful of blog loves into a chart a whole world reads. WIDE

Payment: not cash. An ad-revenue swap — the channel keeps the money a video makes, the song keeps the ears.

Jae-joon listened to the whole plan without interrupting. Then he said the only thing that could have been said.

“That the whole thing hangs on a stranger pressing play, you understand that, right? Not the song. The stranger.”

“I know.”

“And there's a decent chance the stranger presses play, thinks ‘that's nice,’ and moves on.”

“Then the song waits for the next stranger.”

Jae-joon looked at him for a long moment, chewing his cigarette, and then, oddly, smiled — the first real smile Si-on had seen on him in weeks.

“You know, most people would've gone and borrowed money to buy a chart place by now. You're sitting there willing to wait for a gust of wind.”

“I've got time.”

“That's the rarest thing you've got, and you know it.”

Si-on didn't argue. He'd spent so many years being pushed, rushed, commanded — and here, in a small room full of equipment that never quite left him alone, waiting was the one thing nobody could tell him to hurry through.

He uploaded the song to SoundCloud that night. Private. Safe. Then he opened the window and let the city in.

The wind had not yet come. But he'd decided to stand where it would.
