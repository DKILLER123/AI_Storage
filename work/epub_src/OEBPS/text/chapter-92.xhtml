Chapter Ninety-Two

A Rumour Reversal, Self-Directed

September 12 11:20 pm Yongsan CGV · B2 car park A sixth sense, a badge that was not Dispatch, and a plan that grew a tail

The mall’s underground was more than half empty this late.

Fluorescent tubes made the grey concrete bone-white. Now and then a car came down the far ramp, tyres over the hump echoing in the hollow.

Baek Si-on walked ahead, keys turning in his hand.

Choi Jin-ri half a step behind on the right.

At the G2 corner her feet suddenly slowed.

She tipped her head, swept a look back and left.

Took it back fast.

Two steps quicker, up beside him.

“I think someone’s shooting us.”

He scanned once, fast.

No one.

Nothing that felt off.

He did not question her.

With her history of being stolen, her sense for a lens was more reliable than his visual sweep.

“Get in first.”

They picked up to the Carnival. He hit unlock. Both doors almost together. Shut. Locked.

He took out his phone, scrolled the contacts, found a name.

Lim Geun-ho.

Dialled.

Two rings.

Baek Si-on → Lim Geun-ho · Dispatch

“Yo— Best Actor Baek calling in person. Should I frame this Samsung?”

Baek Si-on did not play with him.

“Director Lim. Did you send people to shoot me?”

“You?”

“Kwon Ji-yong’s thing with a Japanese model is boiling. Everyone I can move is on that. What’s wrong?”

Baek Si-on weighed the tone. It did not sound like a lie.

“Then never mind. Bye.”

“Wait, don’t hang—”

Too late. The thumb had already gone down.

He took the phone from his ear and looked at the screen.

Not Dispatch.

Then who?

Other entertainment press? A freelance? A civilian who had recognised them and shot for fun?

From the passenger side, a very small voice.

“I’m sorry.”

“I shouldn’t have been wilful about the movie.”

His finger was on the screen, about to call Son Nam-won.

He heard that.

Turned his head.

Looked at her once.

Index finger vertical at his lips. A hush.

She closed her mouth.

He hit dial.

One ring.

Baek Si-on → Son Nam-won · Insight

“Boss Baek.”

“I’m with Choi Jin-ri in the B2 car park at Yongsan CGV. I think we were shot. I don’t know who, and I’m not sure we were. But Choi Jin-ri felt it.”

“What do you need?”

“Send Park Jae-won now to shoot us, and set the frame as ordinary socialising.”

“Understood.”

Son Nam-won answered, then held a second.

“Boss Baek. I have a thought.”

“Say it.”

“Why not use this to run a dating rumour?”

His pace picked up, as if he were afraid of being cut off.

“You and Choi Jin-ri are both on a traffic peak. Venice hasn’t cooled. Push a romance out, both heat-lines go to the top, and you get another round of press for The Green Fly’s domestic release—”

“No.”

The no came fast. He was not against a push. This was entertainment. More than half of most people’s exposure was stacked by topic-running. Dating rumours, feud rumours, airport coincidences, late dinners — like air. You did not have to like it. You still had to breathe. Pretending you did not need it was the real naivety. This time, no.

“The Green Fly is about violence passed down the bottom. The whole film’s ground is despair and old blood.”

“If we smash pink romance-foam on it now, do you want domestic audiences walking into the hall in a light-comedy mood?”

“A push that runs the other way only wrecks the film’s whole register.”

“And Choi Jin-ri is still an idol.”

“Her fans and film audiences are not the same people. What an idol dating means in Korea’s fan ecology — you know that better than I do.”

“You’re only adding the two heat-lines. You’re not multiplying the fans’ blood pressure.”

“…”

Son Nam-won ran that through once. The film’s tone would break. Her idol status would detonate. Fan backlash would not be steerable. Three lines, each fatal. He had only done the addition of traffic. Not the multiplication of risk.

“Sorry, Boss Baek. I didn’t think that far.”

The apology was clean. No defence, no patch, no “but I still think” tail. Wrong was wrong.

“It’s fine.”

Baek Si-on switched the phone to the other hand.

“Get Park Jae-won here fast. I’ll send the pin.”

“All right.”

“Hanging up.”

He sent Yongsan CGV B2 to Son Nam-won.

KakaoTalk · Son Nam-won

Baek Si-on

Yongsan CGV B2. Pin attached.

23:28

Son Nam-won

Got it.

23:28

He locked the phone into the tray under the wheel.

Choi Jin-ri watched the side of his face from the passenger seat.

She had not expected that a sixth sense — she was not even sure anyone had been shooting — would be enough for this man to handle every risk that might happen, in four minutes.

No questioning her judgment. No “you’re overthinking.” No pause.

She said someone was shooting. He treated it as someone shooting. Then he moved.

In the whole of growing up, no one had stood up in the first second of a thing and said, I’ll handle it.

Never.

She took her eyes off his face, to the passenger window.

The tubes made the concrete pillars white. A tail-light far off turned the bend and died. In the glass, her own face.

Her heart started to go faster, for no good reason.

Not fear of being shot.

That was already handled.

Something else.

A thing that had started in Venice, slow, layer on layer.

She could not say what it was.

Or she could.

And did not dare.

Once it was said, you could not go back.

She took her gaze off the blurred reflection and turned to him.

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

He glanced at her, then back, at a new message from Son Nam-won.

“About twenty minutes.”

“In a bit we walk the lift side again. Walk normally. Don’t keep a distance on purpose. Don’t come close on purpose. Two friends out of a film, walking.”

“…Oh. All right.”

September 12 the same hour Gangnam · Nonhyeon-dong, a too-quiet residential street A Renault on its back, a badge on a forehead, and a logic that was perfect and wrong

Park Jae-won was lying in the driver’s seat of a silver-grey Renault, the seat dropped to the lowest, his back parallel with the cushion.

He had held the pose nearly three hours.

About sixty metres beyond the windscreen, the underground entrance of a high-end apartment block.

He was trying to stay awake.

A little more.

A little more would do.

The phone buzzed.

Son Nam-won.

He picked up.

Son Nam-won → Park Jae-won · Insight

“Sajangnim.”

“Yongsan CGV, B2. Go now.”

Park Jae-won sat up hard from the half-lie. His lumbar made an unhealthy click.

“Sajangnim, Kiko Mizuhara’s manager’s car is nearby. I might be one last step short!”

The voice on the other end changed.

“Do as I say. That’s Baek Si-on and Choi Sulli. Address on KakaoTalk. If you’re not there in twenty you’re done.”

Park Jae-won’s mouth hung. Baek Si-on. Choi Sulli. In about half a second his head finished a priority sort. Kwon Ji-yong and Kiko Mizuhara’s date shots — at least a dozen outlets in the country were sitting them. Even if he got them tonight, the chance of an exclusive was almost zero. Baek Si-on and Choi Sulli — if Son Nam-won was telling the truth, it meant only Insight in all of Seoul knew where they were. Exclusive. A hundred percent exclusive.

“…I’m on my way.”

He hung up, threw the long lens onto the passenger seat, did not even belt, twisted the key. The engine went. The Renault shot off the kerb, tyres screaming on the asphalt, tail-lights around the corner, gone.

Nonhyeon-dong’s street went quiet again.

Quiet for about five seconds.

In a few ordinary-looking private cars at the kerb, small movements.

In the nearest dark Sonata, a man in a baseball cap was hauled out of half-sleep by that tyre-scream.

He opened his eyes at the direction the Renault had vanished.

“Who left?”

In the passenger seat his partner was rubbing his eyes, yawning.

“Looks like the kid with the badge on his forehead.”

“Insight?”

“Mm.”

“What’s he running for?”

What’s he running for?

The question turned about three seconds in the baseball cap.

His job was entertainment paparazzo, and a paparazzo’s core skill was not photography. It was inference.

Last time, the KWHL donation exclusive — Insight took it.

The time before, the Best Actor arrivals flash — Insight beat every outlet on earth.

The time before that, the exact overseas sales figure for The Green Fly — Insight exclusive again.

A shop standing less than two months, four people counting the boss, three exclusives in a row, all tied to Baek Si-on.

What did that mean?

This shop had a line.

A very hard line.

That kid was sitting GD on the same street as them, took a call at midnight, and ran without a word.

The baseball cap slapped his partner’s thigh.

“GD’s definitely not here anymore!”

“Ah?”

“That kid definitely knows where GD went!”

His chain was this: Insight has a line → the line gave him a new place → Insight knows where GD went → follow the kid, find GD.

Logic tight.

Inference perfect.

Conclusion entirely wrong.

He did not know that.

“Itaewon? Cheongdam?”

The passenger had already belted.

“Don’t know. Follow first!”

The Sonata slid off the kerb after the Renault.

About ten seconds after it left, a black Kia K5 behind it lit its lamps. The driver took a radio, keyed it.

Sports Chosun · radio

“Sports Chosun’s car followed Insight. Do I go?”

Static, two bursts.

“Go.”

The K5 came out onto the same road.

Then a third.

A fourth.

One after another, like lanterns on a single string, lighting up along that quiet Nonhyeon street, peeling off one by one.

Five minutes later, the street under GD’s building was empty. Not one sitting car. A streetlamp on empty asphalt. A stray cat came out of a planter, took the moonlight, and crossed, in no hurry.

September 12 11:39 pm Yongsan CGV · B2 Four beams on a ramp, a shutter-storm, and a play that had to be rewritten

Park Jae-won arrived a minute inside Son Nam-won’s twenty.

He parked the Renault in G3, dropped the seat, lens on the lifts.

The two of them were walking, one then the other.

Baek Si-on even picked a path under a tube on purpose, light from above, cleaner composition.

Park Jae-won’s shutter went seven, eight frames. Angle good, light enough, white-balance later and it would work.

While he was on it, Baek Si-on’s feet slowed.

His gaze went past the exit ramp, to a picture that was not right.

A column of headlight from the entrance.

Then a second.

A third.

A fourth.

Less than five seconds apart, one after another down the ramp into B2.

Not coincidence.

Eleven-thirty at night in a mall underground, four cars did not come in like a convoy.

In the Sonata the baseball cap was on the wheel, eyes going over every corner. The passenger was still complaining.

“I told you your call was wrong. That Insight kid was probably just going home to sleep, and you had to say he knew where GD was. We followed all the way to Yongsan. Does this look like a place GD would come?”

“Shut up. Let me look…”

He dropped to idle, left to right across the empty bays.

Then his gaze hit a spot about thirty metres in front of the lift.

Under a tube.

Two people.

A man, black T-shirt, keys turning.

A woman in a cap, half a step right.

The baseball cap narrowed.

That man’s face.

On the three majors’ evening news, on Naver’s entertainment top, on that two-million-view Venice dinner video — he had seen it at least fifty times this month.

“Ssi-bal!”

His hand hit the wheel.

“That’s Baek Si-on!”

The complaining cut off. The partner leaned so far his forehead nearly touched the glass.

“The woman next to him…”

“Sulli?”

The cap’s pupils went a size.

“Shoot!”

He slapped the thigh.

“Shoot, now!”

“You teaching me?!”

Shutters went off like firecrackers.

Behind them the K5 and a Chevrolet had stopped. Black barrels of lenses came out of window gaps almost together.

Baek Si-on saw those lenses and knew the situation had left the rails.

The arranged frame — two friends walking normally — was void the second those press cars showed up together.

He did not keep walking the plan.

“Get in. Fast.”

“Mm.”

In half a second their pace went from walking to leaving. Unlock. Two beeps, very clear in the hollow. Doors almost together, shut almost together. Ignition. Reverse. Wheel. The Carnival out of the bay, onto the lane, up the other ramp.

In the mirror those cars’ lights stayed put. They did not follow.

Shots were enough. You did not chase. Material in hand, the rest was the desk.

He took the Carnival onto the surface road, left hand on the wheel, right into the tray for the phone.

One ring.

Baek Si-on → Son Nam-won · Insight

“Boss Baek.”

“Why so many outlets?”

A stretch of quiet on the other end.

“Little Park got his tail bitten.”

“What?”

“He was sitting GD in Nonhyeon. After my call he pulled out too fast. The others on the same street heard it, decided he had a new lead, and all followed him.”

Baek Si-on kept the right hand on the wheel, took the phone a little off his ear.

“Boss Baek?”

“Let me think.”

Son Nam-won went quiet. He knew Baek Si-on did not need advice. He needed quiet. The line stayed open. Neither end spoke.

The Carnival went north on Yongsan’s main road. Night Seoul did not have many cars. The lights changed on a regular beat. In the passenger seat Choi Jin-ri sat still. She did not know what the other end was saying. She could only see his face going light and dark under the lamps, eyes on the road. Another crossing. A convenience-store sign slid past, a stripe of blue-white on the cabin wall. Baek Si-on spoke.

“Do it your way. Run the piece as a rumour. After it’s up, I’ll name Insight on my own socials and demand a deletion and an apology.”

“You understand?”

Son Nam-won thought a few seconds, and caught up.

Insight first with a dating solid. Title explosive, pictures clean, wording bold. The net forwards. Search goes up.

Then —

Baek Si-on comes down himself.

Insight “forced” to kill the piece and apologise.

The other outlets look: even Insight, which always has his exclusives, got torn by his own hand. If we run, same end.

Forget it.

Kill the photos.

So a rumour-storm that might have run away would, inside an hour, be lit, steered, and put out.

Insight takes a wave of first-run traffic and the whole net’s attention.

Baek Si-on takes a wave of “he speaks, he does not fold” hard-man persona.

Choi Jin-ri’s idol image does not take a hair.

Everyone else’s photos become scrap.

A good play.

From writer to director to lead to the last beat — all one man.

Baek Si-on.
