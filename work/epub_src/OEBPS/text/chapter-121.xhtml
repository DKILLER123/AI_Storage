Chapter One Hundred Twenty One

Lee Ji-eun-ssi Is Very Pretty

Late October After ten in the evening Yeonnam-dong · the living-room sofa A first cut on the television, a towel round the neck, and a song that has been waiting for this room.

Baek Si-on came out of the shower, walked to the sofa, draped the towel around his neck, and sat down. Baek Eun-ah had already plugged the phone into the screen. The television was lit, holding on its black, ready-to-play screen.

“Start?”

“Mm.”

Eun-ah pressed play.

Black.

One second.

Two.

Then Baek Si-on’s own voice, lazy and low and warm, came out of the television speakers and slowly filled the whole room:

For all the times that you rained on my parade…

The picture brightened slowly out of the dark.

The first cut · Love Yourself · opening shot

Baek Si-on sits at the window of the apartment-style set, guitar flat across his knees. Daylight filters through the gauze curtain and paints a thin warm edge around his silhouette.

He is looking down. His fingers sweep the strings.

The camera follows his melancholy gaze — from the coffee cup on the sill, to the open old book on the desk, to the hollow left in the sofa cushion by someone who is no longer sitting in it.

It settles, at the end of the movement, on a single Polaroid clipped to the refrigerator door.

In the photograph, Lee Ji-eun is leaning in from behind Baek Si-on’s shoulder, half her face peeking out. Her eyes are curved. Her arm is raised high.

Frame · the ripple

The picture holds on her smile — one frame of stillness — and then, as if a finger had pushed at the centre of a pond, ripples spread outward from the middle of the frame.

Her smile is swallowed by neon.

The cool room turns into a violet-red nightclub.

Lee Ji-eun stands in the middle of the lights, a glass raised, wrist turning lazily. She has changed. The eyeliner is sharp. The lipstick is heavy. Under the hem of the dress her legs are very white, and the light slides down her calves while a circle of men raise their glasses and cheer her on. The beams from behind the DJ deck cut through the crowd. The whole frame is alcohol and desire and the heat of being watched.

Baek Eun-ah straightened a little without meaning to.

“Whoa…”

She had watched the nightclub sequence live on set. A live set and the finished cut were two completely different things. On set you only felt that the lights were blinding and the music too loud and the crew running back and forth. In the finished cut, Lee Ji-eun was genuinely, dangerously beautiful.

On screen she tilts her head back and drinks. For a moment the lens slides down the line of her throat.

The next second —

Cut back to the empty room.

Cut · the cold room

The light on the ceiling is cold white. Baek Si-on lies alone on the bed, one arm laid across his forehead.

Then the colour temperature of the picture suddenly warms.

The air beside the bed wavers, gently, the way water does.

Lee Ji-eun is there — lying on her side right next to him, a pair of almond eyes fixed straight on his face.

Baek Si-on turns his head slowly in the picture. Sees her.

And smiles.

The corners of his mouth go up far enough that Baek Eun-ah almost fails to recognise her own cousin. Hwang Soo-ah’s lens holds on that smile for two full seconds.

Two seconds. In the time-scale of a music video, two seconds is a long, long blank. It never feels long. Because that smile is worth two seconds of anybody’s time.

✦ ✦ ✦ the edit, on purpose

The pace quickens next. A light percussive tap layers itself over the guitar strum. The images start to fragment — short pieces, full of lived-in warmth.

Montage · kitchen, floor, sofa

Kitchen. At the stove, Lee Ji-eun comes up behind him, both arms around his waist, cheek pressed to his back. He is holding a fork; he lifts a piece of food off it and turns to feed her. She opens her mouth and takes it. Chews. Twice.

…

Living-room floor. Two people fighting over the remote control. He holds it above his head with one hand. She stretches on her toes, can’t reach, and pokes him in the side, crossly, with one finger.

Nothing. She pokes twice more. Still nothing.

Then, suddenly, both hands — and she starts tickling him.

His body finally flinches. Her eyes light up at once and she attacks in earnest. He dodges. She chases. They circle between the sofa and the coffee table until he is cornered against the sofa, her arms loop around his neck, his hand settles on her waist and draws her in.

They kiss. Naturally.

Baek Eun-ah hugged the cushion tighter. Her breathing caught, very slightly. She had watched that take on set — but in the finished cut, with the light, the edit and the music added on top of it, the damage was completely different.

She suddenly hoped Choi Jin-ri would never watch this video. A thing like this could give a person palpitations. Especially a person whose conscience was already hiding something.

The video went on. After the toothbrush shot, Lee Ji-eun in the mirror suddenly disappears. The colour temperature snaps from warm back to cold white. In the music, Baek Si-on’s voice is still gentle — but the gentleness on screen is already beginning to collapse.

The breakdown

He starts packing. Clothes. Books. The watch.

He takes the Polaroid off the refrigerator, looks at it for two seconds.

In the end, he puts it back.

The last shot

Baek Si-on sits on the windowsill, packed suitcase at his feet. The guitar is across his knees again. Outside the window lies the Seoul skyline, dusk pressing against the edge of the city, towers standing like a row of silent silhouettes.

He looks down and plays, and sings to himself.

Cause if you like the way you look that much…

Oh baby you should go and love yourself…

The sound of the door lock.

Click.

Very soft. But in a quiet room, it lands the way a stone lands in a lake. Baek Si-on’s fingers stop on the strings. He turns his head toward the door.

The camera does not follow his gaze. It goes outside the door instead.

A hand is closing around the handle.

Black.

The guitar’s ring lingers in the dark for two seconds.

Then it is gone, too.

Baek Eun-ah hugged the cushion, let out a breath after about five seconds, and turned her head.

“Oppa. How was it?”

“Not bad.”

“That’s it?”

Baek Si-on looked at the black television screen, thinking.

“The warm-and-cold grading carries the emotional split very well.”

Eun-ah: “…”.

Who was talking about colour grading? She meant the kiss! The kiss that would give Jin-ri unnie palpitations if she ever saw it! The kiss where Lee Ji-eun sunbae-nim’s arms looped around his neck and his hand sat on her waist! The kiss that Director Hwang had cut in the finished video to look like they had genuinely been a couple!

Baek Eun-ah screamed all of this inside her head. She did not have the nerve to say any of it aloud. She only hugged the cushion a little tighter and tried to look like a soulless media-player stand.

Baek Si-on, unbothered by the melodrama revolving inside her head, picked up the phone from the coffee table and dialled Hwang Soo-ah.

Late October Same night Yeonnam-dong → somewhere with a phone and an opinion The phone rings twice, and the first note of feedback is about a woman’s honour.

The phone rang twice. It connected.

Baek Si-on → Hwang Soo-ah · the first cut

“Director. Are you resting?”

“Not yet.”

Her voice did not sound like a woman preparing for sleep at all.

“Watched it?”

“Mm.”

“How is it?”

“Overall it’s very good. But there’s one problem.”

“Tell me.”

“The nightclub sequence with Lee Ji-eun. Isn’t it a little too sexy?”

Baek Si-on asked it very directly. He knew the moral demands the Korean entertainment world made of the title “Nation’s Little Sister.” Nightclub. Heavy makeup. Sharp eyeliner. Very white legs. A roomful of men watching her. Put together, the impact was enormous — enormous enough to set off a public backlash.

“It could affect her image.”

Baek Eun-ah: “…”.

She raised her head very slowly and looked at Baek Si-on.

No. Oppa. You watched this whole video and the biggest problem you found is that?

Hwang Soo-ah had evidently not expected Baek Si-on’s first note to land there, either. After two seconds she asked:

“Other than that, is there anything else?”

“No.”

Baek Si-on answered quickly. Hwang Soo-ah was a master of feeling. She had not buried skin-crawling metaphor or political landmines in the background, had not shown off, and had not turned a break-up song into a social-issues thesis. On the technical side he could not find fault.

Hwang Soo-ah laughed quietly on her end.

“Ji-eun’s problem isn’t a problem.”

“What do you mean?”

“She grew up.”

“Grew up?”

“Mm.”

Her voice slowed a little.

“The way a grown child announces herself to the world is by saying goodbye to the past. The title ‘Nation’s Little Sister’ — Ji-eun doesn’t want to carry that one for the rest of her life.”

Baek Si-on listened. Thought about it.

Understood.

That sentence — the grown child announcing herself by saying goodbye to the past — applied to Choi Jin-ri, too. Both of them had been fitted, at their youngest and most beautiful ages, with a perfect shell by Korea’s ruthless star-making machine. One was called “Nation’s Little Sister.” The other was called “the Peach.” And both of them had been squeezed breathless by their shells, both trying, in their own ways, to announce to the world that they had grown up. You could call it rebellion. You could call it self-awakening.

“Then I have no more notes.”

Baek Si-on finished. On the other end of the line there was silence for a few seconds — as if Hwang Soo-ah were confirming that he had really understood — before she laughed.

“Really no more notes?”

“Mm. The final payment will be delivered through the studio. Thank you for your work.”

“Good. It was a pleasure working with you.”

She sounded genuinely light.

“If you need anything in the future, call me any time.”

“All right.”

The call ended. Baek Si-on took the phone from his ear, and quietly gave the director a very high internal score. She knew how to hold an artist’s image boundary, a song’s emotion and an audience’s understanding in the same hand. That was rare. A song like Love Yourself — filmed shallow, it would be a pretty, boring break-up video. Filmed wrong, it would turn Lee Ji-eun into a cheap villain. Hwang Soo-ah had done neither. She had shot the heroine as vain, dangerous and watched — without stripping away her complexity. And she doubled, free of charge, as the artist’s psychologist.

Someone to work with for the long term.

Baek Si-on thought it over, then redialled Hwang Soo-ah. She picked up at once.

Baek Si-on → Hwang Soo-ah · minutes later

“What is it?”

“Director Hwang. You know ‘Way Back Home’?”

“Of course.”

Her voice carried a smile.

“Your first song that really took off.”

“Will you shoot the music video for it, too?”

There was a pause on the other end.

“Now?”

“No rush.”

Baek Si-on said it easily.

“I won’t appear in it. My schedule is tight. Shoot whatever you like.”

Hwang Soo-ah went quiet for two seconds.

“You won’t appear?”

“Mm.”

“Then do you have any requirements for the video?”

“No.”

“…”

She laughed.

“You’re a very low-maintenance client. If there’s no rush, I can have a treatment to you in about a week.”

“Thank you.”

“Fine.”

The phone clicked off again. Baek Eun-ah looked up at him.

“Oppa. You really do whatever pops into your head.”

“The song would just sit there otherwise.”

“And you really won’t be in it?”

“No time.”

Baek Eun-ah considered that. True enough. Her cousin’s schedule was no longer merely full — it was full the way a thing is full when someone has hammered it in with a mallet.

Baek Si-on stood up.

“Going to sleep.”

“Oh. Good night.”

Late October A little past ten Lee Ji-eun’s apartment · one floor lamp burning A face mask, a replayed video, and three words sent to the director.

In Lee Ji-eun’s apartment, past ten in the evening, the living room kept only one floor lamp burning. She lay on the sofa with a sheet mask on. On the low table sat half a glass of warm water that she had not finished, the remote control, a lip balm, a magazine open halfway through, and the first-cut link to the Love Yourself video that Hwang Soo-ah had just sent her.

She had just finished watching it.

When it ended, she very calmly sent Hwang Soo-ah three words.

KakaoTalk · to Director Hwang

Lee Ji-eun

Very good.

Then she turned the phone face-down on the sofa.

She remained calm for about twenty seconds. Then she flipped it back over, opened the video, dragged the progress bar back to the living-room-floor sequence, watched it once, and closed it.

Ten seconds passed.

She opened it again. Watched it again.

The third time, at the instant her own arm looped around Baek Si-on’s neck, she finally pressed the phone face-down on the sofa.

Enough. A work of art. A professional result. Completely normal. She was a professional artist. There was nothing about it worth watching over and over.

Lee Ji-eun closed her eyes and tried to let the mask serum soak in properly. The moment her eyes shut, the phone buzzed.

She opened one eye. Picked it up.

KakaoTalk.

Baek Si-on.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Si-on

Are you thinking of changing your image?

Lee Ji-eun stared at the out-of-nowhere question for two seconds. Her eyebrows climbed slowly.

What kind of opener was that? And what kind of tone was this? Like a teacher walking over between classes to ask: thinking of skipping school, are we?

She wanted to answer at once. Her fingers were already in the input box. Then she stopped.

No. She could not look eager. She pressed the phone down. Decision: let him wait five minutes.

One minute.

Two minutes.

Three. She glanced at the phone. No new message.

Four.

Five.

Good. Lee Ji-eun picked the phone back up and typed.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

How did you know?

Baek Si-on

Director Hwang told me.

Lee Ji-eun looked at those five words and typed back one.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Oh.

Something in her chest felt hard to name. So it was Hwang Soo-ah unnie who had said it. She had half-hoped he had seen it himself.

Baek Si-on ignored her chill. He went on typing, at his own pace:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Baek Si-on

Getting out from under “Nation’s Little Sister” can’t be done with talk, and it can’t be done with one music video where the nightclub scene is a little sexier. You need to play a role people don’t like, to break the filter completely.

Lee Ji-eun looked at the screen. Her fingertips hung in the air.

That landed exactly on the point. It was not the first time she had realised how heavy the title “Nation’s Little Sister” was. It had given her so much. Affection. Trust. Public favour. A nationwide sense of safety. But it was also an invisible glass bell jar, sealed over her. She could not be too sexy. Could not be sharp. Could not be too greedy. Could not, the way a real woman in her twenties can, want things, have flaws, lose her temper, have unlovable moments. Because the Nation’s Little Sister could not do those things. The Nation’s Little Sister was supposed to stay cute, gentle, well-behaved, harmless — frozen, ideally, at some perfectly convenient age.

She typed back.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

What role?

Baek Si-on

Writer Park Ji-eun has a new script. The second female lead has to be an idol — but a lot of idols won’t touch the part because the character is so unlikable. You should try out for it.

Park Ji-eun. Lee Ji-eun obviously knew the name. Queen of Housewives. My Love From the Star. One of the most talked-about writers in Korea right now. A script of hers would never go begging. If a role had made “a lot of idols afraid to take it,” then the problem was not the access. It was the role itself.

She typed:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

How unlikable?

Baek Si-on

Throws diva tantrums. Snaps at fans. Bad temper.

Lee Ji-eun: “…”.

She stared at those three phrases for a few seconds. No wonder other idols would not take it. What did an idol fear most? That an audience would sink too deep into the character and carry its flaws over onto the real person. Especially a setup like “snaps at fans.” Fans were an idol’s roots. If a character snapped at fans on screen and the acting landed too true, someone would cut it into a GIF and caption it: “So this is what she really thinks of her fans?” That was dangerous for an idol.

She typed:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

It does sound unlikable.

Baek Si-on

That role suits you.

Lee Ji-eun stared at the screen, unsure for a moment whether to thank him or swear at him.

Read generously — he thought she had the range to carry a part this hard. Read ungenerously — he thought she had something in common with a diva who threw tantrums, snapped at fans and had a bad temper. And knowing Baek Si-on’s way with words, he probably meant both at once.

She sat up slowly. The sheet mask, tugged by the change in her expression, lifted a little at the corner of her mouth. She pressed it back down with a finger. After a few seconds she asked:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Why?

Baek Si-on

So people can see you’re allowed to make mistakes. To be vain. To not be the porcelain doll the whole country keeps on a pedestal. And to be allowed to look bad.

Reading that, Lee Ji-eun was actually moved. Because it was understanding at a very high altitude — higher than the fans who chanted “Our Ji-eun is the best” every day, closer to what she most wanted and most feared right now. He understood her ambition. He also understood her cage.

Any of those sentences, read into a microphone at an arts interview, would have been enough to bring tears to someone’s eyes.

But.

A woman’s attention works in mysterious, unreasonable ways.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Who are you calling ugly?

On the other side, in Yeonnam-dong, Baek Si-on looked at the murderous retort on his screen and fell silent.

He thought about it for a long time before replying.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on

I meant the character.

Lee Ji-eun

If the character is ugly, isn’t that me?

Baek Si-on

No.

Lee Ji-eun

Then say it again properly.

Baek Si-on

You need to play a role that isn’t pretty in the audience’s eyes.

Lee Ji-eun

……

Lee Ji-eun

You’re still calling me ugly.

Baek Si-on

If that’s how you insist on reading it, I suppose I am.

Lee Ji-eun threw the sheet mask into the bin, wiped the excess serum off her face, and decided not to keep fighting him over the word “ugly.” If she kept at it, she was certain to lose. Because Baek Si-on had a gift: he could infuriate a person using the fewest possible characters.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

What’s the drama called?

Baek Si-on

Not officially announced yet. From what I hear it’s set in a broadcast station’s variety division, in mockumentary form.

Lee Ji-eun blinked. A variety division? A mockumentary? That was a fresh angle.

She asked:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

What’s the second female lead called?

Baek Si-on

Cindy.

Lee Ji-eun looked at the name. Cindy. Pretty. Polished. She was suddenly a little interested.

Baek Si-on thought about it. He could hardly say that he knew this drama would eventually be called The Producers, and that the role of Cindy would become one of the most important entries on her acting résumé. So he improvised — but she asked first.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

How do you know so much?

Baek Si-on

I ran into writer Park Ji-eun gathering material at KBS during a music-show week and we talked a little.

That was not a total invention. He really had been to KBS for music shows. And KBS’s corridors really did swarm with writers and PDs.

From the other side came a new message.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

So you know writer Park?

Baek Si-on: “…”.

He looked at the sentence and understood at once. The subtext was plain — if you know her, could you make an introduction?

By any fair accounting, Lee Ji-eun had helped him more times than he could count. For him to help her once in return was simple justice. The problem was that he did not know Park Ji-eun. Had never met her, not once. The “talked a little” had been invented two minutes ago.

But the words were already out. There was nothing to do but push through, head down.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on

I can introduce you.

The boast was made. Tomorrow he would find a way to make good on it.

The other side went silent for about ten seconds. Then a message arrived.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Why are you helping me?

After sending it, she set the phone on her knee. All at once she felt a little nervous.

On the other side, in Yeonnam-dong, Baek Si-on read the question with his fingertip hovering over the screen.

Why?

When he had first typed to her tonight, he had been thinking of Choi Jin-ri. Jin-ri had chosen the most violent way possible to announce to the world that she had grown up. And “Nation’s Little Sister” and “the Peach” were, at bottom, the same cage — different sizes, different materials, digging in at different places. But leave either of them on long enough and it squeezed the breath out of you. Baek Si-on did not want Lee Ji-eun walking that same road.

Because kind people deserve to be treated gently by the world. Even when the world, most of the time, is not gentle.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on

You deserve it.

When Lee Ji-eun read that, she blinked once.

She had prepared any number of replies. For example: “You talk like you’re the judge.” Or: “I haven’t even decided whether I’m taking it.” Or: “Don’t lecture me like a sunbae.” But after “You deserve it,” none of them would come out. Because it did not sound like Baek Si-on’s usual one-line-and-you’re-choked style. She suddenly did not know how to answer.

She thought, and typed:

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Got it.

Two seconds later she added one more line.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Though there is one thing I want to correct.

Baek Si-on

What?

Lee Ji-eun sat up a little straighter and typed very seriously.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

I am not ugly!

In Yeonnam-dong, Baek Si-on looked at that. Was silent for a second. Replied.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on

Mm.

Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on

Understood.

Lee Ji-eun

Wrong.

Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on stared at the screen for two seconds. Then he typed.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun-ssi is very pretty.

Lee Ji-eun looked at the line. Satisfied. Very satisfied. But she could not let it show.

She set the phone on her knee, waited a dignified three seconds, and only then answered slowly.

KakaoTalk · Lee Ji-eun

Lee Ji-eun

Mm.

Lee Ji-eun

That’s more like it.

After sending it, she finally let the corner of her mouth lift. It lifted halfway and she pulled it back down at once. No. She cleared her throat, even though nobody was in the living room. Then she glanced down at the chat window once more.

Baek Si-on had not replied again. But that line — “Lee Ji-eun-ssi is very pretty.” — was still sitting on the screen. Utterly plain. And the longer Lee Ji-eun looked at it, the more she felt it outshine a hundred fan comments chanting “our Ji-eun is the prettiest” all at once.
