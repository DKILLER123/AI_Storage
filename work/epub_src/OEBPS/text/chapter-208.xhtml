Chapter Two Hundred Eight

The Twenty-Millionth Follow

By the end of the meal, Lee Ji-eun’s mood was unmistakably excellent.

Baek Si-on could tell.

So once the bill was settled and the two of them came out of the booth, he declined to keep letting the conversation wander off into side streets.

“The meal’s bought. Shouldn’t the lyricist be delivering the goods?”

Lee Ji-eun blinked and lifted her shoulder strap up a little.

“You are such a pragmatist.”

“I came for the lyrics today, as it happens.”

“Oh.”

She dragged that “oh” out on a note of pure disbelief, and they walked out of the restaurant together.

April 2

Night

Yeouido · cherry blossoms mostly open · one trap, thoughtfully set

Seoul’s April nights still carried a bite of cool. The street-side cherry blossoms were well past half-open, and the lampposts threw the petals’ shadows onto the pavement — one by one, swaying gently.

Standing in the night air, Lee Ji-eun added, at sloth speed:

“But the lyrics aren’t on me.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“They’re at my place.”

And there she stopped, saying nothing further — waiting for him to ask.

Baek Si-on reached out and opened the rear door directly.

“Get in.”

Lee Ji-eun watched him from beside the door, the triumph in her eyes even more visible now.

“So you’re driving me home?”

“What, should I leave you to flag a cab on the curb?”

“I thought you cared more about the lyrics.”

“I am caring about them this very moment.”

She laughed and bent into the car.

Tonight’s mood had, clearly, already reached the level where “he’s driving me home” auto-translated into several other meanings.

As the car pulled out, Yeouido’s night had spread itself completely open.

The cabin was very quiet.

And quietness amplifies everything.

The scent, for instance. And the stretch of not-far, not-close distance between two people.

❧ ❧ ❧

By the time the car stopped below her building, it was nearly half past eleven.

“Here we are.”

“Mm.”

Lee Ji-eun made no move to get out, one hand still resting on the seatbelt — waiting for his next line.

Baek Si-on, sure enough, did not disappoint her.

“And the lyrics?”

This time she genuinely laughed out loud.

“Baek Si-on.”

“Mm.”

“You truly will not pretend. Even a little.”

“No need.”

“There is need.”

She unbuckled the seatbelt and turned to face him.

“Some of the time, pretending makes a person cuter.”

“I don’t eat based on cute.”

“What do you eat based on, then?”

“On you handing me those lyrics. Quickly.”

“The lyrics are in my home.”

Lee Ji-eun tipped her head; her eyes bent a little under the streetlight.

“Want to come up and get them?”

The car went quiet all at once.

Baek Si-on understood perfectly: if he actually followed her into that elevator, tonight was probably no longer about lyrics.

Did he want to?

That question had no discussion value.

Lately, Lee Ji-eun seemed dead set on mounting the four characters “I am dangerous” as a live exhibition in front of him. From the night in Milan, to the KBS set, to that fluid, rehearsed-looking flirtation across the dinner table just now — every step made him suspect she kept a private notebook of romantic field manuals.

Baek Si-on was not wood.

Still less was he blind.

The problem was that he was not standing in an emotional vacuum zone, either.

“Not coming up.”

He said it very short.

“Bring them down.”

She watched him. The laughter in her eyes stayed — but something else joined it.

“And if I said that right now, I really, really want you to come up?”

“I’d rather not put my professional discipline to the test tonight.”

“Meaning: you know perfectly well you might lose control if you come up — and you still rather want to. Am I right?”

“……”

Baek Si-on suddenly regretted his earlier sentence. He had handed her far too much ammunition.

While he was weighing whether to close the conversation while it was still closable, Lee Ji-eun’s hand came up and caught his collar.

He was tugged slightly down toward her.

She wasn’t using much force.

But the distance had already crossed into dangerous.

The last centimeter, as measured

Close enough that when her eyes lifted, he could see the light caught on her lashes.

Close enough that their breathing had begun to mix — one more inch forward, and neither of them would need to invent an excuse.

Close enough that Baek Si-on suddenly understood: all of his so-called calm and judgment, at one centimeter’s range, was not much more solid than cardboard. She didn’t kiss him. She didn’t advance. She simply held the position — the one where something almost happens — with the bright, satisfied glint of someone who has already won, and said it very softly: “Today, no kiss for you.”

Then she let go of his collar and pulled the door open.

Night wind flooded the cabin, thinning her scent, carrying off a share of the warmth that had been flirting with the line.

One foot already on the ground outside, Lee Ji-eun turned her head and gave Baek Si-on a last look.

“The chance to come upstairs was just offered.”

“Next time, remember to take it.”

“Good night, dear.”

The door shut.

Baek Si-on watched her walk toward the building entrance.

A few fallen cherry petals under the lamppost spun slowly where the wind pushed them, stopping and starting.

The access panel beeped once.

Then her figure was gone.

The driver glanced at him in the rearview mirror and did not dare say a word.

Baek Si-on closed his eyes.

He thought of the line Jung Jae-joon had thrown out that afternoon.

❝

“The excuses are multiplying. It’s a matter of time.”

— Jung Jae-joon, that afternoon; ruled nonsense then, under formal review now

The first week of April

Evening

The Red Velvet dorm · phones restored · one counter about to turn over

The week, as shipped

CJ delivered five BodyArmor trial flavors in one go — each sweetness tuned slightly differently, each bottle color re-graded by hand.

The ad song, finalized: Baek Si-on fused Charlie’s melody with Ji-eun’s rewritten words into something very light and very bright — a song that sounds like what spills out the moment a freezer door first swings open.

Red Velvet did not need to be told any of this item by item. What they knew was this: this week, ‘Ice Cream Cake’ swept every early-April music-show win there was — Music Bank to Music Core to Inkigayo — a run so smooth it felt like someone had paved the road in advance. Even the dorm air lightened, and the manager’s footsteps stopped sounding like debt collection.

That evening, the girls got back from the music show and had barely kicked their shoes off when the manager announced:

“Per the rules — you took a win, so the phones come back.”

Joy pounced first.

“Really?”

“No.”

He put the phone into her hands anyway.

“I bought five dummy phones specifically to mess with you.”

Joy ignored the sass completely. She cradled her phone like a family member lost for a decade and recovered, the smile on her face bright enough to light the kitchen directly.

“Finally — no more borrowing other people’s phones to check messages.”

The happiness in that sentence was so concrete that even Seung-wan laughed.

“If anyone should be borrowing, it’s Yeri.”

“Why me?”

“You’re the youngest. Joy did all the borrowing before you came.”

Yeri backed up a step, hugging her phone — her face full of the brand-new-maknae shock of a girl still adjusting to how dangerous dorm life could be.

Irene got her phone back too.

On the surface, she was the calmest of the five.

Only she herself knew that her heartbeat had gone off-script from the moment the manager pulled out the phones.

Because she remembered a certain contact method that had never been obtained.

“Don’t just play around.”

The manager clapped his hands over the cheering.

“The ad song for the drink you endorse is finished. The demo’s in the group chat — download it tonight and learn it cold. You’ll need it at the shoot.”

Joy’s smile stiffened for half a second.

“The company truly has a gift for strapping bombs to the tail of happiness.”

“Less commentary. Learn it properly.”

The manager, leaving, remembered one more thing and flipped through his phone gallery:

“Right — those group photos from before never got sent. They’re in the group chat now. Don’t repost them anywhere.”

He dropped the photos into the chat and packed up to leave.

Five phones buzzed at the same moment.

Joy opened hers first, gave it one scan, and went “wow.”

“This one came out so well.”

Seung-wan looked too: “Sunbae in the middle is so tall.”

Seul-gi nodded: “I can’t believe I got parked all the way at the edge.”

Yeri said, quietly, “Sunbae is so handsome,” and got her cheek pinched by Joy for it.

The photo, as described

The S4 World Championship backstage. Warm-toned lights; the event’s blue-and-white key visual as the wall behind.

Baek Si-on at center. The four of them split two to a side.

Irene’s gaze found him first, and stayed one second, before sweeping slowly across everyone else. “Alright — learn the song, learn the song.” The tone was aimed at hurrying the others. In practice it was hurrying herself away from the stare.

The dorm quieted quickly. Only a few phone screens stayed glowing, plus the occasional small complaint about a loader spinning on bad Wi-Fi.

The whistle intro had barely sounded before the only thing left in the room was very light breathing.

♪

Ice Cream Cake · BodyArmor ad demo

melody: Charlie Puth · lyrics: Lee Ji-eun · guide vocal: Lee Ji-eun · whistle intro · freezer-door fresh

The melody was good. Light, bright, carrying the cool sweetness of late spring tipping into early summer.

When the female vocal came in, Seung-wan looked up at everyone.

“Isn’t that Ji-eun sunbae’s voice?”

“It really is.”

“Did sunbae record the demo herself?”

Yeri was half a beat slow, but entered the trend-shock state immediately after.

“Wow! Daebak!”

Irene, of course, had heard it too.

Lee Ji-eun’s timbre was instantly identifiable. Even a guide vocal for an advertisement got every riff sung like a finished master recording.

The nitpick ledger, as kept in the dark

That final note is too skilled. Deliberate — obviously deliberate.

And that riff — there’s no need to make it that natural, is there?

It’s an ad demo, not a music-show stage. Was singing it this well necessary?

And this song is supposed to be crisp, by design — yet everything she sings somehow comes out sounding like an invitation.

She knew she was nitpicking. She knew these judgments had subjective contamination mixed in. And still, she could not make herself listen calmly to the end.

Around her: Seung-wan was already dutifully memorizing the melody. Seul-gi nodded along to the rhythm. Joy had, apparently, auto-blocked the exact angle from which she would smile in the ad shoot. Even Yeri sat hugging her phone, biting her lip, following the song line by line.

Only Irene’s mind slid somewhere else.

She opened her Kakao contacts and scrolled down to a person she hadn’t contacted in a long time.

Baek Eun-ah.

She spent a few seconds on the wording, then opened the chat window:

KakaoTalk · Baek Eun-ah

Irene

Eun-ah, it’s been a while~ There are some ad-coordination details on the BodyArmor side I’d like to confirm directly with Baek Si-on sunbae. Could you pass me sunbae’s contact?

Baek Eun-ah

Of course~ Forwarding now!

She sent the message, set the phone face-up on her lap, and went back to listening — as if nothing had happened.

Ten seconds later, Baek Eun-ah replied, and behind the text came a smiley sticker and a pushed contact card.

Irene tapped Add Friend.

In the authentication-message field, she filled in her own name — properly, formally, every syllable of it.

Then she opened Instagram.

She typed Baek Si-on’s name into the search bar.

When the verified official account came up, she saw the avatar first, and then the follower count.

The counter, as watched

19,999,999.

She counted it once, on instinct: nineteen million, nine hundred ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred ninety-nine.

One person short of twenty million.

Irene tapped Follow. The page refreshed.

20,000,000.

At the instant the number turned over, the corner of her mouth moved without authorization. Then she began to scroll.

Baek Si-on’s Instagram updated infrequently, but the engagement under every post was absurd. Tens of thousands of comments was the baseline. The hot comments mixed English, Korean, Spanish, Arabic, Japanese —

❝

like an international airport with no customs.

— the comment section, observed from a dorm bed

She opened a backstage photo from the tour. The first comment was English, the second Portuguese, the third a string of characters she couldn’t parse at all — Thai, possibly. Hindi, possibly.

She opened Twitter next. Over fifteen million followers.

Then YouTube. The official channel was closing in on ten million subscriptions.

She went platform by platform, and every refresh confirmed the same fact.

She knew Baek Si-on was famous.

But “famous” as two syllables of gossip, and numbers like these on a private screen at midnight, were two entirely different species of thing.

Irene had thought her group sweeping every music-show win that week was already an enormous achievement.

Held up against this man —

❝

like a bulb that has only just learned to glow, standing beside the sun and shining with everything it has.

— scale, recalibrated in real time

And she was now preparing to send that sun a message.

Irene left his profile and went back to KakaoTalk to check the verification status. Still pending.

She opened the group photo from the manager’s chat again and stared at it for a few seconds.

Then her fingers zoomed, cropped, and zoomed again — very naturally, as if with a will of their own.

By the time she surfaced, the frame held only the two of them: her, and Baek Si-on.

The crop, as internally justified

Reason one: the composition is cleaner this way.

Reason two: a two-person framing beats a five-person one.

Reason three: that many people crowded behind disturbs the subject.

Reason four: it’s a work photo — foregrounding the focal point is standard practice.

The cropped photo was saved into a brand-new album. The album received no name. Unnamed, she judged, was safer.

“Unnie.”

Joy’s voice floated over from beside her, and Irene locked the screen on pure reflex.

“Are you actually learning the song?”

“Of course.”

“Everyone else is listening to the song. Only you are poking at your phone.”

Irene’s face didn’t move a millimeter.

“I’m reviewing the ad-shoot reference materials.”

“Oh.”

Joy nodded, appearing to accept it, then turned to Seung-wan:

“The company not letting idols have phones is — from a certain angle — genuinely a form of protection.”

“Such as?”

“Such as how some people, the moment a phone touches their hand, have a mind that is one hundred percent no longer at work.”

The sentence had barely landed when a cushion came flying.

It took Joy squarely on the side of the head.

“I didn’t name any names!”

“You had better shut up.”

Irene’s tone now carried the warning label of a person one sentence away from standing up. Joy wisely closed her mouth.

The topic should have ended there.

But Seung-wan suddenly mused:

“Phones really do require care from now on. Young people overthink.”

Seul-gi nodded along:

“Especially puberty.”

Three gazes landed on Yeri at the same moment.

Yeri’s smile slowly solidified.

“No, wait—”

“Phone. Hand it over first.” Joy extended her hand.

“What?”

“Key protection target: a minor.” Seul-gi’s face was utterly serious.

“Prevention of early romance.” Seung-wan supplemented.

Yeri looked from unnie to unnie in disbelief, then turned to the captain — surely Irene would speak up for fairness.

Irene had, by now, completely restored her dignified captain state.

She nodded, solemn and proper.

“Mm. Yeri, no more phone for now.”

“WHY me!”

Yeri’s voice jumped half an octave:

“I’m only FIFTEEN!”

“Which is exactly why fifteen is dangerous.”

“I haven’t DONE anything!”

“Not doing anything now is no guarantee about later.”

“This is prevention.”

“Advance protection.”

“……”

Yeri watched, in real time, as the phone she had re-acquired less than an hour ago was extracted from her hand by Joy again.

The dorm dissolved into laughter.

And while every pair of eyes was busy with Yeri’s collapse, Irene glanced down at her own phone.

On the screen, “awaiting verification” had disappeared.

Baek Si-on had accepted her friend request.

Irene flipped the phone over, face-down on her knee.

Her expression did not change at all.

But her crossed legs swayed — lightly.
