October 4 Afternoon KST Incheon International · arrivals A jetway, a corner, and a wall of LED that had decided to be a bank.

Seoul was afternoon when the wheels touched. Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah came off the bridge, through inbound, a trolley toward the hall. At a corner her step hesitated.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“Look.”

He followed the finger. The hall’s giant LED was playing KB Kookmin’s new spot. Deep navy three-piece. A desk. Cold white lines of a finance tower. The lens walking in. His eye to the glass. Steady. Clean. Logo. A line under it.

KB Kookmin Bank · LED · ICN arrivals

on-air

Trust begins with a spine — KB Kookmin Bank.

She stood two seconds. Then the phone was out. Click. Angle. Click again.

“What are you doing?”

“File.”

“This too?”

“Of course. First national-bank spot in the wild. Résumé, case deck, renewal talk, a quote to another house — all of it uses this.”

A beat.

“And it actually looks good.”

He looked at the man on the wall, two shades paler than the man in the hall.

“Looks like insurance.”

She had no answer that would not make it worse. They walked. The Aslan had been sent ahead. Temp plates gone, real plates on. He opened the door. She took the passenger seat. Not home. Not jet lag. First stop: Bukak.

October 4 Late afternoon Bukaksan lookout · SK Telecom, the upgrade Los Angeles, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Paris, New York in a cut. A sentence about one second. A man who had just gotten off a plane.

The crew was already there. The brief was simple enough to survive any telco conference room. He at the lookout. Phone lighting. Network taking him without being asked. Work pictures from Los Angeles, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Paris, New York cutting through. Back to the face.

TVC · SK Telecom · Connected World

Bukak. Wind. A phone that finds a net the way a hand finds a rail. Cities in a flash. Then the face again.

The world is large. The join takes a second.

On anyone else the sentence would have been packaging. On him it was a calendar.

It went fast. He did not drag a lens. Two and a half hours. Wrap. The brand lead came, shook.

“Mr. Baek. Thank you. Off a plane and straight here.”

“It’s in the paper.”

The man smiled. Other artists said of course and meant a room. This one said contractual duty and sounded like a clause. That was why a brand slept. A man who kept saying contract out loud did not drop a chain for sport.

Baek Jeong-hun was already there. One side of the table, Showbox’s domestic lead, a young planner. Not a commercial film. No love, no spectacle, no comedy hook, no easy in. That did not mean it did not need a push. The heavier the picture, the more you had to stand people at the right door, or they only thought too much, I don’t want it.

Si-on came in. Uncle and nephew looked at each other one second. Neither opened Busan. There was no need. Jeong-hun here, and not at a festival that wanted a signature, was already the sentence.

He pulled a chair.

“Go.”

Open: 1 November. Frame: Venice prize. Korean independent, realist, a subject that did not want to be furniture. Keys: a house that hits. A wound that travels down a bloodline. October 4 10:40 pm Hapjeong · 401 A fist bump. A Worlds cut. A question about a lifeguard that was not about a lifeguard.

Bell. Three seconds. Door. Fist. Jung Jae-joon looked at him.

“You look bad.”

“In.”

“Mm.”

“Site tomorrow. Tonight I have marks.”

“All right.”

Twenty minutes. Legend did not need to be rewritten for a stadium. It needed to become a stadium. Drums larger. Chorus thicker. Eight bars of climb before the hook so lights, players, and the wall could stand up together. Jae-joon knew that job. Not a night through. Not this night. Si-on stood to go. Jae-joon, still in the chair:

“One more.”

Si-on looked back.

“Bong-suk showed me a rough of the MV. He wants the music biting the picture.”

“And?”

Jae-joon thought.

“It’s good.”

“More?”

“Not only you and Kobe. I saw firemen, doctors, a cable crew.”

A beat.

“The idea sits high.”

Si-on put a thumb to the brow. Bong Joon-ho. Of course. Even an MV, and the man would take legend off a single life and push it half a step into the structure of a city. Jae-joon added:

“It still burns. Not a closed room. You and Kobe are the spine. The others are montage walking out.”

Si-on nodded. That he could live with. Then a thought.

“No lifeguard in the water.”

Jae-joon blinked.

“What?”

“A lifeguard.”

“Don’t think so. Why?”

“Nothing.”

A lifeguard, as an idea, was clean. Save, hold, duty, the edge of a life. This year’s Korea was not clean. Water. Rescue. A sinking. In a cut Bong had made, a viewer would take it to a ferry without being asked. Especially with Diving Bell still hot at Busan, and the culture desks and the government learning new ways to look at each other. He did not fear a work having meaning. It could have a society in it. It could not, at home, be read as a flag. Jae-joon watched his face and caught a corner of it.

“I’ll mention it when I talk to him.”

“Mm.”

At the door Jae-joon looked again.

“You going home to sleep?”

“Mm.”

“Really?”

“If the road doesn’t ring.”

Jae-joon laughed once.

“Then luck.”

October 6 10:20 am Sangam · Seoul World Cup Stadium · infield Empty seats. A temp stage. A rundown that owned every second. Soju, because the first pass had been too awake.

Si-on walked onto the grass with Jae-joon’s three-a.m. Worlds file in his pocket. The stands empty. Rows of seats cold in the morning. Centre: a temp stage facing south, two main LED walls, wings, light. Riot Korea, the American show-call team, set, sound, light, player-path, all of them on headsets, writing a global final into a board. Which minute the drummers entered. Which minute the house went down. Which minute the wall played CG. Which minute the truck cut the crowd. Which minute the players walked. Which minute the singer hit the lift.

Eun-ah had a tablet and the rundown. A few months ago this kind of paper had frightened her. It still had too many lines. She at least knew which line to read first.

Downstage, dozens of men in Korean traditional kit were marking drums. Riot’s floor director was about forty, American, named Daniel. Board in hand. Handshake.

Daniel

Show caller · Riot Games · Season 4 Worlds

OC · a rundown, a headset, a stadium that is still empty

He wants a war-drum into a rock chorus without a seam. He wants bodies in the wide shot of a hook. He does not need the singer to dance. He will take a boy group if the boy group belongs to the man who owns thirty percent of their company, and he will call it chemistry.

“Baek. Welcome.”

“Thank you.”

Daniel pointed at the stage.

“We built a traditional drum open. Host country. Slow to fast. Tradition, war, contest, a summoning. Last acceleration dumps straight into Legend’s downbeat. Your lift comes on that switch. Verse one, centre. Eight-bar climb, two teams in from the wings. First line of the hook, truck to the wall — team marks, player close-ups. Song ends, host takes it.”

He looked at Si-on.

“That’s the shape.”

Si-on looked at the drummers. The design was not a costume glued on. The drums were a tempo that became Legend’s tempo. One run of skin, from a peninsula’s old weather into a global match’s now. If the join held, it would burn.

“It’s good.”

Daniel smiled.

“We thought so.”

“I sing it once.”

“Once. Mid-show is other Korean acts. Close is players and a cup.”

“Oh.”

Same work, singing. A month of paid stages had made a little over three hundred million won. One song now was three hundred thousand dollars. The world had not changed. The floor he was standing on had.
