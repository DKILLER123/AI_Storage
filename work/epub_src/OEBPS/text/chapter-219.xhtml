Chapter Two Hundred Nineteen

A Tiny Shock for the Friendly Competition

June 6

First light

Choi Jin-ri’s apartment · east-facing, the duvet gilded

Choi Jin-ri’s apartment faced east, and when the morning light came in, it landed on the duvet in a warm, pale gold.

Baek Si-on opened his eyes, and the first thing he registered was pins and needles.

Choi Jin-ri’s head was pillowed on his left arm, one hand lying across his chest, knees drawn up, the whole of her curled into his side.

Her breathing was very light.

Not awake yet.

Baek Si-on did not pull the arm out.

Because pulling it out would wake her.

Waking her meant getting up.

Getting up meant today had started.

And today held 53.7 gigabytes of homework, a contract-manufacturer visit, and a respectable pile of other work.

So Baek Si-on decided the left arm could go numb a while longer.

Choi Jin-ri stirred at that exact moment.

Her brow creased; she mumbled a few indistinct syllables.

Unintelligible.

Probably negotiating with zombies in her dream.

Baek Si-on brushed the stray hairs off her forehead with his free hand.

Her lashes fluttered. Her eyes stayed shut, and she wormed half an inch closer into him.

The room was quiet enough to hear a door close on some other floor — somebody leaving for work.

That was somebody else’s morning.

Theirs could idle a little longer.

❧ ❧ ❧

The phone rang.

Hers.

The ringtone spilled off the nightstand: ‘Perfect’ — the song Baek Si-on had sung for her on her birthday.

Choi Jin-ri’s eyelids twitched twice.

On the third ring she finally opened her eyes.

The look in them was foggy, pupils not yet locked on anything.

She reached for the phone, patted the nightstand twice, found it.

Answered.

“…Hello?”

Baek Si-on couldn’t make out the voice on the other end.

But Choi Jin-ri’s face completed, in three seconds, the full three-stage transition from still asleep to fully awake to doomed.

She shot upright.

The duvet slid to her waist.

“What TIME is it?!”

The other end said something.

Choi Jin-ri turned to gauge the light leaking through the curtain gap, reading the sun’s angle like a clock.

“I’m coming back right now! Two hours… no, three! Tell the director for me, please!”

She hung up and launched herself off the bed entire.

One second ago: the kitten curled at his side.

One second later: a wage-earner about to be late and yelled at by a director.

The switching speed made Baek Si-on want to applaud.

Choi Jin-ri’s bare feet hit the rug.

The instant they landed, her calves appeared to receive a delayed complaint, filed from the night before.

Her knees buckled.

She sat straight back down onto the bed.

“……”

“It seems Miss Jin-ri requires supplementary physical training.”

Choi Jin-ri’s head whipped around.

“What did you say?”

“I said you need supplementary—”

The second half of the sentence got blocked at the source.

Because Choi Jin-ri pounced.

“You still dare! Whose fault was THAT, exactly?!”

“Your conditioning.”

“……”

The two of them roughhoused on the bed for another thirty seconds.

The final score: three punches and two bites from Choi Jin-ri, and Baek Si-on carrying her into the bathroom to close it out.

❧ ❧ ❧

When they left, Baek Si-on didn’t let Choi Jin-ri drive herself.

“My driver takes you back to Busan. You sleep in the back seat.”

Choi Jin-ri stood in front of the car in a thin coat, wearing Baek Si-on’s Under Armour cap, a base layer of the most basic sunscreen on her face.

“And you?”

“I’m taking Hara’s car. I have things to do.”

“Do you even have a license?”

Baek Si-on: “?”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

She’d noticed that lately her IQ seemed to keep running out.

After the door closed, Choi Jin-ri leaned her head out the window.

“Call me tonight when you’re done.”

“Okay.”

“You have to call.”

“Okay.”

“If you don’t, I’ll turn into a zombie and bite you.”

“……”

The nanny van pulled away from the curb.

Baek Si-on stood below the apartment building, took out his phone, and sent Baek Eun-ah a message.

Message · Eun-ah

Eun-ah

Fifteen minutes. I’ll be downstairs at yours — get ready to leave.

Where to?

Cosmax. Gyeonggi Province.

……Thank you for remembering I’m still alive.

The reply was instant.

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Eun-ah got into the car without granting Baek Si-on a single direct glance, from door to seat.

Baek Si-on didn’t mind.

Engine on.

The 911’s exhaust gave a low growl.

About two minutes passed.

Baek Eun-ah failed to hold it in, in the end.

“Cousin.”

“Mm?”

“Did you have fun last night?”

“Mm.”

“And Jin-ri unnie?”

“What are you trying to say?”

“I’m saying I came home yesterday and discovered my father had made my ROOM disappear, and my dinner was ramyeon. Ramyeon!”

“Which brand of ramyeon?”

“……”

Baek Eun-ah turned to the window in fury.

❧ ❧ ❧

June 6

Mid-morning

Cosmax Korea HQ · Pangyo, Gyeonggi Province

The welcome, as priced by the factory

Baek Si-on was, personally, a walking distribution channel.

If he truly built a brand, then whatever it became, his name and his traffic alone guaranteed a first order that was not small.

For a contract manufacturer, that made this the rarest commodity in the business: a certainty.

Cosmax’s Korean headquarters sat in Pangyo, Gyeonggi Province — a gray-white building that didn’t flaunt itself but covered a lot of ground, the company logo hung on its face.

The visitor lot was huge.

Most of the cars parked there were white or black Hyundais and Kias.

After the Porsche 911 slid in, it looked spectacularly out of place.

Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah got out.

Receiving them: Cosmax’s VP of business and a senior R&D director.

Both wore white shirts, and both were extremely courteous.

The courtesy needed no explaining.

After the pleasantries, the VP walked them into a conference room and opened with a short business presentation.

Baek Si-on listened for about five minutes, then raised a hand and cut in.

“One question.”

“Please.”

“If I want to make a color-cosmetics product, do I need to assemble my own R&D team and start from the formula?”

The VP smiled.

“Not at all.”

He stood and switched the projector on.

A process flowchart appeared on the screen.

“We maintain a complete R&D organization, a mature formula library, and full production lines.”

“You only need to tell us one thing: what product you want.”

He tapped several nodes on the flowchart.

“Say you want a lip gloss.”

“You tell us the finish you’re after — watery shine or matte. Whether the color leans warm or cool. How many hours of wear. How fast it needs to set.”

“Our R&D team pulls the closest base formula from the existing library, then adjusts, trials the color, trials the texture.”

“Until you’re satisfied.”

The R&D director slid a sample book across.

Baek Eun-ah turned two pages.

Every page carried a dozen-plus little shade squares, annotated with formula numbers, set times, hydration indices, and wear parameters.

She gave up at page five.

Because she had already lost count of how many the book contained.

Baek Si-on, by contrast, digested the information quickly.

“So essentially, you’re a ‘Lego brick library for cosmetics.’ The brand supplies the idea and the requirements; you supply the formulas and the capacity.”

The VP nodded:

“First time I’ve heard the Lego comparison. But the meaning is right.”

“Which brands use you for manufacturing?” Baek Eun-ah asked.

The VP reeled off a string of names.

Western, Japanese, Korean, top-tier international houses, rising independents — all of it.

Baek Eun-ah had assumed the gorgeous, exquisitely packaged lipsticks on the department-store counters — the ones that cost tens of thousands of won a tube — were blended by each brand’s own lab, tube by tube.

Now she knew.

Plenty of them were dialed out of a formula library inside this building.

The only differences were packaging, marketing, and brand premium.

❧ ❧ ❧

After roughly forty minutes in the conference room, the visit moved to the laboratory tour.

Baek Si-on changed into a visitor’s white coat.

Baek Eun-ah changed too.

She checked herself in the mirror in the white coat and found it, unexpectedly, a good look.

The lab was very clean.

Constant temperature; the air carried a faint chemical-reagent smell that didn’t sting.

Rows of benches were crowded with test tubes, centrifuges, and instruments of unnameable purpose.

Baek Si-on walked to the lip-product display zone.

The shelves held densely packed sample tubes.

Colors running from nude pink to true red to deep plum — hundreds of shades in a row, like a miniature rainbow wall.

“What’s the dominant lip trend on the market right now?” he asked.

The R&D director hosting them pushed up his glasses.

“First there was the matte gradient bite-lip that Yoon Eun-hye started with ‘I Miss You.’ Then Jun Ji-hyun brought back water-shine lips with ‘My Love from the Star.’”

“The hottest lip right now combines the two.”

“A gradient bite-lip base, but with the water-shine finish of something like YSL’s mirror gloss No. 12 — the overall effect is the pillowed, dewy lip with the gradient color.”

Baek Si-on nodded once.

“How is it applied, exactly?”

The R&D director glanced at the professional makeup artist standing by.

The makeup artist was a woman around thirty, and she looked extremely professional.

She took over:

“If you don’t mind, I can demonstrate it live.”

She scanned the room.

Her gaze landed on Baek Eun-ah.

Before Baek Eun-ah could react, she had been steered to the station in front of the mirror.

“Me?”

“Please,” Baek Si-on said from behind.

Baek Eun-ah filed two objections with her mouth, but her body sat honestly down in the chair.

The makeup artist went to work.

“The standard procedure is four steps.”

The gradient lip, as taught · four steps

One — Conceal: erase the natural lip color with concealer, so the color that follows goes on true.

Two — Tint: a fine-tip tint, applied to the inner half of the lip only, center-weighted — never filled to the edges.

Three — Blur: a cotton swab or fingertip pushes the tint’s edge outward until the color grades into a natural fade.

Four — Seal: a thin coat of clear water-shine gloss over the whole lip — riding the “glass skin” trend — for the pillowed finish.

Total elapsed: seven to eight minutes.

Four steps, done.

Baek Eun-ah studied the mirror.

The center of her lips was a beautiful berry red, fading outward in a clean gradient, the surface carrying a layer of moist, glassy shine.

It was, genuinely, lovely.

She had her phone out instantly, taking selfies.

Angles: straight-on, forty-five degrees, profile.

Then she scrolled the camera roll, picked the best one, and opened the retouching app.

“Cousin, look at this.”

Baek Si-on ignored her.

He was thinking: at home, doing a full face to go out, seven or eight minutes was nothing.

But what about a touch-up — after a date, a dinner, a round of photos?

The touch-up problem, as timed

Dinner ends. Half the color on her lips is gone.

She excuses herself to the restroom. By the book: remove the remains, concealer, tint, blend the edge with a swab, one more coat of gloss.

Ten minutes in the restroom. Her date waits ten minutes outside.

By minute eight, he has begun to suspect she left through the window.

“If it were one step.”

Baek Si-on turned to the R&D director:

“Could that problem be solved?”

“Meaning what, exactly?”

“Meaning: it goes on and delivers eighty percent of that effect. No concealer base, no finger-blending. It films over by itself, locks the color, carries its own water-shine. One product. Five seconds, done.”

The R&D director thought for about five seconds, then said:

“Technically, it can absolutely be done.”

“Specifically?”

“Raise the film-former ratio in the formula, max out the water retention, and build the pigment release as a gradient structure that decays from center to edge. Pressure in the middle, a light drag outward, and you get the bite-lip effect. Once it films, the color is locked; the water-shine holds on the formula’s humectants.”

Baek Si-on was quiet a moment, then asked:

“If there’s no technical difficulty — why is there no product like this on the market?”

The R&D director’s mouth moved.

He stopped himself.

The VP coughed, once, beside him.

The R&D director looked at the VP, then at Baek Si-on, and in the end gave him the answer anyway.

“One step means three SKUs collapse into one. For a brand, that’s slashing your own revenue by two-thirds.”

The revolution, as costed

The consumer’s pain point is, itself, a line item in the industry’s profit structure.

Which meant: step out of line to fix it, and the industry hits back as one body.

Was he afraid? Of course not. The barefoot have nothing to fear from the shod.

The end goal of this brand was never to sell cosmetics for a living. It was one category-killing product, enough to drag the brand’s traffic and valuation, in the shortest possible time, to a number that made conglomerates stand up from their chairs — and then to wait for the shod to come waving checks, queuing to acquire him.

Worst case? The venture dies and the tuition comes due. At his current size, the tuition was affordable.

“Proceed on this direction. Make prototypes.”

The R&D director and the VP exchanged a look.

“You’re certain?”

“Certain.”

❧ ❧ ❧

June 6

Afternoon

Sangam-dong · a conference room beside the MBC Production Center

Kim Eun-sook

Star screenwriter · Descendants of the Sun

Americano, always · a cast list in red crosses · a phone etiquette she wrote herself

Ran the math on a lead actor and crossed out every name twice — then fielded the call nobody scripts: a Venice laureate declining her lead, buying his heroine’s lips instead, and attaching a two-hundred-million-won OST to the no. She greenlit all of it before the line went dead.

Kim Eun-sook sat dead center at the table.

At her right hand sat her new co-director, Lee Eung-bok; at her left, the casting director.

None of the three was talking.

Kim Eun-sook picked up the americano, drank, set it back down.

It tasted exactly as good as the day’s progress.

Descendants of the Sun: the script framework was settled. The military research was done. The broadcaster was found. The female-lead direction was basically locked.

But the male lead sat empty.

On paper, Yoo Si-jin was not a hard character to parse. Soldier. Elite. Dependable. Humor. Could fight, could flirt, held the line when it mattered.

Casting him, though, was brutal.

He needed a soldier’s hardness — without reading as a slab of stone. A romantic softness — without tipping into drama-school cream.

Above all, the audience had to believe that a face this beautiful, put into uniform, would genuinely go to war.

Kim Eun-sook’s pen tip rested on the list.

The first name crossed out: Song Seung-heon.

His conditions actually fit — build, temperament, age, all within range.

But the man was currently dating the Fairy Sister from the mainland, and was therefore a poor fit for rescuing anyone in Uruk.

The second name crossed out: Hyun Bin.

Hyun Bin, for the record, was willing to talk.

But his slate ran through mid-next year; the calendar simply did not meet.

The third: Gong Yoo.

Gong Yoo’s agency replied with great courtesy.

Substance: Mr. Gong Yoo was currently shooting a zombie film and was not considering television at this time.

Three people. Three styles of refusal. One result.

The casting director finished the last red X on the list and looked up.

“Writer-nim — should we ask Baek Si-on?”

Kim Eun-sook stared at the list, mutilated in red.

Five seconds of silence.

Then she shook her head.

“Don’t bother.”

“He’s a Venice Best Actor. Men at that level don’t come do television.”

The wall, as the industry drew it in 2015

Korean film and television were separated by a line everyone could see. A film actor who went down to television was read, by default, as asking whether he’d washed out.

In the other direction, a television actor who wanted up into film had a high wall to cross — a wall that had stood for over a decade, in full, mutual understanding.

Baek Si-on, in a thirteen-episode K-drama? To Kim Eun-sook, the idea sat on the same shelf as asking Bong Joon-ho to direct a weekend variety show.

“Worth a try,” Lee Eung-bok said, suddenly.

“Mm?” Kim Eun-sook turned to him.

“A rejection doesn’t take anything off us. There aren’t many names left who can carry it. One phone call costs less than sitting here.”

Kim Eun-sook considered, then turned to the casting director:

“Call.”

❧ ❧ ❧

The call went to Baek Eun-ah.

Baek Eun-ah’s reply was a masterpiece of standard form.

“Mr. Baek Si-on’s schedule for the second half of the year is fully committed, and he is unable to take on new film or television projects. We look forward to the chance to work together in the future.”

The casting director hung up and relayed the result.

Kim Eun-sook wasn’t disappointed.

It matched the forecast to the decimal.

“What about Jo In-sung?”

She asked.

“Has Jo In-sung been reached?”

“Reached. Still waiting on his reply.”

“Chase it.”

The casting director dialed out again.

About twenty minutes later, the answer came back.

Jo In-sung had also declined.

But unlike the others, he had attached a recommendation to the no.

Song Joong-ki.

“He says Song Joong-ki’s schedule opens up right after discharge — it lines up.”

Kim Eun-sook thought it over.

Sungkyunkwan Scandal. The Innocent Man. A Werewolf Boy.

Clean looks, a boyish quality, real acting. As a soldier…

Worth a try.

“Is he still under agency contract?”

“Should still be Blossom.”

“Then reach—”

Kim Eun-sook didn’t finish.

The casting director’s phone rang.

He glanced at the caller ID.

His face changed.

“Writer-nim — Baek Si-on’s side is calling back.”

Kim Eun-sook blinked once.

In the standard procedure, a rejection was a rejection; nobody called back.

A call back meant something had moved.

Her hand went out.

“Give it to me.”

The casting director handed the phone over.

Kim Eun-sook took it and pressed answer.

Kim Eun-sook ↔ Baek Si-on · voice · the call nobody scripts

“Hello?”

“Hello — this is Baek Si-on.”

“Oh my — Si-on-ssi! It’s really you calling personally!”

Two seconds of pause on the line. He, too, had not expected the writer herself to pick up.

“I apologize for letting my sister answer for me earlier — that was discourteous. I thought it better to tell you directly.”

“No, no — discourteous, not at all.”

Kim Eun-sook said no twice.

She had written countless scenes of important men phoning people. Receiving the important-man call herself, she discovered her dialogue was not going to be enough.

“Writer Kim, honestly — I have deep respect for your work. If time allowed, working with you would be an honor.”

At if time allowed, Kim Eun-sook knew the turn was coming.

It came.

“But the second half of this year genuinely won’t open up.”

“Of course. Of course.”

She steadied her voice and tried: “Though if it were only a month or two pushed back, we on our side aren’t entirely unable to—”

“Writer Kim.”

His voice was very gentle.

“I currently have two signed films.”

Kim Eun-sook made a small acknowledging sound.

“One with my uncle, aimed at next year’s Venice. One with director Damien Chazelle, aimed at the Academy.”

The acknowledging sound stuck in her throat.

“Separately, there’s a second world tour at year’s end. The venue deposits for Dubai and Qatar went out last month. Breach everything, and the total comes to roughly thirty-five million dollars.”

She swallowed the sound.

Lee Eung-bok and the casting director looked up together, eyes pinned to her face.

Her expression was complicated. She liked Baek Si-on the actor. She did not like him enough to spend thirty-five million dollars on his schedule.

“What a shame, then. Here’s to next time.”

“There is also a way to work together this time.”

Kim Eun-sook’s fingers stopped on the tabletop.

“Meaning what?”

Her first guess: he was offering to write the OST?

It was the only reasonable answer she could construct. A Baek Si-on not acting could still touch a drama through a song — a Billboard triple-crown writing the OST. The thought alone was exciting.

What he said next had nothing to do with an OST.

“Writer Kim — I’m preparing an independent cosmetics brand.”

“…Cosmetics?”

“Yes. My question: the female lead’s designated lip-product placement — whether there’s room to reserve it for me.”

Kim Eun-sook went quiet.

He had turned down her drama. Then turned around to buy her heroine’s mouth.

This development was more absurd than anything she had ever scripted.

“Are you serious?”

“Completely. I trust the quality of your work.”

A pause.

“Figures and structure can go through my team with production, in detail.”

Kim Eun-sook leaned back in her chair and ran it in two full loops. From production’s side, the placement was pure increment — no casting impact, no script impact, nothing touched in the main-creative structure. His brand on the heroine’s lips might even generate its own topics.

“It can be done,” Kim Eun-sook said.

“Thank you, Writer Kim.”

“But you’re writing one OST for this drama.”

The line went quiet a while.

“Production only — or production plus my own vocals?”

Now it was her turn to go quiet for a moment.

“Prices for each?”

“The buyout I pay my producer is five hundred thousand dollars a track.”

Kim Eun-sook’s mouth opened. Closed. Opened again.

“…How MUCH???”

“In won, call it five hundred sixty million.”

Her brain ran the numbers at speed. Five hundred sixty million won: in Korea, enough to book a top-tier male actor for an entire miniseries. Here it bought the production rights to one song. Vocals not included.

She was assembling a polite let’s skip it when the voice turned.

“That said — out of respect for you, a friendship price.”

“…And the friendship price is?”

“Two hundred million won, and I sing it myself. Restricted to in-drama original-soundtrack use. The song’s digital streaming and physical rights stay with me.”

Kim Eun-sook chewed through all three conditions. Two hundred million was still a top-of-market OST quote — but against the five-hundred-sixty-million international buyout, it was almost shy. And Baek Si-on singing it himself was a publicity point in its own right. As for the rights staying put — that, strangely, comforted her. It meant he wasn’t quoting wildly, and wasn’t doing charity. He was giving her a friendly collaboration on his own rules. Status: intact. Face: delivered.

“Done. Let’s call it settled.”

She could hear the smile.

“Good — the license scope and the placement details go to the teams from here.”

“Right.”

“Thank you for your time.”

“And you.”

The call ended, and Lee Eung-bok could not stay in his chair; he leaned forward from the seat itself.

“Well?”

“He won’t act. But he bought the placement — and we came out of it with an OST.”

“An OST? A Billboard-champion-grade OST?”

Kim Eun-sook nodded and stood.

“Which is why one must widen one’s frame.”

Lee Eung-bok stood too.

“How do we play it?”

“To the base. To visit Song Joong-ki.”
