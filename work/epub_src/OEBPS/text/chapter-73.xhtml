Chapter Seventy-Three

Choi Jin-ri's Peace-Sign Ambush

August 26 11:27 a.m. local · ~2 hr layover Paris CDG · the stone-paved path outside First time on the continent, and a photo that took three tries

Paris CDG. 11:27 a.m. local time.

Layover, about two hours.

The five of them came through the temporary-entry channel and out of the terminal.

The building was joined to a stone-paved path that led off to somewhere, both sides lined with trimmed plane trees, their canopies meeting overhead into a green roof.

At the end of the path was a small café. A wooden sign in unreadable French, two wrought-iron round tables and four chairs out front.

Not some famous spot.

Not even findable on Google Maps.

But for Baek Eun-ah, Park Ji-hun, and Baek Si-on — three Koreans stepping onto the European continent for the first time — the worn-smooth stone beneath their feet, the Air France jets rumbling overhead every few minutes, the scent of coffee and butter croissants drifting through the air, all of it added up to a picture worth taking.

Baek Eun-ah was first to pull out her phone.

She shot three landscape shots first.

One of the stone path. One of the plane trees. One of the battered wooden sign outside the café door.

Then she flipped the camera.

Selfie.

The bangs were a little windswept, so she flicked them into place with her fingers, tilted her head up forty-five degrees, and pressed the shutter.

Looked at it. Fine.

One more.

This time a pout.

Looked at it, thought it looked a little silly, deleted it.

Then her gaze moved to Choi Jin-ri beside her.

Choi Jin-ri stood beneath a plane tree, cap off, mask off, face turned up to the sunlight filtering through the gaps in the leaves.

On the streets of Paris, no one knew her.

She didn't need the cap, the mask, or the sunglasses.

"Jin-ri eonni! Group photo!"

Baek Eun-ah charged over holding the phone up and threw an arm around Choi Jin-ri's shoulder.

Choi Jin-ri was dragged a step off balance, laughed once, and leaned her head in, tilted.

Click.

Baek Eun-ah looked down. Very satisfied.

The two faces pressed together, the plane trees and the grey-blue Paris sky behind them.

Then she went looking for Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on was leaning on the iron railing outside the café, hands in his pockets, watching a French old man on the far side of the street walking a short-legged corgi.

"Cousin! Come take a picture!"

Baek Si-on turned, walked over, and stood beside Baek Eun-ah.

Baek Eun-ah raised the phone, framing herself and Baek Si-on in the viewfinder.

"Smile."

Baek Si-on didn't smile.

Click.

Baek Eun-ah looked at the photo.

Baek Si-on's expression was about the same as his passport photo.

Fine — being able to get one at all was good enough.

She flipped back through the three shots, and then something occurred to her.

She looked up, her gaze moving between Choi Jin-ri and Baek Si-on.

"Cousin, Jin-ri eonni."

Both turned to look at her.

"Let me take one of the two of you?"

Choi Jin-ri's gaze slid off Baek Eun-ah's face, drifting toward Baek Si-on.

A very quick glance.

Baek Si-on's expression didn't change in the slightest.

He didn't say yes. He didn't say no.

Hands still in his pockets.

Choi Jin-ri looked at him for about a second.

Then she decided to treat it as a "yes."

She walked over, stood to Baek Si-on's right, tilted her head, and flashed a peace sign at the lens.

Baek Si-on stood still.

Baek Eun-ah raised the phone.

Click.

Done.

Choi Jin-ri hurried over to look at the photo.

In it.

She was making the peace sign, face angled slightly toward the lens, smiling sweetly.

Baek Si-on stood beside her.

Hands in his pockets.

Eyes front.

Expressionless.

Like a statue some passerby had dragged in for a photo.

Choi Jin-ri looked at that perfectly anchored, keep-away-from-me aura of his in the photo, and the corner of her mouth turned down a fraction.

She glanced at Baek Si-on.

Looked at the photo again.

Glanced at Baek Si-on again.

"Eun-ah."

"Mm?"

"Let's take another one. I blinked."

Baek Eun-ah looked down at her own crescent-moon eyes in the photo, thought about it, and silently raised the phone again.

Baek Si-on watched Choi Jin-ri walk back and stand beside him, and said nothing.

He didn't change his stance either.

Hands still in his pockets.

Eyes still front.

Choi Jin-ri stood to his right. This time, about ten centimetres closer than before.

Baek Eun-ah stared at the viewfinder, framed both of them from head to toe in the shot.

Behind them was still that plane tree, the dappled light falling through the leaves, scattered across both their shoulders.

"I'm counting."

"One."

Choi Jin-ri faced the lens, the corner of her mouth curved up, the peace sign lifted beside her cheek.

"Two."

Baek Si-on's gaze was still front — hard to tell whether he was looking at the lens or the tree behind Baek Eun-ah's head.

"Three."

At the instant that word left Baek Eun-ah's mouth, Choi Jin-ri's right shoulder suddenly drove left, hard.

Not a lot of force.

About the amount you'd use to nudge a door open with your shoulder while your hands were full.

But enough to make Baek Si-on turn his head to look at her, reflexively.

Click.

The shutter sounded.

The frame froze.

Baek Eun-ah lowered the phone and looked down.

In the photo, Choi Jin-ri's centre of gravity was tipped slightly toward Baek Si-on, the peace sign lowered beside her chin, the corner of her mouth carrying a smile that was amused by its own trick.

Baek Si-on's face was turned toward her.

Brows raised a touch, lips pressed into a very shallow line, as if to say "what are you doing."

But because the sunlight slanted in from between the two of them, that "what are you doing" face had been laid over with a warm-gold side light.

Under that light, the expression didn't read as a question.

It read as a look.

Baek Eun-ah stared at the screen for three seconds.

Good.

Not just good.

The kind of good where, if it went online, the first comment would be "when's the announcement?"

Baek Eun-ah silently gave her own photography skills a perfect score.

Though, strictly speaking, ninety percent of why the shot came out like this was thanks to Choi Jin-ri's shoulder bump.

She was just starting to let her mouth curl upward.

Choi Jin-ri had already come over.

She leaned her head in from the other side, chin almost resting on Baek Eun-ah's shoulder, and stared at the screen a few seconds.

"This one's good."

Her voice came soft.

Likely inaudible to Baek Si-on.

Baek Eun-ah turned her head and glanced at her.

Choi Jin-ri's gaze was still fixed on the screen, the corner of her mouth curved in an arc.

"Eonni, want me to send it to you?"

"Mm."

Choi Jin-ri answered quickly.

Baek Eun-ah used KakaoTalk to send over all three group shots.

The one of her and Choi Jin-ri.

The two of her and Baek Si-on.

Choi Jin-ri's phone vibrated in her pocket.

She reached in and pulled it out, opened the chat, saved the three photos to the album.

Then she opened the third photo on its own.

Zoomed in.

Looked for two seconds.

Then locked the screen and tucked it back in her pocket.

Expression managed perfectly.

August 26 Layover Paris CDG · the other side of the stone path A grey building being photographed, and a shutter for the record

On the other side of the stone path, SM's manager stood beneath a plane tree.

He held his phone up, lens aimed at a grey old apartment block across the road, as if photographing the architecture.

But his eyes weren't in the viewfinder.

He'd seen it.

All of it.

From Baek Eun-ah holding her phone up to photograph Choi Jin-ri and Baek Si-on, to Choi Jin-ri bumping Baek Si-on with her shoulder, to the two girls leaning together over the photo, heads close, murmuring.

He'd worked this business for eight years.

Managed everyone from trainees to top-tier acts. He'd seen more "red lines" than he could count.

Which contact was normal colleague interaction, and which had crossed the company's control boundary — he didn't need his brain to make the call. His spinal cord could react.

What Choi Jin-ri had just done, in SM's artist-management manual, could be categorised at minimum as "abnormal social contact requiring a report."

That was his duty.

Report, record, hand it to the artist-management division, and let the division head decide the follow-up.

He could walk that whole process with his eyes closed.

But he had not the slightest intention of reporting any of it — because in his pocket was still the upgrade stub Baek Si-on had paid for.

The manager's gaze came back.

He raised the phone again.

This time aimed at the café's wooden sign across the street.

The French letters on it were half worn away by time, the remaining strokes catching the sunlight in a warm, wood-toned gleam.

He pressed the shutter.

Click.

Beside him, Park Ji-hun also raised his phone to shoot.

The two men stood side by side, each shooting their own.

No eye contact.

No conversation.

But between the two shutters, a certain unspoken understanding quietly sealed itself.

As long as no one saw, it never happened.
