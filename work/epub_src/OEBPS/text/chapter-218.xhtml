Chapter Two Hundred Eighteen

Entrepreneurship, Chaebol Edition

June 5

Mid-afternoon — still parked

The arrivals lane · a Porsche 911 with its engine running

The door of the Porsche 911 swung open.

Baek Si-on bent in and sat.

Choi Jin-ri sat in the driver’s seat, head turned toward him.

“Slow.”

Baek Si-on pulled the seatbelt across and clicked it.

“A few extra words with the fans. Took a minute.”

Choi Jin-ri gave a small mm.

Didn’t ask anything else.

She turned her eyes back to the road ahead, fingers tapping the steering wheel twice, lightly. Then she reached up, pulled her sunglasses off, tossed them onto the center console — and undid the seatbelt she had just fastened.

The buckle snapped back against the door.

The next second, she leaned across the console entirely.

A fistful of Baek Si-on’s shirt.

Choi Jin-ri buried her face in his chest, forehead pressed to the spot below his collarbone.

Baek Si-on got one arm around her waist. The other hand came to rest on the back of her head, fingers sliding into her hair, kneading gently.

Muffled against him for a good half minute — then her voice surfaced from inside his shirt:

“Were there a lot of pretty women swarming you at the Met Gala?”

Baek Si-on looked down at the top of her head.

“Do you want the honest version, or the one that makes you happy?”

Choi Jin-ri lifted her head out of his chest.

“So there WERE pretty women swarming you.”

“There were.”

“A lot?”

“Not few.”

“……”

Her fingers twisted a handful of his hoodie’s front.

Baek Si-on added one line:

“But right now I’m next to you.”

Her fingers stopped.

She looked at him. He looked at her.

Two seconds of quiet.

Choi Jin-ri buried her face all over again.

“…You and that mouth.”

From the tone, it was impossible to rule either anger or laughter.

Then her head came back up, mouth opening — clearly intending to pursue the full roster of the pretty women —

But Baek Si-on had already lowered his own.

The second half of the question got intercepted somewhere between their teeth.

Choi Jin-ri’s brain finished shutting down its interrogation division in half a second flat.

The boyfriend-audit process was force-quit.

❧ ❧ ❧

The 911, as timed by the vehicle behind it

In the van: Baek Eun-ah checked the clock. Three minutes. Checked again. Six. Again. Nine. The 911 ahead showed zero indication of ever intending to move.

“Could you two PLEASE drive!” The driver stared into his navigation with monastic focus, researching the route. Two bodyguards studied the same street tree out opposite windows, developing a keen interest in bark texture.

Baek Eun-ah turned to look at the tree too. A grey bird sat in it, tilted its head at her, and flew away. Even the bird declined to wait with her.

About a minute later, the 911’s left indicator finally blinked. The van pulled out after it.

❧ ❧ ❧

The 911 merged into traffic, in no particular hurry.

Baek Si-on leaned against the passenger seatback and glanced at the dash.

“New car?”

Choi Jin-ri shook her head.

“Hara unnie’s.”

“Where’s the Lykan?”

“In the garage.”

She didn’t elaborate.

But Baek Si-on understood well enough. The limited Lykan — the only one in the country — driving to Incheon to pick someone up was too conspicuous. A Porsche on the streets of Seoul wasn’t rare.

At minimum, nobody’s first thought would land on Choi Jin-ri.

“By the way.”

Baek Si-on bent the conversation around a corner:

“Do you have any lip-product endorsements right now?”

Choi Jin-ri was visibly surprised. She shot him a look, then put her eyes back on the road.

“Not currently. But Estée Lauder’s been in touch lately. A liquid lipstick — it’ll most likely close.”

“Estée Lauder?”

“Mm — why are you asking all of a sudden?”

Baek Si-on leaned back, eyes on the Seoul streetsheets sliding past the glass.

“I’m thinking of building something.”

“What?”

“An independent cosmetics brand.”

Choi Jin-ri’s right foot eased off the accelerator — the speedo dropping by a whisker under five kilometers.

“You?”

“Mm.”

“Cosmetics?”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on told her about the after-party in the fewest possible words.

Kendall mentioning that Kylie’s lip-kit line was hunting for its Asian entrance. Rihanna negotiating her own beauty line with LVMH. Korea home to contract manufacturers like Cosmax and Kolmar — world-class at exactly this.

Put the threads side by side, and the logic ran.

Choi Jin-ri listened, thought for a moment, and said:

“Then I’ll model for you. I can shoot the ads, do the promo materials, push it on social for you.”

Her reasoning was simple.

Music couldn’t help. Film couldn’t help. Business structuring, even less.

But if exactly one thing fell inside her abilities — she wanted to be the one doing it.

“No.”

Choi Jin-ri’s right hand tightened on the wheel.

“Why?”

“Estée Lauder is a top-three beauty group on this planet. If you land their lipstick endorsement, your commercial standing steps up a whole flight of stairs.”

Baek Si-on paused, then turned his head.

“My thing doesn’t even have an embryo yet. You coming to model for me now means dropping a finished, top-tier resource to front a project that hasn’t even built its shell.”

Choi Jin-ri’s lips pressed together.

“I don’t mind.”

“But I do.”

Choi Jin-ri stayed quiet for a good while.

The wheel turned through another intersection in her hands.

“You’re always like this,” she said, quietly.

“Like what?”

“Thinking further ahead for me than I do.”

Baek Si-on didn’t answer.

Choi Jin-ri didn’t continue either.

The car ran quiet for a stretch of road.

Then she added, in a very small voice:

“But have you considered that some things I don’t do because the math works out.”

“Which is exactly why I can’t let you run a losing play.”

The corner of Choi Jin-ri’s mouth twitched.

Impossible to say whether she wanted to laugh or curse him.

In the end she did neither. Her foot settled the pedal, and the speed returned to normal.

❧ ❧ ❧

June 5

Late afternoon

The CJ beverage shoot · five standees and one empty C-position

The 911 stopped outside the CJ shoot location.

Baek Si-on reached for the door.

“Dinner tonight?” Choi Jin-ri’s voice came from his left.

His hand stopped on the handle.

“Aren’t you still shooting?”

“Mm.”

“Still in Busan?”

“Mm.”

“Then how are you in Seoul today?”

Choi Jin-ri’s fingers scratched lightly at the wheel.

“…I took the day off.”

Busan to Incheon: three and a half hours each way, minimum.

She’d taken a day off, driven three and a half hours to the airport, waited for him to land, picked him up — and still had to drive back. Up before dawn tomorrow for the set.

“What time?” Baek Si-on asked.

Choi Jin-ri’s eyes brightened.

“Is eight okay?”

“Eight works.”

“Really?”

“Really.”

“Then you’re not allowed to stand me up.”

“Not standing you up.”

Choi Jin-ri undid the seatbelt, leaned over, and kissed the corner of his mouth.

Baek Si-on let her stamp it. Because without the stamp, she probably wouldn’t have released him.

“Off you go.”

Kiss delivered, Baek Si-on got out.

This time she didn’t stop him. Before the door closed, he bent down and looked back at her.

“Drive slow on the way back.”

“I know.”

“It’s Hara’s car. Don’t crash it.”

“…Can you be a normal person for one second?”

The door closed.

The 911 blinked its indicator, merged into traffic, and dissolved into the Seoul afternoon light.

Baek Si-on stood where he was, right hand in his hoodie pocket.

The van pulled up behind him. Baek Eun-ah climbed out hugging the day’s work files and hurried to keep pace.

❧ ❧ ❧

Inside the studio, the lights were already set.

Baek Si-on changed into the brand’s tee and took the brightest spot on the floor. Beside him stood five life-size standees — beaming, perfectly postured — and Irene’s standee held the exact center.

The shoot began. Close-ups first: the photographer had him tilt his head a touch, chin up a touch, the mouth smiling naturally rather than broadly. Flash after flash after flash. Then the drink-sipping shots, the copy reads, and the interaction setups with the five standees.

The whole shoot ran about two hours.

Wrapped.

Afterward, Baek Si-on sat down with the CJ beverage project lead for a short meeting — mostly about the ad placement matrix for the coming weeks. Which districts the ground push would cover, which variety-show breaks carried the TVC, when the pre-movie reels would start running, and the rollout plan for the campaign song ‘OOPS’ — plus how the fifteen-second, thirty-second, and full cuts would be edited.

The brand side answered fluently, because these things genuinely had been prepared.

The meeting dissolved quickly.

Baek Eun-ah had just finished squaring her files when she heard Baek Si-on ask the CJ project manager:

“Right — one thing.”

“Go ahead.”

“The temperature-controlled cosmetics warehousing and delivery network around Seoul, on the Daehan logistics side — who’s running that currently?”

The project manager’s face went briefly blank.

“…Cosmetics? Not beverages?”

“Right.”

The project manager opened his mouth.

The project manager’s risk assessment, as computed

Can I answer this question? Yes. CJ Logistics and CJ Foods sit in different divisions, but the all-hands training had covered the briefing.

Should he answer this question? That was a different animal entirely.

“Director Baek” was now a title with weight inside the group. Weigh it, then speak.

“Director Baek… this is about…”

“There’s an independent cosmetics brand under evaluation.”

The project manager’s brain spun two full circles.

He could not make this call.

What Baek Si-on meant to CJ now — everyone inside the group understood. Bibigo’s North America sales numbers sat there in black and white, and how much of that growth curve was Baek Si-on’s doing, nobody said aloud, and the annual report said perfectly clearly.

“I’ll report this upward immediately.”

“Appreciated.”

The beverage-line lead made several calls after leaving the room.

How many, Baek Si-on didn’t know. Working backward from the result, they most likely ended at Lee Seon-ho.

Because twenty minutes later, Baek Si-on took a call from the Crown Prince of CJ himself.

Lee Seon-ho ↔ Baek Si-on · voice · twenty minutes after the question · logistics, secured

“I hear Director Baek is getting into cosmetics?”

“Nothing confirmed yet.”

“A domestic brand?”

“Yes.”

“Then no problem at all. CJ will fully back a Korean-made brand. Once your brand direction is set, connect directly with our logistics division.”

“Thank you.”

“Don’t mention it. Warehousing, logistics, last-mile delivery — we do it already. A bigger brand is good for everyone.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

The call didn’t run long.

And the matter was already settled.

After hanging up, Baek Si-on scrolled his contacts and dialed out.

Chairman Chung ↔ Baek Si-on · voice · the retail channel · a position, reserved

“Chairman Chung — I hope I’m not disturbing you?”

The voice on the other end never reached Baek Eun-ah’s ears. But judging by the rhythm of Baek Si-on’s side, the response came fast.

“Mm. I have an independent cosmetics brand in preparation. When there’s product, I want shelf space in Hyundai department stores and the duty-free channels — reserve a position in advance.”

Inside Hyundai’s system, Baek Si-on had long stopped being a poster boy. He was a stakeholder. So this call was never a request for a channel.

It was a partner notification: new table opening — an invitation to sit in.

The other side said its piece. Baek Si-on mm’d a few times, then smiled.

“Product quality, rest assured. The contract manufacturing will be Cosmax or Kolmar — the same factories as the first-tier brands already sitting in your department stores.”

A few more seconds of listening.

“Good — let’s leave it there. The teams will connect on specifics. Thank you, Chairman Chung.”

He hung up.

Two calls, start to finish: under ten minutes.

Warehousing and logistics: secured.

Offline channels: secured.

Baek Eun-ah sat next to Baek Si-on, slightly stunned.

She had assumed the after-party idea would need several rounds of research, several drafts of a business plan, several internal reviews before reaching execution. She had already built the timeline in her head: market research this month, a feasibility draft next month, supplier contact in month three.

Baek Si-on had just skipped a minimum of three months with two phone calls.

❝

Other people start a company by writing a business plan. Her cousin started one by phoning chaebols.

— the founding document, as witnessed

Off the phone, Baek Si-on ran the remaining items through his head.

Contract manufacturing. Core R&D. Brand positioning. Team assembly.

Any of it could be pushed further today — the R&D line could ride the supply chain introductions.

But he checked his watch.

Forget it.

An hour or two wasn’t the difference. Choi Jin-ri was waiting.

Baek Eun-ah, still feeling the aftershocks of “entrepreneurship can be done like THIS,” stared blankly as he stood.

“Cousin — not calling the factories?”

“Tomorrow.”

Baek Si-on headed for the door.

“You head back on your own.”

“Where are you going?”

“Dinner.”

“Without me?”

The door closed.

Footsteps faded down the corridor.

Baek Eun-ah was struck by a powerful, unsortable mess — no telling which of the two of them to curse first. She’d abandoned a soft landing in America, flown back across an ocean thinking she’d hold the rudder — and been demoted, once again, to the ignored tool-person original form.

So much for the trans-Pacific following.

So much for family devotion.

“Hmph… I curse you both to get meat stuck in your teeth tonight.”

She considered, decided it wasn’t enough, and added:

“In the back molars. The kind no amount of tongue can reach.”

❧ ❧ ❧

June 5

Past eight — twenty minutes late

Jongno-gu · Choi Jin-ri’s rented apartment

By the time Baek Si-on reached Choi Jin-ri’s rented apartment in Jongno-gu, it was past eight.

Twenty minutes late. The cause: two work calls taken the moment he left the studio.

He rang the bell.

The door opened fast — fast enough that he suspected she’d been standing behind it for five minutes.

She wore one of the shirts Baek Si-on had left here, nothing under it, hair thrown into a careless bun, not a trace of makeup on her face.

This was, probably, the best-looking her whole day would offer.

Before he could speak, she had already launched.

Both arms around his waist, tightened, face pressed into his chest.

Baek Si-on looked down.

“Hungry?”

“Charging~”

Her voice was muffled in his shirt.

Two seconds, and another line surfaced:

“In Busan I face a horde of zombies every day. I’m close to losing basic human sensation. Right now I’m recharging off your life force to hold this shape.”

“…That framing is a little horrifying.”

“Don’t care. Let me drain you a bit longer.”

Baek Si-on laughed.

One hand around her back, the other palm covering the back of her head, fingers kneading slowly along the edge of the bun.

The porch lamp warmed the two shadows folded into one.

The corridor was quiet. Somewhere next door, someone was making soup — a faint seaweed smell drifting over.

They stood like that a good while.

Then Baek Si-on remembered something.

“Where’s Brin?”

Choi Jin-ri’s face came up out of his chest.

“Oh no.”

“What?”

“Left him in Busan — my assistant’s looking after him.”

She smacked her own forehead.

“Left in too much of a rush. Forgot to bring him back.”

“So tonight it’s just the two of us?”

Choi Jin-ri’s expression blanked for a full second.

Then it landed — what exactly he was saying.

Her cheeks went red at visible speed.

One slap landed on his chest.

“Go wash your hands.”

“What I said was—”

“Go wash your hands!”

Baek Si-on looked down to change his shoes.

On the mat sat a brand-new pair of men’s house slippers, deep navy blue.

He said nothing about it. The corner of his mouth curved up a little anyway.

Choi Jin-ri stood with her back to him over by the dining table, fanning her own face with her palm.

Fanning fast.

Effect: doubtful.

❧ ❧ ❧

Dinner was delivery.

The dishes sat in white porcelain plates, plated with unexpected care.

Choi Jin-ri sat across from him, one hand propping her chin.

“Eat.”

Baek Si-on picked up his chopsticks and took a bite of the spicy stir-fried pork.

Not bad.

But the seasoning carried a subtle, industrial-standardization texture in its layers.

He drank a spoonful of the soybean paste soup.

Choi Jin-ri wasn’t eating much. Chin in hand, she watched him with a beaming face.

“You made this?”

“Mm.”

“All of it?”

“…Mm.”

Her chopsticks stirred once around her bowl.

“What?”

“It’s good.”

“Really?”

“Really.”

Choi Jin-ri beamed even harder.

Baek Si-on kept his head down and kept eating.

Some things, knowing is enough.

That she had decanted delivery food into proper plates, arranged it with real care, and claimed the cooking as her own — the gesture mattered far more than the chef.

He finished the last of the soup and set the spoon down.

“Got a toothpick?”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

“Something stuck?”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on pressed his tongue against his cheek.

“The kind no amount of tongue can reach.”

Choi Jin-ri, laughing, dug a box of dental floss out of a drawer and handed it over.

Baek Si-on took it and walked to the bathroom.

On the bathroom shelf, her skincare and cosmetics stood in a tidy row.

At the far end, one small gap had been left empty.

Exactly wide enough for his toothbrush, his face wash, his body wash, and his razor.

Measured out in advance, by the look of it.

By the time he finished brushing and came out, the living room was already reset.

Dishes back in place, table wiped clean, every trace of delivery exterminated.

Choi Jin-ri stood at the sink rinsing the last bowl, water roaring. She upended it into the rack, shut the tap, and pulled a paper towel across her hands.

❧ ❧ ❧

By the time she came out of the bathroom, Baek Si-on was already on the sofa.

She walked over and dropped onto him, wholesale — the back of her head on his thigh, curled up, feet tucked at the far end of the cushions.

Baek Si-on’s hand settled into her hair, automatically.

“How’s the Busan shoot going?”

“Fine. Running and screaming, every day.”

“Not tired?”

“Tired.”

She rolled over onto her back, so she could see his face.

“And you? How far did the cosmetics thing get?”

“Logistics and channels are down. CJ committed to warehousing and delivery; Hyundai department store and the duty-free channels reserved positions.”

“That fast?”

“Two phone calls.”

Choi Jin-ri’s expression became, by remarkable coincidence, the same expression Baek Eun-ah’s had worn hours earlier.

She finished with a small hmph — satisfaction or disdain for the glibness, undetermined.

A few seconds later:

“Oh right — what about ‘La La Land’?” she said, suddenly.

“Lots of homework.”

“Lots meaning how much?”

“Fifty-three point seven gigabytes of material.”

Choi Jin-ri tilted her head on his thigh, thinking.

“Can you actually get through all of it?”

“Perhaps.”

She sat up from his lap, folded her legs under herself on the sofa, turned sideways to him.

And suddenly felt that summoning Baek Si-on here to keep her company had been a little selfish.

Baek Si-on wasn’t looking at her. He picked up the phone from the coffee table and opened the music player.

A few scrolls. He found a track. Pressed play.

Piano spilled out of the phone speaker — light, slow, carrying jazz’s particular lazy sway.

One of the pieces from Damien’s training materials. The melody he’d had on repeat over the ocean.

Baek Si-on turned the volume to maximum.

And stood.

Choi Jin-ri tilted her head, not following.

Baek Si-on walked to the small open patch of floor in front of the sofa.

Turned to face her.

Bowed, slightly.

Right hand extended.

“Beautiful lady.”

“Would you do me the honor of completing today’s dance-class homework with me?”

Choi Jin-ri burst out laughing.

“You’re serious?”

“The director says no first day of shooting without it.”

Choi Jin-ri got up, put her hand in his palm.

Baek Si-on drew her up gently — one hand at her waist, the other holding her hand at shoulder height.

“I can’t dance,” Choi Jin-ri said, small-voiced.

“Neither can I.”

“Then what do we do?”

“Step on each other, presumably.”

The two of them swayed slowly around the small patch of living room.

Baek Si-on tried to turn her once; her toe ran over his slipper.

“Ah — sorry.”

“It’s fine.”

“Did it hurt?”

“You’re light.”

Choi Jin-ri kept her eyes nailed to her own feet, terrified of another strike.

“Head up,” Baek Si-on reminded her.

“I’m scared of stepping on you.”

“Then step.”

She hesitated — and raised her head.

And so the two of them turned in slow circles around a warmly lit living room, folded together, to a jazz piano track played off one phone speaker.

Choi Jin-ri suddenly laughed.

“What?” Baek Si-on asked.

“You stepped on me.”

“…Sorry.”

“Didn’t you say step?”

“That license was for you stepping on me.”

“Why?”

“Because I’m the teacher.”
