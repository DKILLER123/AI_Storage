Chapter Sixty-Nine

The Handbook for Getting Rich Off Commercial Gigs

August 2 Weekend · no show Yeonnam-dong · Baek Si-on's flat Two versions of last night's story, and a plate of ribs

What Baek Si-on had done at KBS Music Bank spread through the newcomer community overnight.

The dissemination path was textbook.

First the people who'd been there on the shared floor told their own managers; the managers told the other managers at their companies; the other managers told the artists they managed; and those artists told their trainee group chats.

And of course, in the retelling, a great many non-existent details were added.

Including but not limited to "Baek Si-on gazed at Irene with extremely tender eyes," "Irene blushed on the spot," "the two made eye contact for a full five seconds"...

Though the actual situation was that Irene had a half-mouthful of unchewed bread at the time, choking so hard she nearly rolled her eyes.

But that didn't matter.

By the next morning, the story had branched into at least two versions.

Version one: Baek Si-on sunbae is a third-generation chaebol and hands out a hundred thousand won in cash to every junior he meets.

Version two: Baek Si-on sunbae and Red Velvet's leader Irene have taken a shine to each other.

August 2 Saturday · 6:00 a.m. MBC · Music Core shared floor Expectation fermenting all morning, then the let-down

Today was Saturday.

MBC Music Core promotion day.

From six in the morning, the various newcomer groups arriving at the MBC building, once they'd changed into their practice clothes, pinned on their name tags, and walked into the shared floor, did their first thing not discuss the day's stage blocking.

But crane their necks and peer down the corridor.

"Is Baek Si-on sunbae coming today?"

"No idea — is he on the number-one contender list?"

"If he comes, will he hand out pocket money again?"

"Maybe this time it's an album?"

"An album works too. An album the sunbae handed out personally — how much could it fetch on a resale site?"

"…You've got real ambitions."

The expectation fermented in the corridor all morning.

Then the disappointment came.

Baek Si-on didn't appear at the MBC building.

Not late.

He simply didn't come at all.

Music Core's PD confirmed the news at ten: Baek Si-on had come down with an acute case of gastroenteritis and would not be participating in this week's recording.

On the shared floor, several newcomers who'd been calculating their pocket money looked the way a person does when told their year-end bonus has been cancelled.

August 2 Late morning Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong Ribs, a confirmation text, and a very quiet morning

He was sitting at the table shirtless, eating the ribs Professor Yoon Hye-ja had braised the night before.

Baek Eun-ah sat across from him, phone screen still lit with the confirmation text from MBC.

"Cousin, MBC replied. They understand, and wish you a speedy recovery."

"Mm."

He hadn't gotten arrogant, and he hadn't lost the desire to stay on good terms with the stations.

He just no longer wanted to endure a whole day of not a drop of food, just to keep his jawline sharp in front of a high-definition lens.

And the wallet that had been stripped bare yesterday genuinely couldn't produce the thick stack of cash needed to maintain human relations today.

Of course, those were only the life-level reasons.

The most fundamental reason was still the fear of disappointing people.

This morning, AGB Nielsen had released the previous night's official ratings.

KBS Music Bank: average rating 4.0 percent.

The peak appeared during Baek Si-on's stage, live peak 6.8 percent — a figure that sent a small earthquake through the variety and entertainment departments of every network.

And Baek Si-on knew it very well himself.

The reason the uncles and aunties had held the remote and switched over on the dot yesterday was entirely that veneer of social voyeurism — "Venice shortlist," "grassroots thug flips his fate."

The public's curiosity was an extremely single-use consumable.

Go again today to Music Core, and those uncles and aunties would absolutely not watch a second time.

The ratings would inevitably fall in a straight line.

Once he failed to replicate last night's ratings run, the "carrier of the ratings myth" halo on him would peel off in an instant.

Instead, it would lay bare his fatal weakness — no hardcore fanbase, no fandom block machine to pad the numbers.

Yesterday was a god descending.

Today would be a flash in the pan.

A smart man always knows to leave the table when he's holding the biggest hand and the most chips.

August 4 Daytime Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong A dark-green booklet that arrived on schedule

The passport arrived.

Baek Eun-ah went to pick it up from the Mapo branch and set the dark-green booklet down on the dining table when she came back.

Baek Si-on opened it and glanced at the ID photo.

The photographer was right.

The man really is better-looking than the photo.

August 5 Evening Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong · dining table A stylist contract, and a trip to Paris or Los Angeles

Signed the formal contract with Park Ji-hun.

Same place — the dining table in Yeonnam-dong. This table had been signing a lot of contracts lately.

The contract was drafted by Baek Si-on himself, with Lawyer Choi running an eye over the wording. No problems.

Baek Si-on / Park Ji-hun — stylist engagement · core terms

Clause One · Base

Monthly base salary ₩3,000,000.

Clause Two · Bonus

Should a styling photograph catch fire on social media, an additional bonus whose amount is calculated on the scale of the reach.

Clause Three · Study

One annual study trip to Paris or Los Angeles, two to four weeks, with tuition, room and board, and airfare all reimbursed.

Park Ji-hun swallowed a very audible mouthful of saliva at the final clause.

Park Ji-hun's mouth rushed open at reading the final line, and he gulped down a very audible swallow.

Paris.

Los Angeles.

His longest business trip had been a styling gig with an indie band in Busan. Round trip by KTX, and he'd packed his own lunchbox.

Paris's Make Up For Ever academy, Los Angeles's Hollywood film-makeup school — those things only existed for him in the titles of the YouTube videos in his phone's favourites folder.

He signed.

And after signing, he mentally kowtowed hard to the version of himself who'd chased out of that Hapjeong-dong salon.

August 7 Early morning Baek Si-on's flat, Yeonnam-dong · bedroom One month on the platform, and a balance that won't move

"Way Back Home" had been up on the various streaming platforms for exactly one month.

A song sitting in first place for half a month — the royalties piling up from enormous streaming plays and downloads had to be an astronomical figure.

Baek Si-on woke early.

Lying in bed, staring at the ceiling for a bit, then reaching a hand out from under the pillow to grab his phone.

Opened the banking app.

Balance.

Took a look.

No change.

Closed it.

Scrolled his news feed a while.

7:15 — opened the banking app again.

Balance.

Still no change.

Closed it.

7:45 — opened it a third time.

The same number.

Baek Si-on pressed the phone to his chest and stared at the ceiling.

8:30 — he opened the banking app once more.

Fourth time.

The balance hadn't grown a new digit.

Baek Si-on walked out of the bedroom in his shorts, phone gripped in his hand, slippers slapping as he shuffled to the guest-room door.

Knocked twice.

Baek Eun-ah's muffled voice came from inside.

"What…"

"When does the streaming revenue land?"

Silence from behind the door for a moment.

Then came the sound of bodies shifting, the rustle of blankets, a phone falling off the bed and being picked up again.

After a string of little movements, Baek Eun-ah opened the door.

Phone held up.

She'd pulled up the electronic version of the distribution contract she'd signed with LOEN, scrolled to the settlement clause, and pointed at one line for Baek Si-on to see.

"Settlement cycle: T+2. Streaming revenue generated monthly is aggregated and confirmed by the platform at the end of the following month, then transferred to the rights holder's designated account by the 15th of the month after that."

Baek Si-on narrowed his eyes at the line.

T+2.

A song that went up on July seventh.

July's revenue, aggregated at the end of August, arriving by September 15 at the latest.

"In other words."

"At the earliest, September — before he'd get a single won."

"You didn't notice this when you signed the contract?" Baek Si-on asked.

Baek Eun-ah opened her mouth.

Of course she'd read that clause when she signed.

But at the time all her attention had been on the split ratio and the recommendation-slot resources. A clause like "settlement cycle," one that would "arrive sooner or later anyway," she'd skimmed and skipped.

"I… I thought it was standard industry practice, nothing worth negotiating…"

"Standard industry practice."

Baek Si-on repeated the four words, turned, and walked to the sofa to start running the numbers.

The two hundred-odd million won in World Cup winnings had looked like a huge sum.

But it couldn't hold up against the way he spent.

The new media investment had signed a 150-million-won agreement. Though the first tranche had only transferred 50 million, the rest had to sit in his account, ready to be released at any time.

Han Teuk's editing fee.

The car.

Legal fees.

The cash handed out in the Music Bank waiting room that day.

Kim Jae-kyung's handmade gown.

And the scattered everyday spend.

He added all the funds he could still move, and arrived at an extremely cold conclusion.

—Almost broke.

At a moment when all of Korea believed he was raking in money hand over fist, when some people online were even mocking him for "climbing the class ladder on the back of one hit song," he barely had the money left to book a flight to Venice.

Reality was just that bare-boned.

"Eun-ah."

"What now…"

Baek Eun-ah had barely lain back down for two minutes before being hauled up again.

"Is there any commercial gig I can take?"

The drowsiness was cut in half by that sentence.

She sat up from the blanket, staring at Baek Si-on standing in the doorway.

Six days ago he'd turned down the head PD's barbecue invitation at KBS, turned down the offer to keep promoting the next week, even turned down Music Core's recording request — holding his posture higher than the Blue House.

And now he was asking if there were commercial gigs.

"…I'll look."

She climbed out of bed, slipped on her slippers, and carried her phone and laptop to the coffee table in the living room, starting to search.

Browsing performance-booking platforms.

Browsing the tender notices for local festivals.

Browsing the chat records of a few manager mutual-aid groups in KakaoTalk.

Half an hour later.

"It's exactly the season for all the summer festivals right now. Beer festivals run by local governments, beach music festivals, civic culture festivals — all of them are looking for hot singers to draw the crowd."

Baek Eun-ah swiped the screen twice.

"At your current heat, the quote's around twenty million won a gig. Three songs, plus a short MC-hosted interaction segment."

Baek Si-on thought about it.

Twenty million a gig.

Three songs.

"Way Back Home" counted as one, but that was currently his only song.

The other two could only be covers of other people's work. The old A'ST1 tracks he had no rights to perform.

But no matter what.

Twenty million a gig, real cash goin' in hand.

"Cousin, how many are you planning to take?"

"Unlimited. Whatever I can get, I take."

"Good."

Baek Eun-ah looked down, opened a new document in the memo app.

She titled it Commercial gig schedule.

Then started dialling, one number after another.
