Chapter Ninety-Six

Bong Joon-ho’s Camera, Song Kang-ho’s Script

After Hyundai Seoul already dark KB Kookmin · then a private room Ten billion. Twenty minutes. A table that was not a family dinner.

KB Bank’s signing was faster than Hyundai’s.

Contract, ten billion. Yesterday Baek Eun-ah and Seo Eun-ju had checked every clause to the last punctuation mark.

Baek Si-on went in, sat, signed, stamped, shook hands, came out.

Front to back, under twenty minutes.

The shoot was set for the morning after next. KB’s brand studio. Style: “professional, trustworthy.”

He thought those two adjectives would not be wrong in any bank’s brief. He did not say so.

When they came out Seoul was already dark. A line of lamps. The three of them had barely sat into Seo Eun-ju’s Audi A4 when his phone rang.

Display: Uncle.

He picked up.

Baek Jeong-hun → Baek Si-on

Si-on-ah.

Cups and plates colliding. A few men laughing low.

Eaten yet?

Not yet.

Come eat. I’ll introduce you to a few people.

“Who?”

You’ll know when you get here. Bring Eun-ah too.

No room to ask more. Shop name. Address. Hung up.

Eun-ah turned at once.

“Who?”

He put the phone down.

“Your dad wants us to eat.”

“Me too?”

“Mm.”

She blinked. That meant it was not an ordinary father-daughter family meal. It was a table.

Twenty-some minutes later. Eun-ah followed him and pushed the private-room door.

Hoh.

Four people at the table. Baek Jeong-hun. Park Chan-wook. Bong Joon-ho. Song Kang-ho.

Eun-ah’s eyes went over Si-on’s shoulder and a very rude, extremely accurate phrase rose on its own — the full banquet.

Jeong-hun was already up, face a little red, clearly well into the drink, still clear. He lifted a hand toward the three at the table.

“This is my nephew. Baek Si-on.”

A tip of the head.

“This is my daughter. Baek Eun-ah.”

The two of them bowed almost together.

“Sunbaes.”

As proper as proper got.

Song Kang-ho smiled first, waved a hand.

Song Kang-ho

Actor · Memories of Murder, The Host, The Attorney

Real · a private room, pork, a rice chest not yet named

He smiles first and tells them not to be so formal. The weight comes later, when the chopsticks are down and the film is two people and a chest of rice.

“Don’t be so formal. Sit.”

Park Chan-wook only nodded, light. The look was calm.

Park Chan-wook

Director · Joint Security Area, Oldboy, Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance

Real · DGK, unofficially, over charcoal

He does not waste a greeting. He spends it. He has pulled Bong Joon-ho home from abroad to sit as proof of intent, and he looks at a twenty-two-year-old Best Actor the way he looks at a cut: to see where it will bleed.

Bong Joon-ho looked at Si-on twice, then smiled.

Bong Joon-ho

Director · Memories of Murder, The Host, Snowpiercer

Real · the man the table will call Uncle Bong

Venice Best Actor, a little thinner than the press stills. He will ask for the song twice. Then he will change seats.

“The Venice Best Actor himself. A little thinner than the news photos.”

Si-on smiled too.

“Photos eat angles, Director Bong.”

That line, and the air at the table loosened a little. The cousins sat. Eun-ah sat straighter than she had for a principal. Si-on poured himself Jinro, turned his head, voice down, to Jeong-hun.

“Uncle. How did this table get built?”

Jeong-hun, also down, a little drunk pride.

“Director Park wants to pull me into DGK.”

DGK. Directors Guild of Korea. The name was a little foggy for Eun-ah. For Si-on it was too clear. One of the most core, most voiced organisations in Korean film directing. To show intent, Park Chan-wook had specifically called Bong Joon-ho home from abroad to sit. Song Kang-ho’s appearing was pure accident. He had heard his good brother Bong was suddenly back, and had gone to the airport himself as a free driver.

Si-on listened, nodded. Understood. A very high-spec “admission.” As for why Park Chan-wook would go to this much trouble to pull Jeong-hun into DGK — the logic was close to politics pulling in a new-generation assemblyman. Jeong-hun had just taken Lion of the Future, a globally certified top new director. Taking that new blood in, the organisation’s face shone. Not to mention the free gift of a Venice Best Actor nephew.

Si-on sat to the side, quietly drank a cup, then looked up at Jeong-hun’s red face, excited and a little overwhelmed. He basically had it. Nine out of ten, already yes. Normal. Joining DGK meant Jeong-hun going from “independent film director” to “a piece of Korean cinema.” That admission was not something a festival cup could fully replace. Anyone in his place would, in high probability, find it hard to refuse. Being able to sit at the same table as Park Chan-wook and Bong Joon-ho, call them brothers — for an independent director who a few months ago had been squatting in Anseong mud drawing storyboards — was itself a jump of rank.

✿ ✿ ✿

Wine past three rounds. The pork on the charcoal had been changed four times by Eun-ah. Jeong-hun and Park Chan-wook had got onto the ethical edge of documentary lenses. Bong put in a line now and then. Song Kang-ho and Si-on were in charge of eating.

The air loosened to a critical point. Song Kang-ho put the chopsticks down, wiped oil from his mouth with a napkin, turned to Si-on.

“Si-on-ah.”

“Yes, sunbae.”

“Next film. Any plan.”

Si-on’s chopsticks stopped in the air.

“Still looking.”

“Stop looking.”

“Sunbae’s meaning.”

Song Kang-ho smiled.

“A couple of days ago I sat with Director Lee Joon-ik.”

Director Lee Joon-ik. Credits: The King and the Clown, Hope, and so on. Si-on’s eyes moved a little.

“He has a project. In prep a long time. Joseon royal house.”

“A father and a son. One an old man with the country, the rites, the kingship, the face, all on his shoulders. One a son watched from small, expected by everyone, driven mad step by step.”

“Not the big-set court piece. Not much war. Not a power thriller. The core is two people.”

“Father.”

“Son.”

“And that rice chest.”

Joseon royal house. Father, son. Rites. A crown prince driven mad. A rice chest. Those keywords in combination pointed at only one answer. Sado.

If it was that film. Who did Song Kang-ho want him to play. The crown prince. In the original track that role had been Yoo Ah-in’s. Not an ordinary good part. Core resource enough to let an actor finish a career jump. But if Song Kang-ho was speaking it now, in his own mouth — the project was not fully locked. Or at least the cast list still had give.

“If you’re interested, we two do it together? Old man and boy.”

Song Kang-ho said it easy. The weight of the sentence was not. In Korean film, playing opposite Song Kang-ho was itself a resource. The problem was time. Si-on was going to Los Angeles in a few days. Kobe had already agreed to a video; the MV had to land. Scooter Braun had to be met. North American distribution, splits, promo — every line to talk. And Sado was not a project you could squeeze time into at will.

“Sunbae. Roughly when do they shoot.”

“People have to be set soon. Director Lee can’t wait too long.”

“If it’s soon, I may not be able.”

Si-on said it straight.

“In a few days I have to go to Los Angeles.”

“L.A.?”

Bong’s brow went up first.

“To do what.”

“Shoot an MV.”

“New song?”

“Mm.”

“Put it on. Let’s hear.”

This time it was Si-on’s turn to pause. He looked at the film old-hands at the table. Park Chan-wook, fifty-one, art thrillers. Song Kang-ho, forty-seven, small people at the bottom. Bong Joon-ho, forty-five, social satire. Baek Jeong-hun, forty-three, domestic-violence tragedy. Could this bunch hear what rock was.

But Song Kang-ho had already said it, and even Bong was showing a little interest. Si-on thought. Forget it. Whether they could hear it was their business. Whether he dared put it on was his. He took from his pocket the iPhone 6 Plus bought today, swiped twice, found the demo recorded today. Opened. Played.

Baek Si-on · demo · a private room, pork still on the fire

0:00—

Boil the broken regret into a miracle—

The intro came out and the air in the room changed. A synth riff with distortion split hard into the wine-smell and hot dishes. Kick down after. Clap cutting in. Arrangement logic clearly aimed at a stadium and a large frame, yanking these film people in one pull out of “table chat” into another space.

Si-on had thought they would not get it. He was wrong. They might not know every track in a DAW. They knew where this music wanted to take a person. That sense had little to do with being a professional musician and a lot to do with being a first-rate storyteller.

His half-loose, after-wine voice came out, and pictures started almost at once in several heads. Bong thought of himself. Thought of being softly shut out by the right-wing air and the capital climate at home, having to look overseas for a living road. Thought of himself at the Snowpiercer screening, fake-crying, lying, working the room, finally rather giving up a larger North American release than lose his director’s cut. Did those not line up with the lyric.

Boil the broken regret into a miracle.

Step the darkest mock into a stair.

Do not stop. Until we become legend.

Last second. Distorted guitar and kick cut together. The room went quiet.

Park Chan-wook first.

“This song… has some kick.”

Said light. The eyes much brighter than before. Song Kang-ho nodded after.

“Sounds like the kind they put in a big stadium. Tens of thousands yelling it.”

Jeong-hun’s reaction was the most direct. He stared at Si-on as if looking at someone he had just met.

“You wrote the words?”

“Mm.”

“When?”

“On the plane home.”

Jeong-hun did not follow. Only looked at Si-on once more, then put his eyes back on the grill. Only Bong did not speak. Si-on noticed the silence.

“Director Bong.”

Bong looked up.

“Play it again.”

Si-on said nothing, tapped the glass. Played again. Second time. Pork still hissing on the charcoal, the smell still there, but nobody’s attention was on eating. Another time through. The remainder gone. Bong stared at the iPhone screen in Si-on’s hand a few seconds.

“Si-on.”

“Yes, Director Bong.”

“When you wrote this, what were you thinking of?”

Si-on had been about to say Kobe. He looked at Bong’s eyes. Something moving in them. Not curiosity. Resonance. He swallowed “Kobe.” Turned a corner.

“…At first, people a lot of others said were finished, who still would not stop. Later I thought of my uncle. And of sunbaes like Director Bong.”

Bong’s eyes moved.

“Me.”

“Mm.”

Si-on did not open it. No need. Bong heard it. He looked at Si-on, stood.

“Jeong-hun-ah. Swap with me.”

Jeong-hun froze a second, still stood, swapped. Bong sat beside Si-on.

“You little shit. Young as you are. How do you know this?”

The eyes on the round face narrowed to two seams. After the laugh he put out his right hand and slapped the back of Si-on’s head. Si-on’s neck bounced forward. He did not duck. Did not mean to.

“A lot of people write heat as a pose. This isn’t that.”

“You know what a maker fears most. Not poor. Not no one watching. Other people, with ‘for your own good,’ shaving off the most important thing bit by bit.”

As he said it his hand was already up, drawing in the air.

“Shave five minutes. Then ten. Then add a line of VO. Then make you explain. Then make you fold. The finished thing comes out, box office maybe better, audience maybe more, and it is already not yours.”

“You get me?”

Si-on nodded.

“I get it.”

“You really get it?”

Bong stared at him, liking him more the more he looked, then turned.

“How are you shooting this MV?”

“Haven’t thought it through.”

“Haven’t?”

“Mm. The male lead’s in Los Angeles. Location’s set. Story isn’t.”

“Then that’s convenient.”

Bong’s hand up, pointing at himself.

“I’ll do it.”

“What?”

“I’ll shoot it for you.”

“What?”

First thought: he had misheard. Second: Bong was drunk. Third: Bong was not drunk, he was joking. Bong’s face did not look like a joke.

“True or not.”

Eun-ah turned her head. She had been on her phone, looking at Director Hwang Soo-ah’s reply.

KakaoTalk · Hwang Soo-ah

Hwang Soo-ah

Han-teuk already told me. I can take it. Overseas shoot is fine. Send the demo and a rough concept first.

Bong looked at her once.

“What, no?”

Eun-ah: “…”

On the phone, IU’s regular MV director saying yes. On the table, Bong Joon-ho himself saying I’ll do it. This was no longer a two-choice. This was the last boss question on the suneung, and the teacher who wrote it standing up to say don’t bother, I’ll give you the answer.

Si-on stared at Bong a few seconds.

“Director Bong. You’re serious.”

“Do I look like I’m joking?”

Bong smiled.

“I happen to want to change my head lately. Film projects pressing. Script’s making me sick. A three- or four-minute MV, as a change of head.”

He tapped Si-on’s phone.

“And this song of yours. Worth shooting properly.”

Song Kang-ho laughed out beside them.

“Si-on-ah. Say yes fast. You know how many singers could beg and not get this.”

Park Chan-wook, rare, put in a line.

“This is not a begging problem.”

Jeong-hun sitting to the side, two tenths of the drink gone, looking at Bong, then at Si-on. Face complicated. Like “is my nephew a bit too much,” and like “my nephew actually showed up.”

What rose in Si-on’s head first was not being overwhelmed. It was admiration. A director like Bong, the strength had never been only “can shoot.” It was almost owning the ability to shoot a second layer of meaning out of any material. Other people shoot a train, it is a train. He shoots a train, it is a class society. Other people shoot a monster, it is a monster. He shoots a monster, it is the absurd reality after a whole system fails. That way of pinching story, genre, metaphor, weather together was not technique. It was gift. If you ranked working film directors in the world from fire to flop, Bong Joon-ho was absolutely the fire-exploded rank.

The problem was exactly that word, fire. This MV was not art expression. It had a clear commercial job. Put plainly: it had to hit spread, topic, traffic, Billboard. And Kobe under Bong’s lens would, in high probability, not only be Kobe. He would shoot a gym at four in the morning, and on the way a street-cleaner sweeping at four. He would shoot the old shoulder on Kobe’s jumper, and the lids of a night-shift clerk nodding behind a counter. He would shoot L.A. morning fog, and a homeless man under a freeway. He would let the word “legend,” from one person’s fight, extend into a survival fable inside a whole social structure. The finished thing would certainly be good. Good enough for a Berlin short Silver Bear. The problem was that it might, with what Si-on wanted in his head, not be unrelated — only south and north.

The thought was actually very offensive. A twenty-two-year-old junior, sitting in front of a table of the top of Korean film, thinking: Director Bong, your style may not fit my project. If Jeong-hun heard that, he would probably throw Si-on out of this room for Bong.

Si-on still opened his mouth. He did not hard-say “not a fit.” He was not stupid enough to teach Bong how to shoot. He only stepped back one step, and changed it for a more Korean, more decent phrasing.

“Director Bong.”

“Mm.”

“Would this wrong you?”

The line out, the air at the table paused, subtle. Because in Korea, a director at Bong’s level shooting an MV was not four characters of “crossover play.” It meant a drop in rank. Not an objective drop. Good work did not split by vessel; a three-minute MV was not a rank below a two-hour film. But in Korean film, once “Director Bong shot an MV” went out, the press’s first move would not be “the MV will look good.” It would be “is Bong Joon-ho short of money.” Or the harder “is Bong Joon-ho out of scripts.”

Si-on knew that. So he asked that.

“Kid.”

Bong looked at him, suddenly smiled.

“What I heard in that sentence of yours is not worry for me. It’s worry — I’ll shoot it into some social-structure fable, or something too heavy with fate. Right.”

“That’s not—”

Bong waved, cut him off.

“Enough. Don’t act in front of me.”

Si-on did not try to round it further.

“I’ll tell you. Commercial purpose and art expression were never opposites. They’re two faces of one coin. People who shoot those two into dragging each other usually haven’t truly got either.”

It sounded very posed. In any ordinary director’s mouth the people at this table would probably roll their eyes as a group. Bong clearly had the standing to say it.

“Your purpose is not only to make an MV — you want to borrow a giant’s traffic, identity, and global reach, and push this song to a height a Korean singer usually cannot touch.”

Si-on nodded once.

“Yes.”

“Then. Who is that person?”

“Kobe.”

“Kobe…”

Bong ran the name through his mouth. Chewed two seconds.

“If it’s Kobe… then the core weather of this MV is not ‘a young singer singing heat.’”

“It is—”

“An unfinished legend, walking toward a legend already finished.”

When that sentence landed in the room a current went up Si-on’s spine. Unfinished legend. Baek Si-on. Twenty-two, Venice Best Actor, singer, just starting. Decades of road ahead, everything still growing, everything still rising, nothing yet fixed. Finished legend. Kobe. Thirty-six, five rings. Most of his legend already written; what remained was how he would put a period on the story. One still climbing. One still fighting a downhill. Two people on their own timelines, fourteen years of age and a whole Pacific between them, meeting in the same song. Because the line in the lyric held for both at once —

Do not stop. Until we become legend.

For Si-on, that line was a start. For Kobe, that line was an end he would not admit. Bong had caught the MV’s soul in one sentence. Si-on folded.

“Then I’ll trouble Director Bong.”

That out, Bong’s face changed. Not unhappy. A kind of… how to put it. Mouth still hanging a smile, brows gathering a little toward the middle, chin up two millimetres.

“The way you said that, it’s as if I were rushing to work for you.”

Mm. The tsundere was on. Song Kang-ho across from them watched this and laughed out, pointed at Si-on with chopsticks.

“Si-on-ah. You’re still too young. Don’t give him that empty stuff now. Call him uncle once. He’ll say yes at once.”

Jeong-hun beside them giving Si-on eye-signals like mad. The meaning already clear enough not to need translation — call. Call now. Si-on did not make a show of it. He filled the soju cup in front of him. Stood. Bent. Both hands on the cup, posture very correct.

“Uncle Bong.”

He went on.

“Help a junior.”

Bong’s face finally opened all the way. No longer that half-smile appraisal. A real smile, the kind of an elder looking at a junior and finding him agreeable.

“Since you called me uncle.”

He picked the cup up, touched Si-on’s.

“Would not helping even make sense?”

Said it, touched, drank. Laughter at the table up again. Jeong-hun poured a round for everyone. Song Kang-ho raised.

“To Si-on’s new song—”

Everyone raised.

“Geonbae!”
