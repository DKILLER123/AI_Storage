Chapter Two Hundred One

The Justification for Hand-Building a Nuclear Bomb

March 30 · night · one apartment, one van, and the floor above A caller ID that read fire equipment · three rings and an answer by the wrong person · Milan, a hotel room, and a sentence built entirely of true words · seventeen unwritten sentences in a driver’s seat · one box of ice cream, two spoons, half a sheet mask · a bath towel that destroyed a rehearsed speech · and the doctrine of the hand-built bomb Jin-ri’s kitchen → the underground garage → Yoo In-na’s living room → the sofa, both ends of it

While Baek Si-on showered, Jin-ri was still clearing the table.

Dinner tonight had been a success. Judging at least by the speed of Baek Si-on’s eating — a major success.

The sound of water carried out of the bathroom, even and continuous, like some extremely reassuring white noise.

It occurred to her, suddenly, that today was the best day of her twenty-one years.

Yesterday had been her birthday.

Today was better than her birthday.

Because yesterday was the day he flew back.

Today was the day he was still here.

Jin-ri set the last plate in the sink, glanced down at Blin circling her ankles, and started planning tomorrow’s shopping.

The memo, as dictated before the shower stopped

Men’s face wash.

Men’s toothbrush.

Slippers — one size up.

Towel — dark, so it never trades places with hers.

Clothes — at minimum one set of home clothes: one T-shirt, one pair of sweatpants. Underwear, she didn’t dare buy. Socks, no problem.

What these objects would mean for the household, she knew exactly: from today, it was no longer a home of one. Her face burned, and she typed faster.

She had settled onto the sofa with her memo open when the phone on the tea table — Baek Si-on’s phone — buzzed.

Her fingers stopped. She leaned over and looked.

The caller ID read: FIRE EQUIPMENT.

She stared at the four syllables for two seconds.

Fire equipment?

What did that mean?

Staff?

The fire department?

Or… who?

The water was still running. Jin-ri didn’t move. The ringtone played itself to the end and stopped on its own.

She looked back down at her own memo, meaning to continue with “toothbrush” — and the phone on the tea table buzzed again.

Still fire equipment.

She looked toward the bathroom. The water hadn’t stopped.

Jin-ri hesitated — and again, didn’t answer.

When the third call came in, she finally picked the phone up and slid to answer.

Lee Ji-eun → Baek Si-on’s phone · voice · the third ring · answered by Jin-ri

“Hello — he—”

“Yah, Baek—”

Two voices collided, each delivered half a sentence, and stopped at the same time. Two seconds of quiet. Then both sides spoke almost together.

“Ji-eun unnie?”

“Peach?”

Ji-eun unnie equals fire equipment. Fire means extinguishing. What fire?

Whose fire?

At the other end of the line, Lee Ji-eun completed her own assessment inside the same few seconds: Peach had answered Baek Si-on’s phone. At this hour. In this setting. After three full rings. She declined to think one step further.

“Is something urgent, unnie? Sunbae… he can’t come to the phone right now — I’ll pass along anything you need.”

Jin-ri did not say the words “taking a shower.” Too explicit. Her face was not yet thick enough for that.

“It’s not exactly urgent.”

Lee Ji-eun gave a small laugh down the line. Jin-ri believed it one hundred percent not.

“A few days ago in Milan, he and I were working on an arrangement in a hotel room until pretty late. I finished the piece these past few days — I thought he could listen tonight. But since it’s inconvenient, then — Peach, I’ll trouble you to pass it along.”

Jin-ri felt her ears catching every single word. And felt how none of the words was the point. Milan. Night. A hotel room. Only the two of them. Until pretty late.

“Okay. I’ll pass it along.”

“Thanks, Peach. Sleep early.”

The call ended. The screen went dark. The water in the bathroom was still running.

At some point Blin had jumped onto the sofa and bumped his head against her arm.

Jin-ri looked down at the cat, then at the phone. All those warm, soft things in her chest had just been lightly stabbed by somebody.

She put the phone back exactly where it had been. Then she stood and walked into the kitchen.

Jin-ri only wanted a glass of water.

At least, that was what she told herself. Inside the kitchen, she didn’t take a glass. She dialed Goo Hara.

Jin-ri → Goo Hara · voice · the kitchen consultation · two rings

“What’s wrong?”

Hara’s voice was lazy, and her opening move was entirely disreputable.

“Baek Si-on can’t perform?”

“……”

She had already been wronged enough. This sentence stunned her mid-grievance.

“No!”

“You can still shout that loudly, so apparently not that problem. And here I thought I’d be making an overnight pharmacy run for you.”

“Unnie!”

“Fine, fine. What is it?”

Jin-ri inhaled and emptied the entire contents of the earlier call in one pour.

“Wait— who did you say? Ji-eun?”

“…Mm.”

A pause on the line. Jin-ri thought Hara was digesting. Hara had finished digesting. She was choosing wording.

“Jin-ri-ya. Don’t panic first.”

“I’m not panicking.”

“Your voice is one octave from an electric toothbrush.”

“But she said Milan, a hotel room, at night—”

“Listen to me. Everything she said absolutely has exaggeration in it, so the pictures in your head are not necessarily real. And the very worst thing you can do is wait for Baek Si-on to come out and charge at him like a betrayed wife — what exactly is going on between you and Lee Ji-eun? What did you two do in a hotel room at night?”

“I wasn’t going to ask like that,”

Jin-ri protested, small and aggrieved.

“You aren’t now. But the second he comes out and your eyes go red, your mouth will ask by itself.”

“……”

Jin-ri sagged against the kitchen counter, deflated.

“What you do now is not invent their screenplay, and it is not put him on trial. You tell him exactly what happened, in its original form.”

“Original form?”

“Exactly. I’ll go find out what’s happening on Ji-eun’s side. In short — hold.”

Jin-ri closed her eyes. When she opened them, her voice had settled by half.

“Unnie. Got it.”

“Good girl.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Elsewhere.

When Lee Ji-eun hung up, her expression was very calm. Calm enough to freeze the back of Jeong Han-teuk’s neck.

He checked the back seat through the rearview mirror. She had the phone face-down on her lap, face turned to the window; neon slid across her, and made her look more like the character than the character looked.

Han-teuk opened his mouth. Closed it. He had roughly seventeen sentences drafted, and each one’s survival probability was under three seconds.

The van, after the call

Sentences drafted in the front seat — seventeen.

Survival probability per sentence — under three seconds.

Sentence released — “Home?”

Counter received — “Or should we go to the Gangnam Tax Office?”

Follow-up communication — one text, filed to Baek Si-on: “Ji-eun’s mood is terrible. Extreme danger. Pray for yourself.”

“Am I a manager,” the signalman asked the empty garage, “or a battlefield communications officer.”

Seoul’s night roads were quiet. Traffic lights ticked across the windshield, red to green, green to yellow, back to red. Lee Ji-eun watched the window the whole way.

She knew precisely what she had just said.

Because if she said nothing, Jin-ri would grow comfortable believing that Baek Si-on’s world contained only her.

Lee Ji-eun did not want her confirming that so easily.

It wasn’t fair.

At least — it wasn’t fair to her.

❝

Baek Si-on was not a man who would become somebody’s exclusive safe zone just because a Lee Ji-eun stepped away from his side. The competition was structural. Withdrawing changed nothing except her own absence from the card table.

— the case for staying seated, argued to the window

And besides —

He and Jin-ri had never gone public. Were not married.

So why retreat?

Retreat for whom to see?

The car turned into her complex’s underground garage. Han-teuk parked and looked over.

“We’re here.”

Lee Ji-eun picked up her bag and pushed the door open. Both feet on the ground, she paused, and looked back at him.

“Han-teuk oppa.”

“Mm?”

“Thank you for today.”

“…It wasn’t hard.”

Han-teuk watched her walk into the elevator, and sat in the driver’s seat thinking for a long time.

❧ ❧ ❧

Lee Ji-eun did not go home.

In the elevator she pressed not her own floor, but the one above. Yoo In-na’s place. She knew the password better than her own.

When the door opened, Yoo In-na was curled into the sofa wearing a sheet mask. At the sound of the door she turned; a pair of eyes under the mask scanned Lee Ji-eun once, down and up.

“With that face, you’re not here to borrow seasoning.”

“……”

“Spit it out.”

Lee Ji-eun came in, changed shoes, and sat down beside her.

“I just called Baek Si-on.”

“And?”

“Peach answered.”

“Sulli answered Baek Si-on’s phone?”

“Mm.”

“…”

Yoo In-na was quiet for two seconds. Then:

“What did you say to her?”

“The Milan thing.”

“What Milan thing?”

“…That he and I worked on an arrangement in a hotel room until pretty late.”

“Just the arrangement?”

“…No.”

Yoo In-na looked at her.

“So what are you here for? To confess?”

Lee Ji-eun said nothing. Yoo In-na understood anyway.

“Ohh. You’ve already decided you have grounds for what you did, but you’re afraid the grounds aren’t pretty enough — so you came to have them polished.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

“Fine, here it is. You did nothing out of line. You told a fact to whoever happened to pick up the phone. What she does with it after hearing is her business.”

“Unnie, your logic is thuggish.”

Yoo In-na corrected her at once, with great seriousness:

“The thug is you. I’m only your translator.”

“Although — liking someone was never a kindness contest in the first place. If the two of you genuinely want to compete in kindness, then withdraw together and donate Baek Si-on to the tax office. The tax office seemed quite fond of him today, too.”

Lee Ji-eun was laughed against her will.

“But Ji-eun-ya,” Yoo In-na said.

“Mm?”

Someday, when you write a song cursing a green-tea girl, you’re going to feel a little guilty.

— Yoo In-na’s only dissent, offered with a spoon

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

With that, Yoo In-na heaved herself up, fetched a box of ice cream from the fridge, and tossed it onto the coffee table.

“Eat.”

Lee Ji-eun eyed the box.

“I’ll get fat if I eat this now.”

“You dared become a green tea ten minutes ago, and you’re afraid of getting fat?”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

She picked up the spoon. Yoo In-na took one too, and sat back down. The two of them ate in silence for a few mouthfuls.

“Unnie doesn’t think I’m awful?”

Yoo In-na considered.

“Awful isn’t the word.”

Lee Ji-eun had just relaxed a little when the supplement arrived:

“Annoying is somewhat the word.”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

Yoo In-na smiled.

“If you were truly awful, you’d have said ‘we slept together’ on that call. You didn’t.”

“I’m not that—”

“Not that what? Not that shameless?”

“Unnie!”

“Fine, fine — different word: you still own a sense of shame.”

Yoo In-na reached over and roughed her hair.

“So don’t make yourself out worse than you are.”

Lee Ji-eun dug out a spoonful, eyes down:

“Will unnie stand on my side, then?”

Yoo In-na gave her a look.

“If I weren’t on your side, would I have let you in at midnight?”

Lee Ji-eun said nothing. She just leaned a little closer. Yoo In-na pushed her off with theatrical distaste.

“Don’t rub. The essence hasn’t finished absorbing.”

Her mouth said it. Her hand didn’t really push.

Liking someone was never a kindness contest.

— over two spoons and one box; the night’s working theory

❧ ❧ ❧

When Baek Si-on came out of the shower, Jin-ri was on the sofa petting the cat.

Blin lay across her lap, coaxed into purring like a small motorcycle that had finally started on a winter morning. The purr ran low and steady.

Jin-ri had been ready. Per the Hara protocol: no crying, no scene, no betrayed-wife cross-examination. Smile. Be good. Relay Lee Ji-eun’s words in their original form. Then hand him the pressure.

Then she turned her head — and every rehearsed line evaporated.

Baek Si-on wore a bath towel at his waist. Nothing else. His hair was still wet; drops fell from the tips, ran down his neck, over his collarbone, and disappeared into the shadow of his chest.

Jin-ri found this outrageous. She had seen all of it. Touched all of it. Far more had already happened last night. And yet, re-viewed under fully lit conditions, the impact remained enormous — enormous enough that the freshly trained “calm and gentle Jin-ri” was knocked clean out of the scene by a bath towel.

Baek Si-on walked in, toweling his hair.

“What’s wrong?”

Jin-ri recovered.

“No-nothing.”

He sat down at the edge of the sofa; his warmth arrived together with the clean, faint scent of body wash. This was her window to execute the Hara protocol. She judged that if she opened her mouth now, nothing normal would come out of it.

“I-I’ll shower first.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“Didn’t you have something to say?”

“After I shower.”

She deposited Blin into his arms and fled to the bathroom.

When the door shut, Baek Si-on looked down at the cat now residing on him. Blin looked up at the man. A brief, one-to-one stare. Baek Si-on rubbed its head, then picked up the phone from the tea table.

The screen lit: Han-teuk’s message, still sitting there. He read it for two seconds. Opened the call log. Glanced toward the bathroom — water already running inside.

He understood the shape of what had happened. The call had come from Lee Ji-eun; it had been answered by Jin-ri. And judging by the state of her — a prepared statement, interrupted by a bath towel — she had heard something that could not be filed as ordinary work content.

He set the phone down and went back to petting the cat. The purr started up again.

❧ ❧ ❧

When Jin-ri came out, she had recovered considerably. At least on the surface — light loungewear, hair blow-dried to half, a little steam still on her face; the towel-induced collapse of her defenses was no longer visible anywhere.

She crossed the living room and sat down on the other end of the sofa. Baek Si-on glanced over.

“Can you say it now?”

“Ji-eun unnie called you earlier.”

“Mm. I saw.”

“I didn’t answer on purpose.”

That sentence came out fast — afraid of being misread.

“She called three times. I thought it might be urgent. You were in the shower, so I picked up.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“I know.”

Those two words lightened something in her chest. She pressed her lips together and continued.

“She asked me to pass something along.”

He didn’t rush her. Jin-ri leveled her voice as best she could.

“She said that a few days ago in Milan, at night, in a hotel room, the two of you worked on an arrangement until very late.”

“She also said that ‘Twenty-Three’’s bridge was changed following your suggestion, and it works much better now. She wants you to listen when you have time.”

Saying it was worse than hearing it. Every retold sentence was the nail, pressed back in once more. Jin-ri finished and looked down at her own fingers.

“That’s all.”

Baek Si-on was silent for a moment. Lee Ji-eun truly knew how to choose words.

Every word had been true. Strung together, they were more than facts.

— on sentence craft; the nail, once per retelling

“I am learning arranging from her,” Baek Si-on said.

Jin-ri looked at him. He didn’t avoid her eyes.

“I have a lot of melodies in my head. Until now, all I could do was hum them and hand them to Jae-joon. What happens if one day he decides the arranging split is no longer enough and wants to renegotiate? Or if, at three in the morning, when I need a demo the most, he suddenly goes offline and unreachable — what do I do then?”

Jin-ri startled.

“Jae-joon oppa… would do that?”

Baek Si-on reached instead for the most direct example available.

“Why does the neighbor up north, while unable to feed its own people, melt down its pots and hand-build a nuclear bomb?”

Jin-ri blinked and answered, carefully:

“For… world peace?”

“……”

Baek Si-on pressed a hand to his face.

“So it never has to read anyone’s face again. You may go your whole life without firing. You may barely learn to aim. But there must be a gun in your hand. Without a gun, all you can do is hope the person holding one for you stays loyal forever.”

This time Jin-ri understood. Even blood brothers could turn on each other over a few tens of millions of won — let alone a producer holding the core technology. The justification was too sound. So sound that her anger now looked like the unreasonable position. She managed, small-voiced:

“Still — it didn’t have to be a hotel room, at night…”

“There was no time in the day.”

She looked at him, plainly unpersuaded. Baek Si-on picked up his phone, opened that day’s schedule, and handed it over.

The Milan schedule, as produced in his own defense

Fitting.

Brand shoot.

The show.

Dinner.

Magazine interview.

Business meetings.

Eun-ah’s work handover, before her US flight.

BRIT Awards confirmations.

Two pages of scrolling. The only genuinely free block of time: the night.

Jin-ri handed the phone back.

“It sounds very deliberate, the way she said it.”

“Mm.”

He admitted it so quickly that she startled. He went on:

“It was probably deliberate.”

Jin-ri’s head came up. He did not defend Lee Ji-eun — and that gave her grievance somewhere to land.

“Then what about ‘fire equipment’?”

That one she had been holding in for a long time. Baek Si-on paused.

“Back when Scooter pressed Taylor’s heat with the ‘Love Yourself’ teaser, she asked when exactly she had been demoted to fire equipment.”

Jin-ri frowned.

“So you saved her as that?”

“Absent-mindedly.”

“Absent-minded is also hateful.”

Baek Si-on held the phone out to her.

“You change it.”

She froze.

“Me?”

“Mm.”

“I’m not touching it.”

“Then don’t.”

“…Give me the phone.”

He placed it in her hand. Jin-ri looked down at FIRE EQUIPMENT in the contacts, stared for two seconds — deleted it. Typed fresh: IU. Then, feeling that this made her look alarmingly well-behaved, she added two more characters: IU (work).

She reviewed the result and was satisfied, slightly.

“From now on, this kind of thing gets told to me in advance.”

“I’m not trying to manage you.”

The supplement came fast:

“I just… don’t want to be the last to know.”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“Okay.”

She looked up.

“Really?”

“Really.”

“Are you still calling her back?”

He glanced at the phone.

“Tomorrow. Tonight I’m dealing with you.”

Jin-ri’s face went red by degrees.

“I didn’t ask you to deal with me.”

Saying which, she leaned into his chest anyway.

“I almost behaved very graciously today.”

“Almost?”

“Mm.”

“Why didn’t you?”

“Because I didn’t want to be gracious.”

Baek Si-on lowered his head and kissed the top of her hair.

“Good.”

“What’s good about it?”

“Better than pretending nothing happened.”

At that, the last clenched thing inside her finally let go. She got an arm around his waist and said, small:

“Then tonight you’re not allowed to look at your phone again.”

“Mm.”

“Not allowed to answer messages.”

“Mm.”

“Not allowed to discuss arranging.”

“Mm.”

Satisfied, she tilted her head back and demanded a kiss. And when Baek Si-on lowered his mouth to hers, she was not thinking about fire equipment, and she was not thinking about a hotel room in Milan.

Lee Ji-eun could wait until tomorrow. The arrangement could wait until tomorrow.

Tonight, at least, the living-room light was warm, and Baek Si-on was beside her.

That was enough.
