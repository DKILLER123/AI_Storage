Chapter Fifty

Formal and Casual

July 24 Night OSEN, the entertainment desk A man at the top of his game, by way of the bottom

OSEN's entertainment division.

Editor-in-chief Son Nam-won was lying back in his chair, killing time.

Son Nam-won

Editor-in-chief of OSEN's entertainment desk

The man who lives by the scoop

The kind of editor who dug with his mouth instead of his feet. Publicly bruised by Dispatch and afraid of losing a second exclusive, he is exactly the audience a half-exclusive was built for.

The backrest was at its maximum recline. Both feet propped up on the computer tower under the desk, the phone balanced on his stomach, baseball scores scrolling.

OSEN wasn't one of those traditional big papers with a large investigative team.

Nor was it a paparazzi giant like Dispatch, where staff camped out for the stakeout and lived off exclusive bombshells.

It was an online entertainment-and-sports news portal. Plainly: a media outlet that lived on agency press releases, anonymous insider tip-offs, and headlines its own editors had cooked up after racking their brains. Others dug for news with their feet. They did it with their mouths. Whatever got fed to them, they ate, then topped it with a breathless headline, slapped it on the front page, and the traffic came streaming in and the ad money with it.

Today the whole Korean media circle was scrambling for the Baek Jeong-hun exclusive. Son Nam-won would love to have chased it. He just couldn't win. Four reporters under him: one writing a post-match baseball analysis, one staking out a variety show, and the other two on vacation.

What was he supposed to fight with?

So he chose to lie there and wait for the release. Something related to Baek Jeong-hun would come out eventually; when it did, he'd change the headline, copy-paste, and clock out.

Just then, the phone rang.

Son Nam-won slid it open, lazy.

Son Nam-won ← caller · OSEN office

“Hello.”

The voice on the other end said one thing.

“What?”

His feet slid off the computer tower. The phone went between his shoulder and ear, and both hands flew across the keyboard, opening a browser. Dispatch's front page.

The headline.

Dispatch · front page · exclusive

Seoul · 24 Jul 2014 · entertainment

The nation's little sister IU — a hidden relationship, exposed for the first time

Everyone expected a dating scandal. Nobody expected what it actually was.

IU? A hidden relationship?

Son Nam-won's first reaction was the same as everyone in the country who'd tapped the push notification: Dispatch had caught IU dating?

He clicked in.

Not dating.

It was a long-form exclusive report, tightly structured, the evidence chain closed. He scanned the whole thing at speed.

Dispatch · exclusive · the four points

Four. IU's anonymous repayment of the debt.

By the time he'd finished reading, his back had already gone against the chair.

Son Nam-won finished the comment section, then went back to the back-end editing page.

The cursor hovered over the headline of that "violent debt collector" story. The right-click menu popped up — delete, take down, edit.

Delete? That was openly admitting, in front of the entire country, that the collection exposé was false.

Don't delete? The Dispatch exclusive report was sitting on every phone screen in Korea.

The cursor wavered three times between "delete" and "back."

And right as he was agonising, the phone rang.

An unfamiliar number.

Son Nam-won hesitated a second, then picked up.

Baek Si-on → Son Nam-won · evening · Seoul

“Editor-in-chief Son? This is Baek Si-on. Let's meet.”

One line. Then the line went quiet, waiting.

July 24 Night Yeonnam-dong, the street A number, a breath, and an apology

Yeonnam-dong.

Si-on hung up, pocketed the phone, and lifted his head. The night sky looked the same as always. The stars, buried under the light pollution, left only the moon dangling there, alone.

He stood at the side of the road letting the wind blow at him for a while. Not to gather an emotion. Just to put it off.

In the end he pulled his phone back out and opened the KakaoTalk Han Teuk had just sent.

Han Teuk

Han Teuk

010-9463-5217

Han Teuk

I don't know anything.

Lee Ji-eun's private number · received

Si-on stared at it for a while.

Si-on stared at it for a while.

He remembered the words he'd said to her the last time: don't let everyone know where heaven's door is. He'd said it with such conviction.

And now. To clear his own name, he'd handed "heaven's door" over to the whole country.

He turned the phone over in his hands, and turned it back.

He could not make this call.

Dispatch's report said "IU has neither confirmed nor denied," and LOEN had made no comment. From a PR standpoint, the natural move on IU's side was to go quiet and let the sentiment run its course. The fans moved. The goodwill of the public spiked. Without doing a thing, thanks to the wind alone, they'd gain a whole round of credibility.

Maybe Lee Ji-eun was perfectly satisfied with all this.

Maybe she didn't care at all.

But Baek Si-on cared.

Not about whether she cared. He cared about the words he'd said.

So he dialled.

Two rings. Three. Four.

He was sure she wasn't going to answer.

On the tail of the fifth ring, the "beep" turned into a faint sound of air against the receiver.

Baek Si-on → Lee Ji-eun (IU) · night · Seoul

“Hello?”

Lee Ji-eun's voice came through the receiver, neither warm nor cold.

“This is Baek Si-on.”

“……Oh. What is it?”

Si-on looked at the glass door of the coffee shop opposite the alley. Inside, a girl taking a selfie.

“……I wanted to apologise to you.”

There was about three seconds of silence on the line. Then Lee Ji-eun's voice came again.

“Go ahead.”

Si-on took two deep breaths. The first, to organise his words. The second, to swallow his pride.

“The last time I told you, don't let everyone know where heaven's door is. Then, because of my own situation, I exposed your business. That's on me. I apologise.”

When he'd finished, a very faint sound came from the other end. Si-on couldn't quite tell what it was. It might have been a laugh. It might have been a puff of breath out the nose. It might also have been someone walking past knocking into something. But he leaned toward the first one.

“Formal speech, please.”

“What?”

“That whole apology you just gave — every word was casual. An apology that doesn't even use formal address. Are you apologising, or am I just being informed that you've apologised?”

Si-on ran it again, this time with full jondae. But his temple throbbed a little as he formed each syllable.

“You should be calling me sunbae, not Lee Ji-eun-ssi.”

“Sunbae?”

“By singer, I debuted 2008 and you 2010. By actor, you debuted 2014 and I 2011. So however you slice it, you should be calling me sunbae.”

If Si-on remembered correctly, the original body's group had debuted in April 2008. On the question of whether a debut date should be counted by group or by solo career, he could probably construct about eight angles of rebuttal. But he'd come here to apologise. Arguing with someone while apologising changed the nature of the thing.

“……Understood, sunbae.”

The words came out of his mouth, and Si-on felt his facial muscles perform a movement they had never made before.

There were two seconds of quiet on the other end.

And then a laugh. This time he heard it clearly. It was a laugh.

“Apology accepted,” she said. “Use formal address next time.”

“……Mm.”

“Anything else?”

“Yes.”

“Go on.”

“The music-source thing — there's still no result.”

Clearing it up, on a technical level, was absurdly simple. And yet Melon hadn't issued a single word of official response. The behind-the-scenes negotiation was probably far more complex than he'd first assumed.

“Alright. I'll ask around.”

“That's all, then.”

“Mm.”
