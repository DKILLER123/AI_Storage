Sado’s cameras were November first. On a normal flow an actor in a historical court piece started the relevant skills at least three months out. Riding. Archery. Kneeling, greeting, walking, sitting, holding a brush, holding a fan, the centre of gravity in hanbok. All of it. Crown Prince especially. Not an ordinary sageuk where you put on a robe and hit a few poses.

His yes had come too late. From Song Kang-ho putting the project on a table to now, plus the diary he already had, squeezing time to learn was already the calendar opening a crack. A film was not a documentary. The audience would not require him to actually ride and shoot, hit a hundred paces, write a painting. The lens wanted credibility. Wide could use a double. Fast motion could use a cut. Archery could be a drawing hand, a tight shoulder, the eye before the string. Riding could be mounting, seat, reins, a back. Ink the same. The audience needed to believe this body had been trained in a palace from small. Not to inspect whether the inscription had real brush.

What he had to learn was to let the body look like it first. Once the body looked like it, the rest was the camera’s.

October 12 23:00 PDT · the same night Los Angeles · Scooter’s office · then Las Vegas · a rehearsal room A crisis report. A video. A title. A band that had been swapped.

Los Angeles. Eleven at night. Scooter Braun sat in the office facing a crisis report the PR team had put down, brow tight enough to kill a fly. He was handling Baek Si-on being cursed. Not his first crisis. When he had run Bieber, Bieber had been cursed more often than Scooter changed shirts. Bieber’s crises were Bieber’s own doing. Baek Si-on was different. Si-on had not done anything wrong. Scooter had stuffed him in. So the overseas net cursing Si-on was, in essence, cursing Scooter’s call. That annoyed him. Si-on had not fully stood up in North America yet and already had “paratrooper,” “capital drop” stuck on him. Once a label stuck, tearing it took ten times the force.

He was weighing “cultural fusion” as a soothe, or some other redirect, when the phone on the desk buzzed. A file from Eun-ah. He opened it. Picture a little dark. Studio light uneven, a patch of shade on Si-on’s face. The sound was clear.
