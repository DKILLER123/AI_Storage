Chapter One Hundred Eighty Four

The One-Upmanship of Oil Money

January 2 · from a whiteboard to a dinner table to a dark highway along the water Twenty-eight shows becoming thirty-two · two Gulf capitals bidding against each other in real time · one MV already wrapped and one belt never swung · “you won’t need to talk” · a syllable that arrives notarized · and a romance that lives for exactly three seconds before it is corrected SB Projects war room → the phone → Nobu, Malibu → the coast road back to Beverly Hills A pricing chart engineers a full house; Dubai calls, and Doha calls back; Justin Bieber wants his album; a girl in a white slip dress is introduced to a table of Americans as a lucky charm; and in the back seat, with the whole front of the car professionally blind, a boy considers the logistics of packing into a suitcase.

The SB Projects conference room.

The whiteboard in front of Baek Si-on and Scooter was already crammed with city names, venue codes, and date squares.

Fourteen cities. Two shows each. Twenty-eight dates across North America — a route pulled taut from New York to Los Angeles, Chicago to Vancouver, Atlanta to Houston, across the whole continent in one line.

The tour grid · pricing, five tiers, engineered like a machine

VIP — $299 and $499.

Prime floor and bowl — $129 and $169.

Standard — $49, $69, $89.

The logic, per the room: a VIP ceiling high enough to filter the fans who buy the experience itself; a floor low enough that $49 costs less than a decent dinner and pulls the merely curious off the street. For an Asian artist planting roots in this market, sell-through beats ticket price every time. A full house is news. A half-empty one is an incident.

Scooter turned to the team.

“Presale runs three waves — fan club first, general presale second, public on-sale last. Scalper monitoring starts before tickets exist. I don’t want front-row seats on a resale site tomorrow at a thousand dollars.”

Baek Si-on added one line:

“And if it happens anyway?”

Scooter spread his hands.

“Then we add shows. Demand is where capital lives.”

“……”

Vulgar — but vulgar in an extremely honest way.

With cities and pricing locked, the talk moved to guests.

“I’ll handle Justin and Ariana — two shows each, schedules permitting.”

Baek Si-on had no issue with Ariana. The other name gave him pause.

“Justin’s willing?”

Scooter laughed.

“Ever since you kicked Bruno off the throne, the man has called me three times asking when he can release a new album. Right now he’d gladly rob you of every female fan you have.”

Baek Si-on nodded, understanding completely. Whipping your labelmates forward with a healthy sense of crisis — this, too, was a variety of eco-friendly, in-house wolf culture.

Mid-sentence, Scooter’s phone rang. He glanced at the caller ID and raised an eyebrow. Answered. Maybe thirty seconds of listening. His expression went from calm to peculiar, and from peculiar to a species of pleasure that was hard to classify. He hung up and looked at Baek Si-on.

“Dubai.”

“Mm?”

“Dubai wants you for two public concerts. Venue free. Hotels, transport, security — all of it covered. Plus a three-million-dollar appearance fee.”

Baek Si-on stayed fairly calm. Some Middle Eastern cities’ favorite art form was, after all, slamming money down hard enough to become world news. Tallest tower. Largest mall. Most indefensible fireworks budget in human history. One Asian double-crown Billboard singer joining the collection fit their municipal aesthetic perfectly.

Before the room had finished digesting that, Scooter’s phone rang again.

This time it was Doha, Qatar.

The Gulf bids · two phone calls, forty minutes apart

Dubai — 2 public concerts. Venue: free. Hotels, transport, security: covered. Appearance fee: $3,000,000.

Doha — 2 public concerts. Venue: free. Hotels, transport, security: covered. Appearance fee: $3,000,000.

Auditor’s remark: identical to the dollar. The second caller knew precisely what the first had offered, and considered beating it unnecessary. Showing up was the point.

“Do Middle Easterners have some kind of competitive one-upmanship habit?”

Baek Si-on considered, then said:

“Yes. Last time the Dubai crown prince gave me a watch, the Qatari’s face was off.”

Scooter was silent for two seconds, then nodded.

“Good. Then we do both.”

“Both?”

“Obviously.”

Scooter said it like the man had asked whether water was wet.

“They’re basically on the way. Dubai to Doha is about two hours.”

He pulled the map up on the tablet and zoomed into the Gulf.

“Think Seoul to Jeju — except everyone there has more money.”

Baek Si-on studied the map for a few seconds.

“Then twenty-eight becomes thirty-two.”

Scooter snapped his fingers.

“Correct.”

He added two cities to the tour plan, then added one thing more.

“But those four Middle East dates can’t price off the North American sheet.”

“Why not?”

“Because they will genuinely think four hundred and ninety-nine dollars is cheap.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

For the next two-plus hours the whiteboard was erased and rewritten, rewritten and erased — stage design, lighting plots, band configuration, setlist order, guest entrances, mid-show segments, merch distribution — every detail pulled apart and put back together. By the time everyone finally spilled out of the conference room, the Los Angeles sky had started going orange at the edges.

❧ ❧ ❧

In the car, Baek Si-on took out his phone and called Adam.

Baek Si-on → Adam Levine · voice · the tour ask · the orange hour

Baek?

Adam sounded in a good mood — laughter in the background, someone telling a story badly.

Is the ‘Sugar’ video done?

Wrapped!

Mentioning it visibly perked him up.

It came out shockingly good! Though the weddings at the end were all cleared with the couples and their families ahead of time.

What a shame. Nobody got to whip you with a belt.

Baek, you are genuinely unfriendly sometimes, you know that?

He let the accusation pass unanswered and went straight in.

Do you have time to guest at my concerts?

A beat.

You’re touring?

Thirty-two shows.

Thirty-two? You debuted like a year ago!

Yes.

You’re in LA right now?

Mm.

Wiz is here too — we’re literally together. Dinner tonight, talk in person?

Works. Send me the time and place.

He hung up, looked at the screen for a moment, thought — and dialed Choi Jin-ri.

Baek Si-on → Choi Jin-ri · voice · the dinner invitation · ten minutes after the first call

Hello?

Her voice still carried some uncashed excitement — probably still riding the afterglow of an aurora and a kiss.

Some friends are getting dinner tonight. Want to come along?

…What friends?

American ones.

A lot of them?

He ran the headcount. Adam. Wiz. Probably a few of the Maroon 5 side.

Five or six, maybe.

The line went completely silent. A long moment later, a small mutter:

Wouldn’t it be… weird? Me being there?

Not weird.

But my English isn’t very good…

You won’t need to talk.

It sounded like comfort. Examined twice, it also sounded like you can sit there and be a beautiful ornament. She could not decide whether to be moved or offended. Moved won — he had invited her, unprompted, into a private dinner, and that fact already said plenty.

Then I’ll come.

Good.

What do I wear?

Whatever.

What is whatever?

You’d look good in anything.

The line went quiet again — a different quality of quiet. She didn’t know when he had unlocked whatever door that sentence came out of. Maybe the kiss under the aurora had opened some meridian nobody had charted. Maybe, one step forward into whatever-they-were, he had finally allowed himself to let a few of the things living behind “mm” and “oh” out for air. Either way: it made a person very happy.

What time?

He checked the message Adam had just sent.

Eight. I’ll pick you up.

Okay~

After the call ended, Choi Jin-ri threw the phone onto the bed and dropped into a crouch in front of her suitcase, digging. Every item she lifted, she rejected. Too formal — that’s an awards ceremony. Too casual — that’s a convenience store. Too cute — that’s playing young. Too grown — that’s announcing to the world that she came prepared.

Ten minutes later she sat in the middle of a ring of clothes, newly aware of how much a stylist was worth in this world.

At eight, Baek Si-on called from below the hotel.

“I’m here.”

“Coming down right now.”

Right now, in the female lexicon, is an elastic material. But today it held its true length. A few minutes later she came out of the lobby: a pale knit cardigan over a simple white slip dress, hair down, swinging lightly at her shoulders with each step.

She pulled the car door and folded herself in, asking, a little shyly:

“Do I look… good?”

His gaze traveled from her hair to her collarbone, and from her collarbone to the deliberately visible segment of chain.

“Good.”

“Really?”

“Mm.”

A month ago that same syllable would have landed with a faint air of provocation. Tonight it landed differently.

❝

Tonight it arrived notarized — same one syllable, embossed, sealed, and legally binding.

— the passenger seat, palm trees going by

Seatbelt clicked, Choi Jin-ri reached over, quite naturally, and took the hand resting on the center console. He didn’t dodge. His fingers closed back around hers. Her mouth absolutely refused to be governed, so she turned her face to the window. The Los Angeles night streamed past the glass, and she decided that tonight, even if all they did was drive in a circle, the evening would already have paid for itself.

❧ ❧ ❧

The Malibu coast. Nobu.

Adam had picked the place, and Los Angeles celebrities pick restaurants by two criteria: first, the back door must be discreet; second, it must be close to home. Taste ranks third. This one was close to his house.

The car parked in the back alley; they came in through the rear door and were led down a short corridor. Adam spotted Baek Si-on and stood at once.

“Baek!”

He came over and hugged him, then glanced at the girl beside him, and his eyes went meaningfully crescent-shaped.

“Ohhh~”

Behati waved at him too, grinning.

“Baek — Adam talks about you at home every single day. I’m starting to think he’s going to trade me in.”

Adam: “……”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Wiz rose and bumped fists with him, laughing.

“Brother. You finally made it.”

The Maroon 5 side traded hellos one by one. Choi Jin-ri couldn’t follow their fast English; she kept smiling. Fortunately she was pretty. Pretty is a travel document valid in every language environment.

Behati turned first to the only other woman at the table, searched her memory for a moment — and lit up.

“You’re Sulli, right?”

Choi Jin-ri froze for a beat — she hadn’t expected to be known — then nodded quickly.

“Yes.”

As one of Victoria’s Secret’s hottest wings, Behati kept a live map of the fashion world, and word travels in those circles: a girl from a country the fashion press calls a desert, sweeping a Venice red carpet — that sort of thing gets retold over drinks. Her gaze dropped quickly to Choi Jin-ri’s neck.

“Sweetheart, the malachite on that necklace is gorgeous.”

She tapped her own collarbone to indicate the location.

“Is it a Van Cleef hidden-edition piece?”

Choi Jin-ri looked down. She caught a few of the keywords. The finer adjectives in between, her brain selected right past — the language system was already working overtime in there, and forcing more unpaid hours would have been inhumane. She assembled her available vocabulary and explained, slowly and with great care:

“It is… a Churchill gift. Under the aurora.”

Behati’s face went pleasantly blank. Churchill? Aurora? What kind of limited release channel was that?

But she skipped the details and seized the keyword.

“Aurora? You went to see the aurora? Oh my god, I’ve always wanted to! Was it cold? Was it worth it? How long could you see it?”

Against romantic subject matter, girls mount no natural defense. Choi Jin-ri’s English was scattered goods, but with gestures, expressions, and the occasional surviving vocabulary item, she managed to convey a good seventy percent of the aurora’s scale. Behati listened the way people listen to free vacations.

Down the table, Wiz and Adam traded a look, then both turned to Baek Si-on and lifted their eyebrows toward Choi Jin-ri’s end of the table.

“You gonna introduce her?”

Baek Si-on glanced over. She was deep in an English-language seminar for Behati on how to take photos in snow without resembling a potato wrapped in a down jacket.

“She’s my lucky charm.”

Adam: “……”

Wiz: “……”

“Was that — did Baek Si-on just say something romantic?”

Wiz turned to Adam, aggrieved on principle.

“Most romantic thing he ever said to me was ‘I’m a finished product.’ This is not fair.”

Choi Jin-ri caught the teasing tone and glanced back at Baek Si-on — lucky charm was a phrase her rations could cover. He sat there accepting his friends’ ribbing with a completely flat face. She turned back to Behati and carried on, but the happiness on her face had stopped even pretending to be containable.

Baek Si-on let the topic die and stated his business plainly.

“I’m touring North America. I need you two to help me hold the rooms up.”

“Say less. We’re in.”

Wiz turned serious at once.

“But first — which cities?”

Baek Si-on read the list out. Wiz barely let it finish.

“Chicago.”

Adam was just as fast.

“Los Angeles.”

Baek Si-on looked between them.

“Why did you both pick the biggest cities?”

Adam answered with the righteousness of a man stating natural law.

“Because we like applause.”

Wiz added:

“And good hotels.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Shamelessly practical. Still more honest than the ones who swear wherever you need me and then, in the end, don’t show.

As they talked, the food started landing — Nobu’s black cod in miso, the sashimi selection, wagyu sushi, course after course. In the gap while the table studied the plates, Choi Jin-ri leaned sideways to Baek Si-on’s ear and lowered her voice.

“You told them I’m your lucky charm?”

“Mm.”

“That’s so…”

“So what?”

She looked down at her chopsticks. Quieter still:

“So not you.”

“Not like me how?”

“You don’t say things like that.”

“So I said it in English.”

Choi Jin-ri: “……”

❝

In Korean it would have been fatal. In English, a love line files itself under small talk and gets stamped through.

— her chopsticks, Nobu Malibu

❧ ❧ ❧

The second half of the dinner ran loose and warm. The table’s talk had long since left work behind and sprinted off toward Los Angeles gossip, the engine notes of new supercars, and who had paid what absurd sum for which globally limited sneaker that month. Choi Jin-ri followed about half of it. Her scattered vocabulary was already running a deficit just servicing one Victoria’s Secret model’s enthusiasm; her brain’s language center had submitted something close to a resignation letter. Fortunately Behati kindly dropped her pace, and Baek Si-on would tilt his head, now and then, to translate a key line against her ear.

She felt none of the cold-edged awkwardness of being the one who can’t follow. On the contrary — a few cups of sake in, the nerve that had been wound tight over meeting strangers finally let go, and a nice flush of pink rose onto her fair face and stayed.

Around eleven, the dinner broke up. The party came out through the back door into the Malibu night, the ocean wind arriving with its particular Pacific salt and cool — nothing like Churchill’s knife-wind that froze a person through. This wind was gentle. Like the sea saying hello.

Adam clapped Baek Si-on’s shoulder and said something; Wiz laughed; Behati waved at Choi Jin-ri — “See you!” — and Choi Jin-ri waved back with a completely sincere smile.

Then she stood at Baek Si-on’s side, listening to the waves come down on the sand, and felt suddenly how big the world was. Big enough that between an aurora and a surf line, between an ice-field and this gilded coastline, less than twenty-four hours had passed. And yet, thinking about it, her position had never once changed.

Always beside him.

Goodbyes done, the cars pulled away one by one. The sake’s backdraft rose slowly; Choi Jin-ri’s head went soft and dizzy. The world turned, gently, at reduced speed. Not unpleasant. Just floaty. She didn’t fight the float — she let herself lean, naturally, into Baek Si-on’s chest. His chest was steady. His heartbeat came through the fabric, one beat, the next, a slow rhythm.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“I have to fly back to Seoul tomorrow.”

“Mm.”

“But I don’t want to go back.”

He looked down at her. Her voice was very light — a little of the sake in it, and a little of a grievance she couldn’t have hidden if she tried.

Three months ago she had said almost the same thing. The Venice closing dinner, the Adriatic laid out dark in front of her, her bare feet on a stone railing: I don’t want to go back. Now she was curled against him, Los Angeles streetlights sweeping past the window one lamp at a time, saying the same sentence again. Different setting, different season, a different continent. The reason for not wanting to go back had never changed at all.

“Why aren’t you comforting me?”

No response had come, so she looked up.

“I’m thinking.”

“About what?”

“Whether I can fit you into the suitcase and take you with me.”

She blinked — and then laughed out loud.

“I’m not Blin.”

“Blin doesn’t need to think.”

“……”

Well. The romance lived for three seconds.

But she was still laughing as she burrowed in a little deeper.

“Then you have to miss me.”

“Mm.”

“Every day.”

“Mm.”

“Not only when work reminds you.”

“That part’s difficult.”

Her eyes opened. She reached up and pinched his arm.

He looked down at her.

“But I can try.”

Only then was she satisfied. The sake had made her bolder and her voice softer, and the car ran on toward Beverly Hills. In the front, the driver watched the road with total focus. The bodyguard watched the window with total focus. Everyone was extremely busy — far too busy to notice the two people in the back seat drifting closer and closer together.

That’s called professional ethics.

It is also called the survival instinct.
