Chapter Forty-Three

The Hit That Drifted Back on the Current

July 22 Morning The sofa, a phone, and a KakaoTalk message The day the numbers came home
July 22 Morning Baek Si-on's flat, Mapo Three days since she last opened Melon

Baek Eun-ah had gone three days without opening Melon.

Her battlefield now was SoundCloud and YouTube. First thing every morning, the overseas numbers. Last thing at night, the overseas numbers. Melon — the place that had broken her heart — was a place she no longer had any intention of opening.

At ten in the morning, true to routine, she was curled on the sofa scrolling SoundCloud.

A KakaoTalk message popped at the top of the screen.

Jung Jae-joon

Baek Eun-ah

Oppa?

Jung Jae-joon

Open Melon.

Baek Eun-ah

Why?? Is something wrong??

Jung Jae-joon

Just look.

10:02 a.m. · read

Eun-ah stared at it for two seconds, then switched over to Melon and tapped into the real-time chart.

Number one.

“Way Back Home” — Baek Si-on.

Eun-ah thought she'd misread it. Backed out. Re-entered.

Still number one.

“Cousin!!!”

She came sprinting from the living room to his bedroom door in bare feet, phone held up like a torch, the whole body of her like a grenade that had just had its pin pulled.

Baek Si-on lifted half his head out of the blanket.

“……What time is it?”

“Melon real-time chart! Number ONE!!!”

“Mm?”

He sat up, took the phone, and looked at the screen for about five seconds.

“That fast?”

Si-on knew exactly where the pulse had come from.

YouTube.

The video on MrSuicideSheep's channel had broken four million plays the day before. Then the Western music blogs had started in. A Korean artist just dropped the song of the summer and nobody noticed. This track from an unknown Korean singer might be the best Tropical House track of 2014.

Overseas music press · rolling

“This track from an unknown Korean singer might be the best Tropical House track of 2014.”

A Korean artist drops the song of the summer — and almost nobody in the artist's own country notices at first.

Then those articles were picked up by the Korean study-abroad community. Students reposted them to the forums on Naver and Daum, and the standard title was always a variation on our Korean singer is huge overseas and you don't even know it.

The rate at which the posts multiplied was faster than anyone had guessed it could be.

Koreans' sensitivity to "our culture being recognised abroad" is carved into the national DNA. You don't have to be a fan of the person, you don't have to have heard the song. If the title contains the three keywords — Korea, overseas, going viral — the click rate is guaranteed.

And just like that, the chain came back to the start.

A song that set out from Korea, drowned in Korea, was picked up on the far shore — and then travelled back on the ocean current. This time nobody could ignore it.

Eun-ah bounced onto the bed, feet kicking in the air, in a state close to rapturous delirium.

And that delirium lasted precisely until one in the afternoon.

✿ ✿ ✿

July 22 1:15 p.m. Naver real-time search The word "chart-rigging" entered the room

One-fifteen in the afternoon.

Naver real-time search.

Item four.

# "Way Back Home" overnight chart-surge mystery #

The smile froze on Eun-ah's face.

She tapped in. The first post came from the admin of a SISTAR fan forum, and its title was deliberately restrained:

An analysis of the anomalous overnight streaming data on "Way Back Home."

Fan-forum analysis · SISTAR board · 13:09

An analysis of the anomalous overnight streaming data on "Way Back Home"

Three screenshots accompany the post. The first shows the play curve from July 7 to July 22 — for two weeks it is a line hugging the x-axis, then at the dawn of July 22 it snaps vertically upward.

The second shows the hour-by-hour play count from 1 a.m. to 7 a.m., each hour's increment clearing fifty thousand.

The third is the same window, for comparison, on SISTAR's Touch My Body.

A no-name singer with fewer than eighty comments on Melon is out-streaming, in the small hours, a girl group with millions of fans. Statistically, this does not hold.

The comment section beneath had already gone up in flames.

There's one thing everyone in the Korean music market knows by instinct: the midnight chart belongs to the fans.

Only fans set alarms for midnight, open the app at exactly 0:00, put their idol's new song on loop in the background, turn the volume to the floor, lock the screen, and go on sleeping — letting the play count climb quietly in the dark.

Every large fan site has a dedicated "music source team" — the group charged, during a comeback, with coordinating the fans' streaming windows, tutorials, even shared accounts.

So when a song jumps from two-hundred-something to number one between one and seven in the morning, the instinctive reaction of the fandom world is not "what a great song."

It's:

“Who's streaming it?”

SISTAR's fans were the first to charge. The All-Kill of Touch My Body had been ended by some song nobody knew, and that was a breath they were not about to swallow.

hyeonjini_star1

SISTAR promoted for half a month and now they get trampled by a 'number one' some robot streamed for them? Is that fair?

touch_my_down

Is Melon going to do nothing? This is obviously rigged data and it charts anyway? The platform audit is a joke?

sistar_4ever

@Melon_Official @Korea_Music_Content_Assoc We demand a full investigation!

allkill_dreams

If a no-namer can bot his way to the top at midnight, he can take down SISTAR today and EXO tomorrow. This is anybody's problem.

Two hours after the post went up, the shares passed ten thousand.

The comment section had spread beyond SISTAR's fans to every other fandom. EXO-L, SONE, Panda, VIP — the groups that spent most of the year at each other's throats — had, on this one issue, reached a rare, unified front.

The logic was simple.

If a nobody no one had heard of could bot his way to number one at midnight, then today he could take down SISTAR, tomorrow EXO, the day after anyone at all.

This wasn't one person's problem. This was the rules' problem.

And when fans believe they're defending the rules, their strength goes from "terrifying" to "catastrophic."

The complaint letters poured into the customer-service inboxes of Melon, Genie, and Bugs like an avalanche.

By three in the afternoon, the ranking on Naver real-time search read like this:

Naver · real-time search · 15:00

1Way Back Home data rigging

3Baek Si-on chart manipulation

5Melon midnight data anomaly

8Who is Baek Si-on

Four of the top ten. Three are accusations. One is a question.

Tapping into item eight, the tone was completely different from the first three.

Not attacking him.

Digging him up.

idol_archivist

I looked him up — A'ST1, that old DSP boy group. Debuted 2008, disbanded 2009. Utterly invisible the whole time. His best credit was an OST for Boys Over Flowers?

debut_era_scan

Whoa, this is what he used to look like? (attachment: debut-era photo) He looks nothing like now. Plastic surgery?

credit_checker

Wait, look at the song page — writer: IU??? Lee Ji-eun wrote lyrics for this guy?? What's the connection?

uaena_confused

What what what? IU??

And there, the thread split in two.

One faction decrying the chart-rigging. One faction digging up Baek Si-on's background. And a third — a swarm of Uaena — charging in, some protecting IU's name ("Ji-eun just wrote lyrics for someone, leave her out of this"), others doing the reverse-wholesale ("the song's actually really good, go listen before you start judging").

Several forces churned together.

The comment section had become a battlefield.

Eun-ah read the last of it, and set the phone face-down on the sofa.

“Cousin……”

“Leave it.”

“How can I leave it?! They're saying you rigged the charts! They're slandering you! Every hot search is cursing you!”

Her voice was shaking.

With anger.

“There's someone who'll be more worried than us.”

Eun-ah was still deep in the feeling, and it took a second for the sentence to land.

“Who?”

“LOEN.”

Eun-ah blinked.

“LOEN?”

Si-on picked the cola off the table, unscrewed the cap, and took a sip.

“Think about it. This song is distributed by LOEN. The mud of chart-rigging gets thrown at this song, it gets thrown at Melon too. The largest music platform in the country — its own release standing accused of data fraud on its own chart. If that spreads, who takes the hit?”

Eun-ah's eyes blinked slowly, twice.

“Melon's credibility.”

“Credibility goes, advertisers go. Copyright holders start questioning the integrity of their payout data. The stock price gets dragged along for the ride.”

He set the cola down.

“And if you want to get really paranoid — what do you think Genie is doing behind this whole mess? The eternal second place finally gets a shot at number one making a fool of itself. You think it won't throw a match on the pile?”

“So no matter what, LOEN will clear it up fast. Not to clear us — to clear themselves. We just happen to get washed clean in the process.”

He took another drink of the cola.

Eun-ah stared at him for several seconds.

“Cousin, how do you know all of this……”

Baek Si-on shook his head.

“I don't know everything. I've just lived a few more years than you.”

He said it so offhandedly that Eun-ah took it for nothing more than an age gap. She puffed out her cheeks.

“You're only four years older than me. You talk like it's twenty.”

Si-on didn't take the bait.

He set the cola down, leaned back into the sofa, face empty.

Eun-ah glared at him for two seconds, found herself in the familiar "he won't answer" situation, sighed, and picked up the phone again.

Not the hot search anymore.

SoundCloud.

Over there, no one was cursing him.

Over there the only words were "hidden gem" and "song of the summer."
