Chapter Ninety-Nine

The Black Mamba, Disenchanted

September 16 Morning Lee Joon-ik’s studio A thin script. A rice chest. Six hundred million. November.

September sixteenth. Morning. Director Lee Joon-ik’s studio. Sado’s script was not thick. At least compared with those commercial features that ran to a hundred fifty pages, character charts that could pull half a wall, it even looked a little held. But when Baek Si-on turned the last page, his fingers stayed on the paper a long time.

A rice chest. Eight days. Father. Son. Kingship, rites, expectation, madness. And a crown prince who, from birth, had never truly been a “person.”

He closed the script. Across from him, Director Lee Joon-ik was watching him, quiet. Song Kang-ho was there too. Si-on looked up.

Lee Joon-ik

Director · The King and the Clown, Hope, Sado

Real · a thin script, a November start, a role that is not easy

He does not rush the yes. He waits until the last page is closed. Then he says the rest is schedule, and that this part is hard to play.

“I’ll do it.”

Song Kang-ho smiled once. As if he had already known he would say that. Lee Joon-ik nodded too.

“Then what’s left is schedule.”

Si-on put the script on the table.

“I go to Los Angeles tomorrow.”

“Los Angeles?”

“New-song drop and an MV shoot. I need about two weeks. After I’m back I can go in full-time.”

Said very brief. In front of a film director, explaining too much of a music project’s commercial logic had no meaning. Result was enough. Lee Joon-ik looked at Song Kang-ho once. Song Kang-ho briefly explained Bong Joon-ho directing Si-on’s MV. Lee Joon-ik listened, thought a moment.

“We had meant to start cameras late October, but… sets were still being adjusted anyway.”

He turned the production calendar on the table.

“Palace art still needs a little time. Costume also said the crown prince’s few looks need the details redone. Then early November.”

Si-on bowed a little.

“Thank you, director.”

“Don’t thank too early. This role is not easy.”

“Please rest easy.”

Then the producer pushed another file over. Fee contract. Si-on scanned once. Six hundred million won. Lower than Song Kang-ho, higher than ninety-five percent of actors. Si-on picked up a pen. Signed. Contract closed. Sado, just like that, was set.

✿ ✿ ✿

The next day Daylight in California ICN → LAX · then El Segundo A Hawaiian shirt. A Dodgers cap. A hug that coughed. Four in the morning, already disenchanted.

The next day. Incheon International. Baek Si-on, Park Ji-hun, Baek Eun-ah, Seo Eun-ju, four of them, to Los Angeles. Core jobs of this run very clear. Shoot the MV. Talk Kobe’s on-camera details. On the way, knock off North American distribution, cover rights, sync, the things that needed talking.

When the plane landed at LAX it was still day locally. After immigration Si-on pushed a trolley out of arrivals. California September sun coming in from the terminal’s glass, two colour-temp grades brighter than Seoul autumn. Air dry. Sun direct. Palms everywhere, and people tanned dark. The pickup standing outside the exit channel. Bong Joon-ho. He had arrived two days earlier than Si-on’s party. Today’s Bong in a Hawaiian shirt, with that round face and slightly fat build, looking like a Korean uncle on holiday. A baseball cap on the head. LA Dodgers. Going native, done thoroughly.

“Si-on-ah!”

Bong put a hand up in the crowd. Si-on pushed the trolley over.

“Uncle Bong.”

Bong’s look went around behind Si-on, caught Eun-ah, Ji-hun, Seo Eun-ju.

“Not a small team.”

“All working.”

“Fine. In. I rented a seven-seat.”

El Segundo. Lakers practice facility. The car in a visitor space outside. The party into reception. Names given. The desk checked, picked up an inside line, dialled a number. Two sentences. Then nodded at them, pointed down the corridor.

“Kobe’s waiting.”

The corridor long. Champion stills and retired jerseys on both walls. When Si-on walked those photos his look went over Magic Johnson, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Shaquille O’Neal. End of the corridor. A door half open. Inside, a ball hitting wood. Bang. Bang. Bang. Si-on pushed the door. The inside of the gym emptier than he had imagined. A standard NBA floor, a row of foldable stands each side, industrial lights in the ceiling leaving the space almost without shadow. Half-court. A man in training kit shooting. Motion extremely standard. Jump. Release. Arc. Shh. Nothing but net.

Kobe Bryant turned. Same as the FaceTime. No. More real than the video. Video Kobe was a face compressed onto a five-point-five-inch screen. The Kobe in front of him was a one-metre-ninety-eight, giving off post-training heat, living person. He saw the people at the door, put the ball on the floor at once, came over in large steps.

“Hey, man!”

The same open as the video. Immediate. Si-on put a hand out to shake. Kobe went straight past handshake distance, two long arms open, a standard American hug. Back-pat included. Force not light. Si-on coughed once from the pat.

“Mr. Bryant.”

He came out of that iron-clamp hug without showing it, half a step back, putting East Asian social distance back. Choi Jin-ri had been right. These foreigners’ body smell was extremely aggressive. Kobe half a step back, looking Si-on up and down.

“Taller than the video.”

“You’re taller than the video.”

Kobe laughed. Si-on smiled once too, then turned a little and began to introduce one by one.

“This is my manager, Baek Eun-ah.”

“This is my lawyer, Seo Eun-ju.”

“This is my stylist, Park Ji-hun.”

Kobe shook each, motion natural, no perfunctory. When it came to Bong, Si-on’s tone went a little more correct.

“This is Director Bong Joon-ho. Korea’s strongest director.”

Kobe looked at Bong. He did not know Bong Joon-ho. At this point Bong, in Hollywood, was still far from the later degree where a name said could make a whole room stand and clap. But since Si-on had used “Korea’s strongest director,” that introduction itself was a back. Kobe switched at once to a two-hand shake.

“Good to meet you, director.”

Bong, for the first time in his life, felt this directly an NBA player’s grip on an ordinary person’s hand-bones. The smile on his face trembled from the pain.

“One of. One of, ha.”

He added at once.

“You’re modest.”

“Not modest. Si-on said it too large.”

Bong took his hand back, moved the knuckles without making a show of it.

“Directors in our country are all pretty extra.”

Si-on beside them said nothing. Now it was “one of.” In five years it would be “the only.” Then the whole world would know this Korean fat man’s name in a Hawaiian shirt.

After greetings the work split on its own. Eun-ah and Seo Eun-ju followed the manager with the earpiece at Kobe’s side toward a meeting room off the gym, to talk likeness range, shoot windows, promo cooperation, and later written-contract detail. Ji-hun stayed at the sideline, looking at light and gym colour temp, judging early for later look and makeup. Who actually stayed at centre court: Bong, Si-on, Kobe.

Bong did not waste words. First a rough of the storyboard idea. This MV needed two main spaces at the core. A game floor. A practice floor. Kobe’s side did not need to perform anything on purpose. Bong said it straight:

“What you usually do, do that.”

Everyday shooting. Suicide runs. Ice on the sideline. Therapy. A towel over the head, breathing large, one look at the lens. And a look-back going down steps — that shot later cut in montage to Si-on looking back going up, a look across space. Kobe listened, nodded. Acceptance of this plan high.

Si-on’s side much more complex. What Bong gave him was not a single action line but a set of parallel body clues that could be cross-cut. Swim. Weights. Boxing. Singing at the centre of an empty game floor. And a few walking, stopping, looking-back shots that very much stressed solitude and space. Si-on listening, noting. After the large frame, time. Bong turned a page in the notebook in his hand, looked at Kobe.

“Can we shoot now?”

“Now?”

Kobe put the ball at his waist, looked at the clock above the floor.

“Yes.”

Bong had, after all, spent time in America. The chicken-soup of “Los Angeles at four in the morning” had long been disenchanted. Maybe at peak Kobe had indeed got up at four to shoot. A thirty-six-year-old, major-injured, in a long negotiation with the body’s limit, could not live every day on that marketing legend. Kobe thought two seconds, nodded.

“We can.”

Bong did not waste words either, took out a phone and started calling people. The photography core was already standing by; it only waited on the speed of Kobe’s nod whether today started or tomorrow. Americans at work sometimes dragged, and sometimes were fast enough to scare. Under half an hour, people jumping out of cars. The speed of kit coming out of a van boot said this was not their first “call half an hour ago, rolling half an hour later.”

“A here, low, on the floor.”

“B outside the three, handheld, with him.”

“C upstairs stands, top full.”

“Light.”

A lighting assistant pushed two LED panels to both sides of the floor, colour temp to 4500K, mixed with the industrial ceiling into a cold-warm edge. Kobe standing in the light. Bong behind the monitor five seconds.

“Good. Rolling.”

Kobe, as Bong asked, doing what he usually did, the lens catching him, not interrupting. The whole shoot compressed extremely fast. Therapy area and a few exterior positions also shot in turn. After Kobe’s side ended he fist-bumped Si-on, said “looking forward to the cut,” then was taken by his private trainer.

After Kobe left, Bong did not give Si-on jet-lag treatment, and did not ask if he was tired. No need. Si-on himself would not ask for it. Then, for four days, he was chased by Bong’s lens through every corner of this city. Last day, a few more close-ups and empty shots. Bong behind the monitor watched the last piece, put a hand to his neck, finally dropped half a gear out of several days of high-rpm running.

“Footage is enough. I’ll go back and cut.”

No wrap ceremony. He cut the later plan out at once.

“You still have other things to handle in L.A.?”

“Mm. Rights and distribution still to sit with people.” Si-on nodded.

“Then you do yours. First cut I figure about a week. I’ll send it then.”

Airport send-off, no sentimental scene. Bong’s Hawaiian shirt had been changed, from tropical flowers to California palms. In essence no difference. The same fat man in clothes he should not wear. Si-on and Eun-ah walked him to security.

“Uncle Bong. The footage, you worked.”

“Mm.”

Bong waved a small fat hand.

“Going.”

Said it, turned, into security. After he vanished behind the line Eun-ah still looked in twice.

“Just like that?”

“Mm.”

Si-on pulled his look back, took out a phone, swiped twice, found a name. Scooter Braun. Dialled. Three rings. Connected.

Baek Si-on → Scooter Braun

Hello?

Mr. Braun. This is Baek Si-on. I’m at LAX.

A second of quiet. Then surprise, and a smile, in the man’s voice.

You’re in L.A.?

Mm.

When did you get in?

A few days ago.

Walking out, mask pulled a little higher.

“Are you free to meet?”

Of course.

Scooter Braun’s “of course” came very fast. He had always been curious about Si-on. A person who dared sign a bet with him on What Do You Mean? was already interesting. Not to mention this person had not long ago taken Venice Best Actor. That kind of person, Scooter not meeting once, would not hold.
