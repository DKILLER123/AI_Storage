Chapter One Hundred Ninety Eight

No Need to Go Home to Feed the Cat Tonight

The same night, later · Jin-ri’s apartment · a birthday that refuses to end until the dishes are done A man in a formal state of eating · one demo, and the solo album DSP gave him once · five questions with no comfortable answers · a contract that will not be renewed · the world’s second-largest music market and its connoisseurs · eight floors of elevator excuses · a factory recall, returned for maintenance Jin-ri’s apartment → the street below → the eighth floor

With the cake cut, the living room finally recovered the smell of ordinary human life.

Baek Si-on finished his cake and started eating dinner.

Accurately speaking: he entered a formal state of eating.

Goo Hara sat across from him and watched seaweed soup, steak, sweet-and-sour pork, and fried chicken travel into his mouth in some mysteriously stable sequence, her expression gradually becoming the expression of a person watching a man who has just escaped a refugee camp.

“You didn’t eat on the plane?”

Baek Si-on swallowed a piece of steak.

“I ate.”

“How much?”

“One standard meal.”

Baek Si-on looked up at Hara and returned serve:

“Has your schedule been light lately?”

Hara: “……”

Jin-ri looked up too.

❝

Between idols, the question of whether you have been idle lately is sometimes more dangerous than the question of whether you have gained weight. The first kills professional pride. The second kills weight management.

— table talk, with casualties

Hara put a piece of steak onto her own plate and looked up at him.

“You just finished the birthday dinner I paid for, and you’re already calling me a nuisance?”

“No.”

Hara studied his eyes for a few seconds. Then, at last:

“I’ve been preparing a solo.”

Jin-ri’s eyes lit up:

“Really?”

Hara smiled:

“Really. Although the daily meetings have reached the point where I suspect they intend to replace the production budget with agendas. Theoretically, it’s real.”

“What’s the title track’s direction?” Baek Si-on asked.

“R&B-leaning. Languid. Sexy. High-class…”

Hara counted the words out on her fingers, one by one, and ended up laughing at her own list.

“Want to hear it?”

“Sure.”

Hara got up, went to the sofa, dug her phone out of her bag, tapped a few times, and handed it across the table.

“Listen.”

♪

Untitled demo

Goo Hara · solo demo · guide vocal · unmastered

Low-frequency drums and a very soft synthesizer came out of the phone.

The melody wasn’t bad.

The female guide vocal sat pressed low, touched with breath; the tempo was slow; it built an atmosphere of night, lamplight, perfume, a languid sofa.

The demo finished. The living room stayed quiet for a moment.

Hara looked at Baek Si-on, and asked:

“Well?”

Jin-ri looked at him too.

She hoped he would praise, of course. Hara was one of her closest sisters. If Baek Si-on said one “not bad,” Jin-ri would be happier about it than Hara herself.

Baek Si-on handed the phone back:

“It’s a bit like the solo album DSP gave me back then.”

Hara went still.

Jin-ri looked at him, curious.

“Sunbae had a solo album?”

“He did.”

“And the results?”

“Released it, then enlisted.”

Jin-ri: “……”

Hara had been about to laugh — then registered the problem, and went serious by degrees.

“So you think my solo will flop?”

“On this direction, the probability isn’t low.”

Jin-ri opened her mouth, thought better of it, and swallowed it.

She knew Baek Si-on wasn’t trying to cut Hara down.

If he had wanted to gloss it over, he had safe words in bulk supply: “not bad,” “could use more polish,” “the concept is clear.”

Those were the safest words in the industry.

He hadn’t used any of them.

“Your strength was never the vocals.”

From anyone else’s mouth, that sentence would have drawn blood.

Hara only nodded, lightly.

“I know.”

She had been debuted far too many years to be unclear, even in private, about what she could do and what she couldn’t.

Baek Si-on went on:

“Your strengths are the visual, the proportions, the way a camera finds you, what your face does on a stage.”

“So what is this song doing?”

“Slow R&B, walls of breathy vocal, a chorus that doesn’t hook, choreography space compressed to nothing — live, the sensuality has to stand on voice alone.”

“And if the release window runs straight into a Girls’ Generation comeback — what do you think the result looks like?”

Jin-ri had been tensing up on Hara’s behalf.

But at the words “Girls’ Generation comeback,” it was Jin-ri who blanked first, unable to help herself:

“Sunbae, how do you know the sunbaenims are coming back?”

Baek Si-on looked at her.

Jin-ri heard exactly what she had just exposed, and shut her mouth immediately.

Hara had already turned to her.

“They’re really coming back?”

Jin-ri hesitated one second, then nodded after all.

“I don’t know the date. But something moves this year.”

Hara went quiet.

She was not so naive as to believe that Girls’ Generation minus one member stopped being Girls’ Generation.

If a female solo ran headfirst into a Girls’ Generation comeback, the headlines didn’t even need writing.

The headlines that write themselves

GIRLS’ GENERATION BACK AS EIGHT — TOTAL SWEEP OF THE CHARTS

GOO HARA’S FIRST SOLO — RESULTS FALL SHORT OF HOPE

That would be the polite version.

And if the company’s song couldn’t carry, if the stage got the muted-and-cut edit, if her live breath wavered, and Girls’ Generation’s stage rolled in right behind hers…

The truth was, Hara had been looking forward to this solo.

All her debuted years in the group held glory, held exhaustion, and held many moments of wondering whether there shouldn’t be something that belonged to her alone.

When the company finally said solo, she complained on the surface — too many meetings, too little budget, plans made of air — and was happy underneath the whole time.

Then a few sentences from Baek Si-on peeled that expectation apart, layer by layer, until only one very realistic inventory remained:

Does the song fit her?

Will the company actually spend money?

Will the comeback window get flattened by a mega-group?

Can she hold a live stage?

And — if it all goes badly in the end, whose fault does it become?

Hara looked down at the demo’s filename on her phone screen, and after a long moment asked:

“Then I release it later?”

Baek Si-on didn’t answer that question. He asked one of his own:

“When does your contract expire?”

Hara went still.

“Early next year.”

“Renewing?”

This time, the silence lasted longer.

It was not, technically, a birthday-dinner question.

But Baek Si-on asked it directly enough that it skipped all the beautiful nonsense.

“I don’t plan to renew.”

The instant it left her mouth, Hara heard her own answer — as if for the first time.

She was leaving next year. Which meant this solo album was never, in substance, “Goo Hara’s first solo.”

It was the last single-artist project DSP would ever do on her.

❝

A company does not spend real money on an artist who is already leaving.

— company logic, stated plainly at a birthday table

Still less would it take on complicated co-investment agreements and backend-split negotiations for the sake of one solo album. Release it cheap, sell whatever sells — that is company logic.

“Debut the solo in Japan.”

Hara looked up.

“What?”

Jin-ri blinked too.

“Solo in Korea means facing Lee Ji-eun, HyunA, Sunmi — and beyond them, this summer’s girl-group scramble.”

“Japan is the world’s second-largest music market.”

“There, you have a nostalgia base. You have visual recognition. You have the general-public foundation KARA left behind.”

“And Japanese otaku do not care whether your muted stage went off-key or your high notes held. They check two things: whether your MV is pretty, and whether your photobook is gorgeous.”

“……”

Jin-ri burst out laughing.

Hara turned and glared.

Jin-ri looked down instantly and played dead.

Hara watched her for two seconds, then turned back.

“Was that a compliment, or are you calling Japanese otaku shallow?”

“Both.”

“…But the company won’t do a Japan project.”

Hara said it slowly:

“KARA’s contracts expire next year. The company will not want to sink more budget.”

“Even if I pay for it myself, it’s useless — they won’t sign a complicated co-investment agreement for one solo of mine.”

“So even if the direction you said is right, it can barely be made.”

Baek Si-on listened, nodded, and said:

“If the only problem is budget, I can help.”

Hara looked at him as if suspecting her own ears.

“You?”

“Me.”

“We could not afford you in the first place!”

“Friend price.”

“Do you understand that your friend price is still terrifying?”

“Installments available.”

Hara: “……”

Jin-ri, who had been listening properly this whole time, nearly lost it again here.

Hara rubbed her temples.

“It’s not about the money… no. It is about the money. But it’s not only about the money… I mean — why are you helping me?”

At some point Blin had jumped down from the chair, made one lap of the table legs, and stopped at Baek Si-on’s foot, looking up at him.

Baek Si-on looked down at the cat, and said:

“I’m DSP stock too. That chapter never made me famous — but it made me a great senior to a truly impressive number of people.”

Hara gave a short laugh.

“That part is true.”

“The title has its uses.”

“So?”

“A dignified farewell to the old employer. That’s not a losing trade.”

It sounded like realism. But Hara could hear the staircase he was holding out for her.

Not “I’m famous now, so have some of my resources.”

Rather —

You helped me once.

I remember.

I’m DSP too.

Let this be a dignified farewell fee, paid on behalf of a past that was never glorious but truly existed.

Hara looked down at the cake on the table.

Today was, expressly, Jin-ri’s birthday.

And somehow she felt she had just received a late gift of her own.

Hara picked up her glass and took a slow drink. The alcohol burned going down her throat.

She smiled once, and pressed all of that complicated feeling back down where it belonged.

“Let me state one thing first.”

She set the glass down, and her voice returned to its usual ease.

“Friend price is fine. But not so low that I start suspecting you of charity.”

Baek Si-on got the last piece of beef into his mouth, swallowed, and only then said:

“Think of it as after-sales service.”

Hara frowned:

“After-sales of what?”

“Factory recall. One defective DSP boy-group unit, returned to the factory for maintenance.”

“……”

Hara surveyed the whole table —

One man who had just sung the birthday girl to tears; one birthday girl who had cried at the song and couldn’t hide the happiness; and one cat so cold he could have been auditing a shareholders’ meeting.

She decided her mission tonight was, at this point, essentially complete.

Any longer and she wouldn’t be keeping her little sister company — she’d be providing a live studio audience for couple talk.

She checked the time.

“All right. I’m out.”

Jin-ri startled.

“Unnie, you said you were staying tonight?”

“That was the original plan.”

Hara looked at Baek Si-on, then at her.

“But the sugar content of your apartment’s air now exceeds legal limits. Continued exposure puts me at risk of diabetes.”

Jin-ri’s ears went red on the spot.

“It does not…”

Hara pointed at the Bluetooth speaker, not yet packed away.

“That song earlier — sugar, or artificial sweetener?”

Jin-ri: “……”

Baek Si-on added, with perfect calm:

“No.”

Hara looked at him.

“Shut up. You’re the sugar source.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

The rebuttal difficulty on that one was high.

“But unnie, you’ve been drinking.”

Jin-ri sounded worried.

“So I’m calling a car.”

“But…”

“Then I’ll stay?”

“……”

Jin-ri looked down and arranged the forks on the table with tremendous precision.

Hara smiled, satisfied.

“Obedient.”

❧ ❧ ❧

She called the car; a few minutes later, the driver was downstairs.

Jin-ri insisted on seeing her down. Baek Si-on rose to come too. Hara glanced at him, and didn’t refuse.

The three of them went down together.

The complex at this hour was very quiet. Late March in Seoul still had a bite in it; wind ran between the buildings, and Jin-ri shrank her neck without meaning to.

Baek Si-on draped his coat over her shoulders, naturally, as if it were nothing.

Hara watched this happen and rolled her eyes with feeling.

“Right now I genuinely want to pack myself in a box and mail myself to a country where romance has been outlawed.”

The car stopped at the curb.

Hara opened the door and did not get in.

She looked at Baek Si-on first.

“She waited for you all day today. So — the part where you came: that was decent.”

“Just ‘decent’?” Baek Si-on asked.

“You want ‘excellent’?”

Hara raised an eyebrow.

“That depends on subsequent performance.”

Jin-ri’s face got redder still.

She had the sense that every sentence out of Hara tonight was a match held deliberately to her ear.

And she could not rebut a single one.

Only then did Hara turn to her.

“Come here.”

Jin-ri went, obedient.

Hara tucked the coat a little closer around her shoulders and said, low:

“Don’t be too good.”

Jin-ri looked up at her.

Hara’s voice went lower still — a register only the two of them could hear.

“Want something, say so. Want someone to stay — make him stay.”

“Stop waiting for other people to decide for you.”

Jin-ri’s fingers caught the edge of the coat.

“Unnie…”

Hara poked her in the forehead.

“Don’t go all Blin-in-the-bath at exactly the moments that matter.”

“What’s wrong with Blin in the bath?”

“Stiff. Helpless. Eyes full of accusations against the world.”

Jin-ri couldn’t hold the laugh.

Hara smiled too.

Then she bent down and settled into the car.

❧ ❧ ❧

The car pulled away slowly. Its taillights dragged two red points through the dark, took the corner, and were gone.

Only the two of them remained in the complex below.

Baek Si-on looked at her.

“Let’s go up.”

“Mm.”

They went into the building together.

In the living room, an hour ago, there had been Hara, Blin, cake, seaweed soup, a Bluetooth speaker, and a large pile of things to hide behind.

Now there was nothing.

Only the elevator’s mirrored wall, the numbers jumping on the floor buttons, and less than half a meter between the two of them.

Jin-ri watched the floor numbers.

2.

3.

“Tired?” Baek Si-on asked.

She shook her head.

4.

5.

Jin-ri said, abruptly:

“Tonight you don’t get to say you have to go home and feed the cat.”

Baek Si-on looked over at her.

Jin-ri didn’t look at him. She studied her own reflection in the elevator doors.

“The cat is at my place.”

6.

7.

“Then I can say I have to go home and feed myself.”

Jin-ri whipped her head around and glared.

Baek Si-on added:

“…But I just ate.”

Jin-ri was laughed into fury.

8.

The elevator arrived. The doors opened.

The corridor light was warm yellow, lying quietly on off-white walls.

Jin-ri tilted her head at him:

“Any other excuses left?”

“Do you want me to have one?”

Jin-ri thought of the thing Goo Hara had just said to her.

Don’t be too good.

Want someone to stay — make him stay.

She said, very small:

“No.”

Baek Si-on nodded, took her hand, and stepped out of the elevator.

❧ ❧ ❧

The corridor was short. A dozen steps to her door.

Jin-ri lowered her head and keyed in the password.

The door opened, and the aftermath of the birthday arrived all at once.

The birthday aftermath, as found

Cake — one half remaining.

Candle — toppled, against the rim of the white plate.

Seaweed soup — pot uncollected.

Cups — several, scattered.

Cat — on the table, testing one (1) forgotten piece of sweet-and-sour pork.

Logged by nobody. Witnessed by both.

Blin had jumped onto the dining table at some point and was testing a forgotten piece of sweet-and-sour pork with one paw. Jin-ri snatched him down.

“None of this for you.”

Blin voiced one meow of deep grievance.

Holding the cat, Jin-ri felt suddenly a little embarrassed:

“The place is a bit of a mess.”

Baek Si-on looked around once.

“The birthday was clearly enjoyed.”

Then he took off his shoes, walked into the living room — and did the thing Jin-ri had not predicted in any scenario.

He started clearing the table.

Jin-ri stood where she was, holding the cat, and needed several full seconds to reboot.

“Sunbae… I’ll do the cleaning.”

“It’s your birthday.”

“A birthday doesn’t put a guest on dish duty.”

Baek Si-on paused, and looked up at her.

“Am I a guest?”

Jin-ri opened her mouth, lowered her head, and said, very small:

“…No.”

Baek Si-on didn’t press. He kept clearing.

Jin-ri set Blin down on the sofa and hurried over to help.

One cleared the table; the other wiped the counter. Not much was said in between.

Now and then, their hands touched.

Jin-ri carried the pot to the sink and watched Baek Si-on turn on the tap and start washing.

“You don’t look like someone who does dishes.”

“The army teaches a man many skills — useless, but necessary.”

Jin-ri laughed, picked up the towel beside her, and dried each washed plate, one by one.

The sound of running water filled the kitchen’s unsaid spaces.

For one moment, Jin-ri found the picture strange.

Two hours ago, she had been standing here wishing at candles: hope he hasn’t forgotten me.

One hour ago, he had sung a song for her.

Now he was washing dishes in her kitchen.

What, exactly, had happened in between?

She couldn’t say.

But of one thing she was certain —

The man washing dishes beside her was the same man who had sung Legend for sixty thousand people.

And in this kitchen, he was only a man with his sleeves pushed to his forearms, head down, seriously rinsing sauce off plates.

❝

It undid her more than the stage ever had.

— the sink, the tap, the sleeves

Jin-ri dried the last plate and put it in the cupboard.

Baek Si-on shut off the water and shook it from his hands.

She hurried the towel to him.

When the water stopped, the apartment went suddenly, completely quiet.

The birthday wreckage, cleared away by four hands, little by little.

Jin-ri felt slightly unreal.

“The birthday seems to be over.”

She watched him hang the towel back on its hook:

“But you’re still here.”

Even as she said it, she thought it sounded silly. What did that even mean — it wasn’t as if he was leaving.

But she had wanted to say it anyway.

Baek Si-on looked at her, reached out, and tucked the loose strand of hair behind her ear.

Then he lowered his head: a kiss on her forehead; from her forehead to the space between her brows; from there to the tip of her nose; and at last onto her lips.

This one was lighter than the one at the door. And longer.

Jin-ri’s back came to rest against the edge of the sink, fenced in by his arms.

Nobody knew how long it was before the two of them parted.

Jin-ri’s breathing had gone uneven.

She kept her head down, forehead resting against his chest, not daring to look up.

“Nervous?”

Jin-ri answered into his chest, muffled:

“Mm.”

“Then we go slower.”

She looked up.

This time, she was the one who closed the distance.

Her hands were still gripping the fabric at his chest.

Baek Si-on took her hands off his clothes and laced his fingers through them. His other hand found her waist, and led her away from the sink.

The living-room light went out later that night.

Not because anyone chose to switch it off.

Because Jin-ri, backing up step by step, ran into the wall switch.

She leaned against the wall, fenced in the circle of his arms.

At some point Blin had come down off the sofa, made one circle of their feet — and was gently blocked by Baek Si-on’s foot, and nudged back toward the sofa.

Blin produced one meow as he was escorted out.

The meow’s emotional content appeared to be a formal accusation: that these two-legged creatures were, demonstrably, the type to burn the bridge the moment the river was crossed.
