Bangtan and the Redheads Looking On

October 12–15 Rehearsal floor · then Hapjeong · then a roadside at night Sangam World Cup Stadium · 401 · a Seoul kerb Seven on a floor. Four with a borrowed phone. Sixty and a no. Three-oh-two. A cop at a window.

Seoul. Sangam World Cup Stadium. The seven of Bangtan were lying every which way on the backstage floor. They had just gone another dozen rounds with the floor director on the walk-in and the motion before the chorus.

Kim Nam-joon was not lying. He was on his phone. They were only group-stage support, but they were still part of this stage. If Baek Si-on were boycotted by global esports fans and Imagine Dragons fans, the air at the opening would drop to ice. There might even be real boos. At that hour nobody would care they were only backup. Everyone in a camera would take the weather together. Nam-joon was captain. At such hours he had to look at the wind first. At least know how far the cursing had gone, so the dongsaengs had a mental prep.

He refreshed. Froze. The tags he had expected were still on trending. They had been pushed to third and fourth.

Nam-joon frowned. Bones? Bones? What did that mean. He went into the second tag. Top tweet, an editor at a known American esports outlet: I was about to write a long piece blasting Riot for folding to capital. Then this Korean named Baek put this demo in my face… Okay. I’ll shut up. This groove is lethal.

Right under, a Western music blogger: answering tens of thousands who said he didn’t belong with a new song written in a booth, arrogant to the limit. What kind of star move is this.

Nam-joon: “…?”

He cut back to Korean net. Korean press was faster. Facing overseas boycott, Baek Si-on zero PR: drops a new demo as a hit back. Most arrogant answer in history. Baek Si-on’s 1:32 Bones sweeping global Twitter. No apology, no explanation, only the work. Baek Si-on’s absolute confidence.

Nam-joon had the whole thing. He was now fully sure the nineteenth would not have boos. At least not one-sided boos.

“Daebak…”

Kim Tae-hyung had been face-down on the cold floor cooling. At that he rolled, lifted his head.

“Oppa. What?”

Nam-joon walked it from the start. Overseas net cursing Si-on for dropping in on Imagine Dragons. Half a day of it. Si-on gone red-hot, angry enough to go write a song at the haters. Now the overseas net was talking about that.

The other five “corpses” on the floor sensed melon in the air at once. Jung Ho-seok first off the ground. Speed such that you could not tell he had been saying, thirty seconds ago, “my knees no longer belong to me.” The others piled in. Six heads around Nam-joon’s phone, a sunflower of black hair and sweat.

“The song. I want the song.”

“Yes yes. Play.”

“…”

Nam-joon opened the video. In it Si-on in booth cans, face cold, putting those arrogant lines one by one into the mic. One thirty-two done. Seven people looked at one another.

In Korean entertainment, facing hate and cursing, there was a standard flow almost carved in the bone. Right or not, once you were cursed the first move was always apology. Handwritten letter. Black suit. Ninety-degree bow. Reflect, go silent, stop work. Because you ate public rice. Your weather was not worth money.

And Baek Si-on. Pointed at by tens of thousands of Western netizens. Then he turned into a booth and wrote a song back. No apology. No explanation. No head down. Not even one sentence of PR. Pure work-crush and attitude mock.

Min Yoon-gi looked at the finished video, low:

“That’s much harsher than a statement.”

Nam-joon nodded.

“Because if the song isn’t good they curse you worse.”

“But it is good.”

Jeon Jung-kook said it fast.

“Really good.”

Tae-hyung suddenly remembered something.

“Didn’t SISTAR sunbaes’ fans used to curse Baek Si-on sunbae too.”

They all looked at him. The period when Si-on had collided with SISTAR on a music-show slot.

“So what are you saying — that he didn’t curse back then?”

“Whether he cursed or not!”

Jung-kook stood, one hand on his chest.

“Anyway I declare. Baek Si-on sunbae is my idol.”

He stopped.

“I will take him as faith.”

Kim Seok-jin: “…”

Ho-seok: “…”

Nam-joon looked at him. The kid’s face was extremely serious. Serious enough that Nam-joon could not, for a moment, tell whether he was quoting some chuunibyou anime line or actually conducting some spiritual induction.

Quiet two seconds. Tae-hyung first.

“Me too!”

He put up his right hand. Like an oath. Ho-seok looked at those two, mouth starting to shake. Held three seconds. Could not. Puffed a laugh.

“You two are a new religion.”

“Oppa. This is belief.”

“Belief and chuunibyou are one public occasion apart.”

“Then I can say it in public too.”

“Try. See if manager-hyung kills you.”

“…”

The rest area went to noise. Seok-jin took the chance to imitate Si-on’s tone, voice down:

“They said I don’t belong here—”

Then, with the widest shoulders in Asia, an extremely exaggerated shrug.

“Cool.”

Seven people laughing themselves sideways.

“…did you see Baek Si-on’s tweet.”

Kang Seul-gi’s ears went up first. She put the bottle down, looked at Son Seung-wan beside her. Wendy had heard too. They exchanged a look. Bae Joo-hyun sat farthest in, had not caught all of it, but “Baek Si-on” she had. Park Soo-young lifted her head.

“What happened? What’s with Baek Si-on sunbae?”

“From the staff, it sounds like he posted a really cool video hitting back at haters?”

Four girls looked at one another. Wanted to see. Especially wanted to see. The problem was, as an SM rookie girl group not three months out, their phones had long been taken by the manager. Not a single device that could go online on them.

Joo-hyun looked at Seulgi. Seulgi looked at Wendy. Wendy’s gaze last landed on Soo-young. Soo-young realised, late, she had been chosen.

“Why me?!”

“Your aegyo with oppa works best.” Seulgi, small.

“What do you mean works best? That’s sincerity.”

“Sincere aegyo is still aegyo.”

Joo-hyun pushed her lightly.

“Go.”

Soo-young’s mouth went flat. For the melon, she still stood. She arranged her face, put on the sweetest smile, jogged to the manager not far.

“Oppa~”

Tail up. Two degrees sweeter than usual. The manager looked up.

“What?”

“Can I borrow the phone. Just one look?”

“No.”

“One minute?”

“No.”

“Please please~”

“Soo-young-ah.”

He sighed. Soo-young switched at once. No more aegyo. Information gap.

“Oppa. We’re on the same opening as Baek Si-on sunbae. Shouldn’t we know the situation. If a reporter asks and we can’t answer, that’s embarrassing, right?”

The manager’s fingers stopped at the edge of the phone. He looked at Soo-young. Then at the three farther off. Three heads turned back in a line. He handed the phone.

“Three minutes.”

“Enough! Thank you, oppa!”

Soo-young took it and jogged back. Four heads together at once. Almost the same composition as Bangtan’s just now. Only hair and smell different. Soo-young opened Twitter as if she lived there. No search needed. Trending two, hanging bright.

The melody was already turning in her head. Very earworm. Very like Baek Si-on. Quiet, usually. Once he opened his mouth, you did not forget.

October 12–15 Mail, then three nights Hapjeong 401 Riot wants two songs and another three hundred. Eun-ah says no audio. Jae-joon says overtime. Three-oh-two exports.

When Scooter’s mail came over, Si-on had just come out of the booth. Jae-joon at the desk, second-pass drums on Bones’ chorus. Eun-ah with the tablet, standing by the sofa, reading the mail word by word. Not long. Riot had not expected Bones’ 1:32 to blow a new traffic pool on social. Not Korea talking to itself. Twitter, Reddit, Western esports press, music bloggers all down. The negative around “Imagine Dragons swapped,” “Korean artist dropped in,” had been split open by this song. Some still cursing. Some starting to ask. Some already following Ayi-ya-yi-ya into the earworm.

Riot’s mail said it straight: given Bones’ spread on social had already brought S4 Worlds more topic increment than expected, they hoped Baek Si-on would sing both Legend and Bones at the opening. And add three hundred thousand dollars performance fee. At the same time Riot hoped for sync use of Bones in S4 Worlds opening-related materials.

Eun-ah finished the last paragraph, looked up. Jae-joon first.

“Meaning I have to overtime again?”

She did not answer him. Looked at Si-on.

“Oppa.”

“Mm.”

“What do we do?”

“What do you think?”

Eun-ah’s heart jumped. Because she realised hyung was testing her. Six hundred thousand was tempting. Dual-song at Worlds opening was tempting. She took two deep breaths. Forced her head out of “six hundred thousand is so much money.” Calm. Think. Then she met his eyes.

“I think the nineteenth can do Bones. But we don’t drop audio.”

“Say more.”

“Fans’ energy and wallets are limited.”

Her voice was tight at first. By the second sentence the speed had steadied.

“Bones is hot on social now, yes. But Legend is the title. If Bones comes out now and takes the wind, the result is two songs dragging each other. Once Legend’s charting doesn’t come, the mini’s long tail is all cut. As for when to drop, we need to set it with Scooter against Billboard’s cycle and the MV online time.”

Done, she stopped. Palms a little wet. Looking at him, waiting for the verdict. He leaned on the folding chair, looked at her a solid three seconds. Then nodded once.

“All right. Answer Scooter like that.”

Eun-ah quietly let a long breath, felt the muscle in her back finally loosen. In fact a top manager at Scooter Braun’s level could not possibly not know the harm of two strong singles colliding in the same window. He knew better than anyone. He had not decided for Si-on. He had forwarded Riot’s mail as it was and thrown the question here. There were several ways to read that. One, respect. Two, a test. Three, a line of work. Collaboration was feeling out bottoms and ability. Scooter was feeling theirs.

Si-on looked once more at Eun-ah, head down answering mail. She typed slowly. English not yet at the fly-it-off-the-hand stage. Jae-joon leaned back, put a hand up.

“So.”

Si-on looked at him.

“My overtime.”

“How much do you want?”

Jae-joon had only said it. He had not thought Si-on would actually ask. He froze.

“Ah?”

“A hundred? Two hundred?”

“…”

Jae-joon sat slowly straight.

“The way you are right now is a little scary.”

Si-on thought.

“A hundred, then.”

“Won?”

“Dollars.”

Jae-joon went quiet. Eun-ah looked up too. Si-on added,

“Not arrangement fee. Not producer split.”

“You know?”

“Mm.”

“Your kind of person is the most terrifying.”

“What?”

“I only meant to complain. You just used money to smash through my moral floor.”

“Then work.”

Jae-joon closed his eyes, drew a long breath. Opened.

“Work.”

The next three days. Si-on’s diary became a very unhealthy two-line war. Day: Sado role skills. Brownie could already be ridden at a gallop, at the cost of inner thighs hurting two days. Archery posture already standard; as for where the arrow went, the coach’s original: “the camera only needs the state before you loose.” Ink, hanging wrist, flowing; as for what the rice paper actually held, not important. Court kneeling and sitting, impeccable. Steady. Presence. As for why every kneel needed a three-second pause before rising: the riding-scraped inner thigh had not healed. Rise too hard and the face would snarl.

Night. On time at Hapjeong 401, grinding Bones’ full arrangement with Jae-joon. A hundred thousand dollars of overtime could, in fact, push human potential to a limit. Day one, drums. Day two, chorus. Day three, vocal. In the middle Jae-joon leaned back countless times:

“I think I’m about to die.”

Si-on said,

“A hundred thousand dollars.”

Jae-joon sat straight at once.

“I can live another eight hours.”

Eun-ah on the sofa with the tablet, watching one hum, one mix, one frown, one curse, already fully numb. She had thought recording was art. Now she knew. Recording could also be a worksite. Only they were not carrying brick. They were carrying kick.

October fifteenth. 22:43. Bones full export. Three minutes two seconds. Last note down, Jae-joon went straight back, peaceful as just departed. Si-on put Jae-joon’s tipped head a little more upright, took the left hand that had slid onto the keys and set it on the arm. Then took his coat. Killed the main light. Out.

Late Hapjeong alley, almost no one. A convenience store light far off. He opened the Aslan’s door, sat in. Belt. Ignition. Out of the alley, into the late main. Lights going back one by one. Left hand on the wheel, right hanging. Inner thigh still a little sore. Wrist acid. Knees, needless to say. From head to foot not one part not protesting. He blinked. Blinked again. The third time the lids were less obedient. The lights ahead went from sharp to blurred spots. Spots into tailed rays. His head tipped a little forward. Then snapped. He put the car to the kerb fast. Stopped. Handbrake. Head back. Eyes shut.

“…just a rest.”

He said it to himself. Then no sound. He did not know how long he slept. When the window was knocked, his first thought was Bones’ kick coming in wrong. Dong. Dong-dong. Dong. He opened his eyes slowly. Outside the windshield Seoul’s late lamps were orange rings. By the window a cop in a fluorescent vest, torch in hand, bent looking in. Si-on blinked. Consciousness came back bit by bit from the desk, Jae-joon’s corpse-peaceful sleep, the 3:02 track. He put the window down. Cold air in at once. The cop saw his face and clearly froze.

“Baek Si-on-ssi?”

“Yes.”

His voice a little hoarse. He looked down at the still-buckled belt, then at the roadside. Good. Illegal parking. And asleep in the driver’s seat. Artist image management: a major demerit today. The cop’s professional overlay quickly pressed the surprise of recognising an artist.

“You can’t park here. Are you unwell?”

“No.”

Si-on rubbed his brow.

“Too sleepy. Sorry.”

The cop looked at the grey under his eyes, quiet a second.

“Fatigue driving is dangerous.”

“Mm.”

Si-on nodded.

“Sorry.”

The cop looked down to write the ticket. Si-on did not explain, and did not call anyone to handle it. Pay what should be paid. A few minutes later. Fine done. The cop handed him the slip.

“Please leave soon, or call a designated driver.”

“I’ll call a designated driver.”

“All right. Be safe.”

The cop looked at him again, wanted to say “my daughter likes you,” but in the end did not. Turned and went. Si-on put the window up. The cabin quiet again. He picked up the phone. Screen on. 23:30. Almost half an hour more than the “just a rest” he had imagined. One unread. Bong Joon-ho. [Legend MV, final.] Under it, a video link.
