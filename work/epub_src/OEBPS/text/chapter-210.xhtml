Chapter Two Hundred Ten

Little Schemes and a Big Accident

Mid-April

Late afternoon

Nonhyeon-dong · the last take of the ad-song session

The recording ended more smoothly than expected.

Strict it remained, of course. Baek Si-on and Jung Jae-joon listened line by line behind the console and revised section by section — even a drink commercial was drilled to the standard of a year-end-awards opening stage. Fortunately, Red Velvet was still at the hottest point of its promotion cycle; voices and instincts were fully online. A few rounds in, everything owed had been delivered, more or less.

As the final patch was recorded, Jae-joon pulled his headphones off and leaned back into the chair.

“Alright. That’s it for today.”

The five girls exhaled as one and filed out of the booth.

“The most seriously I have ever sung in my life,” Joy said, drinking water, “and it turns out to be a beverage commercial.”

“On the bright side,” Wendy offered, “we can now put ‘recorded under the Baek Si-on producer system’ on our résumés.”

Irene wasn’t taking part in the discussion.

She was looking down at the shoes on her feet.

The investment, as audited

The asset: the Under Armour collaboration low-top trainers, worn today by deliberate choice. The cost ledger: three extra seconds at the shoe cabinet before departure; a full van ride spent shielding them from other people’s feet; the ankles angled pretty on entering the studio.

And the result? The returns, to date: one glance — the glance he’d given her the moment she arrived. One. And then nothing. No “how is the ankle holding up.” No “are they still comfortable.”

Conclusion: the amount of thought invested in this pair of shoes exceeded the thought invested in today’s entire lyric sheet. And therefore — she would manufacture an opportunity herself. This much small scheming was not going to die quietly in a recording booth.

The plan, as drafted on the spot

Step one — while pretending to adjust her shoes, work a shoelace loose, such that it shows when she passes by Baek Si-on.

Step two — wait for him to say the line: “Your lace is undone.”

Step three — crouch, tie the lace, and drop, naturally, some variation of: “The shoes you gave me really are comfortable.” Risk assessment: none. The plan was, by any internal review, flawless.

The trouble with plans is that they are usually beautiful in the head.

Irene’s attention was concentrated far too heavily on a certain person; her foot caught, and the world moved first and chose for her.

The lace — under her own shoe.

Her body pitched forward, and in the fraction of a second that remained, one extremely clear thought flashed through her head:

This is it.

The next second, a hand caught her from behind — Irene was hauled to a stop in place.

No fall. Feet, stable. Person, stable.

But something was not right.

Slowly, she turned her head. First: Baek Si-on’s face. Then, following his line of sight downward — the hand he had frozen in mid-air. More precisely still: the thing his fingers were holding.

Her ears went hot with a whump.

Shame arrived faster than the fright had.

“You can… you can let go now.”

That was when Baek Si-on fully understood. It was probably the fastest release of his entire life.

And then —

SNAP—

The strap, stretched to its limit, carried a full load of elastic potential energy and snapped back against Irene’s back. The sound was crisp enough to be a finger-crack.

The tableau, as witnessed by four

Irene standing with her back to Baek Si-on, on the exact verge of evaporating on the spot. Baek Si-on, one hand still hanging in mid-air — a man freshly caught on pause.

The four younger members, already at the door, rotated their heads around in perfect unison. Four people exchanged four glances. The room fell very, very suspiciously quiet.

Baek Si-on lowered his hand, slowly, to his side.

He understood completely what had just happened. In the instant Irene’s lace caught and her body tipped, his reaction had outrun his judgment — the hand went out before the brain could file its report on where, exactly, to grab. By the time the fingers’ report reached his brain, everything was already over.

❝

By results-based reasoning, the logic held. Uncaught, she goes face-first into the recording-studio floor; a wrist braced against the ground possibly sprained, a knee almost certainly swollen. The strap, by contrast: physical damage effectively zero. Of two harms, choose the lesser. He had made the correct choice.

— the correct choice, defensible in every forum except the one it happened in

So Baek Si-on opened his mouth and said:

“No need to thank me.”

Irene: “……”

She was entirely certain that if she turned around right now, the face — already burned past containment — would make everything exponentially worse. So she chose walking.

On the first step, her left hand and left foot moved together. She noticed on the second step, and corrected. The third step returned to normal. The fourth nearly took her into the doorframe.

Watching her list sideways toward the frame, Baek Si-on’s hand rose again on instinct — then, remembering the lesson just administered, went back down. Forget it. Calling out to her now would only break her further. Chasing her out was worse. Explanations were useless. Silence was, at this moment, the single correct decision — let time slowly pave over that snap.

Jae-joon rotated around behind the console, looked at Baek Si-on, then at the back disappearing down the corridor.

“You just—”

“Don’t.”

“I only want to confirm—”

“Shut it.”

Jae-joon spun back to his screen and began typing meaninglessly on the keyboard.

Mid-April

Dusk

The van · rear corner · full self-seal

By the time the van pulled out, Irene had folded herself into the farthest corner of the back row. Cap pulled low, brim hiding nearly the whole face. Arms crossed over her chest — a physical, load-bearing self-seal.

Some people, clearly, could not read a room.

“Unnie, what actually happened back there?”

Joy opened. Irene did not acknowledge her existence.

“What did sunbae mean by ‘no need to thank me’?”

Still nothing.

“Did you almost fall?” Wendy tried, more gently.

Beneath the brim: no sound at all.

“Unnie?”

Silence.

Joy looked around at the others and mouthed it: what happened?

Seulgi shook her head. Wendy shook hers too.

Yeri examined the strip of red ear showing below the cap, and asked in a very, very small voice:

“Sunbae… did he do something?”

The entire van went quiet for two seconds.

From under the brim, a sound finally emerged.

“Shut up.”

Every mouth in the vehicle closed simultaneously.

Outside the windows, the streetscape withdrew frame by frame. Under the cap, Irene bit her lip while the footage in her head refused to stop replaying.

Why!

Why was it, every single time she stood in front of him, that it came to this?!

And this time?

The greatest-hits reel, as ranked by social damage

Last time: hauled up in front of everyone like a sack of rice — a textbook fireman’s carry containing not one milliliter of shoujo manga — and dignity partially recovered only via the line about how, at home, her parents called her a princess, which bought her an actual princess carry.

This time: her undergarment strap snapped back in public, audibly, studio-wide.

Other people’s collaborations with sunbaes were romantic, dignified, résumé-worthy experiences. Every single “iconic moment” between her and Baek Si-on so far belonged, without exception, to a socially fatal compilation she would pay to delete from her own memory.

❧ ❧ ❧

Back at the dorm, Irene went straight into her room.

She closed the door. She did not lock it — the door’s aura alone was enough to make everyone stop at a respectful distance without being told.

The evening, as pledged

Cap: removed and thrown onto the bed. The snapped-strap spot on her back, palpated: still a small sting.

The vows, in order: no dinner today. No shower. No going out. Lie here — lie here until tomorrow, until the day after, until every last person on earth has forgotten the sound that recording booth made today.

Irene pressed her face into the pillow.

Could she forget it, though?

Probably not.

She rotated her face inside the pillow, trading cheeks.

She wanted very badly to drag every decision made since that morning out into the street and beat them, one by one.

And, incidentally, to beat up Baek Si-on as well.

❝

If she could win.

— the day’s final account, closed at a loss
