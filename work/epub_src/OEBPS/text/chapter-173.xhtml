Chapter One Hundred Seventy Three

The Highest-Value Steal in History

December 25 · Christmas, in two time zones One holiday special shot in a living room · a league with a viewership problem · a phone call that pulls a manager off a plane · one courtside deal sealed before tip-off Los Angeles · a taping corner and LAX → Chicago · O’Hare, a frozen bronze Jordan, and the United Center A Christmas special is filmed with three props and one directive to smile, the NBA’s showcase game loses its headline act and borrows a singer, an old favor comes back around wearing a rap verse — and a manager discovers his artist now closes deals as a sideline.

December 25.

Los Angeles.

Early in the morning, Baek Si-on was recording the Christmas special version of the ‘Love Yourself’ video.

Special version was a grand phrase for a very simple set.

One Christmas tree.

One guitar.

A few strings of warm yellow lights.

And behind the camera, Baek Eun-ah gesturing at him with her hand, over and over:

“Smile.”

Park Ji-hun said quietly beside her:

“If he suddenly smiles too brilliantly, the fans might decide the company is forcing him to perform.”

“…That’s fair.”

‘Love Yourself’ · Christmas special · take one

One Christmas tree, undecorated beyond the lights.

One guitar, one stool, one take that lasts the whole song.

Artist’s expression: professionally neutral. The director’s hand signals from behind the camera grew increasingly festive.

‘Love Yourself’ · Christmas special · the approved take

Same tree. Same guitar. Same everything.

At the third “smile,” one corner of his mouth committed — provisionally.

The fans would later call it “the twelve-percent smile.” No one at the shoot felt licensed to demand more.

The recording went smoothly.

At noon, the whole party headed straight for Los Angeles International Airport.

Destination: Chicago.

The NBA Christmas games are the highest-rated regular-season broadcast day of every year, and the league puts three times its usual thought into the scheduling.

In this year’s lineup, Lakers at Bulls was a marquee game all by itself.

But Kobe, managing his body, had chosen to sit the game out — and the league’s viewership forecast dropped a full notch.

Lakers–Bulls without Kobe was fried chicken without the sauce. Edible. But something was missing.

So the league came up with a fix.

If the court was missing a story, put one beside it.

And that is how Baek Si-on became the invitation for the opening performance.

They had barely reached the departures hall when Scooter’s phone rang.

He answered, listened for about thirty seconds, and his face traveled from normal, to resigned, to the full palm-over-forehead.

Scooter Braun ← Justin’s side · LAX departures hall · incoming

Mm.

The resignation arrived first. The palm followed thirty seconds in.

…You’re kidding.

The forehead completed its journey.

Fine. Don’t let him near any statements. I’m on my way.

He hung up, turned, and reported to Baek Si-on.

“Baek — Justin’s got trouble. I have to go deal with it. Chicago’s without me.”

“What now? Threw eggs at the neighbor’s house again?”

“……”

For one full second, Scooter genuinely did not know whether to feel relieved.

At least Baek Si-on remembered Justin’s old news.

“No.”

Scooter pressed the bridge of his nose:

“Not eggs this time. I’ll brief you when you’re back.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Mm.”

Before leaving, Scooter looked over at Baek Eun-ah.

“The league and Universal people will meet you in Chicago. Your itinerary is already synced.”

Baek Eun-ah nodded.

“Got it.”

Scooter looked back at Baek Si-on.

“And.”

“Do not evaluate Justin in any interview.”

Baek Si-on said:

“No one will ask me.”

“And if they do?”

“I’ll say I don’t know him.”

“……”

That was crueller than any evaluation.

But there was no time to correct it. Scooter hurried off with his assistant.

❧ ❧ ❧

While they waited to board, Baek Eun-ah started scrolling.

American entertainment news ran at a far higher density than Korea’s, and the gossip traveled at something beyond ridiculous.

It had been less than ten minutes since Scooter’s call, and the whole mess was already unfurling across Twitter and TMZ.

Five minutes of scrolling gave her the full picture.

Ken-doll’s half-sister — the one the tabloids called Kendall — had organized a trip: Baek Si-on’s labelmate in spirit, fresh from her breakup with Justin, plus the friend — Hailey — off to Dubai together.

The three girls departed as one unit.

Photos went up.

Same frame at the airport, smiles at maximum wattage.

It looked exactly like a normal best-friends vacation.

Then, in the very next news cycle, Hailey surfaced in Los Angeles — and the paparazzi caught her pressed up against Justin, intimacy in high definition.

The ex was still in Dubai.

She opened social media and discovered the entire world looking at photos of her former boyfriend wrapped around the friend she had trusted.

Then she unfollowed Hailey.

The comment sections had already boiled over.

TMZ · the comments · Christmas melee

A perfectly standard American-entertainment year-end melee.

Baek Eun-ah finished laying out the whole cause-and-effect chain.

Baek Si-on heard it through, then said something she did not expect.

“When we head back — bring some American specialties for Dispatch’s Director Lim.”

Baek Eun-ah looked up.

“???”

Baek Si-on offered no explanation.

Baek Eun-ah thought about it, and decided not to press.

She vaguely suspected some causal link between this instruction and the Justin mess, but from long experience with her cousin: asking now would produce no answer.

Whatever.

Write it down first.

❧ ❧ ❧

Chicago. O’Hare International Airport.

The moment Baek Si-on stepped out of the jet bridge, the wind made him stop for a beat.

If December in Los Angeles is a spoiled favorite child of the Pacific warm current, December in Chicago is an ice cellar cursed by Lake Michigan.

The wind off the lake came wrapped in fine ice and snow, attacking every living thing leaving the airport doors without discrimination.

Baek Eun-ah walked out the exit and folded herself into a ball.

“Is this a city or a cold-storage warehouse?”

Park Ji-hun clutched his coat shut.

“I now understand why the people here all look so strong.”

“Why?”

“The weak ones got blown away.”

The two bodyguards took their places at Baek Si-on’s sides, faces blank.

Work was work.

Cold was also genuinely cold.

The whole party shivered into the cars and headed straight for the United Center.

Outside the arena, the bronze statue of Michael Jordan stood in the plaza.

The leap was frozen in sub-zero air, a thin skin of frost on the bronze.

Baek Si-on paused under it.

“Cousin. Want a photo?”

“Sure.”

Baek Eun-ah snapped it at once, then studied the result.

“Cousin, the man next to you is the god of basketball.”

Baek Si-on looked at the photo.

“Mm.”

“You’re not nervous?”

“He’s not going to come down and play defense on me.”

“……”

It made sense.

So why did it sound so punchable.

❧ ❧ ❧

Inside the arena, the league staff were already waiting.

Baek Si-on walked the stage once, sound-checked, ran the set with the band.

The rehearsal wrapped quickly.

Everything was ready.

6:30 p.m., Chicago time.

The United Center was packed to the last seat.

Christmas-day atmosphere was a different species from an ordinary regular-season night — red and green everywhere in the stands.

When the intro to ‘Legend’ sounded, the building’s mood was hauled straight up by the collar.

the United Center · Christmas Day opening · ‘Legend’

Center floor, one mic, house lights still up — the intro lands and the crowd hauls itself out of its seats.

“Here we go.”

The first line stood the front rows up. The song had spent months inside the NBA’s season promos; nobody in this building needed to be taught the words.

“Bang bang!”

“Bang bang!”

“Won’t stop till we are legends!”

By the chorus the arena was fully alight — the broadcast camera swept a stand of phones, thirty thousand mouths shouting a hook their teams had been running on screens since October.

When the last line ended, applause and whistles detonated together.

As he came off the floor, a man in the front row — sunglasses, dreadlocks, a gold chain — put a fist out toward him.

Wiz Khalifa

Rapper · the rap half of ‘See You Again’

Pittsburgh · ‘Black and Yellow’ · ‘Blacc Hollywood’

Adam’s friend, the reason an arena playlist runs on ‘Legend’, and a man who keeps the demo Universal threw away saved in his own pocket. He decided Baek Si-on was his brother for life somewhere around three in the morning.

“Hey, brother. That performance was fire.”

Baek Si-on placed the face and met the fist with his own.

“Thank you, Wiz Khalifa.”

“I was just talking to Adam. That man spent ten minutes on the phone cursing you — says that wedding-crash idea you gave him nearly got him whipped to death by the bride’s father.”

Baek Si-on said:

“Then it means he didn’t run fast enough.”

Wiz froze for a beat, then burst out laughing, the gold chain swinging conspicuously.

“Damn. I have to tell him that exact sentence.”

The two of them sat down at courtside.

The players were coming out to warm up, the sound of basketballs on hardwood arriving one thud at a time.

“Actually — Adam tells me you’re a genius.”

Baek Si-on turned to look at him.

Wiz went on:

“I heard your ‘Sign of the Times’ just now too. Genuinely stunning.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Thank you. I like your ‘Black and Yellow’ a lot as well.”

“I’m not making conversation.”

Wiz took off his sunglasses, and underneath were tired eyes.

“I’ve got myself a big problem.”

Baek Si-on asked:

“Fake weed?”

“……”

Two seconds of silence.

“Worse than that.”

“The dollar-sign idiots at Universal — Furious 7 is doing its Paul Walker tribute, and they had me record a rap for it.”

“Those are the most heartfelt bars I have ever written in my life!”

“And now the song is about to die.”

“Why?”

Baek Si-on followed the thread.

“Because the kid who wrote the chorus is a YouTube kid. He went to Universal and said if the song wasn’t his original vocal, they couldn’t use it.”

Wiz spread his hands.

“So Universal’s executives told him to get lost.”

“Now they’re shopping my rap around to every pop queen in the book to re-cut the chorus, and every one of them sings it like an opera showcase — there’s no brokenness in it. No losing-a-brother. Nothing.”

“To hell with it — my best work is about to be ruined!”

Furious 7. The Paul tribute. A YouTube kid. A chorus kicked out the door.

The keywords assembled themselves in Baek Si-on’s head at speed.

The twelve-week world-dominating nuclear single: ‘See You Again’.

His face did not move at all as he asked:

“Is that kid’s demo on your phone?”

Wiz blinked.

“It is.”

He patted his pocket.

“I kept it.”

“Play it for me.”

Wiz fished a pair of earbuds out of his pocket and handed one over.

Baek Si-on took it and fit it into his right ear.

Wiz pressed play.

♪

“See You Again” · demo

Charlie Puth · reference vocal · the take Universal threw away

It’s been a long day without you, my friend—

And I’ll tell you all about it when I see you again—

Very simple piano came first.

Then that voice — clean, with the smallest tremor inside the clean.

Of course.

It was this one.

Baek Si-on took out the earbud and handed it back.

Wiz studied him.

“Adam says when you sang ‘Sign of the Times’ — that thing where you crumble a heart and then put it back together — it would sit right on top of my rap. So I’m asking. Can you try writing a chorus with that feeling?”

“And if it goes badly, nobody’s going to laugh at you.”

Baek Si-on glanced at the court.

The players were still shooting.

The crowd noise spread outward in layers, and the warmth of Christmas Day and the grief Wiz had just described felt like two different worlds sitting in the same building.

Baek Si-on looked back and asked:

“So — Adam sent you to me?”

Wiz nodded.

“He says your head is full of ideas that make no sense and keep working anyway.”

“He also says if anyone on this coast can fish a song back out of Universal’s executives’ hands, you’re on the list.”

He paused, and his face turned serious.

“Baek. If you can save this song of mine — you’re my brother for life. That’s a promise.”

Baek Si-on didn’t answer right away.

A very plain thought floated up in his head.

All he had done was hand Adam a music-video concept.

Granted, the concept had very nearly gotten Maroon 5 hunted down by a father-in-law with a belt.

But somewhere along the results chain, that favor had traveled all the way around to Wiz — and delivered ‘See You Again’ to the seat beside his.

What was this even supposed to be?

Good deeds getting repaid?

Or: a father-in-law’s belt will, eventually, lash a Billboard chart-topper into existence?

Baek Si-on looked up.

“I’ll take the job.”

Wiz’s eyes lit up all at once.

“I knew it!”

He clapped Baek Si-on on the shoulder.

“Adam’s friends are the real deal.”

Baek Si-on stood.

“I’m heading out.”

Wiz blinked.

“Already? The game hasn’t started.”

“Mm.”

Baek Si-on pulled his zipper up.

“I’m afraid Universal will finish deciding things without me.”

Wiz understood immediately.

This could not wait.

If Universal had already picked a pop singer to patch the chorus, then the studio would be booked, the contract signed — and untangling it afterward would be misery.

“Then I owe you one.”

Baek Si-on shook his head, dug into his bag, and produced a bottle of BodyArmor staged in advance for advertising duty. He handed it over.

Wiz looked down at it.

“What’s this?”

“A sports drink.”

“I can see that. I’m asking why you’re giving it to me.”

“If a camera finds you tonight — advertise it. Kobe’s an investor.”

Wiz stared at him for two full seconds.

Then he laughed.

“You’re the kind of man who writes you a song and can’t stop selling.”

“Mutual profit.”

“All right. Deal.”

He raised the bottle and swished it.

“I promise you — when that camera finds me, this thing will outshine my chain.”

Baek Si-on nodded and turned toward the tunnel.

Baek Eun-ah and Park Ji-hun were waiting at the tunnel mouth.

Seeing him come in at speed, Baek Eun-ah was half lost.

“Cousin? Not watching the game?”

“Not watching.”

“Why? Weren’t you just—”

“Right now: find a YouTuber named Charlie Puth.”

Baek Eun-ah’s phone was already out.

“How do you spell it?”

Baek Si-on spelled it.

She keyed it in fast.

Baek Si-on added one line:

“There’s a gap in the middle of his eyebrow.”

Baek Eun-ah looked up.

“What?”

“Identification feature.”

“……”

She looked back down and searched.

Within seconds the screen produced a young white man’s photo.

It was true.

The middle of that eyebrow did look like a goddess of clay had swept an eraser across it, once, gently.

Baek Eun-ah held her silence for two seconds.

“Cousin.”

“Mm?”

“Is your method of identifying people always this specific?”

“Easy to remember.”

“……”

Fine.

At least there would be no mistaking the man.
