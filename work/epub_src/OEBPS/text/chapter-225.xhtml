Chapter Two Hundred Twenty-Five

Jeong Han-teuk’s Million-Won Golden Mouth

May 16 · early afternoon

Moments after a departure — one taillight, gone

Yeouido · KBS · the parking lot exit ramp

Baek Eun-ah’s taillights vanished around the bend at the parking lot exit.

Baek Si-on turned to go find his own driver.

“Si-on!”

Behind him came the sound of a car door closing.

Baek Si-on turned around.

Jeong Han-teuk hopped down from a van, two elegantly packaged boxes of chocolate in hand.

He glanced first in the direction of the departed vehicle, then moved his gaze back to Baek Si-on.

“What are you doing at KBS?”

“Some business.”

Jeong Han-teuk didn’t press for specifics.

The “business” this man discussed had already expanded from song copyrights into automobiles, beverages, film investment, and television placements.

One extra question, and you risked a forced lecture in commerce.

“Ji-eun’s in a bad mood today.”

“What happened?” Baek Si-on asked.

Jeong Han-teuk rattled the two boxes of chocolate.

“The feedback on ‘The Producer’ premiere hasn’t been ideal.”

From the day it was greenlit, this drama was never a “normal” project.

Writer Park Ji-eun had just created a ratings myth with ‘My Love from the Star’.

Kim XX was one of the hottest actors in all of Asia, and with Cha Tae-hyun, Gong Hyo-jin, and Lee Ji-eun added on, the cast was luxurious enough to make the whole industry’s scalp go numb.

KBS’s publicity department had, before broadcast, hoisted audience expectations to the ceiling.

The premiere failed to replicate the nation-sweeping momentum of ‘My Love from the Star’, and public opinion automatically translated “below expectations” into “flopped.”

The mockumentary format drew controversy too.

The media and the online commenters began concentrating their fire:

The verdicts, as circulated

The pacing: loose.

The format: pretentious trickery.

The laughs: not enough.

The luxury cast: wasted.

The performances: below expectations.

Five verdicts, one firehose, zero pause for reply.

Writer, director, and leads were hauled out and judged in rotation.

Lee Ji-eun, an idol-turned-actress, naturally absorbed a denser share of the fire than anyone else.

“What does that have to do with chocolate?” Baek Si-on asked.

“She needs something sweet to take the edge off the anxiety.”

The emotional dead loop was easy to guess.

The loop, as fed

Refresh the hate comments — the anxiety climbs. Sweets, for relief. After the sweets, worry about the weight. And on schedule, a fresh round of anxiety.

Jeong Han-teuk could only treat the first link. Weight management was tomorrow’s problem.

Jeong Han-teuk, for now, could only see to the first link.

The weight-management question could wait for tomorrow.

Baek Si-on leaned against a nearby pillar and thought for a moment.

“If everyone from the writer down to the actors is getting slammed at once, odds are it isn’t just the drama.”

Jeong Han-teuk had only meant to vent for a moment; at this, his brow suddenly moved.

“Meaning what?”

“Someone doesn’t want it to do well.”

The moment the sentence landed, the nature of the topic changed.

The chocolate in Jeong Han-teuk’s hands seemed to be promoted in the same breath — from comfort food to wartime logistics in the war of the entertainment industry.

“Who?”

Baek Si-on listed, offhand.

“tvN.”

A one-second pause.

“Or Kim Eun-sook.”

❧ ❧ ❧

At first, Jeong Han-teuk found it absurd.

But he also knew that the things which sound most absurd are precisely the ones most likely to be true.

tvN was rapidly expanding its drama and variety territory.

KBS was a traditional terrestrial broadcaster; the two were fighting over viewers, advertising, and production talent.

If KBS’s lavish new drama succeeded, the traditional broadcasters’ voice would inevitably strengthen — and tvN quietly paying marketing accounts to push negative pieces and amplify the premiere controversy would be nothing rare in the Korean media ecosystem.

Then, following Baek Si-on’s thread, he thought of Kim Eun-sook.

Kim Eun-sook’s last drama, ‘The Heirs’, had succeeded — but in the same season’s contest for Asian heat, it had lost to Park Ji-eun’s ‘My Love from the Star’.

The two ace female writers were endlessly compared by the media.

Now Park Ji-eun’s new drama had premiered; a rival camp stomping on the opportunity had surface motive to spare.

Extend one ring further, and the suspect pool grew.

The pool, as extended

Same-timeslot productions, fighting for the same hours.

Agencies whose actors were in direct competition with the cast.

Insiders with grievances against the production side.

Media hungry to manufacture controversy into traffic.

And even certain investors Park Ji-eun had once turned away.

Five rings out, five credible motives.

The more he thought, the more it seemed exactly like that.

Jeong Han-teuk had originally come only to deliver chocolate to Lee Ji-eun.

Now he felt as if he had stepped onto one of the hidden rivers of the Korean film and drama world.

Behind those comments, those negative stories and vicious headlines, there seemed to sit a crowd of blurred faces, trading chips with one another across the screens.

“So what you mean is...”

He waited for Baek Si-on’s next-step judgment.

The playbook, as drafted

Investigate the sources of the negative coverage.

Contact KBS public relations.

Counter-place positive pieces.

Help Lee Ji-eun hold the comment trend.

Four items, all statement-shaped. His phone was already out.

Jeong Han-teuk already had his phone out, ready to note down the people who would need calling.

What he heard, instead, was Baek Si-on saying:

“My point is, anxiety usually comes from insufficient skill.”

Jeong Han-teuk’s fingers hung above the screen.

“What?”

“If there’s still time to refresh comment sections, there isn’t enough work. Put the chocolate away for now — buy her two books on acting theory instead.”

Jeong Han-teuk: “……”

The industry-conspiracy model he had spent a full half-minute constructing collapsed on the spot.

So all of Baek Si-on’s preceding theorizing had not been an unmasking of any hidden hand.

It was one single meaning, taking the longest possible detour: Lee Ji-eun was anxious because, deep down, she had no confidence in herself either.

The detour was too wide.

It had very nearly taken Jeong Han-teuk’s whole career around the bend with it.

“That’s it?” Jeong Han-teuk asked, once he had recovered somewhat.

Baek Si-on nodded.

“What else?”

Jeong Han-teuk looked down at the chocolate in his hands.

“You don’t have a single comforting word for her?”

Baek Si-on thought about it.

“Have her cut down on the chocolate. The camera adds weight.”

Jeong Han-teuk: “……”

Wonderful.

Baek Si-on had successfully converted comfort food into the murder weapon of an agent-and-artist falling-out.

The van chose that moment to pull up in front of Baek Si-on.

“I have things to do. I’m off.”

He raised a hand at Jeong Han-teuk.

“See you around.”

And walked off.

Jeong Han-teuk stood in the parking lot, watching Baek Si-on’s figure disappear, and found that the chocolate in his hands had suddenly grown considerably heavier.

❧ ❧ ❧

Same day

One floor up — a phone still refreshing

KBS · the temporary green room

Lee Ji-eun sat on the sofa, a script open on her lap, her phone screen still on the comment page of some related news item.

She had scrolled the comments down through many pages.

Each time, she told herself: one last comment, then stop.

But after the so-called last one, there were usually more.

At the sound of the door, she glanced up.

Jeong Han-teuk set the chocolate down in front of her.

Lee Ji-eun put the phone down, untied the satin ribbon, and lifted the lid.

The chocolates lay in neat ranks on the liner paper, each one wrapped in a different colored foil.

Jeong Han-teuk sat down across from her.

Hesitated.

“I just ran into Baek Si-on downstairs.”

Lee Ji-eun’s hands slowed on the foil.

“Oh.”

But her expression barely changed.

“And then?”

Jeong Han-teuk glanced at the chocolate box.

Then, without so much as a flicker:

“He heard you were feeling low and paid for the chocolate out of his own pocket.”

Lee Ji-eun stared down at the chocolate in her hand.

A moment ago it had been an ordinary piece of candy; now, suddenly, it was something else.

“He bought it?”

“Mm.”

Strictly speaking, the box of chocolate had been selected, purchased, and carried all the way back by Jeong Han-teuk alone.

Baek Si-on’s total participation in the process had been advising Lee Ji-eun to eat less of it.

But an agent’s job description did not end at managing schedules.

It also included, when necessary, a moderately creative edit of the facts, in defense of the artist’s emotional stability.

After all, the emotional value of chocolate signed “Jeong Han-teuk” versus chocolate signed “Baek Si-on” differed by roughly the gap between convenience-store pouch coffee and hand-drip specialty.

“He’s that nice?”

Lee Ji-eun put the chocolate into her mouth.

Bit down.

Dark chocolate, with a trace of citrus and sea salt.

The sweetness spread slowly, and the crease that had been pulled taut between her brows all day eased a little.

“Occasionally.”

She asked again:

“What else did he say?”

Jeong Han-teuk’s mouth opened.

Baek Si-on’s original words began auto-playing in his head.

The originals, as replayed

Anxiety stems from insufficient skill.

Buy two books on acting theory.

Eat less chocolate.

The camera adds weight.

Replay complete. Lines fit for release: zero.

Not one line suitable for saying out loud right now.

And so Jeong Han-teuk performed the day’s second creative edit of the original words.

“He said — stop reading so many comments.”

That one was arguably true; at minimum, the central thesis hadn’t drifted far.

❧ ❧ ❧

Lee Ji-eun gave a soft “oh,” picked up her phone, aimed it at the table, and took a photo.

Jeong Han-teuk was suddenly visited by a sense of imminent doom.

“What are you photographing it for?”

“To send a message.”

“To whom?”

Lee Ji-eun finally gave him a straight look.

The question had never needed asking.

She opened Baek Si-on’s chat window.

Pasted the photo in.

Typed one line on the keyboard.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Lee Ji-eun

[photo — the open chocolate box, foil ranks gleaming]

Lee Ji-eun

Thank you for the chocolate.

Send.

Lee Ji-eun turned the phone face-down on the table, picked up another chocolate, unwrapped it, and sent it into her mouth.

Caramel-filled, this one.

She broke the shell; the caramel flowed out and clung to her fingertip.

She licked her finger.

Then she leaned back into the sofa, legs curling up slightly, the congealed gloom dispersed a good deal by all that pure sweetness.

Jeong Han-teuk, across from her, swallowed.

Lee Ji-eun had just sent off a “verification photo” to claim credit.

If Baek Si-on replied with one line — “what chocolate” — there was a real chance Jeong Han-teuk did not exit the KBS building intact today.

The air in the room seemed to stop moving.

Every faint chewing sound Lee Ji-eun made landed precisely on his heartbeat.

Until an abrupt vibration came from the inner pocket of Jeong Han-teuk’s jacket.

A text-message alert.

Jeong Han-teuk jolted all over and stiffly reached into his pocket, fumbling out his phone.

The screen lit up.

A notification banner from a bank system had popped up.

He slid to unlock and tapped into the unread message.

KB Kookmin Bank

[Deposit notice]
