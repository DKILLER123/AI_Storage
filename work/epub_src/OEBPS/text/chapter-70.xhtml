Chapter Seventy

The Bankrupt Singer's Nationwide Gold Rush

August 8 Evening Incheon · Songdo Beer Festival The first of the long haul, and a crowd that already knows the words

The outdoor stage was set up on the seaside parking lot, with more than three thousand people packed below, plastic cups of draft beer in hand, faces burned red by the stage lights and the alcohol.

The moment Baek Si-on walked on stage, the host had just finished reading out his name, and the crowd blew up.

Then the intro of "Way Back Home" leapt out of the speakers.

That granular pluck, the bouncing rhythm, the kick drum pushing low and dull.

Baek Si-on raised the mic.

The very first line he opened his mouth on.

Someone below sang along.

By the chorus.

The voices of three thousand people merged into one, drowning out the speakers, drowning out the wind, drowning out the traffic coming and going on the road outside the parking lot.

"—making my wandering, my Way Back Home—"

Baek Si-on held the mic and pushed it out toward the crowd.

The three-thousand-person chorus poured in.

The second song, "Moves Like Jagger."

He took off the blazer, tossed it over the edge of the stage, and stood in just a black short-sleeve, the clearly defined lines of his forearms — sculpted by the gym — showing.

Adam Levine's falsetto section he didn't use falsetto, driving it straight with chest voice instead, the timbre a good deal thicker than the original. A few girls below screamed until they cracked.

The third song to close it out, Pharrell Williams's "Happy."

The moment the drum-machine intro came in, the whole atmosphere snapped from restless to warm.

Someone lifted a beer cup and swayed it to the beat, someone threw an arm over the person next to them and rocked side to side, and a middle-aged man in the middle of the crowd broke into some dance that had no name at all, until his wife slapped him back into place.

When the three songs ended and Baek Si-on came down the side of the stage, Baek Eun-ah handed him a bottle of water.

"Cousin, when you took your blazer off, somebody down there shouted 'husband.'"

"Mm."

"It was a middle-aged man."

"…Drop it."

✿ ✿ ✿

August 9, the second gig, Daejeon Expo Plaza Civic Festival.

August 10, the third gig, Gwangju Mudeungsan Summer Night Music Festival.

August 11 was a day off.

Baek Si-on went to Kim Jae-kyung's dorm.

The half-finished blazer was held together with white basting stitches, no buttons, the collar still raw-edged.

But the lines were already visible.

"What about the one for Choi Jin-ri?"

"It's hanging in the back room. Want to see?"

"No."

Once the measurements were done, Kim Jae-kyung wrote the modified numbers in her notebook, and said the finished piece would be ready in two weeks.

August 12, the fourth gig, Busan Haeundae Beach Music Festival.

A stage built on the sand, the speakers underscored by the sound of the waves, and below — a crowd of young people in swimwear and tourists holding up phones to film.

Singing "Way Back Home" by the sea was a completely different thing from singing it in a parking lot.

The salty sea breeze scattered the last note, mixing with the rhythm of the surf hitting the shore, as if the song had been meant to be sung at the edge of the water all along.

Baek Si-on noticed something.

In the second row down, slightly to the right, two girls hadn't put their cameras down from the moment he walked on stage.

Not at the first one.

At the second one had appeared a single.

By the fourth, it was two.

Their lenses stayed locked on Baek Si-on the whole time, catching every movement, every change of expression, every turn of the head or lift of the hand.

Fan photographers.

In Korea's idol ecosystem, a fan photographer sat between fan and paparazzi.

They bought professional photographic gear out of their own pockets, carried telephoto lenses to chase schedules across the country, retouched the shots, posted them to the fan-site account, and released them free for all the fans to download.

Not for money.

For love.

In the gap during the second song, Baek Si-on, still on stage, gave a slight nod toward the direction of those two telephoto lenses.

The shutter clicks grew denser.

August 13, the fifth gig, Jeonju Bibimbap Festival's affiliated concert.

August 14, the sixth gig, Suwon Hwaseong Summer Night Cultural Festival.

August 15, Liberation Day, a national holiday, no gigs.

Baek Si-on had planned to rest at home for a day.

But in the morning Baek Eun-ah asked:

"Cousin, Jin-ri eonni has a press conference for 'The Pirates' this afternoon. Are you going?"

"The Pirates" was a Korean period adventure blockbuster, and Choi Jin-ri had a supporting role in it.

That wasn't what mattered.

What mattered was that this was her first public appearance since SM had announced her "activity suspension."

He went.

The press conference ran for over forty minutes.

Choi Jin-ri was called on by name for a question twice, both about the character itself — routine questions.

She answered smoothly, no problem with her wording.

No one asked her about the "activity suspension."

Not today, at least.

After the press conference, Choi Jin-ri came out the side door, led by her manager toward the van.

Passing the last row of the exit, her step faltered.

Half a face showed beneath the brim of the cap.

She recognised him.

Choi Jin-ri's lips moved, like she wanted to say something, but the manager behind was hurrying her along.

Baek Si-on gave her a slight lift of the chin.

Choi Jin-ri lowered her head and walked on.

Two steps out, she glanced back once.

Baek Si-on had already turned and gone.

✿ ✿ ✿

August 16, the seventh gig, Jeju Island Summer Beer Festival.

Even the fan photographers had followed him to Jeju.

Two became four.

August 17, the eighth gig, Daegu Dongseong-ro Pedestrian Street Festival.

By this one, the "Way Back Home" full-crowd chorus no longer needed Baek Si-on to lead it.

The moment the intro sounded, the crowd began singing on its own, and Baek Si-on could go without even opening his mouth, just holding the mic up and letting the audience sing a full chorus.

He didn't do it.

But he knew he could.

August 18, the ninth gig, Chuncheon Lakeside Music Festival.

August 19, the tenth gig, Andong Mask Dance Festival's affiliated concert.

August 20.

Son Nam-won called.

"Boss Baek, the corporate registration's come through."

Baek Si-on, shoving a triangular seaweed-wrapped gimbap into his mouth in the back seat of the van, chewed twice.

"Name?"

"Insight."

Baek Si-on wired the second tranche of fifty million won to Son Nam-won's company account.

August 21.

The eleventh gig, Wonju Hoetcheon Starlight Festival.

The twelfth gig, Gangneung Dano Culture Experience Festival.

The fan photographers had settled into a steady six or seven.

One of them was retouching at an extremely high quality — a nine-grid set of Baek Si-on's profile had been retweeted over twenty thousand times on Twitter.

August 22.

The thirteenth gig, Pyeongtaek Port Festival.

The fourteenth gig, Gunsan Seafood Festival.

The fifteenth gig, Yeosu Dolsan Bridge Nightscape Concert.

This stage was built on a coastal boardwalk, with the lights of Dolsan Bridge and the night of Namhae as the backdrop.

While Baek Si-on was singing "Happy," a little boy of five or six, sitting on his father's shoulders down below, clapped along to the beat — completely off time, but laughing happier than anyone.

Baek Si-on saw him.

After the last line, he pointed in that direction.

The fan photographers' shutters went down at the same moment.

August 23.

The sixteenth gig, Gimpo Han River New Town Festival.

The seventeenth gig, Uijeongbu Hoeryong Culture Festival.

August 24.

The eighteenth gig, Seongnam Jeongja Park Civic Music Festival.

The nineteenth, the final gig, Goyang Lake Park Starlight Festival.

The Goyang Lake Park outdoor stage was larger than most of the venues he'd been running through, with close to five thousand people gathered below.

The moment "Way Back Home"'s intro sounded.

The five-thousand-person chorus took shape within three seconds.

Baek Si-on stood on the stage, the in-ear monitor in his ear nearly rendered useless by the wave of sound.

He pushed the mic forward.

The sound from below poured in, dense and crowded, every person's key not quite the same, yet mixed together they formed a kind of rough, raw, living harmony.

He remembered that night a few months ago in studio 401 in Hapjeong-dong.

Jung Jae-joon, with a MIDI keyboard and two studio monitors, had stacked this song up track by track.

Back then the song had an audience of three.

Now it was five thousand.

The final note fell.

The applause and cheers burst at the same moment.

Baek Si-on toggled off the mic switch, held it in his hand and listened for two seconds, then bowed deep.
