Chapter Two Hundred Thirty

A Loophole Is Also Part of the Rules

The production shot several more setups after that.

Baek Jeong-hoon adjusted the blocking on one of them twice; every other take passed in one.

Bae Joo-hyun and Son Seung-wan were parked in a spot where they could wait without disturbing the shoot.

Both of their sightlines never left the buzz cut inside the shooting area.

When the final wide finished, Baek Jeong-hoon checked the playback and pulled off his headphones.

“Lunch.”

Order relaxed all at once.

The camera crew powered down the main unit.

Extras cleared the cordon.

Production lunchboxes came in from the equipment truck, box by box.

Some went for water, some stretched their shoulders against pillars, and the hushed working murmur returned to normal volume.

Baek Si-on came out of the shooting area, took the towel his assistant offered, wiped his face, and walked toward the cordon.

Son Seung-wan shot to her feet and bowed at a textbook angle.

“Sunbae, hello!”

Bae Joo-hyun rose too.

“Sunbae.”

Baek Si-on’s attention landed on the insulated bag first, then moved to the bag on Son Seung-wan’s shoulder.

“What brings you two here?”

Bae Joo-hyun lifted what she was carrying, slightly.

“A set visit.”

Baek Si-on took the bag, unzipped it, and looked inside.

Gimbap.

Fruit.

Two bottles of drinks.

“How long have you been here?”

“Just arrived.”

Bae Joo-hyun answered smoothly.

She omitted the guard not knowing Red Velvet, the grape-flavor mix-up, and the small detail of waiting out the entire shoot behind the monitors.

A set visit requires dignity.

The social-death portions may be deleted.

Baek Si-on checked the time.

“Eaten yet?”

Two heads shook simultaneously.

“Can the production lunchboxes be eaten?”

Son Seung-wan answered fast.

“They can.”

Bae Joo-hyun nodded too.

Baek Si-on waved over a set assistant.

“Four lunchboxes, please.”

Son Seung-wan faltered for a beat.

Three people.

Four lunchboxes.

She had assumed the director or Jo Woo-jin-sunbae would be joining.

Only when Baek Si-on led them toward the van did she understand how she had underestimated the other possibility.

❧ ❧ ❧

Same day · noon

Lunch aboard — one table folded down, one name about to be taken in full

Baek Si-on’s custom van · the captain seats

The van door slid open.

Son Seung-wan boarded Baek Si-on’s custom van for the first time in her life.

Airline seats. Independent lighting. A compact refrigerator. A fold-down table.

Her steps slowed by half a beat in the aisle, her gaze sweeping everywhere without permission — and then she turned, instinctively, to Bae Joo-hyun.

Bae Joo-hyun had already settled into an airline seat with perfect composure.

She had even pressed the recline button with an easy, practiced finger.

Son Seung-wan understood.

Unnie had ridden in this van before.

Probably more than once.

The assistant brought the four lunchboxes up.

Baek Si-on unwrapped two and set them before himself, then opened the container Bae Joo-hyun had brought.

The gimbap was cut neatly.

Egg, spinach, ham, and pickled radish in clean layers across the cross-section.

He picked up a piece and sent it straight into his mouth.

Bae Joo-hyun’s chopsticks slowed.

“Well?”

“Edible.”

“Only edible?”

Baek Si-on picked up another piece.

“Then it’s good.”

The supplement barely cleared the bar.

His appetite left her very little time to admire her own labor.

One production lunchbox hit bottom fast.

The second was unwrapped.

The gimbap was also declining at a steady rate.

Son Seung-wan, holding her own box, finally understood that Eun-ah’s “three bowls of rice over kimchi” had not been a figure of speech.

Bae Joo-hyun quietly counted her gimbap.

Eight pieces gone.

She decided to stop counting.

Counting onward would start to look like caring.

❧ ❧ ❧

With the meal mostly gone, Baek Si-on set down his chopsticks.

“Talk.”

Bae Joo-hyun raised her face.

“About what?”

“What you came to ask me for.”

“Is a pure set visit not allowed?”

“Unsolicited kindness is either scheme or theft.”

Bae Joo-hyun’s cheeks puffed, briefly.

Being seen through made her a little defiant.

“I can be considerate too.”

“Then say first who you’re being considerate of.”

Bae Joo-hyun: “......”

Say Baek Si-on, and the purpose looked impure.

Say Son Seung-wan, and the poaching scheme showed.

Say the production, and the quantity of one gimbap container plainly couldn’t carry so grand a target.

Son Seung-wan lowered her head and buried her face in her lunchbox, working hard to lower her existence.

After a struggle, Bae Joo-hyun confessed the purpose.

“Seung-wan’s vocals are excellent.”

“Mm.”

“Kim Eun-sook-writer-nim’s new drama — you are doing the OST, right?”

“Mm.”

“The female part... could Seung-wan sing it?”

Baek Si-on picked up his chopsticks, lifted a piece of fruit, and ate it.

Then he set the chopsticks down, leaned into the seatback, let his gaze pass over Son Seung-wan’s face, and return to Bae Joo-hyun.

‘Descendants of the Sun’ was a long way from broadcast.

The OST production window was roomy.

His original plan: wait until ‘The Three Evils’ finished its current block and ‘The Producer’ neared its end on Lee Ji-eun’s side, then sit with her over the song’s production before flying to America for ‘La La Land’.

Now Bae Joo-hyun had arrived with Son Seung-wan in tow.

Nominally, a set visit.

In practice, a poach.

Son Seung-wan had every right to compete for it. But Bae Joo-hyun could not be allowed to mistake competing for entitled.

“Bae Joo-hyun.”

The full name snapped her spine straight.

“Don’t tell me you think that because you’re pretty, the whole world ought to revolve around you?”

The air in the van dropped several degrees.

Son Seung-wan’s hand was still on the rim of her lunchbox; her expression had visibly frozen.

Bae Joo-hyun had frozen too.

But when she opened her mouth, no usable reason came out.

Lately she had indeed received too much special care from Baek Si-on.

The favors, as itemized

The BodyArmor commercial.

A lunch, just the two of them.

The LAVUE endorsement.

The ‘Descendants of the Sun’ cameo.

The ‘CeCi’ magazine cover.

The Minion harmonies in the studio.

And one set of dance homework with unmistakably retaliatory origins.

Stacked, they had produced a certain illusion: that close was close enough for the world to owe her a yes.

All of it stacked together had bred a certain illusion.

That the two of them were close enough now — close enough that bringing Son Seung-wan to his set entitled her to a chance.

In the dorm, Bae Joo-hyun had run the full calculation: Lee Ji-eun, Choi Jin-ri, her own position, Son Seung-wan’s vocals, the costs.

The one question left out: on what grounds must Baek Si-on accept her arrangement?

Bae Joo-hyun’s eyes went down.

Half the rice was still in her box.

The hand holding her chopsticks loosened; two shallow press-marks stayed behind on her palm.

“I’m sorry.”

Her voice didn’t tremble.

She was still holding the dignity together as best she could.

“I shouldn’t have dragged Seung-wan here out of nowhere and put you in a hard spot.”

Son Seung-wan hurried to apologize after her.

“Sunbae, we’re sorry for the trouble.”

The van went quiet again.

Baek Si-on looked at the two of them.

Then he picked his chopsticks back up and took the last piece of gimbap from the container.

“Chances can be fought for.”

Two heads came up at once.

Baek Si-on tapped the emptied container with his chopsticks.

“But this gimbap doesn’t buy me.”

The corner of Bae Joo-hyun’s mouth moved.

“Should have skipped the ham, then.”

“Why?”

“Saves on cost.”

“...Well. I can’t send you two back empty-handed either.”

Bae Joo-hyun hadn’t caught up.

“What?”

“A contest.”

“Contesting what?”

“First one to blink loses. You win, and Seung-wan gets the audition. Start.”

Bae Joo-hyun’s brain was still parked on “audition” and “the gimbap failed to buy him” — and her eyelids had already snapped shut once on pure reflex.

Baek Si-on announced, calmly:

“You lost.”

“......”

Bae Joo-hyun finally caught up:

“Doesn’t count! You didn’t even do a countdown!”

“Does the enemy count you down before he shoots?”

“This isn’t a gunfight!”

“Same rules.”

“Same how?”

“No blinking.”

The logic was crude, the delivery natural, the shamelessness impressively stable.

Bae Joo-hyun pressed her lips together and glared:

“This is cheating, sunbae!”

Baek Si-on settled back into the seat, apparently in a good mood.

“You get one appeal.”

Bae Joo-hyun swung to the side at once.

“Seung-wan. You be the referee.”

Son Seung-wan pointed at herself.

“Me?”

“Mm.”

On her left: the man who had just handed her a chance. On her right: a leader quite visibly a poor loser.

The referee’s seat was a high-danger position.

Son Seung-wan swallowed the rice in her mouth and accepted the appointment with difficulty.

She raised one hand between their two lines of sight.

Bae Joo-hyun sat tall, hands on her knees.

Baek Si-on stayed lounging against the seatback, posture loose.

“Three.”

“Two.”

“One.”

The hand withdrew.

Two sightlines collided.

At first, Bae Joo-hyun thought only of winning.

Soon, she noticed the bloodshot at the corner of Baek Si-on’s eyes.

Up close, that exhaustion shaved a little of his usual ease.

She wanted to ask whether he hadn’t slept last night.

But a contest was in progress.

No speaking allowed.

Baek Si-on was observing her too.

Bae Joo-hyun held her face stern, brows pressed flat, her expression grave enough for a bet that would decide the group’s next comeback.

Except the eyes were simply too round.

The harder she tried, the more she produced a cuteness entirely against her own will.

Son Seung-wan was still performing referee duties at the start.

Check the left. Check the right.

Soon she realized her position made very little sense.

The two of them were staring each other down across her body.

The air had entirely detoured around her.

Wedged in the exact middle, she had been demoted from referee to a car ornament whose only remaining duty was blocking the air-conditioning vent.

Son Seung-wan shrank quietly into her seatback.

❧ ❧ ❧

The eyes began to sting.

A fine tremor entered Bae Joo-hyun’s lashes.

Baek Si-on held steady.

Yesterday’s lights snapping on, slate cracks without warning, blank rounds exploding on repeat — they had ground his startle reflex smooth.

Today’s blinking contest was, for him, close to a dimensional mismatch.

Keep gritting it out, and Bae Joo-hyun could not win.

She changed tactics.

Her right hand rose beside her face; index and middle finger split into a small V.

Baek Si-on: no reaction.

She swapped the gesture for a chin-propped palm, head tilted a degree.

The focus of Baek Si-on’s gaze shifted — very lightly.

Bae Joo-hyun caught the change.

Her left hand came up too.

Both palms now framing her cheeks, lips curving up, the standard idol service expression deployed at full wattage.

In a fansign line, the gesture was unremarkable.

Inside a van, across a referee who had nearly ceased to exist, its killing power suddenly stopped making sense.

Son Seung-wan’s mouth twitched.

Bae Joo-hyun leaned her face in a little further.

Baek Si-on’s eyelids finally closed.

“Baek-sunbae blinked!”

Son Seung-wan’s hand shot up.

Baek Si-on looked at Bae Joo-hyun’s face — lit up and out of control over one won round — and said:

“Foul.”

“No rule bans expressions.”

“There is now.”

“You can’t change rules mid-match.”

Wedged between them, Son Seung-wan nodded with great seriousness.

“That one’s true.”

Baek Si-on’s gaze moved from Bae Joo-hyun’s face to Son Seung-wan’s.

The fairness on Son Seung-wan’s face vanished instantly; she stared hard at her own knees.

Baek Si-on didn’t press the referee.

That match had, in fact, been Bae Joo-hyun’s win.

The winning method could hardly be called sporting.

She had weaponized a sudden, close-range, palm-framed, excessively cute expression — a flattening strike on the spiritual plane.

But nobody banned psychological attacks before the match. A loophole is also part of the rules.

— the ruling, as accepted: a bet is a bet

“Fine.”

The triumph resurfaced in Bae Joo-hyun’s eyes.

Baek Si-on turned to Son Seung-wan.

“When the song exists, you’ll be the first one called in to audition.”

Son Seung-wan stood frozen until Bae Joo-hyun’s elbow nudged her — then she snapped back to life and rose from the seat at once.

The roof was high enough; her bow met no resistance whatsoever.

“Thank you, sunbae!”

Bae Joo-hyun packed away her small triumph too.

“Thank you.”

Baek Si-on gestured for Son Seung-wan to sit back down.

“It’s an audition, not a coronation. Sing badly and you’re cut like anyone else.”

“I understand! I’ll prepare properly!”

Bae Joo-hyun was demure out loud as well:

“Understood.”

But inside, an enormous breath had already been let out.

At minimum, until the formal audition, that female vocal would not fall straight into Lee Ji-eun’s lap.

Whether Son Seung-wan could take it would be up to her own voice.

Seize the microphone first; everything after is singing.

❧ ❧ ❧

The containers on the fold-down table were empty to the walls.

Two production lunchboxes remained as stacked empty trays; the gimbap had been finished to the last piece.

Baek Si-on wiped his fingers and checked the afternoon’s call sheet.

The action scenes sat right after lunch.

Last night’s training fatigue kept pressing in behind his eyes; a stretch of shut-eye outweighed small talk with two juniors by any measure.

“You two can head back.”

Son Seung-wan began tidying at once.

She snapped her lunchbox shut and wiped the table down with a tissue.

Bae Joo-hyun stayed in her seat.

Halfway through tidying, Son Seung-wan caught a very small signal at the edge of her vision.

Bae Joo-hyun tipped her chin toward the door.

The motion was tiny; the meaning was crystal: you first.

Son Seung-wan’s gaze circled — leader, Baek Si-on, insulated bag — with the empty lunchbox in her arms.

She understood again.

The hush-benefit evidently came with extra duties attached.

Having secured the audition request, she was now responsible for exiting the stage, clearing the props, and manufacturing her leader a private space.

The service scope was remarkably complete.

Son Seung-wan, thoroughly perceptive, stood and stacked the empty lunchboxes, then squared away the insulated bag Bae Joo-hyun had brought.

Baek Si-on glanced at her.

“What are you doing with those?”

“Clearing the trays.”

“The assistant will collect them.”

“It’s fine. I’m up anyway.”

She gathered everything into her arms and collected an approving look from Bae Joo-hyun on the way.

The audition slot had not been given away for nothing.

Son Seung-wan had already learned to expand her job description proactively.

The door opened.

She stood on the step and bowed to Baek Si-on once more.

“Sunbae, I’ll head out first.”

“Mm.”

“Good luck this afternoon!”

The door sealed.

Son Seung-wan stood outside hugging the stack of empty trays, unable to read anything through the dark glass.

She looked down at the load in her arms and sighed.

The title, as changed

Arrived as: main vocal of Red Velvet, first audition in hand.

Departed as: waste-sorting staff, one stack of trays.

She had arrived as the main vocal.

She was leaving as the recycling crew.

In the entertainment industry, a job change can happen in the span of one set visit.

❧ ❧ ❧

Quiet returned to the van.

Baek Si-on pressed the control on the seat’s side.

The moment the backrest tipped, the upstanding posture beside him became conspicuous.

“You’re not going?”

Bae Joo-hyun put her hands on her knees.

“Leaving right after arriving would seem like no sincerity at all.”

“And sitting here waiting for me to wake up is sincere?”

Her prepared reason was dismantled on the spot.

Her lips moved without producing a tighter explanation.

Baek Si-on couldn’t be bothered to keep interrogating.

The exhaustion behind his eyes was past hiding.

“No noise.”

Bae Joo-hyun nodded at once.

“I won’t make a sound.”

And then, unprompted, she muted her phone — vibration killed too — and held it up for his confirmation.

The practiced smoothness of the motion was its own argument.

Baek Si-on pressed the seat control.

The backrest flattened by degrees; the leg rest rose from below.

He closed his eyes and let one arm rest loosely in front of him.

Bae Joo-hyun sat properly at first.

Footsteps of the set crew drifted past outside.

Someone on a walkie confirmed the afternoon stunt route; somewhere far off, equipment wheels ground over the ground.

The door filtered it all into a blurred underlayer.

Bae Joo-hyun scrolled through a few pages on her phone.

Not one line went in.

Sitting too straight made the sleeping man beside her look all the more at ease.

Keep this up and she resembled a family member keeping vigil at a hospital bed. Slightly foolish.

Bae Joo-hyun found the seat control and lowered her backrest.

The amplitude was managed with great care.

The motor’s whine cut into the quiet cabin — she froze, and tipped an ear toward the man beside her.

No reaction.

Only then did she keep pressing, until her body could settle back comfortably.

Their breathing split into two rhythms.

Hers: shallow, restrained. The one beside her: deepening toward even.

Bae Joo-hyun turned her head.

Baek Si-on was genuinely asleep.

The buzz cut made his face sharper than usual.

Nothing left to hide behind — the whole outline of him showed: brow bone, jawline, all of it.

But with his eyes closed, the pressure that normally allowed no one to relax had finally thinned.

Bae Joo-hyun watched his profile, and her chest went soft without warning.

The Baek Si-on she usually saw always had somewhere to be; asleep beside her, defenseless like this, he carried a strangeness that was almost rare.

A thought surfaced from the bottom of her heart.

Bae Joo-hyun raised her phone slowly, flipped to the front camera, and aimed the lens at herself.

The screen held only her.

Her wrist drifted sideways by a little.

Baek Si-on entered the frame.

She adjusted the angle so he sat at the frame’s edge — present in the shot, yet not too deliberate.

The result was still entirely deliberate.

She pressed her lips together: too serious, this face.

One hand rose beside her cheek into a small pair of finger-scissors.

Still not natural enough.

After a moment’s debate, she switched to the gesture that had won the match — palm framing her cheek, eyes widened just slightly.

On screen: a little smug, a little guilty.

Her thumb moved toward the shutter.

The instant before she touched it, the closed eyes at the screen’s edge opened.

Bae Joo-hyun: “!!!”

The phone slipped from her fingers.

Thud.

It fell into the gap between the seat and the armrest.

Baek Si-on turned his face.

Sleep still hung between his brows.

“What are you doing?”

Bae Joo-hyun had gone rigid from head to toe.

The phone was stuck in the gap.

Retrieve it, and she confessed the stealth photo.

Leave it, and the situation only grew more suspicious.

The explanation her brain assembled in panic beat her mouth’s better judgment out the door:

“Checking the front camera.”

Bae Joo-hyun herself knew the excuse would save no one; she ducked her head fast and went fishing into the seat gap before it could be discovered.

Nerves made her fingers skate twice without finding it.

She changed angles; her hair slid down to screen her face — the phone still wouldn’t hook onto her fingertips.

Baek Si-on reached across the armrest.

His fingers went into the gap and drew the phone out.

On the screen: the front camera’s live viewfinder.

Blurred by the shake of her hand — a most honest video record of the stealth photographer’s composure.

Baek Si-on handed it back to her.

“Front camera’s fine.”

Bae Joo-hyun took it.

“The framing, on the other hand, is extremely guilty.”

Heat raced from behind her ear out across her cheeks.

She locked the screen and wished she could uninstall the camera app along with it.

Baek Si-on didn’t pursue it.

He lay back, closed his eyes, voice gone lazy.

“If you want the photo, take it.”

Bae Joo-hyun’s face came up.

“Don’t photograph me ugly.”

The sentence granted permission — and spared her the further humiliation of explaining the stealth motive.

“Also.”

“What?”

“Don’t post it anywhere.”

Bae Joo-hyun protested in a small voice:

“I’m not a child.”

Baek Si-on answered with his eyes closed:

“Very mature, the way your phone fell into the seat gap just now.”

“......”

Bae Joo-hyun bit her lower lip.

She wanted to fire back — but remembering that he hadn’t properly slept last night, she swallowed the retort whole.

She opened the camera again.

This time there was no need to hide.

She sat up a little, raised her arm, and put the two of them into one frame.

In the frame, she flashed the finger-scissors.

Beside her, Baek Si-on’s eyes had closed again, his breath slowing back down — seemingly asleep once more.

Bae Joo-hyun fine-tuned the composition.

Her fingertip dropped toward the shutter.

In the second before she pressed it, Baek Si-on raised his right hand — index and middle finger splitting into a lazy V of his own.

Click.

The shutter sound was muted.

But the frame was locked.

Bae Joo-hyun brought the phone up and studied the photo.

The photo, as locked

Her V: the un-hideable curve of pure delight.

His V: the un-bothered curve of indulgence.

One frame, two versions of the same letter — and both of them keeping it.

Bae Joo-hyun saved the photo.

Then locked the screen.

She pressed the phone to her chest, stared at the ceiling of the van, and didn’t move for a long while.

Outside the window, someone called out something and was shushed by someone else.

The lunch break went on.

Bae Joo-hyun closed her eyes — and the curve at the corner of her mouth never once came down.
