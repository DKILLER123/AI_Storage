Chapter Twenty-Seven

Goo Hara’s Connections

The same afternoon into night 14:00 to 23:00 Hapjeong-dong · 401 Alien language. Five million. A lyricist still missing.

Jung Jae-joon pointed at the booth.

“Go in. Forget lyrics. La-la-la is fine. Alien language is fine. Sing the whole melody, top to bottom, once through. I’ll take it in.”

Baek Si-on walked in, stood at the mic.

The cans went on this time.

Jae-joon outside made a hand. Recording started.

Si-on closed his eyes, from the verse.

● REC3:40 · scratch vocal

La-la, la-la-la, la-la-la-la-la…

By the door, Baek Eun-ah watched her cousin stand in the foam-lined box and sing alien language into a microphone with a straight face.

She bit her lip shut.

Professionalism.

Three minutes forty.

Si-on finished the last note, opened his eyes.

Jae-joon outside clicked a few times. Waveform saved.

He leaned back, stretched.

“This song can be made.”

“Since Hara sent you, I’ll put a real number on the table. Full set — arrangement, recording, mix, plus master — market is two million. For Hara’s face…”

“Five million.”

Jae-joon’s stretch froze in the air.

“Three million deposit first. Condition is you don’t take other work for a while. Grind this one.”

Si-on came out of the booth, leaned on the doorframe.

Jae-joon slowly put his arms down. The look at Si-on was the look at a new species.

Years in the trade. People who beat the price down, he had seen. People who stiffed the bill, he had seen. People who finished, didn’t like it, wanted it torn down and rebuilt with no extra pay, he had seen.

Someone who volunteered a hundred and fifty percent more.

First time.

“…Boss is generous.”

He clicked his tongue.

“Then the credit?”

“Compose mine. Arrange yours.”

Credit · this song

Composer: Baek Si-on

Arranger: Jung Jae-joon

Fist on the table. Learning speed: present.

Jae-joon thought two seconds, then put a fist out.

“Good working with you, Boss Baek.”

This time Si-on didn’t freeze.

Fist met fist, clean.

Learning capacity, at least, he had.

✿ ✿ ✿

When they left the studio, the street had almost no one on it.

The two of them had sat from two in the afternoon to eleven at night.

Delivery once in the middle.

Jae-joon ate jajangmyeon. Si-on ate two gimbap.

Eun-ah held an Americano on the folding chair, slept and woke, woke and slept. Half a page in Notes, a third of it garbage her fingers had smeared while dozing.

Nine hours. The frame of the song roughly stood.

Melody set. Chord walk set. Section structure set. Arrangement direction aligned.

The rest Jae-joon could push on his own.

One thing he could not do.

Lyrics.

“The words you have to find yourself.”

Jae-joon at the door.

“I do sound. I don’t do text.”

Si-on nodded once.

In the taxi, Eun-ah against the window started to droop again.

Si-on leaned into the seat-back, took out his phone.

The screen lit.

KakaoTalk.

A few unread.

A joke image from Han-teuk. Someone in uncle’s work group had said something.

He scrolled down.

Choi Jin-ri’s thread.

The last message still sitting from a few days ago, the one she had sent: “Buy me one too.”

Nothing after.

His thumb hung over the box.

Two seconds.

Then lock screen.

Phone on his thigh. He turned, looked once at Eun-ah beside him, then out the window.

Ginkgo on both sides of Yanghwa-ro going backward. Streetlight on the glass, one lamp, then another, like an ellipsis played in reverse.

He thought of someone.

Picked the phone up, opened contacts, found the name saved only yesterday afternoon.

Dialled.

Two rings. Picked up.

Baek Si-on → Goo Hara · taxi · Yanghwa-ro

“Sunbae?”

A little surprise in Goo Hara’s voice. Not much. The same person calling twice in two days was not that rare in her social life.

“Sorry to bother you. First — the Jung Jae-joon you sent is solid. We already closed. Thank you.”

“Really? That’s great.”

The gladness in it was real.

“One more thing I wanted to trouble you with.”

“Please.”

“This song still needs lyrics. I want a lyricist. Anyone you know?”

Two seconds of air.

“Sunbae. You’re really using one person for two jobs.”

“Who told you your book is thick?”

“I’d love to help, but… you’ve seen the group stuff lately. The company is in meetings every day…”

“Give me a minute though. Let me think.”

“All right.”

The line stayed open. Ten-odd seconds.

“Sunbae, hang up for now. Give me twenty minutes. I’ll ask someone. If it works, I’ll send you the number straight.”

“Fine. Sorry for the trouble.”

“No trouble.”

Hung up.

Si-on put the phone on his leg, leaned back, closed his eyes.

The same night After the call Cheongdam-dong · fourteenth floor A schedule. A contract copy. A box of chocolate. One eonni.

The other side of Seoul.

Cheongdam-dong, an apartment, fourteenth floor.

Goo Hara sat cross-legged on the living-room carpet. The tea table in front of her was a mess.

A schedule. Copies of contracts. A to-do list from the manager. A box of chocolate opened and barely eaten.

Goo Hara

Singer · Dancer · KARA

KARA’s visual, kind even under fire

Born 1991 in Gwangju. KARA’s visual — the one who, while her own group stood blockaded by fans, still had the presence of mind to hand a near-stranger a name and a number, and the stubborn faith that the road back was a road at all.

She flipped the contacts, stopped on a name.

Thought.

Hit dial.

Three rings.

Goo Hara → Lee Ji-eun · night · Cheongdam

“Eonni?”

Soft. A little dull, as if just pulled out of work.

“Ji-eun-ah. Did I catch you at a bad time?”

“No. I was resting anyway. What’s up?”

“A favour. I have a sunbae just out of the army, getting ready to come back with music. Melody’s already written. Just needs lyrics.”

“What kind?”

“Like… the flower-boy kind? In the group he was the cute one, heart-fingers, aegyo, that road.”

She paused, decided this would sell better:

“You’d like him.”

Two seconds of air.

“Eonni.”

“Mm?”

“I asked about the genre!”

“…”

Hara nearly choked on the chocolate. From Lee Ji-eun’s side, a very light laugh.

“Is there a demo?”

“There should be.”

“Send it. If it’s good I’ll write. If it isn’t I won’t. Fair?”

“Fair, fair.”

“Then I’ll give him your number, have him send the demo straight?”

“The work number is fine.”

“Got it. Thanks, Ji-eun.”

“Don’t thank me. I haven’t said I’ll write yet… Right — eonni. What’s that sunbae’s name?”

Hara’s mouth opened. Fingers already on the contacts. Then her hand stopped. Say it or not. She thought. No need. IU listens to a demo for the melody, not the person. An unknown group name up front might even kill the interest. Let the melody talk.

“You’ll know when he contacts you.”

“…Fine.”

Hung up.

Hara put the phone down, pushed another piece of chocolate into her mouth.

Chewed twice.

“Flower-boy puppy. She should like that.”

She nodded to herself, quite pleased with her sales pitch.
