Chapter Sixty-Three

The Buzz Cut Shocks the Commute

August 1 Morning Yeonnam-dong → Yeouido, KBS A driver, a rookie manager, and a walk past the cameras

Baek Si-on woke and washed up.

He walked to the kitchen ice maker, scraped out a full glass of ice, and mixed himself an espresso.

Downing that on an empty stomach was murder on his gut, but it worked fast on the water retention.

No breakfast.

Today was a high-definition camera day. The moment sodium went up, the water in his face would run under the skin — by afternoon, double eyelids would turn to inner folds, and the jawline would smudge into a fuzzy arc.

Iced Americano. A diuretic.

He leaned against the kitchen counter and drank it slowly.

Outside the window the sky was already fully bright. Early August in Seoul — the sun came up around five-thirty, and the heat had been piling up since dawn.

Seven-thirty.

The alarm in the guest room went off.

The sound travelled through two doors into the living room, then got swallowed by a groan.

The turn of a body.

Blanket scuffing the wall.

Then the slap of bare feet on the floor — patter, patter — from the guest room all the way to the bathroom, catching the doorframe halfway with a low, stifled "ah."

Washing took ten minutes.

For Baek Eun-ah, that was already fast.

Then makeup.

The day before, Park Ji-hun had taught her — hand by hand — a "ten-minute quick everyday look," stressing again and again that as a manager the face didn't need to be eye-catching, clean and sharp was enough.

She had followed that ten-minute lesson to the letter.

Then, on the strength of the look that had stunned her the night before, she'd tacked on a full ten minutes of "personal initiative."

By the time she came out of the bathroom, Baek Si-on was already lacing his shoes at the door.

His gaze rested for exactly two seconds on the bright glitter at her eye corner.

He offered no comment.

✿ ✿ ✿

The morning light had already flooded the mouth of the Yeonnam-dong alley.

The black Carnival was parked at the curb.

Standing beside the car was one person.

Park Ji-hun — carrying the same huge black makeup case as the day before, one hand holding a garment bag in a dust cover, standing at the passenger door.

He'd been drafted overnight by a single text from Baek Si-on.

The reason was perfectly sound.

The moment Baek Si-on closed his eyes, he could picture Baek Eun-ah reversing into the spot the day before — seventeen back-and-forths before she barely wedged it in.

Today the destination was Yeouido. Rush-hour lane changes on the Seoul expressway could push a novice driver past the point of tears.

But he didn't feel like driving either.

The back seat could recline. Why sit up front?

And so Park Ji-hun had been handed a second role — driver.

Anyway, the makeup would be done in KBS's waiting room. If he showed up early he'd just be sitting around waiting.

Might as well drive there on the way and go over today's styling plan with Baek Eun-ah en route.

Use everything to its fullest.

Exploited cleanly.

Seeing the two of them walk over, Park Ji-hun washed down the last of his coffee and tossed the cup into the bin.

“Morning, Boss Baek. Morning, Manager Baek.”

Baek Eun-ah, radiant under that twenty-minute face, waved cheerfully.

“Morning~”

She climbed into the passenger seat without a trace of guilt.

“Morning.”

Baek Si-on walked around to the side, pulled open the sliding rear door, ducked in, and folded the Carnival's rear seat down to a comfortable angle.

Park Ji-hun settled into the driver's seat, buckled up, and started the engine.

“Let's go.”

Baek Si-on said it from where he was leaning back in the seat.

The black Carnival eased out of Yeonnam-dong and into the thick of the Seoul morning rush.

Destination: Yeouido, KBS.

✿ ✿ ✿

The car arrived.

The stretch of "commute" outside KBS's side gate had been lined with iron barriers half the morning.

This was a fixture of the music-show tradition.

Journalists from every outlet, fansite photographers shouldering long-lens "cannons," and the curious passersby had packed the hundred-odd metres to bursting.

The black Carnival rolled to a stop.

“Boss Baek, we're here.”

Park Ji-hun dropped the gear, killed the engine, pulled the handbrake — one fluid sequence.

The passenger door swung open.

Today Baek Eun-ah had put on a pair of five-centimetre heeled shoes, purely to look the part.

Her leg came out. The heel landed. She steadied.

Behind the barriers, the several outlets that had been lying in wait instinctively lifted their cameras.

“Shutter, shutter, shutter—”

The flashes went off in a burst.

“Who's that?”

“Never seen her before… pretty enough. A trainee debuting from some small agency?”

“Who cares — shoot first! What if she blows up later?”

“Good point.”

“Shutter, shutter, shutter—”

Baek Eun-ah lifted one hand to shield her face against the flash automatically.

But once her hand was down and she caught the murmurs — "pretty," "newcomer" — the heart she'd carried for four years as a trainee gave a sudden jump.

This was the first time she'd felt what it was like to be surrounded by long lenses.

For that one moment her vanity swelled to its absolute peak.

Just as she lowered her hand and started to arrange a smile that was natural without losing its warmth —

“Eun-ah, open the boot.”

A low, flat voice drifted out of the car.

The mouth that had been rising into a fifteen-degree smile froze mid-air.

“…Oh!”

Reflex beat vanity.

She abandoned the face management entirely and, in those five-centimetre heels, went "clack-clack-clack" around to the back of the car.

The "pure newcomer" image she'd held up for barely ten seconds collapsed on the spot into a grunt worker hauling gear.

And at that moment, the rear sliding door swung open.

A leg in slim black trousers stepped out.

Baek Si-on bent out and stood.

No wasted motion.

The black slim-fit turtleneck hugged his torso; the deep-grey draped blazer hung open; the minimal cut dragged his broad shoulder line into full view.

No eyeliner. No lip tint. Not even a layer of base.

Just that hard-grain, close-cropped buzz cut, sweeping a look around the yard.

The noisy outer scene stalled for a very brief second.

The fansite lenses came down a fraction.

Because he didn't line up at all.

“Who is this?”

“No idea… He's not one of today's idols, is he?”

“Good-looking. But he's got a bit of a hard look to him…”

The fansites didn't know him. The reporters did.

In the crowd, a journalist wearing an entertainment-section badge for one of the sites was down-scrolling his shots of the Girl's Day commute. Out of the corner of his eye he caught the figure on the path, and his head snapped up.

He didn't even have time to take in the whole body.

Just the signature buzz cut and that cold, hard-edged look — utterly out of place among the rainbow of idols around it.

“Baek Si-on?!”

That one shout, cracked and wild, sent every fellow journalist's DNA into motion.

The man who days ago had been the talk of the entire internet, who'd held the top of the hot search for a whole week, who'd single-handedly forced Melon to rewrite its charting — that man, with no filter at all, now stood in front of real lenses.

“Shutter-shutter-shutter-shutter—”

The shutter noise knit into a single thick drumbeat of rain, and the flash frequency chased the daylight out of the whole little block.
