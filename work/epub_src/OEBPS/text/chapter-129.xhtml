Chapter One Hundred Twenty Nine

The First After Half a Century

Late October · the next day 9:40 a.m. Los Angeles · SB Projects · the conference room Five specialists, three looks at the watch, and a webpage that refuses to refresh fast enough.

Nine-forty the next morning. Baek Si-on and Baek Eun-ah were already in their seats.

Two coffees on the table.

One iced americano.

And one caramel macchiato, half drunk, straw bitten completely flat because its owner was that nervous.

Scooter Braun stood at the front of the room.

He looked calmer than she did.

He had also checked his watch three times in the past five minutes.

The calm was strictly cosmetic.

A little further off, the staff who made up the “Baek Si-on core project team” were all failing to sit still.

Marketing. PR. Radio promotion. Digital distribution. Social media.

And one assistant who had only been passing through, and had been physically detained to wait for the result.

“Has it refreshed?” Baek Eun-ah whispered.

“Not yet,” said the staff member beside her.

Ten seconds later, she asked again.

“Has it refreshed?”

The man looked at her.

“Sweetheart, I’m looking at the same screen.”

“……”

Scooter Braun turned to Baek Si-on, who was quietly drinking his coffee.

“Are you nervous?”

“Somewhat.”

“You look like you’re not nervous at all.”

“Being nervous won’t turn it into a number one by itself.”

“……”

That was technically true.

But human beings did not operate on that logic.

Baek Eun-ah shot her cousin a look from the side.

She genuinely wanted to know whether her cousin’s nervous system was factory-original, or whether it had been rebuilt by some Italian deity on the way back from Venice.

Finally.

9:59.

Every sound in the room dropped.

10:00.

The Billboard website updated.

The screen stuttered for a moment — then the page drew itself out.

Baek Eun-ah stopped breathing.

Scooter Braun leaned over the monitor.

Baek Si-on raised his eyes too.

Hot 100.

“Tenth.”

“Ninth.”

“Eighth.”

“Fourth.”

Baek Si-on — Legend had been third. The page was rolling upward, past the middle of the list, past names that no one in the room had time to read.

Billboard Hot 100 · the refresh

All About That Bass

Meghan Trainor

4

The woman who had been sitting on number one dropped to fourth — in the same week a plagiarism controversy loosened her grip on the summit.

Meghan Trainor had fallen to four?

Then what was above her?

The page kept climbing.

Billboard Hot 100 · the refresh

Bang Bang

Jessie J · Ariana Grande · Nicki Minaj

3

Billboard Hot 100 · the refresh

Shake It Off

Taylor Swift

2

Number two — Taylor Swift.

Which meant…

Billboard Hot 100 · the refresh

Legend

Baek Si-on

1

Number one.

The conference room went quiet.

Scooter Braun grabbed the mouse and refreshed.

The page loaded.

Still number one.

He refreshed again.

Still number one.

A third refresh.

Number one.

Scooter Braun slowly straightened up. He stared at the screen, then at Baek Si-on.

Then — as if confirming that this was neither a cached page nor a hallucination produced by several consecutive nights of no sleep — he slammed his hand down on the desk.

“Number one!”

The room detonated.

The PR lead grabbed his own head with both hands and said “Oh my God” three times in a row.

The social-media girl was shrieking and already opening Twitter to post.

People hugged each other.

Someone threw a sheaf of papers into the air.

Baek Eun-ah’s eyes went red in the very second the shouting started.

She looked down and pressed hard on her eyes.

No.

Do not cry.

Screenshot first.

She took the screenshot immediately.

Sent it to Yoon Hye-ja.

To Baek Jeong-hun.

To Jung Jae-joon.

To Choi Jin-ri.

Then her finger hesitated.

And she sent it to Jeong Han-teuk too.

Only after sending did she stop and think.

Why did I send it to Han-teuk oppa?

Whatever.

Han-teuk oppa was basically someone we knew by now, anyway.

Baek Eun-ah’s phone · 10:01 a.m. · screenshot dispatched

Yoon Hye-ja ✓ · Baek Jeong-hun ✓ · Jung Jae-joon ✓ · Choi Jin-ri ✓

… Jeong Han-teuk ✓

Delivered. Read receipts coming in one after another, followed by the sound of a household losing its mind.

“Baek!”

On the other side of the room, Scooter Braun was grinning like a bank robber who had just emptied the Federal Reserve.

He came out from behind the desk, arms wide, walking toward Baek Si-on for the hug of the century.

To be fair — Scooter Braun had already prepared himself.

He had prepared for the possibility that this Korean man, who never returned emotional deposits, would ice him out once again.

He had prepared for his own open arms to hang awkwardly in mid-air for two seconds before he was forced to lower them.

But this time, Scooter Braun guessed wrong.

Baek Si-on stood up. He didn’t dodge. He didn’t go through the motions. He walked straight into the hug.

Scooter Braun froze for a second.

Then, slowly, his arms closed around Baek Si-on’s back and he patted him twice.

In that moment, Scooter Braun understood.

Baek Si-on was not a machine without emotions.

He got worked up. He got his blood up.

It was simply a matter of scale — of whether something was worth getting worked up over.

And this? This was worth it.

“You did it.”

Scooter Braun’s voice came out a little muffled.

Because Baek Si-on was hugging him harder than expected.

“Yeah. I did it.”

If the Venice Best Actor had made Baek Si-on a name the Korean film world could not talk around, then tonight’s number one had just lifted him to a position the Korean pop world had never seen.

Korea’s first in popular music.

The phrase was so big that, dropped into any press-release headline, it would read like a fan gone mad.

But the chart was on the screen.

Nobody could delete it.

Nobody could call it Korean media jerking itself off.

Nobody could claim it was a hallucination manufactured by a domestic streaming chart.

And for an Asian artist — a number one on the Billboard Hot 100 weighed more than a Best Actor at any of the three great film festivals.

That wasn’t prejudice.

It was probability.

An Asian actor — if he picked good art films, found a strong director, and caught enough luck — had a real chance at an international festival Best Actor. Cannes had seen it. Venice had seen it. Berlin had seen it. The image of an Asian face accepting that trophy was not strange to the world’s screens.

But an Asian singer?

One who did it without a Western feature act carrying him, without a viral dance gimmick, who fought his way alone through the North American mainstream and took a Billboard singles crown — only one person in history had done it before.

Kyu Sakamoto.

1963.

“Ue o Muite Arukō” — Sukiyaki, as America renamed it, because that was easier to say than the real title.

Billboard Hot 100 · June 1963

Ue o Muite Arukō (Sukiyaki)

Kyu Sakamoto

1

Three weeks on top. The first Asian recording artist — and, for fifty-one years, the only one — to hold the American chart’s summit. A Japanese song, sung in Japanese, that nobody had to understand to feel.

That was fifty-one years ago.

Baek Si-on was the second.

Half a century in between.

“Okay, okay…”

Scooter Braun’s voice was going thin.

Not from emotion.

From actual, physical oxygen debt.

“Let go, Baek. I still have to stay alive to make you money.”

Baek Si-on released him.

Scooter Braun stepped back and smoothed down the jacket the hug had crushed.

And only then noticed that Baek Eun-ah was staring at her cousin as if she had witnessed some kind of natural wonder.

“Oppa.”

“Mm?”

“You smiled.”

“I’m not allowed to smile?”

“You are!”

Baek Eun-ah began nodding like a chicken pecking rice.

“So allowed! Oppa, your smile is really handsome. I strongly recommend you do it more often.”

Even she felt like a fan saying that.

But there was nothing to be done. Moments like this were too rare.

Baek Si-on ignored the recommendation.

He turned and faced the five members of the core project team who had been working for him.

Digital distribution. Radio promotion. PR. Marketing. Social media.

Since Legend entered its North American cycle, these people had been strapped to the same boat as Scooter Braun.

Worlds. The music video. The athlete challenge. The NBA. The reputation crisis. The second radio push.

Every window had needed a pair of eyes on it.

Every detonation had needed somebody ready to catch it.

Baek Si-on stood still.

Then he bowed to them.

Ninety degrees.

A textbook Korean bow.

“Thank you all for your work.”

The five American staff clearly had not expected that.

They had watched artists scream. Watched artists cry. Watched artists hug their teams.

But a Korean man who had just taken Billboard number one, standing up to bow to the staff and thank them for their work?

The picture was unfamiliar.

And a little bit solemn.

Baek Si-on straightened.

“In a little while, go see my cousin and collect.”

“……”

“A ten-thousand-dollar bonus each.”

He paused.

“Thank you for your hard work.”

Two seconds of silence.

Then —

“Oh my God!!!”

“Are you serious?!”

“Thank you, Baek!!!”

The cheer that had gone up for number one went up again.

Louder, this time. Truer, this time.

After all — Billboard number one belonged to a dream.

Ten thousand dollars belonged to a bank account.

Both mattered. But only one of them made a working person’s soul leave the building on the spot.

Baek Si-on turned to Scooter Braun.

“You’re matching it.”

Scooter Braun pointed at himself.

“Me?”

“They’re your employees. Not mine.”

Scooter Braun looked at him.

And after a few seconds, understood.

Baek Si-on’s bonus was gratitude.

His bonus was what a boss was supposed to do.

Scooter Braun laughed out loud and shook his head.

“Baek. You really are a ‘kind’ person.”

He was only teasing. Today his mood was so good that fifty thousand dollars was genuinely beneath his notice.

He made a grand gesture toward the staff, who were all staring at him with big eyes.

“You heard him! Go to the finance office and collect your ten-thousand-dollar bonus!”

The cheering got louder.

Twenty thousand. Ten each from both of them.

This time somebody literally launched themselves at Scooter Braun.

He stumbled back half a step under the impact.

After a good minute of chaos, Scooter Braun fought his way out of the embrace, brushed himself off, and clapped his hands.

“Celebration dinner tonight! What does everyone want to eat?”

“Wait.”

Baek Si-on raised a hand and cut him off.

“No celebration.”

Scooter Braun blinked.

“Why not?”

“Think about how Justin and Ariana would feel.”

The smile on Scooter Braun’s face dimmed, a degree at a time.

Baek Eun-ah looked up at her cousin too.

She hadn’t expected him to say that.

But the more she thought about it, the more it was exactly him.

SB Projects did not have only one artist.

Justin Bieber was the core of Scooter Braun’s whole portfolio. Ariana Grande was one of the label’s most important female pop voices.

And neither of them had a number-one single yet.

So here was Baek Si-on — an Asian artist barely inside the North American system — the first person in the company to take the Hot 100 crown. If SB Projects threw a loud, company-wide celebration, it would sting the other artists. Badly.

Especially Justin Bieber.

The one who was sensitive, proud, starved for affection, and permanently afraid that Scooter Braun was about to move his attention elsewhere.

Scooter Braun got there on his own a moment later.

Then he looked at Baek Si-on, a little conflicted.

“Then… not holding anything — won’t that feel like we’re doing you wrong?”

“What?”

“This is your number one. A historic number one. You should be celebrated.”

“You can reimburse my fifty grand.”

“……”

The tender moment was yanked back down to earth by financial reality in a single sentence.

Scooter Braun pointed at Baek Si-on and stood there for a full three seconds before he finally broke, shook his head, and laughed.

“You son of a—”

He didn’t finish.

He walked over and slung an arm around Baek Si-on’s shoulders.

“Come on.”

“Where?”

Baek Si-on let himself be steered toward the door.

“I’m guessing Under Armour’s CEO, Kevin Plank, has chartered a helicopter by now and is tearing out of the Baltimore headquarters toward Los Angeles at full throttle.”

“You sure?”

“I’m not sure about the helicopter. I am sure he’s in a hurry.”
