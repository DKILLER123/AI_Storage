Chapter Forty-Nine

Your Idol Is Worth Chasing

July 24 Day Jellyfish Entertainment, a practice room The end of a day's training, and a push

At Jellyfish Entertainment.

Kim Se-jeong screwed the lid onto her water bottle and tucked it into the side pouch of her backpack, then picked a towel off the floor and draped it around her neck. In the practice room, the other trainees were still going. The girls in front of the mirror chased the metronome, breaking down each move a beat at a time, sneakers striking the boards in a steady, even rhythm.

Her training for the day was done.

Two hours of vocals. Three hours of dance. A rice ball eaten somewhere in the middle.

She swapped out of her practice shoes, crouched in the corner to put on her sneakers, and pulled the wired earphones out of her bag, plugging them into her phone.

She opened Melon. First song was always IU.

Her thumb was just about to press play.

A push notification jumped across the top of the screen.

Dispatch · exclusive · video

The nation's little sister IU — a hidden relationship, exposed for the first time

Se-jeong's thumb froze above the play button.

IU.

A hidden relationship.

Exposed for the first time.

The world collapsed in that one second.

IU was dating? Her IU — the only light in the darkest days of the half-basement, the person she'd written a letter to, the person she'd cried over, the person whose posters covered every inch of her wall!

With who?

WHO?!

Her hands began to shake. She leaned against the corridor wall and slid slowly down, all the way to the floor.

A trainee passed and glanced at her.

“Se-jeong? You okay? Low blood sugar?”

She didn't hear. In her ears was only heartbeat and a roaring static in her head. Her thumb hovered over the push, shaking, about five seconds before she tapped it.

The page took two seconds to load. She'd braced herself for the cruelest picture: the back seat of a car in a parking lot at night. A corner booth in a restaurant. A high-res stalker shot of some man hugging IU.

The page finished loading.

The image was a blurry screenshot from a video. Very dark. A man's figure seen from behind, standing somewhere, poor lighting, and opposite him a silhouette you couldn't make out. At first glance it did look like a night-time stalk.

Se-jeong bit her lower lip and scrolled down. The body.

Dispatch · exclusive

Seoul · 24 Jul 2014 · music / investigation

A Hidden Relationship, Exposed for the First Time

The truth behind the "violent debt collector" and the song everyone was cancelling

Recently, singer Baek Si-on became the subject of a music-source dispute over his digital single Way Back Home. At the same time, an anonymous party claimed that, after his discharge from the military, he had worked "violent debt collection" at a private collection firm. After an independent investigation, Dispatch has obtained video material that was never before made public, restoring the full picture of what happened.

One. Baek Si-on did, in fact, briefly work at a private debt-collection agency. However, his actual profession is actor. His reason for joining the collection firm was method study for an upcoming independent film — a film that has today been selected for the main competition of the 71st Venice International Film Festival.

Two. As to the reported "intimidation and threatening behaviour," exactly the opposite is true. In the video obtained exclusively by Dispatch, in one collection case Baek Si-on discovered that the actual borrower had long since vanished, and the collection firm had shifted the repayment pressure onto the borrower's ex-wife and underage daughter. Baek Si-on then returned to the collection firm and, in front of the person in charge, destroyed the original IOU contract on the spot. He additionally took one million won out of his own pocket as compensation for the pair.

Three. Further investigation revealed that the aforementioned debt had, in fact, been repaid in full, at the same time, by a caring individual. Based on the parties' account, that anonymous sum came from IU (Lee Ji-eun). IU has neither publicly confirmed nor denied this, and LOEN Entertainment had made no comment at the time of publication.

Four. The full video follows (identities of unrelated parties masked).

— Dispatch editorial office

Se-jeong's brow furrowed. Collection firm? Baek Si-on? What did one have to do with the other? What about IU's hidden relationship? Did they get the shot? Where was the man? Her heart pounding, her thumb flew down the page.

Se-jeong's finger stopped moving. The three phrases were like three keys sliding, at once, into the same lock. The missing borrower. The ex-wife and underage daughter. The emergency contact. Her breath went shallow. The borrower who'd taken the money and run off — that was her father. The ex-wife — her mother. And the underage daughter — her.

Dispatch editorial office. ……

……

Se-jeong's phone slid out of her hands and clattered onto the practice-room floor. She didn't pick it up. She wrapped both arms around her knees.

That letter. That letter she'd written, one word at a time, hunched over the little table in the half-basement.

IU hadn't just seen it. She'd repaid the whole debt. Without announcing it, without a statement, without letting anyone know.

A memory surfaced in Se-jeong's mind.

The door of the barbecue place. The neon of the screen-protector shop reflecting the half-shadowed face of a man with a crew cut in a flowered shirt.

“Your idol is worth chasing.”

Back then she'd taken it as: keep being a fan, there's nothing wrong with it.

Now she understood. Every word of it.

Your idol. IU. Is worth chasing. Because she'd done something, in a place you never saw, that you never knew.

Se-jeong pressed her forehead against her knees. Her shoulders began to shake. No sound came out.

The fluorescent light of the practice room fell cold and white across the soaked back of her T-shirt, the sweat and the silent crying rising together. Another trainee passed and crouched down, touching her shoulder.

“Se-jeong? What's wrong? Are you not feeling well?”

Se-jeong shook her head.

Couldn't speak.

She reached for the phone that had fallen on the floor. Took two tries to grab it. Flipped it over. A fine crack had run across the screen.

She closed the Dispatch report. Reopened the music app. The first song on the playlist.

July 24 Evening The studio, after the interview The walk downstairs, and a request

IU's voice came through the earphones. She'd heard it countless times, but today that voice carried something more in it. Se-jeong couldn't say exactly what. It was only that every word IU sang felt heavier.

……

Yeonnam-dong, Baek Jeong-hun's studio.

The motion-sensor light in the stairwell lit up. Lim Geun-ho walked ahead, Baek Si-on behind. The Dispatch reporter and photographer had gone down first, waiting at the entrance.

The interview had gone an hour and a half. Baek Jeong-hun ranged from the origin of the screenplay to the casting logic, from the shooting to the rough-cut thinking, and at the end, asked how he felt about the Venice selection. He thought about it a long time, and then said, very simply, "I haven't had time to think about it yet. The film isn't finished."

Downstairs, Lim Geun-ho looked back at the building.

“I never expected Director Baek to be this young.”

“Lucky.”

Si-on's reply was short. Not modesty. Fact. Baek Jeong-hun certainly had talent — that was beyond question. But talent was only the entry ticket in this industry, not the pass. While other indie directors in their twenties were still hauling tripods on other people's sets, Baek Jeong-hun was already taking his own short to Busan. The starting line was different. It didn't shame a person to admit that.

“That's too modest.”

Lim Geun-ho laughed and waved a hand.

“What the family gave you is only the conditions. The thing itself, you shot it.”

Si-on nodded but didn't pursue the thread.

“Director Lim, one more favour, if you would.”

“Go on.”

“Do you have the number of the editor-in-chief at OSEN?”

Lim Geun-ho looked at him. Didn't ask why. Or rather, didn't need to. OSEN was the outlet that had run the debt-collection story first. The words "an anonymous insider" sat in the second paragraph of that piece. Whoever had supplied the lead, whoever had let the piece go, whoever was pushing from behind — that chain hadn't been pulled out yet.

Dispatch had solved the clearing of his name.

But the source was still out there.

Lim Geun-ho pulled his phone out of his pocket, poked around, and sent a number over KakaoTalk.

“There you go.”

“Thank you.”

“You're welcome.”

Lim Geun-ho put his phone away, waved at the team behind him, and started for the car. Two steps on, he turned back.

“Actor Baek.”

“Mm?”

“That thing you mentioned last time — about Girls' Generation.”

Si-on looked at him.

“We've already started looking into it.”

The corner of Lim Geun-ho's mouth curled, very faintly.

“If it checks out, a coffee's on me then.”
