Chapter Thirty-Five

A Convenience Store Door and a Royce Chocolate Bribe

July 2 Afternoon Hapjeong-dong, Jae-joon's studio The master, and the cover
July 2 Afternoon Jae-joon's studio, Hapjeong The finished track, and the speakers go still

Si-on and Eun-ah were sitting on the couch in Jae-joon's studio, the monitors in front of them playing the finished track. The last line dropped. The speakers went still.

Jae-joon turned the volume down a touch and swung the chair around.

“How is it?”

“Good.”

Jae-joon nodded and didn't press it.

“Then, the cover.”

He picked a pen off the desk and spun it between his fingers.

“A digital album needs artwork. It's the first thing a person sees when the song goes up on Melon.”

Eun-ah sat up on the couch, eyes lighting up.

“Take a photo of my cousin! It'd be a crime not to put a face like that on it.”

Jae-joon shook his head.

“That's idol logic.”

“What does that mean?”

“What do fans buy an album for? The face. The photocard. The handshake ticket from the signing. The music's a bonus, not the selling point. So yes, putting a face on the cover is fine — the fans are there for the face.”

“But what's the situation here?”

That question didn't need an answer. Si-on had no fans.

“Then what do we do?”

Jae-joon pulled a sheet of paper out of a drawer and drew a few lines on it. A convenience store's automatic door, blurry neon reflected in the glass, three words in the bottom-right corner: Way Back Home.

“Shoot it at two or three in the morning. The store's lights are on, the street's dark, the door glass throws back the light from across the road. The image lines up with the first line of the lyric — “the sensor door at two a.m.” A listener sees the cover, hasn't even clicked into it yet, and the picture's already in their head.”

Si-on looked at the sketch for two seconds.

“Good.”

“Phone's fine. A little blur only makes it read as texture.”

Jae-joon slid the paper over to Eun-ah.

“You shoot it.”

She took the paper and looked at it. She still believed her cousin's face was ten thousand times better than a convenience store door, but she also knew she couldn't argue with the logic Jae-joon had just laid out.

July 2:30 a.m. Hongdae, a 24-hour convenience store One door, one title, one light

The next morning, two-thirty a.m.

A 24-hour convenience store near the Hongdae entrance. Eun-ah stood across the street, phone held up, and rattled off a dozen shots of the lit glass door. She picked the one she liked best and sent it off to Jae-joon.

By noon the next day, the graded, typeset artwork came back. The image ran very dark. The store's light pushed through the dark the way a single eye stays awake in a sleeping room. In the bottom-right corner, Way Back Home sat in a thin, unassuming sans-serif, small enough not to intrude.

Si-on was satisfied.

“This one.”

Digital single · cover · Melon

Art A convenience store at 2 a.m., lit against a dark street — neon bleeding through the glass.

Type “Way Back Home” · thin sans-serif · bottom-right

Keeper Minimal, cold, one waking eye in the night.

No face to sell. Just a door, and a title, and the light that stays on.

✿ ✿ ✿

July Morning LOEN, underground parking A USB stick, and a mouse-over-play

The next morning, in the underground parking garage of the LOEN building.

Lee Ji-eun pulled open the rear door of the van, tossed her bag onto the seat, and folded herself into the backrest. She pulled out her phone, lit the screen, and started scrolling. The AC hummed at just the right temperature; the seat was at the angle she liked. She was waiting for Han Teuk to get in and drive her to the first schedule of the day.

She waited.

The driver's seat stayed empty.

She looked up and glanced out the window. Han Teuk was nowhere to be seen. She opened her mouth to call for him — and there he came, jogging back from somewhere, pulling open the driver's door and climbing in, a paper bag in his hand.

“Oppa, where'd you go?”

“Ah — a delivery. The front desk asked me to sign for it.”

Han Teuk set the paper bag on the passenger seat, then pulled a USB stick from his pocket and plugged it into the centre console.

IU didn't pay it any attention, head down over her phone.

Han Teuk tapped the console screen, called up the music player, and selected the file he'd just plugged in. His finger hovered over the play button. He glanced up at the rearview mirror. IU was curled in the seat, thumb sliding across the screen, face lit by the glow, wearing that bored “waiting for the car to start” expression.

He pressed play.

The van rolled out of the parking bay and toward the exit.

Out of the speakers came the synth pad laying down its floor first, then the drum machine, then the bass at the very bottom, the intro running eight bars before the voice entered.

“The sensor door at two a.m.——”

In the rearview, IU's thumb came to a stop. Her eyes lifted from the screen and drifted toward the console. The sliced Ding-dong popped out of the arrangement, lodged inside the synth texture like a bubble rising to the surface and bursting.

“Spitting out a receipt printed with snacks——”

She laid the phone down on her lap, screen facing the seat.

“The street lamp drags my shadow long — long as a joke.”

She sank deeper into the seat and closed her eyes.

Not out of sleepiness.

Out of wanting to hear it more clearly. With the eyes shut, the visual channel cut off, the ears became the only entrance; every note pressed in closer, close enough that it was almost as if someone were sitting beside her, speaking to her ear.

“No matter how far I walk——”

“This city's neon——”

“They're all smiling at me asking: hey, where are you going?”

Her head had begun to sway.

Not on purpose. It was the body following the beat before the mind caught up. Han Teuk saw it in the mirror and quietly turned the volume knob up a notch. Louder by one. IU didn't open her eyes, and she didn't tell him to turn it back down.

“Take the wandering as my Way Back Home——”

The last note fell, and the synth pad kept going, its tail trailing maybe four seconds before it faded. Then it looped back to the top. The intro again. The granular texture of the pad.

Han Teuk didn't skip it.

And IU didn't ask him to.

The two of them rode on — one driving, one listening with her eyes shut — and let the whole song play through a second time, all the way to the end.

✿ ✿ ✿

July Cheongdam-dong, the van The track, heard twice

When it finished the second time, IU opened her eyes and looked out the window. The van was heading up the Cheongdam-dong road, the ginkgo trees on either side green enough to glisten, sunlight strobing through the branches as they passed.

The track was better than she'd expected.

Those ding-dong and tick-tack shards had been sliced well. Her sense for the words had survived; they hadn't been ground flat under that low voice. The chorus held its own, and the Tropical House base against the lonely core of the lyric made for exactly the contrast it should.

The producer had done fine.

Her words had done fine, too.

As for the person singing them…

Average.

IU picked her phone up again, swiped, put it back down.

“Where's the song releasing?”

Han Teuk glanced at her in the mirror.

“Our company.”

“Oh.”

The van stopped at an intersection, waiting out the light. While it was stopped, Han Teuk reached for the paper bag on the passenger seat, turned, and handed it back to the rear.

“Oh — this is for you.”

IU took it, opened the mouth of the bag, and looked inside.

Royce nama chocolate.

Original flavor.

Her eyes brightened — then dimmed, sliding into a narrow, suspicious look as she tipped her face up at him.

“Why are you bribing me?”

Han Teuk shook his head and pointed a finger at the centre console.

“He is.”

IU followed his gaze to the screen. It showed the name of the file still playing.

Way Back Home.

She stared at those words for two seconds, and pressed her lips into a line.

“Tch.”
