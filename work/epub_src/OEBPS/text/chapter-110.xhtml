Baek Si-on arrived to Lee Joon-ik already at the head of the table. Song Kang-ho on the director’s right, head in a script. He looked up, smiled.

“You’re here.”

“Sunbae.”

A proper bow. Song Kang-ho waved it off.

“Don’t be that formal. Save it for when you have to call me Father-King.”

A few laughs. Si-on too. Lee Joon-ik looked at him the way a director looks when he is weighing a morning, then:

“Sit.”

“Yes.”

Script open. Ten o’clock. Twenty minutes of the room first: Joseon. Yeongjo. Crown Prince Sado. Father and son. A throne and a bloodline. Instruction and a room with no air. In one sense it was not far from The Green Fly. A house at the bottom passing a hit down. A palace passing a father down. Different skin. Same bone.

Then they read. Si-on held Sado small in the first half. Not fear. The part was not madness on page one. He should try. Try to be a good son. Try to be a crown prince the court could use. Try to live the shape a father, a court, a rite had drawn. Then, a little at a time, be forced until he split. Midway, a father-son stand. Song Kang-ho put weight on the table. Si-on took it.

Three hours. No clap. This material did not want a clap. People stood, said the polite things. Lee Joon-ik, to him only:

“Before November, lose more.”

“Yes.”

Song Kang-ho laughed once beside them.

“Hear that? Your director has started eating you.”

Si-on closed the script.

“Better than being eaten by sunbae.”

Two bigger laughs.

“This kid.”

Director · Love Yourself MV · a woman who waits until the weather in a room is useful

She books apartments with nail holes. She does not shoot a pretty couple in a pretty house. She knows a director’s first virtue is when not to unmask an actor. She will ask for a kiss the way a couple who had actually dated would kiss, and she will call it natural.

Shoes. The sofa already had two. Jeong Han-teuk at the far end, a lift of the hand. Si-on nodded. Then the person beside Han-teuk. Right hand out. Lee Ji-eun looked at him, put her hand in. His palm made hers a rice cake that had gone into a fridge and not yet set. One squeeze. Off. Back on her knee. Hwang Soo-ah watched the edge of her own brow move. Too polite. Polite like two people meeting for work for the first time in a director’s house. She knew it was not that. She also knew a director’s first virtue. She did not take the mask off.

“Good. Everyone’s here. Concept first.”

Love Yourself · MV · Dir. Hwang Soo-ah · v1.0

His spine is simple: one man, one guitar, a cold room, window light, a lot of unpainted air. Flash: neon that spills. A woman under purple-red, heavy mouth, a sharp line, a glass, a crowd moving with a DJ. Then a past that is warm — two people at a counter feeding each other, two brushes at a glass, a forehead kiss at a bed. Not a line. A weather against a weather. He sings now. They loved then. She is still being looked at, in another life.

Ji-eun heard the outline and felt twelve kinds of wrong. She had dressed herself, in her head, in a knit, an autumn look-back, a café window, a lane of leaves, a book, and a soft I-learned-to-love-myself when the chorus said baby you should go and love yourself. Healing. Warm. A song that fit Lee Ji-eun. Club armour. Heavy makeup. Toasts under neon. What was that.

She looked up.

“Eonni.”

“Mm.”

“It is. A breakup is a kind of love song.”

Ji-eun looked at the English lyric in her hand. At Hwang Soo-ah. At the lyric. Something, from the start, had been read wrong. Phone. Translate. Paste. Three seconds. Korean. First verse: quiet. Chorus: still quiet. My mama don’t like you and she likes everyone — the face began to set. If you like the way you look that much, you should go and love yourself — the lights in her head went out.

So. She was the vain, selfish woman who used a boyfriend’s name to get a booth. She lifted her eyes. Hwang Soo-ah had seen the fog. Had been waiting for it.

“She isn’t only bad. She is used to being preferred. Used to other people’s patience as weather. Pretty, sharp, she knows she is loved, and she knows how to spend it. She isn’t even cruel in her own head. In her system, being spoiled, being yielded to, being held without a condition — that is what love looks like.”

Then the man’s frames. Then Si-on.

Si-on nodded. The angle was new. Ed Sheeran had written it to swear at an ex. Bieber had picked it up. Bieber had handed it to him. On the words you could shoot a man walking out of a bad woman and call it candy. Hwang Soo-ah had not. She was shooting a thing in which neither side was clean. A woman who sat in the centre of herself. A man who had known and would not cut. No good. No bad.

Ji-eun looked at the translation again. The face got more complicated.
