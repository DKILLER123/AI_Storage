Chapter One Hundred Twenty Six

One Elbow, and He Could Send You Back to Korea

Late October The opener · second half Los Angeles · Staples Center Basketball does not read the rundown, and it does not care about the storyline.

After the game started, things slid quickly in a direction that did not suit the narrative loop of “Legend.”

Scooter Braun’s original plan had been simple.

Kobe’s comeback.

Staples Center.

The live “Legend” performance.

The “legend already more than half written” from the music video walked back out under the game lights, and the song, riding the secondary wave of the national broadcast and the sports media, kicked the final push toward number one on Billboard.

It was a beautiful logic.

So beautiful that Scooter Braun had already lined up the second-round radio push, the sports-media press release, and the NBA’s official highlight window before the game even started.

The problem was.

Basketball is not a music video.

Nor is it a PR draft.

Basketball hits you back.

Not long into the first quarter, Harden reminded everyone, with two drives and a drawn foul, that tonight was not a comeback documentary about Kobe alone.

Howard owned the paint — boxing out, grabbing boards, protecting the rim — and the Lakers’ paint looked like it was made of paper.

The Harden–Howard pairing was at the height of its power.

And the Lakers, on the other side, were old, injured, and slow.

Their defensive rotations looked like they had been thrown together at halftime of a retired-players’ charity game.

The first quarter, chasing.

The second quarter, still chasing.

The third quarter, chasing on.

By the fourth, the deficit had stretched past twenty.

The noise in Staples Center was still there.

But it had shifted, slowly, from “we came to watch a legend return” to “please, don’t lose this ugly.”

Scooter Braun’s face had moved from pre-game excitement to silence.

He looked at the scoreboard. Then at the Lakers’ defence on the floor.

Finally he shook his head.

Forget it.

You could not expect Kobe to pull a Hollywood script like “turns it all around and blows out the Rockets” in his very first game back.

Second place would do.

Number two on Billboard would match the best PSY had ever done.

The Korean media would still lose their minds.

The global market would still open.

“Bones” still had an official version coming, and maybe that could make another run.

Just as Scooter Braun was running through the chart strategy in his head, something happened on the floor.

Fourth quarter.

Seven minutes left on the clock.

The Lakers’ possession came up empty.

Howard pulled down the defensive rebound.

Kobe was guarding him tight, right up on the man who had just grabbed the board, trying to disrupt the outlet.

Honestly, there was no reason for Kobe to fight this hard.

The score was down this much.

The clock was short.

His body had just come back.

The smartest move was to get back on defence, save his energy, and live to fight the next game.

But Kobe was not a reasonable man.

Tonight’s comeback ceremony had been this grand. Twenty thousand people had chanted his name.

If, in the end, he just stood there and watched Howard flip the ball out with no trouble at all, that would look worse than losing.

So he got up in him.

Howard, annoyed by the pressure, started swinging his elbows with much bigger arcs as he protected the ball.

The first elbow swept across.

Thump.

Kobe’s body jolted.

Before he could fully back off, the second one came.

That one was harder.

It swung straight into his face.

A long “Oooooh—” went through the building.

Kobe clapped a hand over his face on the spot and staggered two steps.

The next second, he looked like he had been set on fire.

He charged in.

His finger was nearly touching the tip of Howard’s nose.

The cursing out of his mouth was not fully caught by the arena mics, but the TNT cameras caught every word of it in clear lip-read.

“Touch me again, motherfucker!”

Then the line that cut deeper.

“You soft piece of shit!”

Howard had been standing there with his hands out, playing innocent.

When he heard that, his face went cold too.

He looked down at Kobe.

The corner of his mouth moved.

“Old man.”

“I see you, old man.”

The instant those words landed, Kobe’s eyes changed completely.

The booing in the building exploded.

Teammates charged in.

Referees charged in.

Rockets players hurried to pull Howard back.

Kobe was still pushing forward.

Still cursing.

And Howard had not fully backed off either.

Referees, teammates, assistant coaches between them, and still the two men looked like they would collide the next second.

VIP row one.

Baek Si-on stood up.

Adam Levine jumped.

Almost the instant Baek Si-on was on his feet, Adam had grabbed his arm.

“No no no no no—”

“Baek. Sit down!”

Adam hung onto him with everything he had.

“That guy is six-foot-eleven and two hundred sixty pounds of pure muscle!”

“You hold a microphone! Calm down!”

Baek Si-on: “……”

“Let me go.”

Adam panicked harder.

“No!”

“He’s my friend.”

Adam: “……”

“No — you literally cannot beat Dwight Howard!”

Baek Si-on’s face stayed cold.

“For a friend, I’d walk through a knife wall.”

Adam completely broke down.

“Bro! You can’t!”

“One elbow from him and he’ll elbow you straight back to Korea!”

Baek Si-on: “……”

The metaphor was a little absurd.

But Adam was genuinely desperate.

Tugging at Baek Si-on with one hand, he turned and shouted for his wife:

“Help me!”

Little Pumpkin snapped out of her daze and grabbed Baek Si-on’s other arm.

The TNT cameras had been on the altercation on the floor.

The director had been about to cut to a close-up of Kobe — after all, every storyline tonight revolved around him.

But out of the corner of his eye, the director caught something at the sideline.

VIP row one.

An Asian man in Tom Ford, his left arm in a death grip held by the Maroon 5 frontman, his right arm held by the Victoria’s Secret model.

Both their faces were written with the words “please sit down.”

And the Asian man’s face was written with “let me go, I’m going.”

The director froze for a second.

Then instinct beat judgment.

Cut.

The big screen switched.

TNT · Staples Center · LIVE

On the floor, Kobe and Howard — being separated by referees and teammates — vanish from the frame. In their place, the sideline: the Maroon 5 frontman with an Asian man in a Tom Ford suit in a death grip, the Victoria’s Secret model pressing down on his other arm. Both their faces read “please sit down.” His reads “let me go, I’m going.”

“Wait — is Adam Levine trying to stop Baek Si-on?”

“Looks like he’s trying to rescue him from Dwight Howard!”

The whole building blinked first — then laughter and screaming blew up together. Even the commentators were lost for a second.

Late October Moments later · courtside media zone → VIP row one Los Angeles · Staples Center A panic attack in a suit, and the third line of physical defence.

In the courtside media zone, Scooter Braun saw the picture on the big screen and nearly had a heart attack.

He broke into a sprint toward the VIP section.

But by the time he was even moving, the argument on the floor had already been put down.

The referees called one technical foul on each side, with an extra flagrant on Howard.

The players were pulled apart by their own teammates.

Kobe was still cursing, but Jeremy Lin and Carlos Boozer had him by the arms and were walking him toward his own end of the floor.

Howard stood with his hands spread, his face saying “I didn’t even do anything.”

At the sideline, the Levines had successfully prevented a one-sided massacre titled “rock singer vs. NBA center.”

Scooter Braun reached Baek Si-on, chest heaving.

“Are you insane?”

“No.”

Baek Si-on smoothed the crumpled sleeve of his suit jacket, his expression perfectly calm, and sat back down.

Scooter Braun pointed at the floor.

“Then what the hell were you running in for? To get yourself killed?”

Baek Si-on looked at him.

“Kobe is my friend.”

Scooter Braun: “……”

For a moment, he had nothing to say back.

Beside them, Little Pumpkin had heard it. She murmured to Adam:

“That’s sweet.”

Adam turned to look at her.

“Sweet? He almost got killed.”

Little Pumpkin shrugged.

“But sweet.”

Adam: “……”

Fine.

If his supermodel wife said it was sweet, then it was sweet.

Scooter Braun was not so easy to fool.

He knew exactly who he was dealing with.

The man who had just performed a whole “through a knife wall for my brother” act for the national broadcast was a genuine Venice Best Actor.

So — was that real feeling, or a textbook-grade piece of improvisation?

Scooter Braun remained deeply suspicious.

But whatever the truth was, Scooter Braun was not going back to his old seat.

He dropped straight into the seat on Baek Si-on’s other side and, together with the Levines, formed the third physical line of defence around this Best Actor.

✿ ✿ ✿

Late October The rest of the night Los Angeles · Staples Center → the ride back to the hotel A lost game, a found friendship, and a cousin at the end of her rope.

The game went on.

The pace on the floor settled back into its groove.

Kobe, playing through the sting of that elbow on his face, kept fighting on both ends.

Too bad — the fight was real, and so were the bricks.

Still, even so, every time the camera found him, the crowd instantly broke into “KOBE.”

“Bro.”

Baek Si-on turned. Adam was holding out his phone.

“Let’s trade contacts.”

Baek Si-on: ?

“I just saved your life.”

“You wrinkled my suit.”

“I also saved your face.”

“……”

Baek Si-on took the phone and typed in his number.

Then the two of them followed each other on Twitter and Instagram.

The final buzzer sounded.

Lakers 90, Rockets 108.

Lost by eighteen.

Not a blowout.

But definitely not a “glorious in defeat” score either.

Adam and Little Pumpkin got up to leave as well.

“Baek — if we use your idea for the video, want to come guest-star and raise some hell?”

“Depends on my schedule.”

“Right. Oh — and don’t ever charge at an NBA player again. Your face is your meal ticket.”

“And yours isn’t?”

“I work with my voice. The face is just a bonus.”

Little Pumpkin put in from the side:

“A pretty good bonus.”

Adam froze, turned to look at her.

Little Pumpkin was already walking toward the tunnel, bag over her shoulder.

Adam watched her back and rubbed his chin.

“She’s in a good mood today.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Later.”

Adam waved.

The two figures disappeared into the VIP tunnel entrance.

✿ ✿ ✿

Late October A few minutes later · floor level Los Angeles · Staples Center Kobe Bryant has a few things to say, and none of them need a hug.

After the Levines left, Scooter Braun finally breathed a little easier.

Only a little.

Because Kobe was walking over.

His agent walked beside him, having clearly just filled him in on what had happened at the sideline.

Walking over, Kobe kept looking at Baek Si-on.

His expression was hard to read.

Like he wanted to laugh.

And also like he thought this kid was a little crazy.

When he was close enough, he opened his arms.

“Come here, man.”

“No.”

Baek Si-on raised a hand and blocked it.

Declined.

Kobe stopped.

“?”

Baek Si-on pointed at his own suit.

“This is borrowed.”

“You hugged me at the start.”

“You weren’t sweating at the start.”

Kobe blinked twice, then suddenly burst out laughing.

“You’re an interesting guy.”

“Thanks.”

“That wasn’t a compliment.”

“Sounded like one.”

Kobe shook his head, smiling.

In the end, he did not force the hug.

He just reached out and bumped Baek Si-on’s fist.

“I heard about what happened.”

“Mm.”

“Next time don’t charge at Howard. That guy’s elbows really hurt.”

“……”

Not far away, Jeremy Lin, on his way out, stopped.

As the Lakers’ point guard, he had absorbed the brunt of Kobe’s white-hot fury tonight.

And this old man, who had death-stared his own teammates all game, turned around and grinned ear to ear at some Korean singer.

Jeremy Lin sighed.

And silently turned toward the locker room.

Late October After the game · on the road Los Angeles · the ride back to the hotel A trial by jury of two, and the defendant is completely unrepentant.

In the car back to the hotel, the atmosphere was very serious.

Baek Eun-ah’s face, normally soft with a layer of fangirl admiration, was uglier than the Lakers’ fourth-quarter defence.

“Oppa!”

“Mm?”

“Do you have any idea what you almost just did?”

“Stood up.”

“Not the standing up!”

Baek Eun-ah’s voice dropped very low:

“You almost ran into the middle of a fight on live national television! What were you going to do when you got there — hit him over the knee with your Venice trophy?”

“I didn’t bring it.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

“This is not a joking matter.”

Beside her, Scooter Braun joined the tribunal:

“I was in the middle of talks with Nike about your sneaker deal.”

“You know how hard it is for an actor to land a sneaker deal? Sports brands go to athletes. To rappers. To basketball-culture figures. But an actor? An Asian actor, especially? Very hard.”

That was not exaggeration.

The only reason Baek Si-on could make sports brands sit up was not his looks — it was that “Legend” had strapped him to the sports symbols of Kobe, the NBA, and the global athlete challenge.

“But now —”

“You almost showed them an endorser who will run into a fight at an NBA game.”

“Isn’t that good for sneakers?”

Scooter Braun: “……”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

“Are you serious?”

“Sneakers aren’t fashion items. What they sell is testosterone. Right?”

Scooter Braun could not argue for a second.

Because in a certain sense, that statement was annoyingly correct.

Correct, but that still did not mean he could accept his artist personally running in to die.

“Marketing conflict is not the same as personally joining a conflict.”

Baek Si-on leaned back in his seat.

“I didn’t join it.”

“You almost joined it!”

“I was stopped.”

“Because Adam and his wife were fast!”

Baek Si-on said nothing.

Baek Eun-ah looked at that face of his — water off a duck’s back — and her temper flared again.

“Oppa, exactly which part of you do you think is the problem?”

“I don’t think any part of me is a problem.”

“No part?”

“Between friends, reason doesn’t apply.”

When Kobe had appeared in Baek Si-on’s music video on the strength of being understood rather than being used, their relationship had long since stopped being client-and-vendor. It had become mutual respect.

“Even if, out there tonight, Kobe was the one who started it, the one in the wrong — the moment someone is cursing him to his face, I’m going to stand with him.”

Baek Si-on turned to Scooter Braun.

“If someone beat you up, I’d come help too.”

Scooter Braun had been gearing up for a full group attack.

Brand risk.

Public image.

Sports partnerships.

“You’re not an ordinary celebrity anymore, you’re a long-term asset gathering snow.”

And then that one sentence came out, and all his fire went out.

He looked at Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on looked back at him.

“Baek. If I got beaten up, you could hire the best lawyer in Los Angeles and sue the guy into bankruptcy. What I really don’t need is you personally taking off this goddamn Tom Ford to throw hands.”

Baek Eun-ah: “……”

“???”

She whipped her head around to Scooter Braun.

The American agent who, a minute ago, had stood shoulder to shoulder with her in righteous fury, putting Baek Si-on on trial —

He had just defected?

Because of one sentence — “if someone beat you up, I’d come help too” — he had laid down his weapons entirely?

What even was friendship between men?

Baek Eun-ah gritted her teeth.

Fine.

Scooter Braun was hopeless.

She looked down and opened her tablet.

First, let’s see what Korea was saying.

The screen lit up.

As expected.

The Korean media never failed to be fast.

And the headlines were wilder than the last.

The same day · Seoul time Afternoon Seoul · Nonhyeon-dong · Big Hit practice room Seven boys on the floor, one screenshot, and a revival that no coaching could produce.

Seoul. Nonhyeon-dong. The Big Hit practice room.

Seven boys lay sprawled across the floor.

Training was still on this afternoon, but right after a full round, none of them wanted to move a muscle.

Kim Tae-hyung had been scrolling through his phone when, all of a sudden, he shot up off the floor like a spring.

“Daebak!”

Kim Nam-joon looked up.

“Now what?”

“Baek Si-on sunbae!”

Those four words were like a summoning spell.

The six bodies on the floor revived at once.

“What? What?”

“Did he hit number one on Billboard?”

“No!”

Kim Tae-hyung held up his phone.

On the screen, a screenshot from the TNT broadcast.

VIP row one.

Baek Si-on rising from his seat.

On his left, Adam Levine gripping his arm with everything he had.

On his right, Victoria’s Secret model Behati reaching over to press him down too.

In the frame, Baek Si-on’s face was expressionless.

But the posture — however you read it, it looked like he was about to vault the barrier and storm the court in the next second.

Jung Ho-seok understood first.

“He was going to back up Kobe sunbae?”

Kim Nam-joon skimmed the news quickly.

“Howard elbowed Kobe, and Kobe went at him. Baek Si-on sunbae tried to go over, and Adam stopped him.”

The practice room went quiet for one second.

Then Jeon Jung-kook said, in a low voice:

“Cool.”

Kim Seok-jin nodded.

“Manly as hell.”

Park Ji-min leaned in to look at the second GIF.

“He really almost charged in, huh.”

Min Yoon-gi shook his head.

“That hyung’s got a screw loose.”

“Hyung!”

Min Yoon-gi added:

“But it’s cool.”

“See? I told you! Baek Si-on sunbae is faith itself!”

Kim Nam-joon looked at the comment section on the phone.

Korean netizens were cursing.

NBA fans were making memes.

And a crowd of people had crowned Adam Levine and Behati “the most important defensive line-up of the night.”

He was quiet for a few seconds.

“It really was dangerous, though.”

Jeon Jung-kook nodded.

“Yeah.”

“But if a friend got beaten up…”

Kim Nam-joon stopped there.

Because everyone in the practice room understood.

If, someday, any one of the seven of them was cornered outside and pushed around in front of everyone, the other six probably would not stop to calculate brand risk and public image first.

They would stand up first.

That was not rationality.

But some things were never held together by rationality in the first place.

Jung Ho-seok lay back down on the floor and let out a sigh.

“Baek Si-on sunbae really is…”

“A legend?”

Jung Ho-seok smiled.

“A troublesome legend.”

The same time · Seoul Late afternoon Seoul · Green Bottle Fly media preview · Q&A A soft voice that has never once been hard, going hard on live television.

At the same time, at the media preview of Green Bottle Fly.

Since Baek Si-on was in America, the stage today was held mainly by the director, Baek Jeong-hun, and the female lead, Choi Jin-ri.

The interview session after the screening had already run twenty minutes.

Most of the questions were about the film itself.

“Sulli-ssi, how did you land this role?”

“Director Baek Jeong-hun, what mattered most to you in casting?”

“What does the Venice experience mean to you?”

Choi Jin-ri answered them one by one.

But just as the interview was winding down, a reporter raised his hand.

A staff member passed him the microphone.

“Sulli-ssi.”

Choi Jin-ri looked up.

“Yes.”

The reporter’s tone was very polite.

But the question was anything but.

The atmosphere in the room changed instantly. Director Baek Jeong-hun’s brow furrowed. The agent standing at the side of the stage tightened.

The question was sharp.

And the malice ran deep.

He was not asking about Baek Si-on alone. He had tied together the violence of the Green Bottle Fly role, Baek Si-on’s real-life conduct, and Choi Jin-ri’s working relationship with him.

If Choi Jin-ri said “I don’t know,” that was evasion. If she said “he shouldn’t have,” the media would write “Sulli also thinks Baek Si-on lost control.”

Down in the audience, silence.

But the camera flashes had already started popping, early.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Baek Jeong-hun turned his head and gave Choi Jin-ri a look. His meaning was clear — you don’t have to answer. The press-conference host also had a smooth exit line ready.

But Choi Jin-ri looked the reporter straight in the eye:

Green Bottle Fly media preview · press Q&A · the last question

“A little while ago, Baek Si-on-ssi appeared to try to rush into a conflict zone at an NBA game, and it has caused considerable controversy. Some argue this is a kind of real-life extension of the violent role he plays in Green Bottle Fly. As an actor who co-stars with Baek Si-on-ssi in this film and shares a great many scenes with him — how do you view his conduct in front of the overseas broadcast cameras? Do you think he lacks the restraint a public figure ought to have?”

“Have you watched the full video?”

“I… saw the news screenshots and the related coverage.”

“So you’re taking things out of context. That’s fine — but let me ask you: if your friend were being beaten in front of you, would you sit there and not move?”

“But he’s a public figure—”

“Public figures aren’t allowed to have friends? If a person can watch a friend get hit and feel nothing, that isn’t rationality. That’s cold blood.”

Baek Jeong-hun turned his head and looked at Choi Jin-ri again.

There was a little surprise in his eyes.

And a little approval too.

He had expected her to duck the question.

After all, in public she had always been soft.

Gentle.

Compliant.

Almost eager to please.

Whatever the media asked, she would answer in the safest possible way, wrapped in a smile that made everyone think “Sulli is so well-behaved.”

But today, she was hard in a way that was unfamiliar.

The reporter tried to press on.

Baek Jeong-hun picked up the microphone directly.

“Next question.”

The meaning was clear.

This topic ends here.

The same evening · Seoul Right after the preview Seoul · the venue’s backstage lounge A message typed, deleted, retyped — and finally sent anyway.

The preview ended.

Choi Jin-ri stood up from behind the long table.

Walking off the stage, her legs were a little weak.

Not from exhaustion.

Because the words she had just said had taken all her courage.

She had never been a tough person.

Back in the backstage lounge, the first thing Choi Jin-ri did was grab her phone.

She opened the news.

The Korean media headlines were as ugly as expected.

The more she read, the tighter her brow drew.

Her heart sank, a little more with every line.

Then she opened KakaoTalk.

Her chat with Baek Si-on was still sitting on the earlier exchange about his safe arrival.

Looking at those few lines, her eyes suddenly stung.

This man really knew how to be infuriating.

And really knew how to make you worry about him.

Still no reply.
