Chapter Twenty-Eight

The Composer and the Lyricist

The next morning Before ten Yeonnam-dong · then LOEN, Gangnam KOMCA for the cousin. A flower boy in the lobby. Debt collectors write songs now.

“Eomma, I’m going out.”

Baek Si-on at the genkan, changing shoes.

Yoon Hye-ja put her head out of the kitchen, half a cup of coffee still in her hand.

“Where?”

“Gangnam. Meeting someone.”

“Want a ride? No class this morning anyway.”

“Taxi’s fine.”

Si-on pulled the door, thought, turned back one more line.

“I’ll be home for dinner.”

Yoon Hye-ja nodded once, didn’t ask further.

Once he was out, Si-on sent Baek Eun-ah a message.

Baek Eun-ah

Baek Si-on

Don’t follow me today. Go to the Korea Music Copyright Association and register me as a member. I’ll send the materials.

Baek Eun-ah

Got it.

[salute]

Baek Eun-ah

Oppa, the bag is so pretty. My friends all asked where I got it.

Si-on didn’t reply.

✿ ✿ ✿

The taxi left Yeonnam-dong, turned up Yanghwa-ro from the Hongdae entrance, crossed Mapo Bridge, all the way toward Gangnam.

Twenty-five minutes.

The car stopped in front of a dark-grey office building.

LOEN Entertainment.

The building was not tall, ten-odd floors, but the dark-grey stone on the facade was clean. The ground-floor entrance used a large pane of glass. The logo over the door was silver, throwing a little light in the morning sun.

Si-on pushed in.

Two girls in white shirts at reception, makeup precise, hair not a strand off. The one on the left on a call. The one on the right looked up at him.

“Hello, do you have an appointment?”

“I do. Ten this morning.”

Reception flipped a book, had him sign the visitor log.

Si-on signed.

“One moment, I’ll put you through.”

Reception picked up the handset, dialled an internal number.

Si-on stepped back, waited in the lobby.

The lobby was not large, but it was kept very clean.

A few grey fabric chairs along the wall. Magazines on the table.

In the corner, a screen cycling album covers and event posters of LOEN artists.

One of them was IU.

Si-on looked once, looked away.

About two minutes.

A ding. The lift arrived.

The door opened. One person came out.

Black T-shirt, black cargo pants, a badge on the chest.

Jeong Han-teuk.

He walked looking at his phone, still muttering something, probably reciting the job Lee Ji-eun had just given him.

A sunbae Goo Hara had sent.

The flower-boy type.

Here to talk lyrics.

Go down and meet him.

He pocketed the phone, lifted his head, scanned the lobby.

Past the sofa, past reception, onto the man in the white T-shirt, both hands in his pockets, buzz cut, standing dead centre of the visitor area.

Han-teuk’s feet stopped.

“Si-on? What are you doing here?”

Si-on looked at him, nodded once.

“I have an appointment.”

He pointed at the sign-in book.

Han-teuk slowly turned, looked at the name on the book, turned back.

“You?”

“Me.”

Han-teuk stood there with his mouth open.

His head was doing a maths problem.

The sunbae Hara mentioned = the person here to talk lyrics = Baek Si-on.

Each equals he understood.

Three in a row, he did not.

“You… you write songs too?”

Si-on didn’t answer that.

“She upstairs?”

“Who?”

“Your boss.”

Han-teuk only then remembered he had come down to fetch someone.

He forced the maths into the brain’s “pending” folder and did the job first.

“Come on. Upstairs.”

The two of them walked to the lift side by side.

Han-teuk hit the floor. The door closed.

“Really didn’t think it’d be you.”

Han-teuk against the wall, looking at him sideways.

“Ji-eun said go down and meet someone, a sunbae Goo Hara sent, and she made a point of saying the flower-boy kind. The whole way down I was wondering which flower-boy idol…”

He looked Si-on up and down.

Buzz cut. Jawline. Skin baked wheat. Under the white T the line of shoulder and back.

“Flower boy.”

He repeated the two words.

The tone complicated.

Si-on didn’t pick it up.

A ding.

Arrived.

The door opened.

The corridor was not long, grey carpet, the soundproofing good enough that footsteps were swallowed clean.

To the end, a wood door, no plate on it.

Han-teuk lifted a hand, knocked three times.

“Come in.”

The voice from inside.

Han-teuk pushed.

The room was not large, but the light was good.

By the window a pale wood desk, a laptop and a lamp on it, a few lyric notebooks open beside.

Lee Ji-eun sat at the desk.

Hair in a bun, a red-and-black plaid shirt, a grey cotton T underneath.

On the laptop an audio editor, the waveform parked on the chorus.

She heard the door, lifted her head from the screen.

The look went over Han-teuk’s shoulder, onto the person behind him.

The arc that had just started at the corner of her mouth stuck halfway. Didn’t go up. Didn’t come down.

Blinked once.

Blinked again.

The brow slowly knotted.

“You came again?”

Si-on lifted an eyebrow.

“I’m not allowed to come?”

Lee Ji-eun leaned back, both arms crossed on her chest.

“We don’t welcome rascals here.”

“That’s a pity.”

Si-on took a step in.

“Hey—”

Lee Ji-eun half stood, palm on the desk, as if ready to hit the security call at any second.

Han-teuk stood between them, head going left-right like a ping-pong match for two rounds, finally found a gap to breathe and stuffed the line in:

“Ji-eun. He’s the appointment. Goo Hara sent him. Here to talk lyrics.”

Lee Ji-eun’s fingers slowly tightened on the desk. She turned to Han-teuk.

That look, translated: say that again?

“It’s him.”

Han-teuk said, “That demo was his.”

Lee Ji-eun slowly sat back, looked at the waveform on the screen.

That melody.

Last night when the demo came in, she had opened it and listened once.

The chorus change too smooth, like water from one step to the next, and before you noticed you were in another key.

She had even scrolled back and listened again, to confirm she hadn’t heard wrong, then replied that they could talk.

“It was really you?”

Si-on didn’t explain.

From a pocket, the phone, KakaoTalk, scrolled to the demo he had sent last night, screen out, held in front of her.

Lee Ji-eun looked down at the thread.

Looked up at him.

Looked down again.

Time, avatar, the lines, one by one they matched.

She put her back into the chair, accepted the fact. Accepting the fact was not accepting the person.

“Debt collectors write songs now?”

The line went up at the end.

Not mockery. Teasing.

Because that melody really did catch the ear.

A man who had collected a debt writing a thing like this — the gap itself had a force that made you want to comment.

“You think you’re funny?”

The sound of Si-on’s sentence hitting the floor was almost audible.

The room quiet one second.

Lee Ji-eun’s face went cold in real time.

“You never learned honorifics?”

“Or do debt collectors all ask for help in this tone?”

Si-on snorted through the nose.

Then the phone back in the pocket, turned, toward the door.

Han-teuk stood still, head first following Si-on’s back ninety degrees, then back to Lee Ji-eun’s face.

Si-on’s back said: I’m leaving.

Lee Ji-eun’s face said: let him.

The door opened.

Si-on went out.

The door shut.

Footsteps in the corridor, unhurried, toward the lift.

Han-teuk looked at Lee Ji-eun.

Lee Ji-eun looked at the waveform on the screen.

The chorus boxed, zoomed, peak after peak packed tight.

About half a minute.

Outside a car horn, dull, coming up from ten-odd floors, like a reminder posted from very far away.

“Han-teuk oppa.”

“Here.”

“Go bring him back, please.”

“All right.”

Han-teuk turned to go. Hand on the handle, Lee Ji-eun added from behind:

“Walk slow. Don’t let him think I’m in a hurry.”

Han-teuk nearly tripped.

He adjusted his stride, and in a rhythm that could still catch a person without looking eager, pushed out.
