The Kiss Was Too Real

October 8 Still morning The same apartment · a monitor, then a floor A take Jin-ri did not want to watch. A kiss the board had asked for. A second kiss the board had not.

Choi Jin-ri let herself be walked to the glass. Baek Si-on was already there. Half-crouch, hands on knees, a little forward, pinned to the screen. She had seen this version of him on The Green Fly — a man who could find a back that was wrong when no one else had a language for backs. She cleared her throat.

Nothing. The attention stayed nailed. She did it again, lighter. Still nothing. Lee Ji-eun’s mouth, on her arm, bent a little more. Jin-ri regretted, suddenly, the whole morning. A set visit. Why did it feel like being seated in the front row of an exam she had not agreed to sit.

No choice. She stood beside him and watched the thing she had not come to watch.

PLAYBACK · the pull

He uses a little force. She comes into him. Hair half a shoulder. Face in the knit, laughing hard enough the shoulders go. His hand on her back. Chin down. A mouth that has agreed to bend. Light good. Frame good. The bodies speaking a language that does not need subtitles.

Last still: she lifts off his chest, a strand on the cheek, the laugh still in the eye.

Jin-ri felt it like a thumb and finger on the heart, not hard, just exact.

The playback ended. Ji-eun turned.

“Peach.”

“Mm.”

“How was it?”

Soft. Still smiling. So natural Jin-ri could not tell if it was clean or high enough that the work did not show. Side-eye: he had finally turned. Looking at her. Waiting. Jin-ri thought, then:

“The acting’s good.”

His brow moved.

“What’s wrong with it?”

“Eh?”

She looked at him.

“I asked where you think it fails.”

Stuck. She had called it acting to give herself a door. She had not counted what those three words did to him. For him, good acting was not praise. It was a mark. The best work was the work you could not catch doing work. The head turned two circles. Then another fact stood up. He was asking where it failed. His eye, from the first, was on whether the take had finished itself — not on whether his heart had gone up when he held her, whether the laugh was only a cue, whether the look had walked off the call sheet. The cloud in Jin-ri’s chest got a hole. Light came through. Overcast to clear.

“It’s just… mm. Too perfect.”

A small frown.

“Too perfect.”

“Mm. Real love isn’t that. Not that it isn’t pretty. It is. The light, the move, that little fall. But real love isn’t this clean.”

Hwang Soo-ah’s eye came up. Ji-eun looked at her too. Jin-ri went on. Real love was not every frame a gaffer had tuned gold. It had ugly minutes. Those minutes were why you believed two people had been in a room together. He was about to say that was fair when Ji-eun spoke.

“Peach.”

Jin-ri turned.

“Do you know why a first love stays beautiful?”

A blink. She had not seen the turn coming.

“Because it doesn’t live in the present. It lives in both people’s later. Memory grades the picture for you. The fight fades. Twenty minutes of freeze get cut. What stays is the sweet. And what we’re shooting is memory.”

Jin-ri looked at her three seconds. Several turns. Was it a reason. It was. Full enough that the rice-and-oil speech she had just given felt, suddenly, a little off-axis. She took her eyes off Ji-eun’s face and put them on his. Ji-eun turned with her. Two looks landing on the same man. One he had shot with in Anseong, lent a shoulder in a cinema, pointed at on a Venice night and made sing. One who had written him a lyric, sent him a lawyer, and spent a morning laughing on his chest. Two temperatures. Two waits.

He thought. Looked down at Hwang Soo-ah in front of the glass.

“Director Hwang.”

“Mm.”

“How do you see it?”

The mouth did a thing. The look said, roughly: you kicked this to me. You’re serious. I am a director, not a counsellor. She looked at Jin-ri, at Ji-eun. One had said real love is not this clean. One had said memory grades the tape. Both had a point. The question was whether they were still talking about an MV. Hwang Soo-ah was not sure. Or she was sure they were not. A director’s first virtue, again: knowing when to pretend not to have seen. She cleared her throat.

“Both right. So we don’t go again.”

It sounded like a split. It was, in fact, a sentence both could live in. For Ji-eun, not going again was the director taking her side. For Jin-ri, not going again was better. She would not have to stand here and watch them make it “more real.” The eyes spared a second pollution. That, itself, was a win. As for him. A small nod, thinking.

“Understood.”

Hwang Soo-ah looked at that and wanted to sigh. Understood what. You had better have actually understood.

The little balance had just sat down when Jin-ri’s manager, coffee done, came close, voice down.

“Sulli-yah. Time. We should go.”

Phone. Forty-five minutes from the first Kakao. Short in a civilian mouth. Dangerous in an idol’s. Afternoon still waiting: a drive, a hold, a powder, a press line, the kind of accident that liked to be born in a corridor. She had meant to drop coffee. She had sat half a war. Lock. Up.

“All right.”

To Hwang Soo-ah, a small incline.

“Director. I’ll go. Sorry for the interruption.”

A wave.

“You didn’t. The coffee was good. Go easy.”

Then Ji-eun.

“Eonni. Good shoot.”

Still the smile.

“Mm. Your afternoon too.”

Last, him.

“I’ll go, then.”

“I’ll walk you.”

Natural, from him. Jin-ri’s mouth had started up when Ji-eun, equally natural:

“I’ll walk you too.”

The mouth came back to flat.

“…All right.”

Three of them to the door. Him left, Ji-eun right, Jin-ri the middle. Eun-ah found the blocking uncanny and all three faces annoyingly calm. His calm was real; he had probably not walked that far in his head. Jin-ri’s was work. Ji-eun’s was too much of it. Down. Van at the curb. Door. Jin-ri turned. Ji-eun waved first.

“Peach. Go easy~”

A wave back.

“You too, Ji-eun eonni. Good shoot.”

Then him. On the step, October taking a corner of the grey knit.

“Next visit, just come. You don’t have to bring anything.”

Jin-ri blinked.

“What?”

“I’m cutting weight.”

The second half did not land. She had the first two words. Next. Not don’t come. Not thanks for today. Next. The eyes came on a little at a time. The place the monitor had pinched let go. A bright smile, no cover.

“Mm!”

A nod with force. He thought the face was a little too glad. He did not ask. Last, Eun-ah. Door. Van. Film on the glass. In the back she still turned. The two of them on the pavement, seeing her off. A corner. Gone. Phone. The two bubbles still unread. And, suddenly, she did not mind. She had an answer. He was busy. Busy enough not to open a chat. Busy enough to shoot. Busy enough to be on a bed with Lee Ji-eun. The mouth flattened, then found a bend again. Next. He had said next. Then there was a next.

Tail-lights into an October street. They stood together under the building. Eun-ah two steps back with the tablet, a historian who had walked into a three-kingdom painting and would write none of it. Ji-eun turned for the stairs first. He followed. Eun-ah made herself as small as a person with a tablet can be. The lift. Ji-eun watched her own blur in the metal.

“Peach came, and you didn’t look even a little rattled.”

He looked at her.

“Why would I be rattled?”

“Right. Best Actor.”

It could have been praise. He heard that it was not.

“You had her watch the take on purpose.”

The face did not change. A smile.

“You show a friend the work.”

“Your friend did not look like she was watching work.”

“And you?”

She turned. The metal held both of them.

“You really did look like you were only watching work.”

“What else would I be watching?”

The mouth opened. Closed. The chain had been complete — test him with Peach, needle the composure, push him to admit the morning had not only been a job. Every step had a back door. None of them had budgeted for what else. The subtext was: what do you want me to have been watching it as. Too straight. Straight enough that answering was not a dark war. It was a card on the table. She was not ready to put that card down. A small heat.

“You make it hard for an actress to feel like she did anything.”

“Which kind?”

“This kind.”

“This kind is which?”

“Like just now.”

“Just now which?”

She glared. He looked back, serious enough she could not tell if he did not know or had decided not to. A breath.

“Forget it.”

Ding. She went first, half a step faster than her usual. He did not change his. Eun-ah last. She watched the quicker back, the unmoved side-face, typed a line on the tablet, deleted it, typed, deleted. Left nothing. Some things did not belong in a file. Especially this kind of thing.

A little off, Han-teuk with an Americano that had forgotten how to be ice. Drink, don’t. Put it down, don’t. The face a mess. Chasing Si-on home, tipping him that Ji-eun liked Royce — that could still be called a manager helping an artist work. This picture was harder. His artist was going to kiss. The other person was his friend. He, as manager, had to sit and watch. Some kind of ethics pit.

“Ji-eun. Toward him.”

A shift.

Another.

ACTION

He moves first. Right hand at the back of her head, fingers into the hair, a pull toward him. Her hand on the shoulder, then up the knit, an arm around the neck. Breath hits breath. He changes the angle. She goes with it. Not the idol-drama stamp-and-orbit. A real taking, a real giving-back. Her fingers at the nape, a small close. His hand from her waist to the back, a small gather in. The camera walks. In the glass they knot in the backlight, two things that have been warmed until they start to run. On the wood, one shadow.

Hwang Soo-ah taps a knee. The tempo is right. Not rushed. Two people who have done this enough to know where the other’s mouth is kind, when to take air, when to use a little teeth.

A few more seconds. They come off. Breath heavier than it had been. He looks at her. She looks at him. Five centimetres of quiet collision. From the glass, the look is a picture. Hwang Soo-ah had meant to hold three seconds of it and call—

Ji-eun went forward without a cue and took his mouth again.

His body went stiff for a blink. The move was not on the board. An actor’s animal is faster than a thought. He did not push. He did not steal it back. The hand already on her back only closed a little, and he walked the feeling she had put down. Her arm still around the neck. The kiss serious. Not a grab for a frame. More like putting every unsaid thing back where it had come from. Don’t say. Don’t ask. Don’t admit. Don’t step off either. He took it. Then, slowly, gave it back.

In the glass the thing stopped being a pretty close in a man’s memory. It became two people who knew the road went bad and had still, in one minute, wanted to keep the other body in the room. Hwang Soo-ah’s eyes came on. This take was better than the one she had drawn. Not pretty. Pretty was easy. A handsome man, a pretty woman, gauze, a floor — you could not make that ugly if you tried. The hard part was they had not played a kiss. They had played a relationship. Her going forward had a refusal in it. His answering had a tiredness in it. The DP did not touch the stick. The walk stayed. Light on both of them. Grey knit and cream sweater deciding, again, to be one soft colour.

Eun-ah watched Ji-eun go in the second time as if she could not not. And understood, a little late: her cousin was not a man who did not pull. He pulled and did not know he had. That was worse than doing it on purpose.

A few seconds. Ji-eun came off. Her hands had not left his neck. His had not left her back. They looked. This look was not a board’s look. It was that neither of them moved first.
