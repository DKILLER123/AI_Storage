Chapter Seventy-Nine

Red-Carpet Visual Bomb, Screening-Hall Applause Nuclear Blast

September 2 1:32 am (KST) Seoul · Rainbow dormitory, the living room A dress made by hand, watched by the whole country

Seoul.

One thirty-two in the morning.

Rainbow dormitory, living room.

Kim Jae-kyung should have been asleep.

There was a variety-show recording at eight tomorrow morning.

Her manager had sent her "sleep early, wake early" three times, the last one with an exclamation point.

But she couldn't sleep.

Because the clothes she'd made with her own hands were, right now, being worn by two people and examined by the entire world.

So as not to disturb the members sleeping, she wore earphones and held an iPad in her hands.

The screen showed the Naver TV Cast live page.

In the top-right corner of the frame, red text:

LIVE · 71st Venice International Film Festival · The Green Fly premiere red carpet.

The image quality wasn't great.

The signal from Venice to the server in Seoul bent around who knows how many corners.

But it was watchable.

The special reporter stationed in Europe held a microphone, standing at the media area on the side of the red carpet.

Behind her was the curved entrance of the Lido film palace. The dark red carpet stretched from the left of the frame to the right, and behind the metal railings on both sides stood photographers, cameras raised.

Naver TV Cast · live · a dress made by hand, watched by the whole country

Rainbow dormitory, 1:32 a.m. KST. The living room, one person awake, one iPad.

"Now approaching us is the crew of The Green Fly, representing Korean cinema in the race for the Golden Lion!"

The signal bent around who knows how many corners, but it was watchable. That was all she needed.

The camera swung toward the entrance of the red carpet.

The first black sedan stopped at the start of the carpet.

The door opened.

Baek Jeong-hun got out.

Dark suit, hair combed back, the nasolabial folds appearing extra deep under the red-carpet flash.

He stood still for a second, gave a slight nod to the media areas on both sides, then began to walk up the carpet.

The barrage exploded.

The barrage scrolled so fast it was nearly impossible to read a single line. A white blur of captions covered most of the frame.

Kim Jae-kyung swiped at the screen twice, lowering the barrage's opacity a touch.

The picture cleared some.

The camera followed Baek Jeong-hun for a few steps, then swung back to the red-carpet entrance.

The second car stopped.

On the side closer to the camera, the door opened first.

Baek Si-on got out.

The blue suit from the morning was gone, replaced by Kim Jae-kyung's handmade suit.

The cut was extremely fitted.

The shoulder line to the millimetre, the inward curve at the waist exactly right, just making a clear inverted-triangle silhouette.

Kim Jae-kyung pulled the blanket up and tightened it around herself.

Not cold.

Her hands were shaking.

In the frame, Baek Si-on didn't walk straight on.

He stood by the car, turned slightly to one side, and waited.

The attendant went around to the other side of the car and opened the door.

A foot in a silver strappy-heeled sandal came out first.

Then the hem of the gown.

The deep red satin flowed out of the car door like a length of silk being slowly unfolded.

Choi Jin-ri stepped out of the car.

The moment she lifted her hem and stood upright, Kim Jae-kyung's eyes left her face, involuntarily, and landed on the gown.

The waistline.

She checked whether the cinch point was right.

Right.

The shoulders.

The angle of the off-shoulder matched perfectly the collarbone extension line she'd marked during the fitting.

The hem.

The flare from the waist, adjusted three times, was the A-line cut she'd wanted; as Choi Jin-ri moved, it swung out in a modest, just-right sway.

Kim Jae-kyung mentally went down the checklist.

Like an architect watching the building she designed stand for the first time against a city skyline.

In the frame, Choi Jin-ri walked up to Baek Si-on.

She extended her hand and gently slipped her arm through his right.

Baek Si-on turned slightly to the side, moving his arm out a little to give her hand a comfortable place.

The two of them, shoulder to shoulder, walked up the red carpet.

The barrage completely exploded.

Kim Jae-kyung couldn't see anything.

She turned the barrage off.

The picture finally came clean.

On the red carpet.

Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri had walked to the core of the media area.

The flashes from several hundred cameras on both sides went off together, one wave after another of white light beating over them, illuminating them head to toe in detail.

The satin weave of Baek Si-on's suit was an inward, reined-in light; the satin of Choi Jin-ri's gown was a flowing, radiating light.

One absorbed light, one emitted it.

Standing together, they didn't compete and didn't crowd each other.

Each beautiful in its own right.

And still like a couple.

Kim Jae-kyung pressed the barrage switch at the bottom again.

The dense barrage re-covered the whole screen.

Kim Jae-kyung stared at those comments.

The barrage asking about the clothing brand scrolled past, line after line. Each one that passed made the curve of her mouth a little wider.

She watched them for a long time.

So long that the iPad screen dimmed once, and she quickly tapped again.

In the frame, the three had reached the end of the red carpet. At the entrance of the film palace they faced the camera one last time for a few seconds, then walked into the hall side by side with the festival president.

Their backs disappeared into the shadow of the arched doorway.

The live feed switched back to the special reporter.

The reporter was doing a wrap-up broadcast facing the camera.

Kim Jae-kyung didn't listen.

She set the iPad on her knee and looked down at her own hands.

The clothes these hands made had just walked the Venice red carpet.

Kim Jae-kyung pulled the blanket up to her chin, buried her face in it, and laughed, muffled.

✿ ✿ ✿

The screening hall.

On screen, the final image froze on a long shot of Yeon-hui's younger brother interwoven with Sang-hun's figure.

Dusk pressed down slowly from the top of the frame.

The image faded.

Black screen.

Then the credits began to roll.

White Korean and English subtitles rose line by line from the bottom of the frame.

The house lights came up.

From the highest point of the dome, the light spread down layer by layer, like a sun rising from the screening hall's ceiling.

When the light reached the third tier.

The first clap came.

From the jury section in the front row.

Baek Si-on wasn't sure who had started it.

But the applause merged within five seconds, colliding with the dome of the screening hall, bouncing back, stacking with a fresh wave of applause. The air in the whole space vibrated.

Baek Jeong-hun sat motionless in his seat.

From the day he'd submitted to Venice to today, nearly three months.

From script development to wrap to post-production to submission, nearly two years.

From shooting his first short film to sitting in the main screening hall of the Venice Film Festival, listening to more than a thousand people applaud his film, nearly ten years.

Something gathered in his eyes.

It had been gathering for a long time.

From the moment the credits began to roll.

Now it finally couldn't be held back.

It slid down the corner of his right eye, turned past his cheekbone, and fell into the nasolabial fold at the corner of his mouth.

He didn't wipe it.

Just let it hang there.

The applause went on.

Tim Roth, in the jury section, stood up first.

Then Desplat.

Sandy Powell.

Philip Gröning.

Jessica Hausner.

One after another, they stood.

The entire jury rose to their feet.

That signal travelled faster than sound to the back rows.

Middle rows.

Back rows.

Last row.

More than a thousand people stood, clapping.

Baek Jeong-hun finally stood, wiped his face, wiped the teardrop into the hollow of his palm, turned to the audience, and bowed deeply.

When he straightened, Desplat had already come over from the jury seats.

He said something, the words swallowed by the applause, and Baek Jeong-hun didn't entirely catch it, but he read it from the man's expression.

Then Tim Roth.

The Englishman walked with the same loose gait as he did on screen, but the grip of his handshake wasn't loose. He said a short phrase of English, which Baek Jeong-hun equally didn't catch.

Didn't matter.

The applause was speaking for them.

✿ ✿ ✿

Baek Si-on stood beside Baek Jeong-hun.

The applause roared around him.

For a moment he went a little dizzy.

At noon, he'd seen a lot of reviews of this film.

Different words, but essentially all language.

Language is delayed. It's the thing that reaches your ears after thought, after selection, after translation, after the polish of phrasing.

Intangible.

But applause is not.

Applause is immediate. It's physical. It's recognition made concrete, measurable in decibels.

Baek Si-on stood in that vibration and let it come at him in waves.

His hands hung at his sides.

A few seconds later, he turned his head.

Choi Jin-ri stood to his right, hand over her chest, bowing again and again to the applauding audience all around.

When she turned toward his side, she straightened up.

Baek Si-on saw her face.

She was smiling.

Almost identical to the smile on the wrap photo.

But at the same time.

Two lines of tears slid down the corners of her eyes.

Those two things, by rights, should not have appeared together. But right now they were, illogically, mixed across her face.

Neither in front, neither behind; neither covering the other.

Tears of joy.

In the literal sense.

Baek Si-on thought for a few seconds.

Raised his right hand, pressed it against her cheekbone, and with the pad of his thumb traced the track of the tear, from below the corner of her eye all the way to her chin.

Choi Jin-ri's body went rigid.

The applause still roared around her, but suddenly she could hear nothing.

She could only feel the line on the left side of her cheek where his thumb had brushed, like a warm thread, top to bottom, slowly going cool.

Baek Si-on drew his hand back.

Didn't say anything.

Began bowing to the audience applauding them.

Choi Jin-ri stood frozen for two seconds, then raised her own left hand and brushed it lightly against her right cheek with the back of her hand.

Now both sides were symmetrical.

But they didn't feel the same.

✿ ✿ ✿

The night breeze outside the film palace scattered the residual heat the screening hall had carried with it.

The dispersing crowd was still excitedly discussing the raw drama they'd just seen.

Finecut's overseas distribution head, Lee Seung-cheol, came straight against the tide, two assistants in tow.

"Congratulations. The premiere was a great success. The standing ovation lasted about eight minutes. I timed it outside."

Baek Jeong-hun nodded, his eyes still a little red.

Lee Seung-cheol didn't linger on the subject.

"There's an arrangement next."

"We've booked a seaside restaurant on the Lido. Tonight we'll hold a targeted dinner to sell the rights. Distributors and rights buyers from regions around the world — about forty people invited."

He glanced at the three of them.

"The three of you need to be there."

Baek Jeong-hun rubbed his face, smoothing away the bit of sentimentality.

"When?"

"Starts at ten. The boat is already waiting at the pier. About ten minutes over."

Baek Si-on checked his watch.

Nine twenty.

"Don't rights sales happen in the market hall?"

Lee Seung-cheol nodded.

"Selling rights runs on two tracks. One is the public channel. During the festival there's a dedicated rights market — all the participating films' posters and promo material are hung on the booths, buyers browse on their own, and when they see something they like they sit down and talk. Volume, high efficiency, but limited bargaining room."

He paused.

"The other is the private channel. Like tonight."

"Invite the core buyers with the strongest intent into a private setting, and under the catalysis of top-grade wagyu, caviar and sea breeze, rely on the personal charisma you project on site to turn the reputation accumulated in that screening hall into cash, on the spot."

"The price negotiated in this kind of setting is usually twenty to thirty percent higher than in the market hall."

"…"

The three of them exchanged a look in the night breeze.

Baek Jeong-hun nodded:

"Let's go."
