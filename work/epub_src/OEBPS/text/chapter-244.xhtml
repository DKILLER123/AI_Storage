Chapter Two Hundred Forty-Four

The Recording Studio Doesn’t Believe in Pink

August 3 · after the credits

Twenty minutes of Q&A · one foreign hand raised

Megabox COEX · the screening hall

The end credits finished their roll.

When the house lights came back up to half, only then did someone in the seats begin to applaud.

The sound spread from the front rows to the back, gradually gathering into one sheet.

The host walked on stage.

Megabox COEX · post-screening

Thank you all for watching ‘Sado’ to the end.

Next we’ll hold a twenty-minute post-screening exchange. Any question about the film, the characters, or the shoot — direct it to the director and the cast.

Lee Joon-ik led Song Kang-ho, Baek Si-on, Jeon Hye-jin, and Moon Geun-young back onto the stage.

The first few questions came from the press seats and the general audience.

Post-screening Q&A · first round

What was the hardest part during filming?

What was it like acting opposite Song Kang-ho sunbae?

How did actor Moon Geun-young understand Lady Hyegyeong?

……

After a round or two of answers, Baek Si-on swept the audience from the stage, and his gaze stopped on one spot.

Alan looked up and met Baek Si-on’s inquiring eyes.

After a moment’s hesitation, he gave a small nod.

Only then did Baek Si-on say to the staffer beside him:

“Please take the microphone to that foreign audience member.”

Quite a few people in the hall turned to follow his line of sight.

The staffer jogged over and put the wireless microphone into Alan’s hands.

Alan stood up.

The Korean audience around him looked with curiosity at this young foreigner.

He began in English:

“Thank you all for a wonderful performance. The film’s emotion is incredibly contagious.”

He gathered his thoughts for a moment.

“I don’t know this period of Korean history, but I could feel the pain of a father and son torturing each other and walking toward ruin.”

“But I have one question I can’t understand logically.”

“In the second half, the crown prince Sado takes up a blade and storms the palace. In the Western history or power struggles I understand, at a moment like that, his closest wife and his own mother would normally join the family forces behind them to help him seize the throne.”

“Yet they did exactly the opposite.”

The question landed in the hall and stirred a light ripple.

Korean audiences understood this history, because they had known the ending for years from textbooks, unofficial histories, and popular culture.

That information was automatically filled in by Korean viewers into the blanks the film left out.

But Alan had none of that prior memory, and Baek Si-on had not let him read up on the history beforehand; he could only follow the causality presented on screen.

So the picture he saw was:

An heir long persecuted by his father finally takes up a weapon, only to be betrayed by the two women closest to him.

The host caught the gist, but when it came to Joseon-dynasty political concepts and family relations, she wasn’t confident she could translate accurately.

She was just about to look for an on-site interpreter.

Baek Si-on had already taken the mic and restated the whole question in Korean.

Lee Joon-ik listened, glanced at Song Kang-ho, then turned to Baek Si-on:

“You answer this one.”

Baek Si-on nodded and brought the mic closer:

“First, this question shows the film left part of the historical information incompletely explained.”

“Fitting decades of court history into two hours inevitably compresses other scattered threads.”

“According to Lady Hyegyeong Hong’s ‘Memoirs of 1805’ and parts of the court records, after Sado’s mental state worsened, he lived long in alternating fear, rage, and loss of control.”

“In his madness he killed quite a few attendants. The records differ on exact numbers, but the victims were clearly not in the single digits.”

“To keep the father-son relationship as the spine, the film condensed that ‘serving a ruler is like sleeping beside a tiger’ bloody daily life into one short shot — a maid killed because the clothes she prepared didn’t please him……”

The seating area where Red Velvet sat.

Baek Si-on and Alan had been exchanging in English all along, and the simultaneous interpretation ran a brief beat behind.

Son Seung-wan lowered her voice, translating in fragments for the members beside her:

“Sunbae says Sado had no real coup plan at the time.”

“And in the true history, his wife survived for many years in extreme fear, dreading every day.”

“Under those conditions, still risking death to stop Sado’s folly — you could call her a very clear-headed woman……”

Park Soo-young, listening, couldn’t help a small exclamation:

“Sunbae even understands his scene partner’s character that well.”

She paused, then added:

“No wonder he won the Venice Best Actor.”

Son Seung-wan finished the last fragment and nodded lightly.

She remembered the single-take of Baek Si-on’s gun scene, and she also remembered hearing only later from the crew that behind that sub-three-second shot was him staying alone in a parking lot training until deep night.

A trophy records only the final result.

The homework an actor does for a role off camera is not usually engraved on the trophy’s base.

Kang Seul-gi asked in a small voice:

“If it had been another actor, how would they answer a question like this?”

Park Soo-young thought it over seriously, then said:

“They might say: read the book a hundred times and its meaning reveals itself. Please watch it twice, three times, and you’ll understand the character more deeply.”

The few of them laughed at once.

Bae Joo-hyun also lifted a hand to hide the smile at her lips, but her mouth warned the four younger ones:

“Keep it down.”

Yet she herself felt Park Soo-young’s summary was very precise.

❧ ❧ ❧

The post-screening exchange ended.

The audience rose and filed out in turn.

The aisles were crowded; some discussed the rice-chest scene, some wiped their eyes, some bent over their phones typing first reactions.

The creative team stepped down from the stage.

Baek Si-on took off his suit jacket, draped it over the crook of his arm, and followed behind Lee Joon-ik.

The director’s pace was slower than usual.

Originally he had thought ‘Sado’ had its completeness, Song Kang-ho’s performance, and Baek Si-on as a calling card of Venice Best Actor plus the North American market; add overseas distribution and a sufficient PR budget, and an Oscar push had at least a seventy-or-eighty percent chance.

But Alan’s question tonight threw a bucket of cold water on that optimism.

Money could make Academy voters see the film, but it couldn’t explain decades of Joseon royal history for the film.

Lee Joon-ik said in a low voice:

“If even a young man willing to watch seriously needs this much explaining, what about the Academy voters who see it once?”

“Don’t be too pessimistic, either.”

Lee Joon-ik waited for Baek Si-on to go on.

“Maybe those Western voters actually enjoy this half-understood, mysterious, repressed Eastern court.”

“Do you believe that yourself?”

Baek Si-on weighed it seriously.

“Thirty percent.”

“Your comfort is far worse than your acting.”

“I still gave thirty percent.”

“……”

Lee Joon-ik gave a humorless laugh and didn’t bring up “a sure thing” again.

Forging iron ultimately requires the metal itself to be hard.

PR can buy an entry ticket, but it can’t buy the resonance after crossing a cultural wall.

❧ ❧ ❧

9:50 at night · outside the hall

One OST demo · one captain with a secret agenda

Megabox COEX · the lobby

The guests had mostly left; mall staff began striking the railings and the check-in supplies.

The five of Red Velvet, led by their manager, prepared to head back to the dorm.

Per the original plan: shower on return, then cross-check tomorrow’s schedule.

If the members gave up discussing why Bae Joo-hyun suddenly laughed, they could even sleep a little longer.

The odds of that were low.

Park Soo-young had already saved the question in her heart.

She intended to ask it once inside the dorm.

Behind them came a sudden hurry of footsteps.

“Wait a moment!”

The few turned at once.

Baek Eun-ah jogged over out of the not-yet-dispersed crowd.

She first nodded a greeting to Red Velvet’s manager, then let her gaze pass over the other four and land precisely on Son Seung-wan.

“Unnie, do you have time tonight?”

Son Seung-wan pointed at herself.

“Me?”

Four heads swiveled over in unison.

Baek Eun-ah didn’t keep them guessing:

“The demo for the ‘Descendants of the Sun’ OST is finished.”

“My cousin wants you to come record at the studio tonight.”

Son Seung-wan’s eyes went round at once.

“Tonight?”

“Mm.”

Baek Eun-ah added, precisely:

“It’s only a first-round trial vocal. Whether it’s adopted in the end depends on the recording result.”

“I can!”

Son Seung-wan quickly realized everyone was watching her and immediately lowered her volume.

“I’m free today.”

Park Soo-young and Kim Yerim exchanged a look.

The OST for Kim Eun-sook’s new drama, personally produced by Baek Si-on — and Son Seung-wan had quietly landed the first trial-vocal chance?

What situation was this?

The group’s hidden royalty had finally been exposed?

Son Seung-wan suddenly panicked.

She didn’t want the younger ones thinking she had secretly climbed the connection herself.

All the more so when the person who had truly taken her to the ‘The Three Evils’ set and fought for her audition chance stood right beside her.

“It was Joo-hyun unnie who won the audition chance for me.”

Bae Joo-hyun didn’t even have time to stop the sentence.

Three heads had already turned to her at once.

Park Soo-young was the first to lean in, hugging one of Bae Joo-hyun’s arms.

“Unnie~~~”

The voice was sweet to the point of ill intent.

“I’m your good little sister too~”

Kim Yerim immediately occupied the other side.

“I’m still the youngest in the group, the little sister who most needs looking after~”

Kang Seul-gi, having lost both sides, could only stand directly in front of Bae Joo-hyun and look up at her with an extra-sincere gaze:

“Unnie, next time there’s a food resource, can you win one for me too?”

Bae Joo-hyun: “……”

Why had Kang Seul-gi’s wish suddenly dropped from OST to eating?

Park Soo-young kept up the wheedling, swinging her arm:

“You can’t favor only Seung-wan unnie.”

Kim Yerim added:

“At the very least it should rotate by age order, right?”

Bae Joo-hyun’s head began to ache.

This matter had originally been known only to her, Son Seung-wan, and Baek Si-on.

Son Seung-wan’s mouth was indeed tight.

But this main vocal would always, at the least appropriate moments, suddenly display a moving quality of honesty.

Like now.

Bae Joo-hyun, expressionless, withdrew her arm and first tapped Park Soo-young lightly on the head.

“Back to the dorm.”

Then another tap on the right.

Kim Yerim covered her forehead.

“Why hit me?”

“By age order.”

Kim Yerim: “……”

This order seemed different from what she had imagined.

Bae Joo-hyun lifted her hand again and pushed the Kang Seul-gi blocking the front toward the manager.

“Everyone, move out.”

Being led toward the elevator by the manager, Kang Seul-gi still turned her head back.

“Then what about the food resource?”

“None.”

“Oh.”

The three members were shepherded to the elevator.

Before stepping in, Park Soo-young made a fighting-go gesture at Son Seung-wan with both hands.

Kim Yerim, beside her, mouthed: when you land the chance, remember to treat us.

The elevator doors closed.

Bae Joo-hyun straightened the cuffs the two younger ones had tugged crooked.

“Let’s go.”

Son Seung-wan was just about to follow, then suddenly reacted.

“Unnie is coming too?”

Bae Joo-hyun gave her a sidelong glance.

“If the captain’s company isn’t needed, I can go back to the dorm and sleep.”

On the surface: a considerate unnie, a good captain accompanying the overtime.

In Son Seung-wan’s ears it auto-completed into another translation — dare to make me miss tonight’s chance to see Baek Si-on, and you can start preparing your will tomorrow.

The will to live swiftly took over the body.

Son Seung-wan immediately looped her arm through Bae Joo-hyun’s.

“Of course it’s needed!”

“Unnie is the best~”

“I’d definitely be scared alone~”

Bae Joo-hyun was very satisfied with her quick wits.

❧ ❧ ❧

Past ten at night · Nonhyeon-dong

One pink dress in charge · three cameras felt, none seen

The studio · seventh floor

Twenty-some minutes later.

The car stopped at the door of a building in Nonhyeon-dong.

Night wind scraped in off Seoul’s streets.

Bae Joo-hyun and Son Seung-wan followed Baek Eun-ah into the lobby and rode the elevator to the seventh floor.

The studio door was ajar; Baek Eun-ah pushed it open.

Bae Joo-hyun and Son Seung-wan crossed the threshold and both froze in the doorway.

In the control room the lights were soft, every piece of equipment powered.

Baek Si-on sat at the very center of the console, confirming the ‘Everytime’ project file with Jung Jae-joon and Lee Ji-eun.

On the lounge sofa sat Choi Jin-ri; in a chair sat Alan, who had originally planned to go to a PC bang for Korean-server LOL.

Leaning against the wall by the door was Jeong Han-teuk, dragged along in the dead of night to work overtime with his own artist.

❧ ❧ ❧

The control room went quiet at the two of them arriving.

Baek Si-on rose from the console.

“Seung-wan.”

Hearing him call her name, Son Seung-wan immediately stood a little straighter.

“Ne.”

“First, my apologies.”

She froze.

“Why?”

Baek Si-on looked at the wall clock; it was close to ten-thirty at night.

“‘Sado’ has theater stage greetings coming up, and in a week I leave for America to prepare the next film.”

“From tonight until six tomorrow morning is my only complete free stretch in the near future.”

“So I called you in on short notice and asked you to fit my schedule.”

Son Seung-wan had thought Baek Si-on was going to say the audition arrangement had changed.

Hearing it through, she was only more at a loss.

A great sunbae with three Billboard chart-toppers, calling a rookie in late at night on short notice, personally explaining his schedule and seriously apologizing.

This treatment was beyond the coverage of any rookie survival handbook.

Son Seung-wan waved both hands, her body bending along with them.

“It’s nothing, sunbae!”

“Just getting the audition chance is my honor. Any time works.”

Baek Si-on raised a hand, signaling her to stop bowing.

Son Seung-wan straightened again, and only then noticed the obvious red threads pressed in Baek Si-on’s eyes.

Clearly this man had been working continuously for more than ten hours.

Then she instinctively added:

“Rather, sunbae, with a schedule this full, please take care of your health.”

The words landed, and Son Seung-wan only belatedly realized that this most ordinary junior’s pleasantry had, tonight, dropped into a not-so-ordinary room.

Nobody was speaking, yet she inexplicably felt three cameras trained on her back at once.

❧ ❧ ❧

“Wendy, right?”

The first to break the quiet was Lee Ji-eun.

Son Seung-wan turned at once and bowed:

“Ne, sunbae.”

“The female vocal on ‘Everytime’ is recorded by me.”

Lee Ji-eun picked up the lyric sheet and handed it over.

“Listen to the full version first. Familiarize yourself with the lyrics, the sections, and the male-female transitions.”

“The demo only marks the melody and the basic emotion. At the official recording, you don’t need to imitate my diction or phrasing.”

Son Seung-wan took it with both hands.

“Understood.”

“One more thing.”

Lee Ji-eun didn’t give her much time to relax.

“I’m quite demanding in the booth.”

“Pitch is only the baseline. Diction, breath, tail notes, the link to the male vocal — if any one of them hurts the emotion, I will stop you.”

“Time is limited today. I hope you get into state quickly.”

Son Seung-wan nodded seriously.

“I’ll do my best.”

Lee Ji-eun added:

“If you’re slow to get in, that’s fine too.”

“We can keep recording until six in the morning.”

Son Seung-wan: “……”

Was this encouragement, or a threat whose deadline happened to fall at dawn?

She had no spare capacity to judge.

She could only nod more seriously.

“I’ll get into state as fast as I can, sunbae.”

“Good.”

Lee Ji-eun reached out and pressed play.

Son Seung-wan quickly put on the headphones, sat in the empty seat at the console’s side, and began to listen.

♪

‘Everytime’ · Descendants of the Sun OST · working demo

Baek Si-on × Lee Ji-eun · prod. Baek Si-on

Oh everytime I see you——

❧ ❧ ❧

Alan hadn’t understood that stretch of Korean just now.

He had only noticed that after the woman in the pink dress took over control, the room’s atmosphere changed.

The female singer who had just come in was clearly much more tense.

He leaned sideways and asked Baek Eun-ah in a small voice, in English:

“What did she just say?”

Baek Eun-ah translated, voice lowered:

“She said she’s very strict when recording. If your state is bad, she can keep working until morning.”

Alan re-examined Lee Ji-eun.

Her styling today easily brought to mind the most exquisitely wrapped strawberry dessert in a cake-shop window.

“She doesn’t look it.”

Baek Eun-ah was silent for a beat.

“Don’t trust the styling.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Baek Si-on also flicked a glance at Lee Ji-eun.

A few hours ago, fans had been screaming over this very exterior.

He had known then: the sweet-little-sister mode was limited-hours service.

Lee Ji-eun sensed his attention and turned her face.

“Why are you looking at me?”

“Confirming business hours.”

She narrowed her eyes.

“What does that mean?”

“Nothing.”

Baek Si-on handed her the control, then turned to Bae Joo-hyun, who was still standing by the door on guard duty.

“You came?”

That little dissatisfaction in Bae Joo-hyun’s chest rose again.

She had been standing quite a while since following Baek Eun-ah in.

This person explained the schedule to Son Seung-wan first, then chatted recording with Lee Ji-eun, and only now did it finally come around to her.

The bad boy’s tally of offenses, accumulated all the way from the premiere, was still growing tonight.

But Bae Joo-hyun didn’t let the mood reach her face.

“Mm.”

“How was the film?”

“Good.”

She paused, swapping the offhand review for a more serious answer.

“Sunbae acted very well.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Thanks.”

The reply was concise enough to leave Bae Joo-hyun no room to unfold.

Just as the atmosphere was about to go cold, Choi Jin-ri beckoned from the sofa:

“Joo-hyun unnie.”

“Sit here.”

Bae Joo-hyun walked over and sat beside Choi Jin-ri.

Choi Jin-ri asked first:

“Unnie came with Seung-wan?”

“Mm.”

Bae Joo-hyun gestured toward the console.

“It’s Seung-wan’s first time at such an important OST audition. As captain, I should accompany her.”

She politely returned the question:

“And sunbae?”

“Me……”

Choi Jin-ri’s words stopped there.

The real answer was a little hard to say aloud.

After the premiere, hearing Baek Si-on was going to record ‘Everytime’, Lee Ji-eun had declared, in her capacity as producer, that she was coming to the studio to supervise the recording.

Choi Jin-ri rather didn’t want her boyfriend and Lee Ji-eun spending the night together.

So she gestured toward where Lee Ji-eun sat:

“I came with Ji-eun unnie.”

By the console, Lee Ji-eun’s hand turning the lyric sheet paused a beat.

Bae Joo-hyun followed Choi Jin-ri’s gaze over, a smile at her lips:

“You two sunbaes are so close.”

“Yes, indeed.”

Choi Jin-ri answered quite naturally.

“Ji-eun unnie even wrote a song for me~”

Lee Ji-eun: “……”

Tonight’s sisterhood seemed to operate on a compulsory-allocation system.

❧ ❧ ❧

Son Seung-wan still wore the headphones, familiarizing herself with the song.

Choi Jin-ri didn’t understand arrangement; the vocal tracks and waveforms on the screen were, to her, only slightly livelier than a hospital ECG.

After Bae Joo-hyun sat down, the two fell into natural conversation.

The topic drifted from the Girls’ Generation sunbaes’ comeback to makeup, skincare, and fashion.

The atmosphere looked quite friendly.

Yet whenever Baek Si-on’s voice came over the console, both of their attentions would briefly leave the talk.

One lifted her drink.

One smoothed her skirt.

Then they resumed exchanging recently used skincare products, as if nothing had happened.

The minute hand on the wall crossed half a circle.

Son Seung-wan took off the headphones.

“Learned it?” Lee Ji-eun asked.

“Mm.”

“A cappella from the first verse.”

Son Seung-wan stood and sang the female-lead verse per the lyrics.

First round over, Lee Ji-eun set a finger directly on the middle of the pre-chorus.

“Pick up from here.”

Son Seung-wan neither leaned on the intro nor re-confirmed the key; she entered the melody exactly.

Lee Ji-eun drew two more fragments at random, one of them landing right on the male-female handoff.

Son Seung-wan caught them all.

Lee Ji-eun finally lifted her chin a fraction.

“Go in.”

“Ne.”

Baek Si-on stood too.

The two walked into the vocal booth one after the other.

The soundproof door closed.

Jung Jae-joon shifted aside, yielding the controls.

He had been out of his body too long; having someone take over the recording flow was a late-night blessing.

Lee Ji-eun pushed the talkback key.

“Can you hear me?”

Son Seung-wan steadied her headphones.

“I can.”

Baek Si-on answered too:

“Yes.”

“Let’s run the whole thing first. No demand for a one-take.”

The instrumental began.

Son Seung-wan stood at the left mic.

Baek Si-on stood on the other side.

The ‘Everytime’ intro flowed through the headphones; Son Seung-wan drew a breath.

Opened her mouth.

“Oh everytime I see you——”

The talkback key pressed down.

“Stop.”

Lee Ji-eun’s cold voice came through the monitor speaker.

“What this spot wants is heart-flutter and anticipation. Don’t process every tail note into a technique showcase.”

“Pull it in a little.”

“Understood.”

A new track was created.

Second take began.

The first line had just passed.

“Stop.”

“The intake is too loud; the mic caught all of it.”

“Breathe off to the side. Not straight into the diaphragm.”

“Again.”

Third take; Lee Ji-eun stopped it again.

“Right now you’re just completing a task.”

Through the glass, Son Seung-wan’s expression had taken on a touch of blankness.

“Imagine you haven’t seen the person you like in a long time.”

“He appears from far away.”

“You haven’t decided whether to smile, and your voice is already glad.”

“Don’t put the emotion all into technique.”

“From the top.”

Son Seung-wan drew a deep breath.

Lee Ji-eun immediately warned:

“Breathe quieter.”

“……”

Son Seung-wan closed her eyes for a beat.

As a rookie, of course she dared not hold an opinion against the producer.

But this pink-dressed sunbae’s attack range seemed to include even the breathing before recording started.

And yet every problem was pointed out accurately.

She couldn’t find an entry point to argue.

She could only readjust her headphones.

“Ready.”

“Begin.”

❧ ❧ ❧

Alan couldn’t understand what Lee Ji-eun said each time she called stop.

But he could hear the same female vocal being re-recorded over and over, and from Son Seung-wan’s increasingly cautious movements he judged that the woman in the pink dress was applying continuous pressure.

He hesitated, then asked Baek Eun-ah in English:

“Are Korean pop singers this aggressive when recording?”

The expression on Baek Eun-ah’s face was a little complicated.

She knew why Lee Ji-eun was especially strict.

The professional demand was real.

The responsibility to the work was real.

As for whether a pinch of punishment for Son Seung-wan’s careless greeting at the door was mixed in — hard to grade.

Baek Eun-ah finally answered only:

“Tonight is a bit special.”

Alan asked:

“Because the song is important?”

Baek Eun-ah swept a glance around the control room.

“Because there are too many people.”

Alan still didn’t quite understand.

Baek Eun-ah added one line:

“Just remember one thing: roses all come with thorns.”

❝

The recording studio doesn’t believe in pink. A strawberry-wrapped producer, a Venice laureate on the other mic, and a captain who came “just to accompany” — the demo never stood a chance of being simple.
