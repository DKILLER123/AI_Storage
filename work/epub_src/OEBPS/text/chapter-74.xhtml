Chapter Seventy-Four

An Adriatic Fashion Warning

August 26 Afternoon Venice · Marco Polo Airport → Giudecca A salty wind, a silver watch, and a lecture on the red carpet

The two hours at CDG passed faster than expected.

Baek Eun-ah chewed a baguette sandwich in the waiting area. It was bad, but she insisted on finishing it, on the reasoning that "this is my first French meal."

Baek Si-on couldn't be bothered to correct her that a baguette sandwich didn't count as French cuisine.

Park Ji-hun drank a four-euro espresso, the cup barely half a thumb in height. He stared at the bottom of the cup for ten seconds, apparently pondering whether he'd been conned.

At 1:40 p.m. they boarded.

Flight time, one hour thirty-five minutes.

Short enough that Baek Si-on's copy of The Venice Travel Guide had only been turned a few pages before the plane began to descend.

August 26 Afternoon Venice · Marco Polo Airport → Giudecca A mercantile van, and a briefing on what to save the handmade gowns for

Marco Polo Airport.

The terminal was small — a few sizes smaller than Incheon.

But the air was different.

Not the city-worn wind of Seoul, filtered through tall buildings and asphalt, but a humid, warm wind blowing in off the Adriatic.

As Baek Eun-ah walked out dragging her suitcase, she breathed in deep.

"So salty."

Baek Si-on didn't comment on the taste of the air.

His gaze had already landed on the two people standing at the exit.

Baek Jeong-hun.

Beside him stood an Asian man in a suit. Hair combed immaculately, shoes polished to a shine, a simple silver watch on his left wrist.

Baek Jeong-hun saw Baek Si-on first.

Raised a hand.

Baek Si-on walked over.

"Uncle."

"Good, you made it."

Then Baek Jeong-hun turned his head to look at Choi Jin-ri.

Choi Jin-ri stepped forward and gave a slight bow.

"Director, it's been a while."

"You've worked hard, Jin-ri."

The tone was two degrees warmer than it had been for Baek Si-on. After all, she was the leading lady of his film.

After ruffling Baek Eun-ah's head, Baek Jeong-hun turned toward the suited man and gestured.

"Let me introduce you. This is Lee Seung-cheol, the head of the overseas distribution department at Finecut, the international film-sales agency. Every external matter for The Green Bottle Fly here in Venice, he's in charge of."

Lee Seung-cheol stepped half a pace forward.

Gave a slight nod.

"Everyone, welcome to Venice."

His Korean carried an English lilt — perhaps a trace of having lived abroad a long time.

No extended pleasantries.

He lifted his left wrist and checked the time on the dial.

"The car's waiting outside. Everyone, bring your bags and get on. It's about forty minutes from the airport to the Cipriani Hotel. We'll go over tomorrow's opening-ceremony schedule on the way."

He had already turned and was walking toward the parking area.

Out of the terminal, the scene was a parking lot bleached white by the afternoon sun.

The sky was very blue.

Blue in a way that didn't feel real.

Baek Eun-ah looked up and let out a "wow," then caught a suitcase wheel on a gap in the stone paving and stumbled.

A Mercedes van sat at the curb.

The driver was a middle-aged Italian man, laughing as he helped stuff the luggage into the back.

The van pulled out, off the parking lot, taking a road lined with low pines. The skyline far ahead sat low, and a faint glint of water could be seen.

Lee Seung-cheol turned half his body from the front seat, facing the middle and rear rows.

"Roughly, here's the opening-ceremony schedule."

"Between nine and eleven in the morning, PR teams from the big luxury houses will come to your hotel suite with their garments and jewellery for you to choose from."

At the phrase "the houses come to you," Baek Si-on's brow gave a very slight twitch.

Lee Seung-cheol went on.

"The Venice red carpet is different from a Korean awards show."

"At home you wear whatever you've prepared, no one cares. But in Venice, actors in the competition section become objects the luxury houses fight over."

"Houses like Armani, Valentino, Dolce & Gabbana will contact the festival's PR coordination team and ask to send clothes to the actor's hotel room for you to pick."

He paused.

"Free. Wear it and return it after. The house wants you walking their designs on the carpet, photographed by the whole world's press. You get a couture gown without spending a cent. Everyone takes what they need."

From the back seat, Baek Eun-ah raised her hand.

"Um, Manager Lee."

Lee Seung-cheol looked over.

"We made our own gowns. Brought them from Korea."

Her tone carried a small pride, the "we came prepared" kind.

"How many?"

"One set."

Baek Eun-ah leaned partway out of the back row to add.

"One men's and one women's. We had them custom-made by a designer we commissioned."

Lee Seung-cheol smiled slightly.

"Bringing your own gowns — very good. It shows you respect the occasion. But I'd suggest you save the ones you made for the most important event."

Baek Eun-ah's mouth opened.

"The most important event? Isn't the opening ceremony—"

"No."

Lee Seung-cheol shook his head.

"The Venice red carpet isn't just once. You walk it once for the opening, once for your own film's premiere, and once more for the closing."

"Every walk, the fashion press worldwide will shoot it and publish it to their websites and magazines. If you wear the same outfit all three times—"

He didn't finish the sentence.

No need to finish.

The next day, the global fashion media would grab that very photo and spin it.

You'd be a laughingstock abroad.

"So I'd suggest you keep the gowns you made for The Green Bottle Fly's premiere."

Baek Eun-ah slowly lowered the hand she'd raised.

She turned to look at Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on leaned back in the seat, gazing out the window at the Italian country road receding behind.

"Listen to him."

Lee Seung-cheol nodded and went on with the schedule.

"The afternoon is free. You can rest, or take a walk on Giudecca. But I'd advise against the main island — the ferry back and forth eats too much time, and you won't make the evening schedule."

"Six in the evening, after the red carpet you'll go into the Palazzo del Cinema hall for the opening ceremony."

"After the opening ceremony, there's a beach dinner. Held on the private beach of the Excelsior Hotel. Directors, producers, and distributors from all over will be there. It's a social event — meet whoever you can."

Lee Seung-cheol paused, his gaze sweeping the inside of the van.

"That's the gist of it. Everyone can get a good night's rest at the hotel tonight and shake off the jet lag."

Silence a few seconds in the van.

Baek Si-on gave an "mm," and went back to looking out the window.

Baek Eun-ah, meanwhile, looked down and re-checked her rushed memo from start to finish twice, confirming it matched what Lee Seung-cheol had just said word for word, then pressed save.

As for Choi Jin-ri.

She, too, kept her head down.

Phone in hand.

Screen brightness turned down low, the angle tilted toward herself, just out of sight of the rear seat where Baek Eun-ah sat behind.

No memo on the screen. No schedule.

But a single photo.

Taken four hours earlier, on the stone path outside Paris CDG.
