Chapter One Hundred Thirty Three

Good Night, Peach

In the end, the cake was cut.

Nobody, of course, actually had the heart to sit down and eat it slowly. Baek Si-on took one small piece. Choi Jin-ri took one small piece. The staff split the rest among themselves.

Baek Eun-ah glanced at the time, then finally walked over and said, in a low voice:

“Oppa. We should go.”

Baek Si-on looked down at his watch.

Nine-seventeen in the evening.

From Seoul to Jeonju was roughly a three-hour drive. When he got there he still had to sit down with the director and the assistant director over the shot breakdown for tomorrow’s scenes. Add washing up, line memorisation, and song practice on top of that, and by any honest accounting he would be lucky to sleep four hours tonight.

Choi Jin-ri heard it, and spoke up first:

“Go on.”

Her voice was very light.

Light enough that she was clearly afraid that speaking any heavier would make Baek Si-on think she didn’t want him to leave.

Even though she really didn’t want him to leave.

At that, Baek Si-on turned and looked at her.

Under his gaze, Choi Jin-ri’s fingertips curled, once, very slightly.

Then he did something nobody had expected.

Baek Si-on turned and walked toward Baek Jeong-hun.

“Uncle.”

Baek Jeong-hun was standing nearby talking to the Showbox people. At the word, his movement paused — barely perceptibly.

The truth was, uncle and nephew had not spoken properly in a good while.

Ever since the Busan film-festival business, Baek Si-on had kept him at a careful distance, and Baek Jeong-hun knew exactly where the problem lay. The expression-drive of the left-leaning filmmakers, the political posture at festivals — Baek Jeong-hun believed he had his reasons for all of it.

But Baek Si-on’s wariness back then had not been without cause, either.

They were both adults.

And cracks between adults, sometimes, do not argue very loudly.

They simply go quiet.

Which was why Baek Jeong-hun’s willingness to take part in tonight’s little celebration had, in itself, carried a meaning of breaking the ice. However you looked at it, they were family.

But he had not expected Baek Si-on to walk over so naturally, as if nothing had ever happened, and hold out his hand.

“Car keys.”

Baek Jeong-hun:

“……”

He looked at Baek Si-on.

Baek Si-on looked back.

His expression was flat. His tone was flat. Flat in a way that suggested there had never been a stretch of cooled silence between them at all.

Baek Jeong-hun suddenly felt like laughing.

This kid.

If you claimed he held grudges — here he was, asking for the car keys with total entitlement.

If you claimed he didn’t — he hadn’t made a single proactive phone call in all this time.

Baek Jeong-hun fished the keys out of his pocket and slapped them into Baek Si-on’s palm.

“Drive slow.”

Even as he said it, Baek Jeong-hun felt it sounded like useless words.

But that was what elders did. They said the thing even when they knew the other person would not listen.

Especially since, not long ago, Baek Si-on had been woken up by a police officer tapping on his window after pulling over from sheer exhaustion. There was not a single member of the Baek family who didn’t know about that one.

Baek Si-on took the keys.

“Mm.”

“Don’t ‘mm’ me. Actually drive slow.”

“Got it.”

Baek Jeong-hun was grudgingly satisfied.

Baek Si-on turned and walked back over to Choi Jin-ri.

“You have no schedule left?”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

“No.”

Her premiere-related obligations for the night were done. What remained was only going home.

“I’ll take you back.”

“Huh?”

Choi Jin-ri knew, of course, how tired he was today.

Incheon. Gyeonggijeon in Jeonju. The Green Bottle Fly premiere. And then this small celebration cake.

Every item on that list was a major event that had been forcibly crammed into a single day.

He had more than enough reason to get straight into the car and go.

And yet he chose to drive her.

And so she chose to ride.

Some things, after all, did not need to be pulled apart and explained thread by thread.

It was just that simple.

“Then… sorry for the trouble.”

Baek Eun-ah immediately shoved the paper bag — the one Baek Si-on had handed her earlier — back into his hands.

“The gifts.”

Baek Si-on took it.

Baek Eun-ah beamed at him.

“Giving them in person carries more sincerity.”

The same night 9:25 p.m. Seoul · the underground car park, level B2 A car borrowed from an uncle, a passenger seat borrowed from a peach.

Baek Jeong-hun’s car was parked in a corner of level B2.

Baek Si-on opened the passenger door.

Choi Jin-ri ducked her head and climbed in.

Seat belt.

Baek Si-on walked around to the driver’s side, sat down, and set the paper bag on the centre console between them.

Ignition.

The engine note hummed through the concrete box of the underground car park.

Choi Jin-ri’s eyes settled on the paper bag.

“What is this?”

Baek Si-on turned the wheel and pulled the car out of its space.

“Sand.”

Choi Jin-ri:

“……”

She looked at him.

In the dim light of the car park, Baek Si-on’s profile looked extremely calm. Calm enough that she genuinely could not tell whether he was joking or had actually brought her a bag of sand back from Dubai.

Given Baek Si-on’s range of behaviour, both were entirely possible.

Choi Jin-ri opened the bag.

And looked inside.

There was, of course, no sand.

Two boxes of neatly packaged camel-milk chocolate. A box of dates. And two small tins of saffron.

Choi Jin-ri picked up the camel-milk chocolate and turned it over to read the ingredients on the back.

“Camel milk?”

“Yes.”

“Actually from a camel?”

“Yes.”

“Is it good?”

“I haven’t tried it.”

Choi Jin-ri went “oh,” unwrapped one piece, and put it in her mouth.

The chocolate melted across her tongue. The milkiness sat a little heavier than ordinary milk chocolate, with a strange, fresh quality that was not at all cloying.

Her eyes brightened slightly.

“It’s good~”

Then she unwrapped a second piece.

The car was easing out of the underground space.

She held the chocolate out to Baek Si-on’s mouth.

“Taste it.”

Baek Si-on kept his eyes on the traffic ahead.

“Choi Jin-ri.”

“Yes?”

“I’m driving.”

Choi Jin-ri’s hand stopped mid-air.

“Ah…”

She pulled it back.

“Sorry…”

But the apology was not the least bit sincere. She even snuck a wrinkle of her nose at him, then popped the chocolate into her own mouth.

Baek Si-on saw it in his peripheral vision.

Said nothing.

Night pressed in through the windscreen. Seoul’s streetlights fell back one by one.

Choi Jin-ri put the chocolate back in the bag and picked up the dates instead.

“I know what these are.”

“Mm.”

“I saw them at the airport duty-free before.”

She gave the box a little shake.

“These are really sweet, right?”

“Probably.”

“How have you not tried any of this?”

“I bought them to give away.”

Choi Jin-ri looked down at the bag, and the corner of her mouth lifted before she could stop it.

She worked to press it back down.

Then she spotted the tin of saffron at the very bottom.

She picked it up and peered through the little glass jar at the fine red threads inside.

“Is the saffron for me too?”

“Yes.”

“Do I look like I need to supplement my health?”

During the pause at a red light, Baek Si-on turned his head. His gaze travelled once over her face, appraising.

Then he nodded very seriously.

“Yes.”

Choi Jin-ri:

“……”

She took a deep breath.

“Baek Si-on-ssi.”

“Yes?”

“Do you know what a normal person is supposed to say at a moment like this?”

“No.”

“They’re supposed to say: it’s not that you look like you need the supplement — it’s that I thought this thing was precious, so I wanted to give it to you.”

Baek Si-on thought about it.

“Like that?”

“Yes.”

“Then that’s not it.”

Choi Jin-ri:

“……”

Fine.

She should never have expected anything.

The car ran all the way up into Seongbuk-gu.

Compared to Gangnam or Hongdae, the district was far quieter at night. The shadows of the roadside trees ran deep; the windows of the apartment blocks glowed a few lights at a time, like warmth that someone had left on, cell by cell.

Baek Si-on pulled into the temporary parking spot beneath the building where she rented.

Stop.

Handbrake.

Once the engine died, the silence inside the car suddenly became a little too complete.

Choi Jin-ri looked down and unbuckled her seat belt.

“Thank you for driving me back.”

Baek Si-on took his hands off the wheel.

“And thank you for the cake.”

Choi Jin-ri’s movements stopped.

“The cake?”

“Yes. When I was in America, no one lit candles for me.”

If Scooter Braun had heard that sentence, he would probably have launched himself out of his chair, pointed a finger at Baek Si-on’s nose, and said:

“No one lit candles for you? I negotiated you a ten-million-dollar Under Armour deal! I’m the candle! The most expensive kind!”

But Scooter Braun was not there.

Choi Jin-ri was.

And what Choi Jin-ri felt, at that moment, was glad.

Because she had always believed that, in Baek Si-on’s world, there was nothing she could give him. He did not need comforting. He did not need anyone to block the wind for him.

But tonight he had said, “when I was in America, no one lit candles for me.”

Which meant that the cake she had bought was meaningful to him.

She was not someone with nothing to give him.

She had given.

At least at this moment — she had given.

“Then next time I’ll prepare a bigger one.”

“What?”

“The cake.”

Choi Jin-ri’s eyes curved at him.

“Next time you win another number one, I’ll prepare an even bigger one.”

“No need.”

Choi Jin-ri’s happiness, which had been about to bubble right out of her, was pricked by those two words.

Pop.

The bubble broke.

Her fingers tightened around the paper bag of camel-milk chocolate, dates, and saffron, and her voice came out a little smaller.

“……Why?”

No sooner was it out than she felt the question had given too much away.

So she hurried to add:

“I mean… cakes aren’t even that expensive.”

“Afraid you’d go broke.”

Choi Jin-ri:

“……”

Broke.

Broke from buying cakes?!

Exactly how many number ones did this man think he was going to win?!

Honestly.

Not one bit modest.

And yet she had to admit — that confidence of his came with receipts.

“Then I’ll give you something else instead.”

“What?”

“Not telling you.”

Choi Jin-ri unbuckled, gathered her bag and the paper bag, pushed the door open, and got out.

The night wind carried a little chill.

Choi Jin-ri pulled her coat up onto her shoulders, stood beside the car, bent down, and looked in at Baek Si-on in the driver’s seat.

“You should go too.”

Baek Si-on did not move.

He only rolled down the window.

“You go up first.”

Choi Jin-ri blinked.

“You go first.”

“You go up.”

“Aren’t you still driving to Jeonju?”

“Yes.”

“Then hurry up and go.”

“You go up first.”

They stared at each other across the window.

One second.

Two seconds.

Three seconds.

Choi Jin-ri had meant to hold her ground. After all, letting him leave sooner was the considerate thing.

But Baek Si-on’s gaze was far too steady.

Steady enough that he could have sat in this car until she walked out of that door tomorrow morning.

After ten seconds, Choi Jin-ri conceded.

She let out a soft sigh.

“Fine.”

Baek Si-on nodded.

“Mm.”

Clutching the paper bag and her purse, Choi Jin-ri turned and walked toward the building entrance.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

At the door, she took out her access card, swiped it.

The door opened.

She turned sideways and looked back at the car waiting by the roadside.

The window was still down.

Between the streetlight and the faint glow of the dashboard, half of Baek Si-on’s profile was hard to read.

But she knew he was watching her.

Choi Jin-ri raised her hand and waved once. Only then did she carry the paper bag inside.

The glass door slid shut behind her.

Baek Si-on watched her figure disappear around the corner of the lobby.

Until one of the lights in the stairwell came on.

Only then did he roll up the window.

Restart the engine.

In the rear-view mirror, the apartment building grew smaller and smaller. The light that had just come on one floor up turned into a single point, then vanished behind a turn.

The Seoul-to-Jeonju expressway still stretched three-plus hours ahead.

Before he left the city, Baek Si-on turned on the car stereo.

Shuffle play.

The first song that jumped out made him glance at the screen.

“Way Back Home.”

He didn’t skip it.

Under his own song, he merged into the night expressway.

The same night Just past 10:00 p.m. Seoul · Seongbuk-gu · Choi Jin-ri’s flat Three gifts on a coffee table, and one video call from Japan.

By the time Choi Jin-ri got home it was past ten.

She changed into slippers and set the paper bag on the coffee table in the living room.

First she took out the camel-milk chocolate.

Then the dates.

And finally the tin of saffron.

She held the little glass jar up to the light; the red threads swayed gently inside.

“Steep it in water…”

She murmured it to herself once.

Then couldn’t help laughing.

Honestly.

Who, upon receiving saffron, has their first thought be “steep it in water”?

Oh.

Baek Si-on would.

She was about to head to the bathroom to take off her makeup when her phone suddenly vibrated.

A KakaoTalk video call.

Goo Hara.

Choi Jin-ri looked at the name on the screen, and something in her chest softened.

She answered.

The picture wobbled once. Then Goo Hara’s face filled the frame — and behind her, through the small window of the video call, a hotel room in Japan: a white curtain, a suitcase, and a table of skincare products not yet put away.

KakaoTalk · video call · Goo Hara

Japan · a tour hotel · hair freshly tied back, forehead bared for an interrogation

“Ya. You’re home?”

“Yes, just got in.”

Choi Jin-ri propped the phone against a cushion on the sofa and sat down on the floor herself.

“I saw the news.”

“What news?”

“A certain Miss Sulli, standing up for Baek Si-on at the press conference. Very coolly, I might add.”

“Eonni, are you on 2G? That was days ago.”

Goo Hara pouted.

“It’s not like I’m free every day! This tour leg has my schedule packed. I finally found time to binge-read the whole Korean internet.”

“Eonni, are you tired? Tours are gruelling.”

“It’s fine.”

She re-tied her hair, baring her clean forehead.

“The Japanese fans are warm — every show’s been full. Honestly, more fulfilling than music-show promotions back home.”

“Then when is eonni coming back?”

“Why? You miss me?”

“I do.”

“How much?”

“So, so much.”

“More than your quota for Baek Si-on?”

“……”

The air went still. Goo Hara lunged at the screen.

“Ya! Choi Jin-ri!”

Choi Jin-ri’s gaze drifted, then she burst out laughing.

“A tiny bit more. I’m joking.”

“Joking is not allowed either.”

Goo Hara put on a deeply wounded face.

“Then… two tiny bits more?”

“……Get lost.”

The two of them dissolved into laughter across the screens.

“Oh right, eonni. Baek Si-on brought me a gift back from Dubai.”

“Ooh, ooh — show me.”

Choi Jin-ri flipped the camera toward the coffee table — the camel-milk chocolate, the dates, and the little tin of saffron set out in a row.

“Is the camel-milk chocolate any good?”

“It’s pretty good. Rich milk flavour, not cloying. I’m saving it so eonni can have some when she gets back.”

“Good girl.”

Goo Hara was satisfied — then her gaze shifted again.

“What’s the red stuff?”

“Saffron.”

“Why would he give you something like that?”

“No idea…”

Choi Jin-ri genuinely had no idea.

If she had to guess — Baek Si-on had probably seen the saffron in a Dubai duty-free or a local speciality shop, decided it was “precious,” “local,” “giftable,” and bought it.

Whether the person who received it would find it romantic, whether a girl’s heart would feel properly tended to — that sort of question had never once entered his purchase-decision chain.

Goo Hara, for her part, let the strange gift go. Her voice dropped half a tone, and the call turned.

KakaoTalk · video call · Goo Hara · still connected

The same hotel room in Japan · the mood shifts one notch softer

“Jin-ri.”

“I’m here.”

“You really were brave at that press conference.”

Choi Jin-ri’s fingers curled slightly on her knee. She knew her eonni meant the moment at the Green Bottle Fly media preview — the sharp question, the legs gone weak under the table, and the sentence she had said anyway.

“Eonni. I’ll get better.”

“You were already good.”

Choi Jin-ri lowered her head and hummed — not a perfunctory mm, but the obedient kind.

“Alright. Go remove your makeup, shower, sleep. Don’t sit there grinning at chocolate until past midnight.”

“I’m not grinning.”

“You will be.”

“……”

Goo Hara waved at the camera.

“Good night, Peach.”

“Good night, eonni. Break a leg on the tour.”

“Love you.”

“Love you.”

The call ended the way their calls always ended — “love you” doubled on both sides until someone had to go first.

Choi Jin-ri put the phone down and sat in the quiet for a while.

Those two words, coming out of Goo Hara’s mouth, had always weighed differently than the same words from anyone else. And tonight, on a hotel-room screen somewhere in Japan, her eonni had told her she was already good.

She looked at the little glass jar of saffron catching the light on the coffee table, stood up, and went to wash her face.
