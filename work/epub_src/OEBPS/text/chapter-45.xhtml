Chapter Forty-Five

Trading Jessica for a Clean Slate

July 23 Noon Dispatch HQ · Nonhyeon-dong, Gangnam The seventh floor, and the whiteboard

Dispatch's headquarters sat on the seventh floor of an office building in Nonhyeon-dong, Gangnam.

Baek Si-on arrived just after noon.

He pushed the door open. The whole open-plan floor held maybe a dozen desks, seven or eight people, takeout boxes and folders piled on them. A printer in the corner was spitting out paper.

The wall, though — the wall was worth something.

Dispatch · investigation board · nonhyeon-dong

Artifacts licence-plate numbers · beauty-salon cards · celebrity photographs
Centre a relationship chart in red marker, arrows crossing like a metro map

The wall that every idol in Korea knew to fear.

“Mr. Baek?”

The desk by the window. A round-faced man in his forties stood. Silver-framed glasses, two buttons of his shirt undone, twirling a pen.

Im Geun-ho.

Dispatch's editor-in-chief.

He looked remarkably like a high-school homeroom teacher.

They shook hands.

“Director Bae filled me in on the gist. You brought it?”

Si-on took the USB stick from his pocket and handed it over.

Im Geun-ho took it, plugged it into the computer, and double-clicked.

The video opened.

The masks had been applied carefully. The boss's face was a block of flesh-toned pixels, the company sign and the street signs unreadable smears. But the actions and the dialogue were intact.

When it ended, Im Geun-ho leaned back in the chair.

“You're granting us exclusive use of this footage?”

“Of course.”

Im Geun-ho smiled and extended his hand toward Si-on.

“Rest assured. We'll clear your name.”

It was said grandly.

Si-on was in no danger of being moved to tearful gratitude.

Each side took what it needed.

“Then I'll leave it to you, Director Im.”

After the handshake, Si-on bent down, picked up the duffel bag near his feet, and set it on the desk in front of Im Geun-ho.

He pulled the zipper open.

Inside, stacked stacks of won, the bank seals still on them.

“A small token, to cover Director Im's trouble.”

Im Geun-ho's eyes travelled between the duffel and Si-on's face, once, and his expression turned strange.

“I won't take the money.”

He zipped the bag shut and slid it back toward Si-on.

“But I'll do the job.”

Si-on was caught off guard.

Not take money?

This was the second time someone had turned down his payment to get a thing done. The first had been the owners of those YouTube channels. They'd refused, because the "taste-maker" image couldn't afford a crack.

Dispatch then?

Si-on understood.

The same logic.

Dispatch didn't take PR money. That was the foundation it was built on. The day it accepted a fee like this, everything it published stopped being investigative reporting and became something else. A bespoke advertisement.

“My apologies. That was careless of me.”

Im Geun-ho waved the hand in a dismissive, unbothered gesture.

Si-on drew the duffel back to his feet and straightened, sitting up.

“I don't like owing people a favour. Since you won't take the money, then I'll trade you for a piece of information of equivalent value.”

Im Geun-ho's hand, twirling the pen, stopped.

“Go on.”

“Super Junior's Lee Sung-min is in secret preparations for a wedding. You can go look at the booking records of wedding-hotel venues in Gangnam-gun, focus on the December dates.”

Im Geun-ho listened, said nothing, and used the pen to point at the whiteboard behind him.

Si-on turned and followed the tip of the pen.

Dispatch · board, bottom-right

Subject Lee Sung-min · photo pinned
Note Yeoksam-dong, Gangnam-gun · a certain venue · December · confirmed.

“……”

Si-on stared at the line for two seconds.

Well now.

This was Dispatch, all right.

He cleared his throat.

He managed to hold his expression together, but a little heat still pricked at his face.

“There's one more.”

Im Geun-ho spun the pen once between his fingers and leaned back.

“Go on.”

The voice was exactly as polite as before.

“Look into Girls' Generation's Jessica.”

Im Geun-ho's eyebrow moved.

“Not her dating life. Look into the 'Blanc & Eclare' brand she registered in Hong Kong with Kwon Young-il. Focus on the money trail.”

Si-on held Im Geun-ho's eyes.

“She wants the brand and the group both. But SM and the other eight members have reached their limit. The internal conflict is far worse than anyone outside can see.”

“Within two months, Girls' Generation is going to split.”

Im Geun-ho's pen stopped turning.

“Girls' Generation.”

He repeated the four characters.

Si-on nodded.

“Split.”

Si-on nodded again.

Im Geun-ho looked at him for about five seconds.

“Is it on the level?”

“Once you look into it, you'll know.”

With that, Si-on gave a slight bow in his direction, picked up the duffel at his feet, and turned toward the door.

“Then I'll leave it to you, Director Im. Here's to a good working relationship.”

He pushed through the glass door and into the corridor.

The elevator doors closed.

Si-on leaned back against the elevator wall, looked down at the duffel at his feet.

Zipped. Bulging. Heavy.

The bank probably hadn't closed yet.

Take it back. Put it away.

July 23 5:00 p.m. Yeonnam-dong, the walk home A figure at the door

Just past five in the afternoon.

The end-of-July sun came slanting in from the west and stretched the shadows of the ginkgo trees in the Yeonnam-dong alley crooked. Si-on got out of the car and turned into the entrance of his building.

Upstairs.

Just as he reached his own door, he stopped.

Someone was standing in front of it.

A black baseball cap pulled low over the eyebrows, a mask up to the highest point of the nose, sunglasses covering every bit of the rest of the face.

In late July in Seoul, wearing this getup, they looked like they were about to commit a robbery.

One hand hovered in front of the door. First half-raised, then lowered. Then raised again. Lowered again.

The other hand carried a plastic bag.

They'd completely failed to notice the person coming up behind them.

“Ahem.”

The head spun around.

The body lurched a little, the bag catching against the knee, something inside knocking against it with a low thud.

Behind the glasses, the eyes met Si-on's face, and the whole person went rigid for about a second and a half.

Even through the mask, the cap, and the sunglasses, he was very sure.

Choi Jin-ri.

Why she'd come, he didn't need to ask. Maybe she'd seen what was online. And then she'd come anyway, at the risk of being photographed by the press.

“I……”

Jin-ri's voice came out muffled from behind the mask. All the lines she'd rehearsed in the doorway had deserted her.

Si-on glanced down at the bag in her hand. He could just make out the contents — two square cardboard boxes, a bottle of something, a small package he couldn't identify.

He had a guess.

In Jin-ri's world, food was the thing that healed Baek Si-on.

So she'd bought some things to eat. She'd come to see him.

The same way he'd gone to see her.

Si-on took his eyes off the bag and brought them up to her sunglasses.

“Did you bring any pork cutlet?”

“Huh?”

“Pork cutlet. Do you have any?”

Jin-ri looked down and rummaged through the bag.

“Ah…… yes.”

She drew out a white square box — the convenience-store pork-cutlet bento, the kind sealed in plastic with a little packet of sauce.

Si-on nodded, fished the keys out of his pocket, stepped around her, put the key in the lock, and turned it.

“Alright. Come in and sit down.”

Jin-ri stood there a moment, watching his back go through the doorway.

The same as always.

Even a little more at ease than usual.

The hot search had cursed him all day. A petition page was calling for him to be blacklisted. The fandoms of the whole country had united to nail him to the post of shame.

And he'd asked her whether she'd brought pork cutlet.

Jin-ri switched the bag to her other hand and followed him in.
