Chapter Eighty-Four

Pyjamas, Bare Face, and the Woman in the Next Seat

September 7 evening Venice Marco Polo Airport A small airport, a cup in a hoodie, and a line that did not want to leave

Evening.

Venice Marco Polo Airport.

Coming in, this airport’s first impression on Baek Si-on had been “several grades smaller than Incheon.”

Leaving, the impression had not changed.

Still small.

But this time the smallness made you a little unwilling to go.

The party queued at the check-in counters.

Baek Jeong-hun had the Lion of the Future’s carrying case in his left hand, passport and boarding pass clenched in his right, the whole of him still carrying the wilt of a hangover, his face a curious grey-green.

Baek Eun-ah pushed a luggage cart stacked with four people’s cases, pushing it crooked.

Choi Jin-ri and the SM manager brought up the rear.

The Volpi Cup was wrapped in Baek Si-on’s own black hoodie and stuffed in the very bottom of his carry-on.

No time to buy a dedicated shock case.

The hoodie would do.

✿ ✿ ✿

Air France AF1527.

Marco Polo to Charles de Gaulle.

One hour thirty-five minutes.

The road they had come by, walked backward once.

September 7 the connection Paris Charles de Gaulle · Terminal 2E A bowl of miso ramen, and a duty-free run that would not admit it had lost money

Paris Charles de Gaulle.

Connection.

Holding.

Last time Baek Eun-ah had come through here she had gnawed a baguette sandwich. This time she went straight to a Japanese place in the terminal and ordered a bowl of miso ramen.

Baek Si-on did not go to eat.

He went to duty-free.

Terminal 2E’s duty-free had every mark it was supposed to have.

Duty-free · for Yoon Hye-ja · paid in euros

01 — La Mer cream, one jar. TAKE

02 — Chanel No. 5, one bottle. TAKE

03 — SK-II Facial Treatment Essence, two bottles. TAKE

04 — Sulwhasoo ginseng essence set, one. TAKE

05 — Hermès scarf, one. TAKE

Everything his mother could use, Baek Si-on bought through once.

Coming out of duty-free with the bags, Baek Eun-ah was standing by a pillar at the exit, waiting for him.

She scanned the logos on the bags, then looked up at Baek Si-on.

“Cousin, you really are…”

“Don’t say ‘great filial son.’”

“Great sucker.”

“…”

“Do you know how much cheaper this stuff is in the Korean duty-frees than here? And if you swipe a Korean card there are extra points. You bought at euro list at de Gaulle, converted to won plus the exchange float, that’s at least three or four hundred thousand extra, for nothing.”

Baek Si-on stood in the flow of Terminal 2E and was silent for two seconds.

Think about it carefully, and the way he had spent money this whole road did look a little like the landlord’s stupid son.

He did not feel there was a problem with the behaviour.

Paying Jung Jae-joon above the market was not buying a song. It was buying the exclusive time in which he put every other client’s work behind and worked for you first.

Kim Jae-kyung’s gown was the same logic.

The law of the transaction had always been the premium of one-plus-one-greater-than-two.

If the cash you put down only sits on the passing line of breaking even, you only get the perfunctory of the standard issue. If you smash the price past the red line, the experience you get will absolutely exceed expectation.

Like the Korean Air they were about to board.

When the flight attendant pushed the cart through economy and handed out meals, she bent at the waist, smiling, and met your eyes level. When you sat in a first-class suite and she poured champagne for you, she crouched, and looked up.

Bending to meet your eyes, and crouching to look up — were those the same?

That said.

This duty-free shop really had lost a little.

No additive of “exclusive service.” No logic of “relationship investment.” Only an exchange gap and a tax gap. Three or four hundred thousand won, given away.

But as long as Baek Si-on did not admit the loss, it was not a loss.

September 7 KE927 · night Korean Air first class · Charles de Gaulle to Incheon Eleven hours twenty minutes, a pair of airline pyjamas, and a video at 537K

Korean Air KE927.

Charles de Gaulle to Incheon.

Flight time eleven hours twenty minutes.

This time there was no upgrade theatre.

The way they had sat coming, they sat going back.

The first-class layout was the same as on the way out, 1-2-1 staggered.

Baek Si-on walked to his seat, about to sit, and found Choi Jin-ri sitting in the seat beside it.

On the last flight, that seat had been Baek Eun-ah’s.

Baek Si-on looked at Choi Jin-ri, then looked forward.

Baek Eun-ah was in a window seat, fiddling with the reading light over her head, her expression as natural as if this had been the arrangement from the beginning.

The SM manager was in the last row, eyes closed, earplugs already in.

Baek Si-on took his gaze back and looked at Choi Jin-ri sitting beside him.

Did not ask.

Sat down.

By the time they had changed into the pyjamas first class issued, the plane still had not moved.

The door was open. Light from the jet bridge came in at the forward door. The attendants were doing last checks in the aisle.

Choi Jin-ri beside him was in the same dark-grey pyjamas, sleeves rolled to mid-forearm, a small stretch of wrist out. The ponytail of the day had been taken down; the hair was on her shoulders.

The makeup had already come all the way off on the Venice-to-Paris leg.

Bare face, now.

Baek Si-on leaned into the seat, took out the tablet, and, while there was still signal, opened Instagram and searched himself.

The first hit was the video Baek Eun-ah had mentioned.

The view-count was no longer a hundred thousand.

The number in the bottom right showed: 537K.

Still climbing.

Baek Si-on tapped in.

REC 0:37 IG · handheld · 15° off

The frame shook hard. The person shooting was in the middle of the crowd, phone up. The picture was blocked by a head in front, then knocked aside by someone’s elbow. The angle was off by at least fifteen degrees.

But the subject was clear.

Baek Si-on’s mouth against the silver mesh of the microphone. The chorus squeezed out of the phone speaker, compressed until it broke, the high end a little torn, but that lazy vocal texture still there.

“I love you baby, and if it’s quite all right —”

The picture started to sway.

The person shooting was swaying too.

The sound around was even louder than the singing.

Whistles, cheers, shouting in some language you could not place, mixed into a sheet.

Someone at the left edge of the frame waving a white napkin. Someone holding a prosecco glass, swaying left and right with the beat. A man in a tailcoat shot past the bottom of the frame, as if running toward a stage.

“I need you baby —”

The picture suddenly steadied.

The person shooting had probably lifted the phone high, and finally found an angle that was not blocked.

Then Baek Si-on’s left hand came up, pointing at some direction on the right of the frame that you could not make out.

“Oh pretty baby —”

A scream burst outside the frame.

The person shooting screamed too, the hand shook, the picture went soft again.

The video ended there.

Thirty-seven seconds.

Baek Si-on looked at the frame frozen on the last still.

The last still was locked on the instant he put his hand out and pointed off-frame. The direction of the finger was blurred by the out-of-focus torchlight. You could not tell who it was pointing at.

Thank God.

You could not tell.

He scrolled down.

Baek Si-on was about to scroll further, to see if there were any more threatening comments.

The air to the left of his shoulder suddenly warmed a little.

Choi Jin-ri’s head came slowly over from her own seat area.

Very carefully.

Neck stretched, chin tipped up a little, eyes stealing a look this way.

The collar of the airline pyjamas had gone crooked to one side because the shoulder line had slipped, half a collarbone out.

She probably thought Baek Si-on was still watching the video and had not noticed her.

But Baek Si-on’s peripheral-vision system had not failed once in more than twenty years of life.

He was about to turn his head.

The forward cabin announcement went.

“Dear passengers, the door is about to close. Please set all electronic devices to flight mode or switch them off. Thank you.”

Korean first, then English, Chinese, French, each once.

Baek Si-on killed the tablet.

Then put his index and middle fingers together, and pressed them to the exact centre of Choi Jin-ri’s forehead.

Pushed, lightly.

Choi Jin-ri’s head was pushed back into her own seat’s territory.

“Flight mode. Nothing to watch.”

She did not resist.

Pushed back, she curled into the seat, mouth pressed, smiling.

“I wasn’t watching anyway.”

“Your whole head came over.”

“I was looking out the window.”

“I’m on the aisle. There is no window on this side.”

Choi Jin-ri said nothing, and smiled for a good while.

The plane taxied, took off, went through the cloud.

The sky outside the porthole went from the grey-blue over de Gaulle to the deep purple of the stratosphere, then darkened within a few minutes.

Europe’s night chased the tail, and could not catch it.

They were flying east.

Toward the direction the sun rose.

After the aircraft climbed to cruise, the cabin lights went down.

Choi Jin-ri’s seat had gone quiet.

Probably asleep.

Baek Si-on closed his eyes too.

Did not sleep.

His head was turning.

According to the roadmap he had set for himself since the rebirth, the next step should be appearing in tvN’s Reply 1988.

The role Baek Si-on wanted was Choi Taek.

The genius baduk boy.

Silent, wooden, always half a beat slow, but in the moments when no one was looking he had already quietly given all his gentleness away.

But now a problem had arrived.

He was carrying a Volpi Cup in his hand.

Twenty-two-year-old Venice Best Actor.

First in Korean film history.

His price, overnight, had been forced up to a terrifying height.

And what was Reply 1988?

A mid-budget ensemble on cable.

Director Shin Won-ho was also a notorious miser.

He would absolutely not smash eighty percent of the whole crew’s actor-fee budget onto a newly minted Best Actor just air-freighted back from Europe.

The production could not eat a premium like that.

Then what about cutting his own fee?

Going to Shin Won-ho himself and saying, Director, I don’t want market. Give me a newcomer’s price.

Even zero-fee.

I just want to play Choi Taek.

Baek Si-on ran that thought around in his head once.

No.

He was not fighting alone anymore.

Baek Eun-ah — his manager.

Her market value was bound directly to Baek Si-on’s price. Whatever Baek Si-on was worth, she was worth fifteen percent of that.

Park Ji-hun — his stylist.

Now his résumé said “exclusive stylist to a Venice Best Actor.” The gold in that title would let him walk sideways through the Seoul stylist circuit, on the condition that Baek Si-on’s price could hold up the two words “exclusive.”

Son Nam-won — his new-media partner.

Insight’s core competitiveness right now was “the exclusive information channel with Baek Si-on.” The higher Baek Si-on’s price, the more that channel was worth.

Jung Jae-joon — his music producer.

No direct relation to the Best Actor title, but the tag “music partner of a Venice Best Actor” was enough to lift his bargaining power in the music circuit by a step.

All of these people were tied to Baek Si-on’s signboard.

Whatever the signboard was worth, they were worth.

If the eldest brother, for some damn artistic pursuit, led the way in cutting his own price and breaking the trade’s rule, that would be the same as personally smashing the rice bowls of everyone eating off him into a rare mess.

However much his hands itched for Choi Taek, he could not cut.

This was not a question of being wilful or not.

It was responsibility.

Baek Si-on opened his eyes and stared at the bulkhead for thirty seconds.

Forget it.

Yesterday he had just taken Venice Best Actor, and now he was lying at thirty-eight thousand feet worrying about next year’s drama casting. That was too little respect for the Volpi Cup.

Let it be glorious for at least a while.

Thinking that.

He pressed the reading-light switch on the side of the seat.

Took a blank notebook issued by the airline from the storage on his left, and a black pen.

Pulled the cap.

Under the dim focused light he thought for a moment.

The nib came down on the page, with an extremely light scratch, and wrote a few lines.

KE927 · first page · 38,000 ft

This is everything I ever dreamed of.

But this is absolutely not my end.

I will become a legend.

I will write my own history.

My name will be passed down through the ages.

When I leave, people will remember.

When he had written the last character, Baek Si-on put the cap back.

Read it through from the first line to the last.

A bit chuuni.

Not “a bit.”

Considerably chuuni.

If Baek Eun-ah saw this, she would photograph the page into her camera roll and keep it as nuclear-grade material for mocking him for the next thirty years.

But so what?

A twenty-two-year-old man, in the first year after a rebirth, had walked from having nothing to the podium of the Venice Film Festival.

Now, in a first-class suite with no cameras, no reporters, no outsiders, to the white noise of the engines and the starlight outside the porthole, on the first page of a brand-new notebook, he was inflating himself, thoroughly, for a few minutes.

Who was going to stop him?
