Chapter Two Hundred Six

Watermelon Princess-nim

April 1

Almost eleven at night

The Red Velvet dorm — post-stage wreckage

Red Velvet got back to the dorm at almost eleven.

The door shut behind them. The spring night outside had been almost gentle; the exhaustion inside pounced first.

The dorm, as found, 11 p.m.

Seul-gi — first to surrender: shoes half off, dropped straight onto the entryway floor.

Seung-wan — braced against the wall changing into slippers, kneading her calf with a hiss: “That stage floor today had a personal grudge against my heels.”

Joy — announcing “I’m dying” in a voice with plenty of wind left in it; the type to run on five percent battery and still win an argument.

Yeri — last one in. Joined the group not long ago; too new to complain loudly. She simply curled up in the corner of the sofa with her little bag hugged to her chest and collapsed, obediently.

Irene — standing in the middle of the living room. She had danced all day like everyone else, but captain’s instinct would not let her be the first to fall. “Seul-gi, off the floor, it’s cold.” “Seung-wan, hang the jacket up.” “Joy, don’t ball that thing up and leave it on the sofa.”

Three members replied through three different postures with the same message: Noted, unnie — but we genuinely cannot move. Irene looked them over, sighed, and finally sat down herself. When she went to unhook her earrings, her fingers weren’t quite obeying either.

Just as everyone was entering the “tonight we die on this sofa” state, the dorm door sounded again.

The manager came in.

“Everyone, listen up.”

The sprawled bodies raised their heads in slow motion.

“The company landed you an endorsement today.”

Two seconds of silence in the living room.

Then Joy laughed first.

“Oppa, that scam is way too old.”

Seung-wan was laughing too:

“Sounds like the fake news in an April Fools’ gag reel.”

Irene smiled a little as well — deciding the manager was probably trying to manufacture a bit of joy for April Fool’s Day. Promotions had been brutal lately; morale needed maintenance.

But the manager’s face carried no joke whatsoever.

“BodyArmor.”

At that name, the room grew only more certain it was an April Fools’ prank.

Completely unheard of.

If it had been some familiar soda, a coffee, even any domestic Korean functional drink, they might have let themselves hope for a second.

Instead the name was foreign with a thoroughness that felt personal.

Yeri, who had been about to laugh, saw that none of her unnies were laughing properly, quietly swallowed her own laugh, and asked in a small voice:

“You’re really not messing with us?”

“I’m not at the point where I skip sleeping at home to come lie to you at the dorm at midnight.”

Joy had been winding up for another jab —

— and then the word BodyArmor rolled around her brain and knocked against something half-buried in memory.

She frowned at the ceiling for a few seconds, and suddenly went “AH.”

The others jumped at the shout.

“Baek Si-on sunbae!”

“What?” Seung-wan stared at her, blank.

“How did this become Baek Si-on sunbae all of a sudden?” Seul-gi was still on the floor, asking the ceiling.

Irene, who had been half-asleep against a cushion, let her lashes lift, very slightly, at the name Baek Si-on.

Joy smacked the sofa armrest, wearing her full look-how-good-my-memory-is face.

“That’s the drink Baek Si-on sunbae handed out at his North American concert — one bottle to every single fan!”

At that, Irene finally glanced at her, light as a feather.

“Why are you so attentive to Baek Si-on sunbae, Joy?”

Joy turned around. The captain’s face was perfectly expressionless.

“Was I?”

She tipped her head.

“I don’t recall a certain unnie being attentive enough to copy ‘Legend’ lyrics in the middle of the night?”

Irene’s face caught fire inside one second.

“Yah, Joy.”

Her voice was still fairly level.

“What nonsense are you saying.”

Still contained.

“Did you go through my notebook AGAIN?!”

That one was already not holding steady.

Joy launched off the sofa, dodging behind the coffee table, yelling as she went:

“I didn’t sneak! It fell out on its own last time! And I only saw one page! ONE page!”

“…One page is not allowed either.”

Irene stood up from the sofa too.

The pursuit, as witnessed

The physical facts: Irene had danced eight full rounds on stage today. Rehearsal from seven in the morning to wrap at eight at night; one and a half rice balls and two sips of water in between. Five minutes ago she had been wondering whether the soles of her feet still counted as attached.

The anomaly: once a certain sore spot gets hit, the body conjures, out of the ruins of exhaustion, a power source of unknown origin. Probably adrenaline. Possibly face. Possibly a name that must not be said out loud.

The engagement: cushion, thrown — miss. Joy, screaming, routing around the coffee table. Irene, committing to a full chase. “Unnie, weren’t you out of strength!” — “This doesn’t take strength.” — “Then what does it take?” — “It takes you shutting up.” — “Love really does replenish stamina— not the face!”

Field reports: Seul-gi extended a hand to intervene, in a posture closer to applause. Seung-wan silently folded her legs up and ceded space along the kill corridor. Peace was restored when the manager clapped twice — not loud, but enough. Joy took cover behind Seul-gi, using her shoulder as a shield. Irene caught her breath, tucked the chased-loose hair behind her ear, and sat back down with a perfectly blank face — as if the person who had chased her member around the entire room had been somebody else. Only her breathing was still slightly uneven, and her ears carried a thin layer of pink. The manager looked at her once and wisely said nothing.

“Sit. Let me finish talking.”

“It’s real. The brand is entering the Korean market officially — there’s going to be a localized launch campaign, and the five of you map to five fruit flavors.”

Seung-wan blinked:

“Printed on the bottles?”

Seul-gi followed up:

“It’ll really be in convenience stores and everything?”

“It will.”

The manager flipped a page of the paperwork.

“Irene is the watermelon.”

“Why is Irene unnie the watermelon?” Joy asked.

Seul-gi thought about it for one very serious second and produced an answer that sounded reasonable:

“Because watermelon’s the most expensive?”

The rest nodded along:

“Makes sense.”

“Plus the most eye-catching.”

“Summer center position.”

Yeri stole a quiet glance at Irene — apparently conducting a genuine compatibility study between “watermelon” and “captain.”

The flavor lineup, as argued

The manager read out the remaining assignments. Ten minutes of “that one sounds SO like you,” followed by ten more of whose flavor sounded the tastiest — a happy racket.

Irene’s attention, meanwhile, was not on the fruit.

When the noise died down, she asked the first question that actually touched the ground:

“Isn’t that drink Baek Si-on sunbae’s endorsement? Why would they come to us?”

At that, the voices in the living room sank on their own.

Everyone understood that this was the real point.

The manager thought about it. He didn’t force out details he himself didn’t fully have; he gave the core piece only:

“I don’t know the business plumbing. The version the company received is: this is a joint campaign with Baek Si-on sunbae’s side.”

Two seconds of quiet in the living room.

Joy suddenly clapped her hands:

“Got it!”

Every head turned to her.

Joy delivered the conclusion in dead earnest:

“Baek Si-on sunbae’s side obviously grabbed us along on the way.”

It was said too bluntly.

Right up until the manager discovered there was, in fact, no rebuttal available.

“But who it’s specifically FOR…”

Joy paused there, deliberately — and swung her gaze, with extreme slowness, toward Irene.

❝

Must be for the Watermelon Princess-nim.

— Park Soo-yeong, business analysis

The living room was silent for half a second.

Then Irene stood up.

The captain’s dignity held for approximately two seconds.

Then the watermelon herself launched the charge.

This time Joy didn’t even have time to circle the coffee table.

“That was commercial judgment!”

“It was a reasonable inference!”

“Unnie, I can apologize, but could you please put down the slipper!”

“……”

The manager sighed, checked the time on his phone, and decided the remaining briefing items could wait for tomorrow.

Before he got out the door, Yeri asked, carefully:

“Oppa, this endorsement… it’s really not an April Fools’ thing?”

“It’s not. Everything stays confidential until the shoot. Don’t go talking.”

“Yes…”

❧ ❧ ❧

After the manager left, the dorm quieted down by degrees.

The dorm, as it went quiet

Water running in the bathroom, on and off. A hair dryer humming through a few rounds. Lights going out one by one.

Last audibles: Seul-gi, still defending “watermelon really is expensive”; Seung-wan, shutting it down without mercy — “Do not ask the company that tomorrow”; Joy, laughing so hard she nearly swallowed toothpaste foam. Then, little by little, the night went still.

April 1

Deep in the night

Irene’s room — lights out, brain not

Irene lay in bed.

By rights, at this hour the brain should have gone empty, and she should have been asleep by now.

Instead she was completely, unforgivably awake.

BodyArmor, joint campaign.

Watermelon.

And Joy’s punchably smug “for WHO, though.”

She rolled over, buried half her face in the pillow, and first gave herself a calm, orderly explanation.

This was normal work. Baek Si-on sunbae was working with CJ now; brand tie-ins needed a young girl group — perfectly reasonable.

Besides, he had always looked after his juniors.

The concert was like that; a hitch along the way this time would hardly be strange.

She got the logic running smooth.

But analysis was analysis — certain details kept surfacing without permission.

The evidence file, as reviewed in the dark

Exhibit one — the on-site medical room, and the way he had bent down to ask the doctor about her ankle.

Exhibit two — the night he had the tvN director cut the big-screen close-up to her.

Exhibit three — the collaboration sneakers delivered to the dorm.

Exhibit four — tonight’s drink deal.

The card she had kept from the shoe box, kept to this day: “Give the ankle a vacation.”

Take any one of them apart and it reads: taking care of a junior. But when all of them land on one person, it becomes very difficult not to think a little further. Could it be… just the tiniest bit… because of her?

The moment the thought surfaced, Irene pressed it back down.

Overthinking.

Watermelon princess.

Juvenile.

She wiggled her foot under the blanket.

The band of her ankle still carried the day-after ache of a full day of dancing.

And so, involuntarily, she thought of those shoes again — and of the little card in the shoe box that she had kept until now.

Just an ordinary sentence, really.

But at night, in this kind of silence where nobody was talking, it somehow invited far more speculation than it did in daylight.

Irene pulled the blanket up to her chin; her cheeks grew hotter by degrees in the dark.

The ankle, to be fair, had gotten its vacation.

But the heart, meanwhile, was becoming more and more disobedient.

— Bae Joo-hyun, lights out, blanket to the chin
