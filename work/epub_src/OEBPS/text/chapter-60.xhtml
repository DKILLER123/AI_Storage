Chapter Sixty

A Push Notification That Aged SM's PR Department

July 29 9:10 a.m. Cheongdam-dong, SM Entertainment HQ · Public Relations An exclusive, a headline, and a decision made at the top

Park Eun-su set her iced americano down on the desk and opened her computer, waiting for the Naver homepage to finish loading.

It was the first thing she did every morning when she got to work.

Skim the day's entertainment news, pick out the reports and the public-sentiment currents that touched SM, compile them into a short briefing, and get it into the department group chat before eleven.

She'd been doing this for three years. This was the hardest year yet.

She took a sip of coffee.

The Naver entertainment page loaded.

The headline was an OSEN exclusive.

The moment she saw the word “OSEN,” she let out a conditioned sigh of relief.

Good news. Positive coverage. No fire-extinguishing required.

Her gaze slid to the right and ran down the whole headline.

OSEN · Exclusive

[Exclusive] The leading lady of Venice-shortlisted “The Green Bottle Fly” is revealed for the first time — f(x)'s Sulli, possibly absent from the festival for personal reasons

The Korean film The Green Bottle Fly (director Baek Jeong-hun), shortlisted for the main competition of the 71st Venice International Film Festival — its leading actress's identity has been confirmed for the first time today: Sulli, a member of SM Entertainment's girl group f(x).

It is understood that Sulli plays the lead role “Yeon-hui” in the film — also her first time starring in an independent feature.

Earlier this month, however, SM Entertainment issued an official statement announcing that Sulli would “suspend all entertainment activities to focus on rest and recovery.”

Given Sulli's current activity status, whether she will be able to attend the Venice festival on schedule is highly uncertain.

Worth noting, The Green Bottle Fly is the only Korean film shortlisted in this year's Venice main competition, carrying the hopes of Korean cinema in the race for the Golden Lion.

The film's male lead is the singer Baek Si-on, also making his silver-screen debut. As of press time, SM Entertainment has not yet responded to this matter. OSEN will continue to follow up.

The article ended.

Park Eun-su stared at the phrase on the screen: “SM Entertainment has not yet responded.”

What did “not yet responded” mean?

Did you ask us before you published?

Wasn't OSEN one of our own?

She scrolled down fast.

The comments were already going.

Naver · top comments

“Korea's only film shortlisted for Venice, and the female lead is locked at home by her own company? What exactly is SM doing?”

“Hold on... when did Sulli go make a film? Does SM even know about this?”

“So SM's ‘suspend all activities’ — did that also suspend her road to Venice?”

“An actor carrying Korea to Venice, locked in the country by her agency on the grounds of ‘rest.’ Is that shelving or is that kidnapping?”

“Say what you like about Sulli's past controversies — you don't make her skip the Venice red carpet. That changes the nature of it.”

Park Eun-su closed the comment section.

She picked up the desk phone — and then her finger stopped on the dial.

Who first?

The division head? Artist management? Or straight up to the floor above?

In the end she dialled the division head's extension.

Two rings. Answer.

“Division Head, have you seen OSEN?”

“I have. Meeting in ten minutes.”

✿ ✿ ✿

July 29 Morning SM Entertainment · the President's office Face, money, and a system built on obedience

The President's office.

The moment Public Relations Division Head Jo Myeong-hun pushed the door open, Kim Young-min was standing with his back to the door, facing the floor-to-ceiling window.

The Cheongdam-dong skyline cast a grey-blue across the glass, and the Han River gleamed in the distance.

Jo Myeong-hun clutched a stack of printouts and gave a dry little cough first.

“President, the meeting is over.”

Kim Young-min didn't turn around.

“Say it.”

Jo Myeong-hun set the printouts down on the desk. The top page was a full screenshot of the OSEN article, its key sentences already marked out in yellow highlighter.

“OSEN ran the exclusive at 9:03 this morning. The core of it is this: the ‘suspend all entertainment activities’ notice we put out will stop Sulli from attending this year's Venice festival.”

He paused.

“The Naver comments are already past a thousand, and it's spreading on Daum and Nate too. The main direction of public opinion is...”

Kim Young-min turned around.

Jo Myeong-hun saw the president's face and automatically dropped his voice three notches.

“...SM is obstructing Korean cinema from reaching the world.”

Kim Young-min walked to the desk, picked up the stack of printouts, and ran his eyes over it from the top.

“Has Son Nam-won lost his mind?”

Jo Myeong-hun pressed his lips together and didn't dare answer.

Kim Young-min looked over the comment screenshots a second time, then threw the papers down on the desktop.

“What plan has Public Relations come up with?”

“We drafted a line.”

Jo Myeong-hun cleared his throat.

“Sulli recently has been receiving professional treatment for serious mental-health issues. SM, as a responsible agency, acting from the angle of protecting the artist's health and after evaluation with the medical team, determines that Sulli's current physical and mental condition is not suited to intense international travel and public activity. SM deeply regrets Sulli's inability to attend the Venice festival——”

Kim Young-min raised a hand and cut him off.

Jo Myeong-hun's mouth shut.

“You're going to use depression as a shield? The second that gets out, the first thing every emotional netizen thinks is — SM drove Sulli into depression.”

The plan was dead.

The office went quiet for a few seconds.

“President... then we can only let her go. But if we open that precedent... how does the company manage its artists from here on?”

Jo Myeong-hun was right.

SM's entire management system was built on absolute obedience.

The company says pause, so you pause. The company says comeback, so you come back. The company says you can't go, so you can't go.

This wasn't one artist's problem. It was the whole system's problem.

Tear a gap here today, and tomorrow a second artist, a third, would slip through the same hole.

An ant-hill breaks a dike.

But if Kim Young-min refused to release her?

The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism would dial this very desk directly.

A lawmaker might, in the middle of some routine inquiry, “casually” mention whether Korea's entertainment industry was over-exploiting its artists.

Kim Young-min shut his eyes in pain.

He was choosing between “losing face” and “losing money.”

Lose face, and you take a few days of scolding. SM had been cursed before; which of the big three hadn't? Cursed today, and they'd still buy the albums tomorrow.

Lose money, and it was really lost.

“Let her go.”

Jo Myeong-hun nodded, half a sigh of relief escaping him.

“But—”

Kim Young-min opened his eyes.

“The public-relations line has to change.”

He walked to the window, back to Jo Myeong-hun, and said it line by line.

“The statement goes out like this: Sulli's recent period of rest was, precisely, an adjustment phase that SM Entertainment arranged so that she could prepare for the Venice festival in the very best condition. SM Entertainment has always attached the greatest importance to the international development of its artists and will give its full support to Sulli's attendance at all events of this year's Venice festival.”

Jo Myeong-hun scribbled fast on the back of the A4 page.

Kim Young-min looked at his watch.

“Have the statement out before four this afternoon.”

“Understood.”

Jo Myeong-hun assumed that was the end of it and turned to leave.

“Wait.”

Kim Young-min sat back in his chair.

“Contact YG's PR director and the head of JYP's planning office. Tell them — starting today.”

“As long as Son Nam-won remains OSEN's editor-in-chief, then any comeback announcement, exclusive material, artist interview, or event invitation from the three agencies — OSEN gets nothing. Not a single word.”

“Get rid of him.”

Kim Young-min flipped open his laptop, his eyes already moving to the screen.

The conversation was over.

Jo Myeong-hun folded the A4 twice and tucked it into the inside pocket of his suit jacket, bowed, turned, and strode quickly out of the office.

SM Entertainment has always attached the greatest importance to the international development of its artists and will give its full support to Sulli's attendance at all events of this year's Venice festival.

July 29 Afternoon · two hours later OSEN HQ · the acting director's office Polite, obedient, and out the door

Slap.

A termination agreement was thrown down onto the solid-oak desk, sliding across the smooth surface until it came to rest at Son Nam-won's hand.

“Sign it. Go clean out your personal belongings. You have half an hour to be out of this building.”

Son Nam-won stared at the agreement, cold sweat running down his spine.

“Director, the content I published was extremely polite to SM. I didn't use a single negative term. It was all written from an ‘objective’ angle—”

“Polite?”

The acting director let out a dry laugh, looking at him the way you'd look at an idiot.

“They don't need you to be polite. They need you to be obedient.”

Then, with an impatient wave of the hand:

“Go to Finance and settle your accounts. And forget about the media world from now on. The big three have spoken — there isn't a news outlet in Korea that will dare hire you.”
