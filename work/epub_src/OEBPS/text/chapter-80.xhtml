Chapter Eighty

Shock! The New Best Actor Demands His Back Pay from the Stage

September 3 6:27 am (KST) Seoul · the Insight office A one-line message, and the article that would burn the servers

Seoul.

Six twenty-seven in the morning.

Son Nam-won sat in his office, the computer screen in front of him split into two windows.

On the left, the KBS morning-news live stream.

The anchor, in that standard broadcast voice:

"The seventy-first Venice International Film Festival is being held in Italy. The independent film The Green Fly, directed by Korean filmmaker Baek Jeong-hun, held its world premiere yesterday…"

A clip of the red carpet was embedded in the top-right corner of the news frame.

Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri walked the carpet side by side, the flash picking out their silhouettes.

On the right, the article editor of the Insight backend.

In the title bar, a line of text:

Insight · exclusive

Insight · the title bar

Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri dazzle the Venice red carpet — the gown was made by her?

The body had already reached the sixth paragraph.

Kim Jae-kyung's name appeared in the second paragraph, her career bio laid out in the fourth.

Son Nam-won was, at the same time, doing two things at once.

His phone on the desk buzzed.

He picked it up.

KakaoTalk.

Baek Si-on.

The message was a single line.

Baek Si-on — KakaoTalk

"Eight minutes of applause, rights sold at 1.4 million US dollars."

Son Nam-won's gaze ran back and forth over that dozen-or-so characters twice, and a headline long enough to crash the whole country's servers had already auto-typeset itself on his retina.

Insight · the headline in his head # The Green Fly receives 8-minute standing ovation at Venice, and the title sales break the all-time record for Korean cinema at Venice #

His hand had barely touched the keyboard before he stopped.

Then he opened the browser and typed in director Kim Ki-duk's Pieta.

Two years earlier.

That film had taken the highest honour in Venice, the Golden Lion — one of Korean cinema's brightest moments on the international stage.

Wikipedia and a few older news reports came up.

Son Nam-won scanned the pages quickly.

Variety's report, Screen International's market analysis, some translated Korean media pieces.

He clicked them one by one.

The specific amount of the title sales hadn't been publicly disclosed.

That was common in the independent-film world.

Rights-transaction figures are usually kept as commercial secrets, and neither buyer nor seller wants them public, for fear of affecting later negotiations.

But there were indirect data points.

Pieta had sold distribution rights in more than thirty countries and regions during Venice.

Based on the average independent-film rights price and regional distribution of that year, the estimated total was roughly between one million and one point five million US dollars.

Son Nam-won matched that number against the "1.4 million" Baek Si-on had just sent.

One point four million.

Sitting near the upper limit of the Pieta estimate.

Strictly speaking, "breaking the record" had a certain amount of water in it.

If Pieta's actual total was around one point five million, then The Green Fly's one point four million was actually a touch short.

Son Nam-won stared at the screen and agonised for only three seconds.

Then, with extraordinary decisiveness, he clicked the X in the top-right of the browser.

Who cares?

It's a record now.

Pieta's side never published an official figure; who could jump out and refute it?

Kim Ki-duk himself?

Right now he was probably shooting a new film somewhere in the mountains with no signal. By the time he saw this headline, the trending list would have churned through three rounds.

Besides, the phrase "the record for Korean cinema's title sales at Venice" was itself very fuzzy.

You could read it as "the first-day sales," or "the total sales over the festival," or "the confirmed, contracted sales."

Different brackets, different conclusions.

One of the things media people are best at is choosing the bracket.

Son Nam-won clicked the "Save draft" button in the top-right of the editor.

The Kim Jae-kyung piece could wait.

The gown story could run later; it wasn't urgent.

But the one-point-four-million-dollar title-sales data had a shelf life of only this morning.

New article.

A blank page popped up, the cursor blinking in the title bar.

His fingers fell to the keyboard and began to type.

Fast.

Those years at OSEN had carved typing speed into muscle memory.

Title, lede, body, image slots, keyword tags — the whole sequence was already arranged in his head; his fingers were only executing.

The lede took two minutes.

The body, fifteen.

In between, he wove in excerpts from the THR and Variety reviews, the description of the standing applause at the premiere, the title-sales figure, and one carefully crafted closing line:

Insight · the typed article

"This was an independent film with a budget of only two hundred million won. It had no stars, no big company, no safety net. But on the water of Venice, it blew the loudest boom Korean cinema has produced in a decade."

Son Nam-won typed the last character, moved the cursor up to the "Publish" button.

Paused for a second.

Glanced at the time.

Six fifty-eight in the morning.

Korea's office workers probably started scrolling their phones between seven and seven thirty, and the Naver entertainment editors refreshed the front page around seven fifteen.

If he posted at exactly seven, it would hit the first second everyone opened their phones.

Son Nam-won waited two minutes.

Seven on the dot.

He clicked publish.

The article went live.

He leaned back in his chair, stretched, both hands behind his head, and stared at the real-time read count on the screen.

Starting from zero, it began to jump.

One.

Three.

Seventeen.

Eighty-four.

Two hundred.

Five hundred.

One thousand two hundred.

The numbers jumped faster and faster.

Son Nam-won watched the soaring figure, and the corner of his mouth curved up.

When he'd been blacklisted at OSEN, he'd thought his journalism career was over.

Then Baek Si-on appeared.

A hundred and fifty million won in angel funding. A single sentence, "the name's up to you." A fifty-one-to-forty-nine shareholding structure.

Now, with the site built on Baek Si-on's money, he'd run an exclusive about Baek Si-on blowing up Venice.

This was probably what people meant by "the investment loop closing."

Son Nam-won fished a packet of Lotte instant coffee out of the drawer and kept watching the number still climbing on screen.

Three thousand four hundred.

Five thousand one hundred.

Seven thousand eight hundred.

✿ ✿ ✿

Eight twelve in the morning.

The office door opened.

A young man in his early twenties came in, backpack on his shoulders, an iced Americano in his hand.

Park Jae-won.

Park Jae-won

Reporter · Insight

OC · early twenties · one of three full-time staff

The junior who still asks where a number came from, and who will, when the time comes, tie a three-thousand-won badge to his forehead in a scrum of thirty cameras. Earnest. Slightly too short for the steel forest of ENG lenses. Not a fan — his older sister is.

Insight currently had exactly three full-time employees, and he was one of them.

"Boss, did you write that piece by yourself?"

Son Nam-won swivelled his chair around to face him.

"What?"

"The one-point-four-million title-sale figure… where did you get it? Finecut wouldn't give an external outlet that level of commercial data, would they? Even THR and Variety didn't report a specific number! We're faster than the Hollywood media?"

Son Nam-won calmedly folded his hands behind his head.

"Who doesn't have a few overseas sources with only-single-line access. News is about timeliness. Now go work."

He would never, under any circumstances, reveal that Baek Si-on owned fifty-one percent of Insight and was its largest shareholder.

Maintaining the image of a well-connected, unfathomable godfather of journalism in front of subordinates was the most effective way to hold the team together.

"Boss, you're really something."

Park Jae-won said it sincerely, then pulled out his phone and swiped Naver's real-time trending list.

Naver · real-time trending

2# The Green Fly Venice#

5Baek Si-on Choi Jin-ri #red carpet#

8#8-minute applause#

Three related tags were on the trending list at the same time. The Insight exclusive was the most-cited source under all three.

Other media were re-syndicating.

Sports Chosun was carrying it.

Star News was carrying it.

Even the Hankyoreh, which generally didn't reprint pieces from small outlets, had used their data — though they'd repackaged the headline and lede, the phrase "according to an Insight exclusive" was right there in black and white.

Park Jae-won put his phone down.

"Boss. Can I ask something personal?"

"What?"

"Can you… get me Baek Si-on's autograph?"

Son Nam-won was a little surprised.

"You're a fan?"

"I'm not."

Park Jae-won shook his head immediately.

"My older sister is. Last month she went crazy, carrying a DSLR and chasing him across most of the country, from beer festivals to beach resorts, even Jeju Island."

Son Nam-won listened, looking at the young man who'd barely graduated.

Thought for two seconds.

"Just an autograph? That's too little ambition."

He pointed at the work badge on Park Jae-won's chest.

"When he comes back, take your camera directly to the airport to meet the flight. And make sure your badge is in the most visible spot."

"Then?"

"Then just shove the microphone right in his face. Ask him a question. The moment he sees you're carrying an Insight badge, he'll absolutely stop, and answer all your questions seriously. A photo is nothing."

Park Jae-won looked down at his own badge.

A laminated white card, printed with the Insight logo, his ID photo and his name.

This company was only a month old.

The office was a residential-commercial apartment converted to use. The server room was a converted bedroom. The badge was printed at a quick-print shop in Hongdae for three thousand won.

Just this one card.

And his boss had told him:

"Carry that, and he'll absolutely answer your questions."

Park Jae-won flipped the badge over, then back, and looked up at Son Nam-won:

"Boss… is our badge really that strong?"

September 5 10:14 pm Venice · hotel suite The phone call that means you've won

September fifth.

Ten fourteen in the evening.

Baek Jeong-hun was leaning against the headboard, a stack of itinerary-confirmation slips strewn in front of him.

Flight, checkout, luggage check-in…

As he confirmed, his phone rang.

Baek Jeong-hun glanced at the caller ID.

An Italian country code.

He answered.

Venice Film Festival secretariat — incoming call

"Good evening. This is the secretariat of the Venice International Film Festival. Has the crew of The Green Fly left Venice yet?"

Baek Jeong-hun's back came up off the headboard, the itinerary slips sliding off his knees onto the floor. He didn't pick them up.

"N-no! We're still here!"

"Then wonderful. Tomorrow evening at seven, please be sure to wear formal dress and attend the closing ceremony. Have a pleasant evening."

The beep-beep-beep of the dial tone rang in his ear.

Baek Jeong-hun held the phone and slowly lowered it onto his thigh.

He'd never been to Venice, but he'd heard of the unwritten rule.

The night before the closing ceremony, or on the morning of it, if the committee called this "summons" phone call, it meant you'd won.

A hundred percent.

No exception.

Because the committee would not go to the trouble of telling a crew that had won nothing to stay for the closing ceremony.

That would just be wasting both sides' time.

But what exactly the prize was, they wouldn't say.

✿ ✿ ✿

Two in the morning.

Baek Jeong-hun lay in bed staring at the ceiling.

Rolled over.

Staring at the curtains.

Rolled over again.

Staring at the table lamp that wasn't on.

In his head, every possible prize combination raced by in a revolving lantern.

If it were the Golden Lion.

Then this was only the second time Korean cinema had won it, after Kim Ki-duk, and he'd become part of history.

If it were the Grand Jury Prize.

That was the second-highest prize after the Golden Lion, enough to glorify his ancestors.

If it were Best Director.

That was the highest recognition of his personal ability.

If it were Best Actor.

Then Baek Si-on would become the first Korean male actor to win best actor at one of the three major festivals…

Wait!

Baek Jeong-hun suddenly sat up.

He hadn't told Baek Si-on yet.

He couldn't be the only one enduring the agony of the blind box.

✿ ✿ ✿

September 6 7 pm Sala Grande · closing ceremony Five prizes left, and a name that will shake the roof

September sixth.

The closing day of the Venice Film Festival.

Seven in the evening.

Sala Grande.

The red carpet was walked.

Same flow as opening night — same flash, same shutter sound.

The seating was different, though.

At the opening, seats were assigned by film — whichever day your film screened, that's where your crew sat.

The closing ceremony was different.

The front few rows of the closing ceremony were filled entirely with the crews that had received the "summons" phone call.

Everyone knew they'd won something, but no one knew what.

That produced a very delicate atmosphere.

Everyone was smiling, shaking hands with the people around them, saying pleasantries — "congratulations, your film was amazing," "thank you, yours too."

Each crew was secretly sizing up the scale and make-up of the other crews, trying to calculate what grade of prize the other side might be taking home.

The logic was simple:

If the Swedish director got the Grand Jury Prize, then Best Director might land on the Frenchman; if the Frenchman took Best Director, then Best Actor was unlikely to go to a Frenchman too; the jury usually did regional balancing…

As the house lights dimmed slowly, the spotlights over the stage came up, striking the podium at the centre.

The hostess, Luisa Ranieri, walked out.

Italian actress.

Her stride carried a lazy, Mediterranean sensuality.

Luisa Ranieri

Italian actress · host & red carpet, Venice

Real-name counterpart · Luisa Ranieri

The face of the Venice red carpet — the Italian actress who draws the flashbulbs and opens the night. Warm, unhurried, and photographed against the Lido's sea-light; the woman who makes the premiere feel less like a competition and more like a night out.

"Welcome to the closing night of the seventy-first Venice International Film Festival…"

"Tonight, we pay our highest respects to those creators who, with their images, illuminate the dark places of the human soul…"

The speech ran about three minutes.

Into the awards.

First came the prizes chosen by the independent juries.

The Venice Classics section.

Best restored film.

Best documentary about cinema.

The winners of these were mostly sitting in the back rows.

When a name was read, someone in back stood, applause rose, quickly settled.

During all this, Baek Si-on looked at his watch three times.

Not disrespecting the winners.

It was nerves.

Even though his expression said he wasn't nervous at all.

✿ ✿ ✿

"Next, we invite Mr. Aurelio De Laurentiis to reveal this year's Luigi De Laurentiis Award."

An old man with white hair came out from the side of the stage.

An Italian film tycoon.

The award was named after his father.

The simultaneous Korean translation in Baek Si-on's earpiece followed:

"The Luigi De Laurentiis Award, also known as the De Laurentiis Award…"

This award occupied a rather distinctive position in the festival's prize sequence.

Its scope wasn't limited to the main competition. Every debut feature in every section of the entire festival — regardless of category, regardless of level — was pulled onto the same starting line, judged by a single independent jury.

In short.

A battle royale of debut directors from the whole planet, with one champion.

Aurelio De Laurentiis stood at the podium, said two lines of opening in Italian, the translation tracking in the earpiece.

Then he turned the envelope over, thumb sliding into the seal.

Tore it open. Pulled out a card.

Looked down at it.

Looked up.

"The Green Fly — Baek Jeong-hun."

Luigi De Laurentiis Award

✦ best debut feature · whole festival · single champion ✦

Baek Jeong-hun — The Green Fly

100,000 USD · a winged-lion trophy · the committee's seal

Applause surged in from all corners of the screening hall.

Baek Si-on turned to look at Baek Jeong-hun on his right.

He was reacting exactly as he had when the previous prizes were announced — clapping the "congratulations to the winner" applause, until Baek Si-on gave him a little shove and he came to, startled:

"M-me?"

"Yes, uncle, it's you."

"…"

The spotlight tracked Baek Jeong-hun's slightly unsteady gait.

On the stage, waiting for him, was a small winged-lion trophy, and beside it a hostess holding an acrylic placard with "100,000 USD" printed on it, along with the committee's official logo.

Baek Jeong-hun went up the steps, took the trophy and the cheque with both hands, bowed, then walked to the microphone.

The applause slowly receded.

The whole hall went quiet.

"Thank you…"

"This trophy matters a great deal to me. But what matters even more are the people who brought me here. My father. My sister-in-law. My daughter, crying in front of the television right now in the hotel room…"

Someone in the audience laughed softly.

"…Finally, I want to thank the dozen or so people who came with me from the set to Venice. Camera. Sound. Lighting. Art. Gaffers. Actors. Their names roll quickly in the credits — too quickly to remember. But without them, this film couldn't get its first shot done."

He paused.

"This trophy, today, is theirs."

Baek Jeong-hun bowed.

The applause exploded again.

✿ ✿ ✿

The ceremony pushed on.

Baek Jeong-hun carried the trophy and the cheque placard, taking a wobbly step here and there, back to his seat.

Baek Si-on and Choi Jin-ri both stood, clapping to welcome back this genius director whom Venice had just put its seal on.

"Uncle."

"Mm?"

"Shouldn't the acting fee be paid by now?"

Baek Si-on pointed at the cheque placard under his arm.

On Baek Jeong-hun's face, the three emotions of being moved, excited and proud were all shattered at once by that sentence.

"…Could you let me be moved for thirty more seconds?"

Choi Jin-ri looked down at the red-lion trophy in Baek Jeong-hun's hand, her shoulders shaking.

Not crying.

Trying not to laugh.

✿ ✿ ✿

Over the next twenty-odd minutes, prize after prize was unsealed.

Best Screenplay.

Orizzonti Best Film.

The Marcello Mastroianni Award for an Emerging Actor or Actress.

Baek Si-on clapped at each announcement, the force and frequency of the applause steady, but his attention had left the people being awarded.

He was counting how many prizes were left.

The moment the Special Jury Prize was handed out.

The air in Sala Grande thinned noticeably in an instant. Even the producers and distributors in the very front row unconsciously adjusted their posture.

The hearts of every crew in the hall, at that same moment, were put on high gear.

Because what remained were the big ones:

Grand Jury Prize.

Best Director.

Best Actor.

Best Actress.

Golden Lion.

Five prizes.

Each one a weight that could redirect the whole course of a filmmaker's life.

The hostess walked to the centre of the stage once more.

"Next, we invite a member of this year's jury, Mr. Tim Roth, to present the award."

There was a different layer to the applause below the stage.

Tim Roth came out from the jury seats on the side, his gait exactly as relaxed and lazy as in his films, as if two of his bones were missing.

He didn't tear open the envelope right away.

First he surveyed the front rows, and with a few turns of very British humour teased the nerves of those seated below, stretched so taut they were about to snap, even more fragile.

Even Baek Si-on, who was always as calm as still water, couldn't help clenching his fist at this moment.

He was nervous.

This was one of the few times since his rebirth he'd truly been nervous.

Not because he feared losing.

Because he wanted to win.

Very much.

Tim Roth picked up the envelope.

One hand holding the seal, the other thumb sliding in from the side, tearing upward.

He tore very slowly.

Not deliberately building suspense.

It was just that everything this man did was at that tempo.

The card came out.

Tim Roth looked down at it, raised an eyebrow, then looked up with a smile.

Across a distance of more than ten metres.

Baek Si-on wasn't sure whether Tim Roth was looking at him.

But his instinct told him he was.

The next second.

Tim Roth tossed the card lightly onto the table, leaned toward the microphone, and a pure London accent came down through the whole hall's sound matrix.

"Best Actor."

Paused a beat.

"Baek Si-on."

The moment that syllable detonated in the air, there were about a few tenths of a second of absolute vacuum in the hall.

Volpi Cup · Best Actor

✦ the 71st Venice International Film Festival · main competition ✦

Baek Si-on — The Green Fly

The first Korean male actor to take best actor at one of the three major festivals.

Immediately after.

An applause that nearly lifted the dome off the film palace roared like a tsunami through every aisle and corner.

The noise around him had drowned everything out, but Baek Si-on could still faintly hear Baek Jeong-hun beside him crazily shouting his name.

Two beams of the brightest spotlight came down from overhead, turning his area bright as day.

He became the single, absolute focus in this hall crowded with a thousand people.

Baek Si-on raised his right hand, slid it into the suit jacket, and pressed it, over the shirt, hard against his left chest.

Fast.

Beating very fast.

At that moment this body abandoned every rational mask, and with the violent pump of the heart in its chest, proved to him, honestly, the terrifying weight of this award.

The feeling was absorbed.

Baek Si-on stood.

The surface of the skin relaxed, a genuinely real smile set into the bottom of his eyes.

He turned and hugged Baek Jeong-hun, whose eyes had gone red again, hard.

Baek Jeong-hun's palm slammed heavily against his back, producing two dull thuds.

Then he turned to the other side.

Choi Jin-ri had long since stood.

Baek Si-on reached out his hand and gave the female lead, with whom he'd scraped through three months in front of the lens, a hug.

Not a ceremonial, perfunctory one, but a solid, real embrace.

He let go.

Baek Si-on turned, and unhurried, walked toward the podium at the centre of the stage.

The aisle was long.

Walking the whole way.

Baek Si-on's gaze swept over the standing, clapping people on both sides.

He could clearly feel the surprise in the eyes of those old European actors;

could see the stiff back of the French actor who'd been the hot favourite;

could sense the unwillingness and regret radiating from those rivals clapping only with barely sufficient force.

But.

Those sticky, complicated emotions did not cause even a millimetre of lag in his stride.

In this vanity fair, sympathy and concession were the cheapest waste products.

Since the nine most selective judges in the world had written the final result on that card.

Since Tim Roth had read out his name in a London accent.

Then right now, he stood among the top actors on this planet.

Not convinced?

Bring your new film next year.
