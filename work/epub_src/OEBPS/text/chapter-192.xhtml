Chapter One Hundred Ninety Two

This Lesson Happens Whether You Take It or Not

The same Milan evening · one backdrop, two logos, three of them An embrace engineered for the lenses · an introduction declined mid-sentence · one man who gives away the center of a photo without noticing he has given away anything · a composition that cuts an ambiguity clean out of the frame · a wine-velvet look that erases a prepared opinion · and an arrangement class scheduled for tonight, attendance mandatory The Fendi backdrop → the Gucci corner → the walk to the cars Two women fence in perfect smiles across a strip of Italian pavement; a man solves the whole thing as a composition problem; and the Nation’s first love loses an after party to a music lesson that is not entirely a music lesson.

By the time Lee Ji-eun took off her sunglasses, her smile was already in place and fully staffed.

“Suzy-ya.”

Bae Suzy turned as if only now seeing her, eyes bending into crescents on impact.

“Eonni!”

The two of them embraced outside the show space. The flashbulbs lit up at once. A foreign country. Milan fashion week. Two of Korea’s top actresses in a surprise reunion. Judged by photographs alone: genuinely warm. As for whose hands loosened first during the hug, and whose smile stopped strictly at the corners of the mouth — those details fall outside the interpretive jurisdiction of news photos.

“It’s been too long,” Lee Ji-eun said, smiling.

“Eonni’s been too busy to book,” Bae Suzy answered, also smiling.

“You’re one to talk. That suit today is beautiful.”

“Gucci suits Eonni too.”

Eonni traveled back and forth between them, ping-pong-perfect. And then, having released Bae Suzy, Lee Ji-eun turned — very naturally — to Baek Si-on.

“Si-on oppa.”

At that form of address, the corner of Bae Suzy’s eye moved — one millimeter, no more. Lee Ji-eun didn’t look at her. She kept smiling and said:

“This is my very dear little sister — Suzy.”

It sounded like an introduction. It was actually a map: Lee Ji-eun had just assigned herself the position of “Baek Si-on’s person,” and filed Bae Suzy into the box marked “people introduced by her.” If this small social play ran its intended course, Baek Si-on would nod, Bae Suzy would greet, and the three of them would complete a beautiful, safe, closed loop of a first meeting.

Bae Suzy had no intention of letting the loop close.

“Eonni, no need for introductions.”

She cut in, smiling.

“Sunbae and I already talked inside the show.”

A beat.

“And we’re meeting again at Fendi’s after party tonight.”

Then, as if a thought had only just occurred to her, she turned back to Lee Ji-eun.

“Eonni — won’t you join us?”

The smile on Lee Ji-eun’s face did not change. Inside, she pronounced one silent phrase, two syllables short and not elegant at all: scheming bitch. She was here on Gucci’s invitation.

“I have other plans tonight.”

“What a shame.”

Bae Suzy’s voice was pure regret. The regret was extremely sincere. The sincerity was extremely annoying.

Lee Ji-eun looked at her, and made her own smile one degree more affectionate.

“Suzy-ya.”

“Mm?”

“Have you always made friends this quickly?”

Bae Suzy blinked.

“Maybe today’s just good luck.”

And as she finished, her gaze grazed — lightly, plausibly by accident — across Baek Si-on. Lee Ji-eun saw it. Precisely because she saw it, it irritated her more.

❧ ❧ ❧

The Korean press on site finally found their gap.

“Could we get the three of you in one photo?”

The moment the request went up, several brand publicists’ eyes lit. This frame was worth too much. A man just put on the cover of TIME. Lee Ji-eun, invited by Gucci. Bae Suzy, seated in Fendi’s front row. If the Korean media missed this photograph, the editors back home would hang them on the wall for reflection.

Staff moved in and guided the three toward the backdrop. By standard logic, Baek Si-on should stand in the middle — international heat, height, and the “cultural asset” status the Korean press had spent a season building all made him the natural center. But Baek Si-on looked at the backdrop lighting, and then at the two women beside him.

“Ji-eun should take the middle.”

Lee Ji-eun’s eyes lit for an instant — and her feet were already sliding, effortlessly, half a step toward center.

“Me?”

The tone asked for confirmation. The body had finished arriving.

Bae Suzy smiled at her.

“The middle suits you, Eonni.”

“Does it?”

Lee Ji-eun curved her eyes.

“Then I’ll stand here.”

Baek Si-on offered no explanation, because in his view the matter required none. Him in the middle, the three heights and shoulder lines would have made a genuinely hostile pyramid. Ji-eun in the middle flattened the height differences on both sides; the frame read far better. Photography is, in the end, a composition problem.

Lee Ji-eun, plainly, did not understand it as a composition problem. She stood in the middle — Baek Si-on on her left, Bae Suzy on her right. Position: exact. Distance: exact. The faint ambiguity that had been accumulating between Bae Suzy and Baek Si-on got sliced out of the picture, cleanly, like a thread cut with scissors.

the Milan three-shot · what the frame shows, and what it refuses to

LEFT — cold: the flat expression, camera-approved, judged by a professional to be “actually insane.”

CENTER — sweet: standing a degree taller than usual, eyes curved, territory defined.

RIGHT — soft: the spring the lenses favor, still unaware she has just been outflanked by an accident of geometry.

The photo explains none of the following: why the center stands straighter than usual, why the left smile runs a little deep, why the right has no idea an unintentional favoritism just occurred. Harmonious. Extremely headline-ready. Entirely misleading.

When the shooting ended, Bae Suzy took her phone from her assistant.

“Sunbae, I’ll send you the after party address—”

“Si-on oppa.”

Lee Ji-eun’s voice landed precisely in the instant before the sentence could touch ground. Baek Si-on turned to her.

“Gucci is still waiting for you to change, aren’t they?”

Then she looked at the nearby staff.

“If you don’t go now, the interview order slips.”

❝

With a man like Baek Si-on, feelings are optional. Itinerary always works.

— Lee Ji-eun’s field manual, applied at the Fendi backdrop

And indeed — Baek Si-on checked the time.

“Let’s go.”

Gucci staff closed in at once. Bae Suzy’s phone never got unlocked. She watched his back as he walked away, lowered the phone slowly back into her assistant’s hand, and turned to Lee Ji-eun.

“Eonni and sunbae really are close, aren’t you.”

Lee Ji-eun looked in the direction he’d gone, and took back her gaze one second late.

“Suzy-ya.”

“Mm?”

“Too much curiosity starts to look like no composure.”

Bae Suzy blinked, as if the line had amused her.

“Then was Eonni rushing him off just now — great composure?”

The two of them held eye contact for one second. Outside, the crowd flowed on; Fendi and Gucci staff each cleaned up their own territory.

“I was afraid he’d be late.”

“He doesn’t look like someone who needs looking after.”

“He doesn’t. But sometimes he forgets to refuse things that aren’t necessary.”

Bae Suzy studied her. A few seconds. Then the smile got sweeter.

“So Eonni knows him that well.”

“A little more than someone who just met him.”

They laughed at the same time, again. If a lens had caught this frame, the caption would have written itself: Milan fashion week — Lee Ji-eun and Bae Suzy, close as ever, friendship intact.

❝

Relationships between people are not necessarily real. Photographs, thank God, always are.

— the entertainment industry, on its own evidence standards

❧ ❧ ❧

Over the next half hour, Lee Ji-eun executed a flawless stretch of standard brand labor. Looked at new pieces. Nodded. Smiled. Gave a short interview. Told the cameras, on record, that this Gucci season had found a very interesting balance between vintage and modern. When a staffer asked which coat she liked best, she could cite cuff construction and waistline placement from memory. The homework, clearly, had been done to a professional standard.

Half an hour later, Baek Si-on came out in his Gucci look.

Lee Ji-eun had been about to deliver an opinion on an embroidered coat. She looked up — and briefly forgot the opinion she had prepared.

the Gucci look · itemized, from the floor of the venue

JACKET — deep wine-red velvet, cut close.

SHIRT — ivory silk, collar deliberately not closed.

SCARF — one thin dark length, knotted carelessly, hanging exactly wrong on purpose.

REGISTER — vintage. Ornate. With a faint, dangerous air of looseness about it.

On anyone else, this is a rich man’s son over-trusting his own charm. On that face — cold to the point of inhumanity — every ounce of ornament gets pressed flat and re-read as something else: the pattern stops being decoration and becomes aggression stored under the cloth. The wine-red stops being romance and becomes blood color, dimmed by stage lights.

Lee Ji-eun looked for a while. Then she took her gaze back.

“It’s fine.”

Jung Han-teuk, standing nearby, quietly looked down at his own shoes. Fine? He had personally witnessed her gaze stay on that outfit for a long, long moment.

❧ ❧ ❧

When the interview segment wrapped, Gucci staff led the way toward the show entrance. Jung Han-teuk followed behind with the schedule in hand, doing his best impression of an agent who had no ears.

A few steps in, Lee Ji-eun said, in the tone of a passing remark:

“Suzy’s good at getting close to people, isn’t she?”

“What?”

“I mean — her personality’s always been warm. She’s like that with everyone. It’s very easy to start believing you’re the special case.”

Baek Si-on considered this.

“So?”

“So her motive for inviting you to that party is not pure.”

“Am I not allowed to go?”

Three words, and she was cornered by them. There were exactly two ways to take that sentence: “you can,” or “you can’t.” The first was surrender. The second required a reason. She didn’t want to let go. She wanted even less to be asked for her reason. Her pride and her self-respect both forbade the sentence that would have explained it.

So Lee Ji-eun changed weapons.

“Have you been learning arrangement lately?”

He gave her a look. The swerve was sharper than anything on that runway.

“No.”

The answer was never in doubt. For more than a month he had been either on tour or on a plane; either singing or being stuffed into Scooter’s itinerary. Enough sleep would have counted as a medical miracle. Learning arrangement had plainly not found a window.

Lee Ji-eun nodded.

“Then tonight works perfectly.”

“What works perfectly?”

“I have time.”

She said it with total naturalness — natural enough that the entire preceding slalom of indirection officially did not exist.

“Didn’t you say you wanted to learn? A producer can’t just hum melodies and grind Jung Jae-joon sunbae into the ground until midnight.”

“It’s not because of anything else. You said you’d learn. I happen to have time.”

“And ‘Producers’ starts shooting soon. I need to reset my creative state anyway. Both things can happen in the same evening.”

Jung Han-teuk looked down at the schedule. If his memory served, tonight’s previously scheduled “creative reset” had been titled go back to the hotel and sleep. He said nothing. An agent’s duty, on certain evenings, is to maintain a polite silence while the artist in his care says something that is obviously, structurally untrue.

❝

Policy, Jung Han-teuk Agency: when the artist is visibly talking nonsense, the correct professional response is silence — polished, courteous, billable silence.

— the job, as practiced on a Milan sidewalk

“Location?” Baek Si-on asked.

Lee Ji-eun looked straight ahead. Not at him.

“My hotel room.”

“…You’re sure this is an arrangement lesson?”

“What else would it be?”

She said it with the full confidence of the completely unbothered, as though she genuinely could not hear what he was implying. Baek Si-on studied her for a few seconds, and said, slowly:

“I’m not entirely sure of your motives.”

“My motives are extremely pure.”

“Will Han-teuk be there?”

“I can be,” Jung Han-teuk heard himself answer, on pure professional reflex.

Lee Ji-eun turned her head, slowly, and looked at him with no expression whatsoever. Jung Han-teuk understood at once that the answer had been perfectly professional — and fatally wrong on some other dimension entirely.

“But I’ve suddenly come down with a touch of illness from the water and food. I’ll need an early night. I won’t intrude on the music discussion.”

Lee Ji-eun took back her gaze and kept walking.

One way or another — Bae Suzy’s after party had lost.

As for whether tonight would actually be an arrangement lesson… that depended on Baek Si-on’s attitude as a student, and on her own professional ethics.
