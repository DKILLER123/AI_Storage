Chapter Fifteen

The Phone Number in Front of the Portrait

Spring Ansan The mourning hall — the last scene

Thirty sunrises. Thirty sunsets. The trees of Ansan went green; the clothes on the drying-lines in the alleys switched from thick jackets to short-sleeved tees. Baek Jeong-hun sat behind the monitor, his thermos tea gone from hot to cold. The smile on his face had grown, and grown again. Because he knew it: this film had become.

✿ ✿ ✿

The last day. The last scene. The mourning hall.

Chrysanthemums. A black-and-white photograph. Cheap offering fruit. The face in the photograph was Sang-hun. Yeon-hui knelt before the portrait in black mourning dress, hair loose, no make-up, crying until she couldn’t breathe.

Who had killed Sang-hun? It was the very kid he’d beaten and cursed like a dog every day — Yeon-hui’s rotten little brother. A cruel and perfect closed loop: Sang-hun had taught the boy to collect debts with violence, and that violence had turned and devoured him. Of course, Yeon-hui knew none of this. All she knew was that the one pillar she had in this shitty world had collapsed.

“Cut. That’s a wrap!”

The crew began to clap. It wasn’t a roar — only a dozen-odd people, but everyone was clapping hard. Si-on walked in from outside the hall, a bouquet in his hands, went to Jin-ri and crouched down in front of her.

“You did great.”

Jin-ri was still on her knees. She didn’t get up. The tears were still falling, her shoulders still shaking. Si-on laid the flowers in front of her and said nothing, only reached out and gave her shoulder a soft pat.

The crew started taking the set apart. The gaffer was breaking down the lamp stands, the cinematographer packing the lenses, the script supervisor tidying the slate. Jin-ri slowly lifted her head and looked at that black-and-white portrait.

✿ ✿ ✿
Spring The mourning-hall set Packing up after the last take

Around her, people moved boxes, unplugged cables, packed away the mics. She knelt alone in the centre of the hall, smelling the last of the cheap incense, and she didn’t want to get up.

“Hey.”

Jin-ri looked up, red-rimmed eyes finding Si-on, who had come back.

“I wanted to get closer to Miss Jin-ri, but the shoot’s over. Can we trade contact info?”

Her brain short-circuited. In this industry, when a male actor asked for a female actor’s contact after a wrap, it almost always came wrapped in a plausible excuse. “Let’s work together again sometime.” Or, “Feel free to reach out if you want to talk about a script.”

Jin-ri had never seen this move. Straight out. No preamble, no cover, just laying the purpose bare on the table.

“…What?”

“Contact.”

Si-on fished his phone out of his pocket and flashed the screen at her.

“Can I?”

Before her head caught up, her hand had, with total honesty, reached into her own pocket and pulled out her phone. It was only when the screen lit up that she realised what she’d done, and a heat rose across her pale face.

“Um…”

She tried to find a word to ease the sudden ambush.

“I’m not very funny, and I can’t banter… it might be boring—”

“I’m not looking for a comedy duo partner.”

Jin-ri blinked at that matter-of-fact face, and smiled.

Fair enough. She opened her KakaoTalk QR code and held it out. Si-on scanned it, saved it, noted the name, and slipped the phone back into his pocket.

“Alright. Happy wrap.”

“Mm… you too.”

Si-on turned and took two steps, then stopped.

“Oh, and—”

Jin-ri looked at him.

“If anything comes up after you get back, you can message me.”

“Might not be able to help. But I can listen.”

Then he left. Jin-ri stayed where she knelt, watching that flower-print shirt walk away, until it vanished into the light of the hall’s entrance. She looked down at her phone. A friend-request notification had popped up. The note read “Baek Si-on.”

She opened the chat and stared at the blank screen for a long time. A very long time.

✿ ✿ ✿
Spring Yeonnam-dong, the half-basement Yoon Hye-ja's empty house

When Si-on got home, the place was empty. Yoon Hye-ja was teaching at the university. He showered, changed into clean clothes, and threw the flower-print shirt into the washing machine. No sooner had he dried his hair than the door was knocked on. Knock, knock, knock. Urgent.

He walked over and opened it. Baek Eun-ah stood there, hair a mess, eyes red, a suitcase in her hand. The moment she saw him, she shouted.

“Cousin! I’m done with trainee life! I’m not staying at that damn company!”

Si-on stared at her. Then he scanned the corridor to make sure no neighbour was peeking, pulled her inside, and shut the door.

“Calm down first.”

He went to the kitchen and poured a glass of water, holding it out. Eun-ah took it, drained half of it in gulp after gulp, gasped for air — and then the tears came. Si-on leaned against the counter, arms crossed.

“What happened?”

Eun-ah sniffled and cried, on and off, for a good ten minutes before she got the whole story out. It turned out that in the two months Si-on had been on set, deaf to the outside world, a real earthquake had hit the Korean entertainment scene.

Industry news · morning edition EXO's Kris files to leave SM — stock slides

The hugely popular Chinese member of EXO had suddenly filed to terminate his contract with SM and bolted back home. The moment it broke, SM’s stock plummeted. The shareholders ganged up on management, demanding an immediate plan to steady the market and the fans.

To change the subject, SM’s top brass quickly decided to pull the debut of their new girl group, Red Velvet — originally planned for 2015 — forward to August this year.

The plan changed, and so did the lineup. To keep to schedule, the five-member debut lineup was hastily squeezed down to four. And Baek Eun-ah, whose test scores were decent but who’d always lacked a certain “irreplaceability,” was the easy one to shed.

Young and hot-headed, she didn’t want to be the next over-age trainee, so she grabbed her bag and bolted. But she didn’t dare go home. She hid in a jjimjilbang for two days, and today, out of money, she’d come here.

Si-on listened to this ridiculous butterfly effect, his expression flat.

“This family just wasn’t born to be idols. Come on, let’s go eat first.”

“Huh?”

Eun-ah, red-eyed, looked up, blank. I just lost my job, my dream is shattered, and you’re telling me to eat?

“Huh what. Even if the sky falls, you still have to fill your stomach.”

He pulled the door open.

“What do you want? Korean barbecue or army stew?”

✿ ✿ ✿
Spring Yeonnam-dong Eun-ah, turning grief into appetite

After one barbecue dinner, Eun-ah, turning her grief into appetite, had steadied noticeably. Si-on paid, and walked her home. As they went, he spoke.

“If you’re still interested in the business, you could try moving behind the scenes.”

“Behind the scenes?”

Eun-ah sucked on a cup of ice. “Doing what, a gofer PD at a TV station?”

“Like, my manager.”

“Pfft — ack!”

Eun-ah nearly choked, coughing hard.

“You? A has-been with no company and no presence — you need a manager?”

“What’s wrong with has-been? A has-been still needs someone to answer his calls and mind his schedule.”

“You’re serious?”

“Mm.”

“But I don’t know anything.”

“Learn.”

As they reached the entrance of her building, Eun-ah’s steps slowed.

“Cousin, if my mom asks, what do I say?”

“Tell the truth.”

Eun-ah stood at the unit door, looking at him, mouth opening and closing.

“Cousin, I don’t dare. Walk me up.”

“…Fine.”

The two of them walked up the stairwell. On the second floor, they stopped.

✿ ✿ ✿
Spring Eun-ah's door A middle-aged man on the threshold

Because standing in front of her door was a middle-aged man in a suit, briefcase in hand, knocking. He heard the footsteps, turned, and looked at Eun-ah.

“Are you Miss Baek Eun-ah?”

She nodded.

“I am. And you are?”

The man pulled a document from his briefcase and held it out.

“I’m from SM’s legal department. Your phone’s been unreachable, so the company sent me over directly. This is a settlement sheet of the training, lodging, and other costs the company advanced on your behalf during your trainee period. Per clause thirteen of the contract, a trainee who withdraws mid-way is responsible for these costs.”

Eun-ah took the document. Her hands shook a little. She opened the first page and read the figure at the bottom.

Thirty-two million won.
