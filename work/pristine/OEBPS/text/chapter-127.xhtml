Chapter One Hundred Twenty Seven

The Suit Gangster

Late October Midnight Los Angeles · Beverly Hills · the Four Seasons Three endorsers, three very different readings of the same man.

Midnight.

Los Angeles.

Beverly Hills. The Four Seasons.

Baek Si-on sat on the sofa, working through the feedback from his three Korean endorsers.

KB Kookmin Bank’s wording was the most serious.

In essence: in light of the broad social discussion in Korea triggered by Mr. Baek Si-on’s recent conduct at a public event overseas, KB Bank — as a financial brand that faces the entire population — needed to reassess how well the partnership matched its tone.

Translated into plain speech, it meant —

We’re thinking about cancelling.

What KB sold was “trust.”

And Baek Si-on had just, on live national television, nearly charged into an NBA player altercation. Between that and the slogan “Trust begins with steadfastness,” there really was a gap that was hard to explain.

SK Telecom was a completely different story.

What came back from them was a joke: “Next time you get in a fight, could you wear an SK ad tee?”

What they wanted had never been “safe” and “stable.” What they wanted was “global,” “connected,” “getting out there.”

So tonight’s incident was risk in KB’s eyes.

And material in SK’s.

Hyundai Motor fell somewhere between KB and SK.

By the commercial data, they had no reason whatsoever to turn on Baek Si-on.

But the problem was that Baek Si-on’s impulsive act had broken the tone he had personally set for the Aslan.

So they wanted to watch the market’s direction before deciding.

✿ ✿ ✿

Late October Same night · after the calls Los Angeles · the Four Seasons · suite window A question asked honestly of the glass, and an answer that arrives quietly.

When the three calls were done, Baek Si-on put his phone down on the table and stood up to look out at the Los Angeles night.

Beverly Hills lights ran together into a low orange band in the dark. Far off, a plane was circling above LAX, its tail lights tracing an arc across the night sky.

He asked himself honestly.

For Kobe. For so-called “loyalty to a friend.” He had nearly thrown away top-tier endorsements worth billions of won, and even faced an astronomical penalty clause. Was it worth it?

He thought about it for a long time.

Yes.

Money gone could be earned again.

Endorsements gone could be renegotiated.

In how you live and what you do, you only ask that your conscience runs clear.

Baek Si-on turned and went to wash up.

Half an hour later, he came back drying his hair.

His phone screen was lit.

A mountain of unread messages.

Baek Si-on sat back down on the sofa.

And answered them one by one.

All of them were the safe-arrival kind.

I’m fine.

Don’t worry.

Then he kept scrolling.

Choi Jin-ri.

A long string of messages.

Baek Si-on read those words, every line of them full of worry. He thought for a moment, then took a photo of the coffee table.

The late-night snack the hotel had sent up was already finished.

The plate sat empty.

A little sauce and some crumbs still left on it.

Beside it, half a glass of water, drunk down.

The composition was completely casual.

The lighting dim.

Not at all the kind of photo a Venice Best Actor ought to post.

But it was persuasive.

At least it proved he could still eat.

Sent.

Then he typed.

KakaoTalk · Choi Jin-ri

Baek Si-on

Thank you.

Baek Si-on

Don’t speak up for me. It could drag you in.

Baek Si-on

Rest early.

Sent.

Locked the screen.

Put the phone down.

Baek Si-on walked to the window and looked at the Los Angeles night one more time.

Then he drew the curtains.

Turned off the light.

And went to sleep.

October 29 · Seoul time 7:13 p.m. Seoul · a car, leaving the second Green Bottle Fly event An empty plate is worth more than a hundred “I’m fine”s.

Seoul. Seven thirteen in the evening.

Choi Jin-ri had just finished the second media event for Green Bottle Fly, with a radio appearance still ahead.

The car door shut.

Outside, the flashes and the shouting were sealed away by the glass.

She took out her phone at once.

She read Baek Si-on’s reply carefully, twice, unsure whether she felt more let down or more pleased.

Baek Si-on’s core was too strong.

The rumours and abuse that could shatter her — the online violence that had once made her cry into her pillow until she dehydrated at three in the morning — to him they did not even seem to raise a ripple.

He even had the presence of mind to tell her, “Don’t speak up for me. It could drag you in.”

Choi Jin-ri had thought about it.

Or rather — she had never thought about it very clearly, but some very soft place inside her had hoped for it.

If he were hurting.

If he were under attack.

If he, too, had moments he could not hold up under.

Could she let him lean on her, the way he had once lent her his shoulder?

But the truth was —

He did not need a harbour.

He was himself a harbour, solid, silent, needing no one to shield him from the wind.

There was nothing she could give him.

At least in the matter of “being needed,” she could not yet find an entrance.

And that left her feeling a little… let down.

But —

The part that pleased her was just as clear.

Because he had not replied with “mm.”

He had typed all those words.

And said thank you.

And told her not to speak up for him.

And told her to rest early.

By Baek Si-on’s linguistic standards, this was no longer “improved a lot.”

This was a version update.

Choi Jin-ri lowered her head and read those few lines over again.

The corners of her mouth slowly curved up, just a little.

“Honestly…”

She said it softly.

“Who asked you to say that.”

Her agent looked up at the rearview mirror:

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Choi Jin-ri hugged the phone to her chest.

After a few seconds, she picked it up again.

Typed.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Choi Jin-ri

Got it.

Paused.

Deleted it.

Typed again.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Choi Jin-ri

Mm, I won’t say anything reckless.

Paused again.

Deleted it again.

In the end, she sent one line.

KakaoTalk · Baek Si-on

Choi Jin-ri

Then you rest early too.

After sending it, she thought for a moment, then added a goodnight sticker.

Baek Si-on did not reply.

He was probably already asleep.

Choi Jin-ri turned to look at the street scenes rolling backwards past the window.

It was strange.

A photo of an empty plate had been more reassuring than a hundred “I’m fine”s.

She locked the phone, put it in her pocket, and let out a soft breath.

It was all right.

This time he did not need her to be his harbour, so she would stand on the shore for now.

One day, when he was truly tired —

At least she would still be there.

October 29 · Los Angeles time Morning Los Angeles · Beverly Hills · the Four Seasons While America looked somewhere else, a hashtag was born in the corner of its eye.

Los Angeles time. October 29. Morning.

When Baek Si-on woke, the unread count on his phone had already turned to 99+.

He squinted at it, thinking he had not woken up properly yet.

He rubbed his eyes.

Looked again.

No change.

The moment he opened Twitter, he understood.

This was what had happened.

Last night — the same night as the Lakers’ season opener — something happened on the American internet that had completely swallowed every headline.

The iCloud vulnerability.

Hackers had exploited a security flaw in Apple’s cloud service to break into the private accounts of more than a hundred female celebrities.

Private photos.

Private videos.

All dug out.

And dumped onto Reddit, 4chan, Twitter and every gossip forum there was.

In a single night, the whole of America had glued its eyes to those photos and videos. Who had any attention left for a few grown men shoving each other on a basketball court?

But by that morning, the shock of the photo-leak scandal had begun to fade.

The FBI had issued a statement saying they were investigating.

Apple had issued a statement saying they were fixing it.

The lawyers had issued statements saying they were suing.

The victims had issued statements saying they were fighting for their rights.

Everything that could be said had been said.

Everything that could be condemned had been condemned.

And everything that could be looked at… had been looked at too.

So.

American netizens finally found the room to process last night’s backlogged Kobe comeback game.

The Howard elbow.

The sideline altercation.

An Asian man in a Tom Ford suit who had nearly run onto the court.

That story had started to gain heat at around seven that morning.

By nine —

It had blown up.

And the direction it blew was the exact opposite of Korea’s.

In the North American Black sports and hip-hop world — the culture of the streets, of brotherhood, of ride-or-die — what did they call something like what Baek Si-on had done?

They called it “Ride or Die”!

They called it “Real G”!

On Twitter, a large number of people inside the game came out publicly in support of Baek Si-on.

Carmelo Anthony. Allen Iverson. Matt Barnes…

Even Shaquille O’Neal jumped in to make noise.

Every NBA fan knew the bad blood between him and Howard. The two of them had been at each other for years, from the “Superman” nickname to the centre-crown succession.

So when Shaq jumped in, his tone was extremely cheerful.

He retweeted the video.

With the caption:

Twitter · Shaquille O’Neal

@SHAQ · repost

Baek (White) has more fight in him than White.

The instant that went up, the replies lost their minds laughing.

Howard’s fans naturally refused to accept it.

But a man like Shaq was a traffic black hole by nature.

Once he opened his mouth, the matter stopped being a mere sideline sideshow involving Baek Si-on, Kobe and Howard.

It became a collective vote, from the NBA’s old-school tough-guy culture, on “loyalty,” “brotherhood” and “never backing down.”

The rappers came next.

Snoop Dogg posted first.

Twitter · Snoop Dogg

@SnoopDogg · 7:04 a.m.

Korean hardman.

50 Cent retweeted the video:

Twitter · 50 Cent

@50cent · retweet

Guy didn’t even take the suit off. Too hard.

The Game knew how to fan the flames better than anyone — he posted a screenshot of Baek Si-on being held back by the Levines, captioned:

Twitter · The Game

@thegame · 7:19 a.m.

Tom Ford Gangster.

That phrase began spreading immediately.

A gangster in a Tom Ford suit.

Absurd.

But perfect for the internet.

Then the story began moving outward from the basketball world to the hip-hop world.

From the hip-hop world to the music world.

And finally across the whole of pop culture.

Every circle was saying the same thing in its own way —

This Asian man had real loyalty.

Whether he threw a punch or not did not matter. Whether he could beat Howard did not matter.

What mattered was that when Kobe was elbowed, cursed at and cornered, Baek Si-on stood up.

That was attitude.

That was a position.

That was “Ride or Die.”

Carried by that wave, at the same time, iTunes charts in a dozen countries began to move.

iTunes multi-country · the morning after #TomFordGangster

United States — Legend climbing

Canada — Legend climbing

Australia — Legend climbing

United Kingdom — Legend climbing

Even a few East Asian markets, which usually reacted far less strongly to an English rock anthem, saw “Legend” shoved from below and climbing again. Spotify’s real-time plays were climbing too — and the official MV’s comment section was adding new comments at a pace not seen since the day of the S4 World Championship final.

October 29 Later that morning Los Angeles · SB Projects · Scooter Braun’s office The one variable the whole campaign model had failed to price in.

SB Projects.

Scooter Braun sat in his office, watching the trending list, the real-time download curves, the Spotify backend numbers and the YouTube comment velocity on his screen.

He sat in silence for a full half a minute.

Then he swore, under his breath.

He had calculated the Billboard cycle window.

He had calculated the second-round radio timing.

He had calculated how the music-video release would stack with the Worlds hype.

He had calculated the full national-broadcast reach of Kobe’s comeback game.

He had calculated the chart loosening the “All About That Bass” plagiarism controversy might cause.

The only thing he had not calculated —

Was the cultural worship of “loyalty” and “brotherhood” in North America’s Black sports and hip-hop world.

In Korea, the moment Baek Si-on stood up had been called a “violent streak,” “embarrassing,” “no sense of public-figure decorum.”

In America, the same image was called “loyalty,” “guts,” “this guy’s a real one.”

The same action.

Two systems of judgement.

Two outcomes.

Korea was cursing.

America was canonising him.

Scooter Braun let out a sigh of feeling.

Fate could be this absurd sometimes.

An impulse that had nearly become a PR disaster had, in the end, turned into a perfect piece of street-culture marketing.

He grabbed the phone off the desk and called Baek Si-on.

“Baek.”

“Mm?”

“Tell your cousin to reschedule the flight home.”

“To when?”

Over the phone came the clatter of a keyboard — Baek Si-on was apparently checking his schedule in real time.

A few seconds later.

“Early morning the day after tomorrow.”

“That long?”

“Mm. Billboard updates tomorrow. Stay here and wait for it.”

“Wait for what?”

“For the result. If it’s number one, I’m taking you to Under Armour to get paid.”

Baek Si-on was quiet for a moment.

“Under Armour?”

“Right.”

“Not Nike?”

Baek Si-on still had a picture of the sports-brand landscape at this point in time.

Under Armour was the underdog at the bottom of the pile, with annual revenue of about three billion dollars.

Nike was at thirty billion.

“Baek, Nike is too crowded. Their basketball narrative is too mature — mature enough that once you’re in there, the most they’ll give you is a contract in the two-million-dollar range.”

“That sounds like a lot.”

“A lot? It’s less than you made singing two songs for His Highness in Dubai.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

Fair point.

Dubai, two songs: one point five million.

Nike, a whole year: two million.

“You know that Tom Ford suit from last night has already blown up? #TomFordGangster has spread from Twitter to Instagram. Hip-hop, the NBA world, fashion media — everybody’s reposting it.”

“The fact that you nearly charged Howard was dangerous in itself. But you, in that Tom Ford suit, being held back by Adam and Behati — that image has transmission power. It’s like a man in haute couture crashing into the middle of street culture.”

“You know what that means?”

Baek Si-on leaned back against the headboard.

“It means I nearly ruined a borrowed suit.”

Scooter Braun: “……”

Two seconds of silence on the other end of the line.

“Baek.”

“Mm?”

“You’re really asking for it sometimes.”

“Keep going.”

Scooter Braun took a breath.

“It means you can help Under Armour move from pure sports equipment toward lifestyle, street fashion, male-hormone culture. With that kind of crossover ability — guess what kind of money Under Armour would offer you?”

Baek Si-on thought about it.

“Three million?”

Scooter Braun laughed out loud.

“Forget it. Don’t guess.”

Baek Si-on: “……”

“We’ll settle that tomorrow.”

Scooter Braun reined in his smile.

“First, come to the office. Let’s lock down the album and the new song.”

Baek Si-on raised an eyebrow.

“New song?”

“You’ll find out when you get here.”

“Okay.”

“I’ll send a car to pick you up?”

“Sure.”

“See you in a bit.”

The call ended.

Baek Si-on put the phone down and got out of bed.

He washed up, changed.

Pushed open the door of the suite, called to Baek Eun-ah — who was out in the hall clutching her tablet, face flushed red with excitement — and set out for SB Projects together.
