Chapter Fifty-Seven

The Hair-Salon Chart Battle

July 27 Afternoon Hapjeong-dong, a corner hair salon Two songs, a testing smile, and a card that had to be opened

Hapjeong-dong. A certain hair salon.

The second shop down the alley. Out front hung a faded barber's pole, red-white-blue, turning with all the energy of a dying clock.

The shop wasn't big — four chairs, two mirrors.

When Baek Si-on walked in, the earring-wearing young staffer behind the counter looked up, let his gaze linger on Baek Si-on's face for one second longer than usual.

He said nothing.

Jung Jae-joon pointed at the black that had eaten through most of his roots in the mirror.

“Re-dye. Flaxen. Same as before.”

The barber nodded and started mixing the colour.

Baek Si-on dropped onto one of the waiting chairs, crossed his legs, and flipped open a magazine.

Not a fashion one. It was the only thing on the coffee table with Korean in it — the Seoul Waterworks Company Quarterly — and there was no explaining what it was doing in a hair salon.

He'd only turned two pages.

The background music in the shop suddenly changed.

The song before it hadn't even finished; someone cut it off mid-way, and a familiar synth tone leapt out of the speaker. A very grainy pluck, a bouncing rhythm, the kick drum pushing dull and low —

Way Back Home.

Baek Si-on cocked his head toward the counter.

Behind it sat a young man in his early twenties, some sort of assistant, his fingers just drawn back from the computer keyboard.

The young man felt Baek Si-on's gaze and looked up.

The two of them met eyes.

The young man smiled at him.

Baek Si-on slowly drew his gaze away, lifted the Waterworks Quarterly, and held it up in front of his face.

Three pages into the water-quality report, his phone rang.

Baek Eun-ah.

He answered and rested the magazine on his lap.

“Oppa, they've replied. The songwriting — they've agreed. Eighty-twenty. Us eighty, them twenty.”

Baek Si-on made a sound of acknowledgement.

“But the four points for Teacher Jung, they won't accept.”

“Reason?”

“Their exact words—”

Baek Eun-ah seemed to be scrolling through the chat log on the other end, then paused for a beat.

“‘Grammy-winning producer Max Martin took only three points when he worked with Justin. A new producer who hasn't yet earned international recognition cannot be given terms higher than the industry's own benchmark.’”

Baek Si-on pulled the phone away from his ear and angled it toward the mirror.

“What song did Grammy-winning producer Max Martin do for Bieber?”

Jung Jae-joon was sitting with his eyes shut, waiting for the dye to set, seven or eight silver foil sheets sticking up out of his head like a Christmas tree wrapped in aluminium.

“Beauty and a Beat.”

Answered fast. So fast you'd think he'd rehearsed it.

“Are you a Bieber fan?”

Jung opened one eye.

“No. Because Max Martin only ever made that one song with him. A one-off doesn't require being a fan to remember.”

“Oh.”

Baek Si-on lifted the phone back to his ear. He was just about to speak to Baek Eun-ah when —

The background music changed again.

The final syllable of Way Back Home's chorus got cut off.

The shop's speakers switched to another song.

The drum machine came in first, then a synth riff, then Nicki Minaj's voice detonated.

Beauty and a Beat.

Baek Si-on's mouth was still holding the shape of whatever he'd meant to say. He paused, then angled his head toward the counter.

The young man smiled at him again.

Mm.

Baek Si-on took his gaze back.

This shop's membership card — he'd have to get it now.

“Oppa? Hello? Are you still there?” Baek Eun-ah's voice came through the receiver.

“Wait a second.”

Baek Si-on took the phone down from his ear and set it on his lap.

Then he leaned back and listened.

Beauty and a Beat poured out of the speakers.

Nicki's rap verse drove like an over-horsepowered sports car drifting a corner, the electronic drums landing dense and hard, and Bieber's voice slid into the break — sweet, bright.

Was it good?

It was good.

The hook was crisp, the production was top of the line. Max Martin was, as expected, Max Martin.

But it wasn't the same league as What Do You Mean?

Beauty and a Beat was a passable pop-dance record.

Drop it into a 2012 playlist and it wouldn't fall behind, but it wouldn't be remembered either.

What Do You Mean? was different.

That song would define a sound, steer a whole lane, and make producers all over the world turn toward it at once.

One was “a good song.”

The other was “an era.”

Baek Si-on · on the two songs

Beauty and a Beat — a fine piece of craft. Good enough to leave you nodding, too polished to leave a mark.

What Do You Mean? — the kind of record that redraws the map. The first one earns a spot in a playlist; the second one earns a footnote in the year.

One is a song. The other is a moment.

Baek Si-on waited for the chorus to run one full lap, then looked over at the mirror.

“Teacher Jung, how did that one chart?”

Jung Jae-joon, head crowned in seven or eight sheets of silver foil, answered with his eyes closed.

“Billboard Hot 100. Peaked at number five.”

Billboard Hot 100

Beauty and a Beat

Justin Bieber

5

Peak. A fine result — and not a number one.

Five.

Not the top.

Baek Si-on turned that number over in his head.

Then he picked up the phone from his lap.

“Eun-ah.”

“Yes!”

“You tell them this.”

Baek Si-on's pace was unhurried, the gap between each word deliberately long enough for Baek Eun-ah to keep up.

“Max Martin gave Justin Bieber a song that peaked at number five on the Billboard. That's why he takes three points.”

“But the song we're handing over — it will be the first Billboard number-one debut of Justin Bieber's entire career.”

“Price matches result. The distance between a number one and a number five isn't something a single percentage point can cover. Four points. Not one point less.”

On the other end of the line, Baek Eun-ah went quiet.

She was digesting the weight of the words “first number-one debut.”

“Then... what if they still won't budge?”

“Then tell them Shawn Mendes's manager is also waiting on our reply.”

“……”

Baek Eun-ah threaded every piece of it together in her head, checked she hadn't missed a single thing.

“Understood.”

Then, cleanly, she hung up.

Baek Si-on pocketed the phone and picked the Quarterly back up.

Turned to page four.

“Notification of Progress on the 2014 Second-Quarter Yeonnam-dong Pipeline Renewal Project.”

In the mirror, Jung Jae-joon opened one eye.

“When did you get in touch with Mendes's side?”

Baek Si-on turned a page.

“I didn't.”

Jung closed his eye again.

For about three seconds.

“Boss Baek.”

“Mm.”

“You're a frightening person.”

“………”

In the speakers, the last chorus of Beauty and a Beat was playing.

The young man behind the counter stole a glance at Baek Si-on, his fingers already back on the keyboard, probably weighing what to play next.

Baek Si-on looked down at the Quarterly and gave him no clue at all.

The young man thought it over.

Then he played Way Back Home.

From the very top.

✿ ✿ ✿

Forty minutes later.

Jung Jae-joon stood up from the chair and turned his head back and forth in the mirror.

The flaxen colour had won back every inch of territory. The black at the roots had vanished completely, and the whole man looked sharper by a couple of notches.

Baek Si-on closed the Seoul Waterworks Company Quarterly — which he'd now worked through to page twenty-eight — set it back on the table, and walked to the counter.

“A membership card.”

The young man jumped a little, then hurried to dig a blank card out of the drawer.

“Load one million won.”

JB Hair · Hapjeong-dong · membership

Standard cut25,000 ₩

Priciest dye package120,000 ₩

Card load1,000,000 ₩

≈ cuts covered40 (once a month → 3+ years)

One million won.

A cut at this shop ran twenty-five thousand won; the most expensive dye package was a hundred and twenty thousand. A million was enough for forty cuts here — at a once-a-month rhythm, three years and then some.

The young man looked up at Baek Si-on, confirming whether he'd heard right.

Baek Si-on had already set the cash out on the counter.

“Open the card.”

The young man stopped hesitating and started entering the information into the system.

Jung Jae-joon watched from the side, said nothing, but the corner of his mouth twitched.

The two of them walked out the door.

The barber's pole was still turning, listlessly, its red-white-blue even paler under the afternoon sun.

Baek Si-on had made it two steps out when —

“Hey —!”

The sound of a door being pushed open, and hurried footsteps.

The young man had chased out of the shop and run up to him.

“Sir... would you happen to need a styling team? I did three years at a salon in Cheongdam-dong before this, and I've done styling for a few artists...”

Baek Si-on looked at him.

Then at Jung Jae-joon.

Jung shrugged — meaning, your call.

Baek Si-on considered it.

“I'll give you a number. Write it down.”

The young man whipped his phone out of his apron pocket in a hurry.

Baek Si-on recited Baek Eun-ah's number.

“Get in touch with her then.”

“Yes! Thank you!”

The young man bowed at nearly ninety degrees.

Baek Si-on waved a hand and kept walking.

Jung Jae-joon caught up, and the two of them went down the Hapjeong-dong alley shoulder to shoulder.

“Shovelling work over to your cousin again?”

“Mm.”

“That's called exploiting a minor.”

“That's called social training.”

“………”
